I like Anne Stuart's writing enough that I have many of her books, but she has also written many,  about which I was ambivalent, or outright hated. I liked this book and felt it was well written.I approach books on their own merit and read different books according to my mood. To me, this book was an historical gothic romance, and that is how I judged it. I don't expect a gothic romance to read the same as a straightforward romance or mystery.The characterization was good. For example, we know what Lizzie looks like and we know some of her personality quirks, hopes and dreams. She wants to throw off the rigid restrictions of her society. Her dislike of shoes and confining clothing reflects her dislike for the confining society that restricts women of her time into very circumscibed roles. She wants to marry for love rather than for social and economic factors. She wants children, but only with a man she loves. She wants freedom to be herself, to dance in the moonlight and to embrace what the old witch has taught her. She has an affinity for the forest, in particular, the oak trees. This is something she shares with the hero, who feels as she does. This tie is very significant and is what creates the strong bond between them. She does not want to spend her life trying to be dutiful, to abide by the social conventions and to never be able to express herself. She has a temper, can have a sharp tongue and has a strong character. Most of the other characters were also written well enough to let us see their looks and personalities.The plot was typical of a gothic romance, in a good way. This book had some dark overtones, but was lighter than some of Stuart's books. I don't care for her extremely dark overtones and excessively dark heroes in some of her books, so I was pleased that this book did not have that problem. Suspense added to the mood and furthered the relationship between the hero and heroine, but it wasn't a driving force, as we knew who the villains were and what they had planned. The suspense was created by not knowing if the villains were going to be able to carry out their plans.The romance and suspense elements were well balanced, although most of the book was character driven.  As in a lot of gothics, the sexual scenes were not a significant part of the book. There were a few towards the end, but they were not graphic.The setting was well done, easy to visualize, and it had the requisite ruined building, in this case an abbey. The ghosts fit perfectly into their setting, and the descriptions of how evil affected the setting were a very nice touch.The pacing was even, not very fast and not very slow. The story unfolded in a leisurely manner, although it did quicken during the fire scene.There wasn't much profanity but there was some. For the reviewers who give one star because of profanity, avoid this book. Yes, the "F" bomb is dropped.I liked this book and consider it to be well written. It is a light read that should appeal to those who like a traditional gothic romance with just a bit of sex. It isn't scary suspenseful and it isn't hot or swoony romantic; it is simply an entertaining traditional gothic romance.Read moreRead less
