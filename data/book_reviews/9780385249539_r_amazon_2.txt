As others have already said, this is a beautifully-written novel about a difficult subject. A lesser writer may have felt the need to either spoon-feed or lecture, and Alexander Stuart wisely does neither. He is more interested in the interior life of this story, in the way abuse corrodes and destroys, and this makes "The War Zone" even more challenging than it might have been otherwise. I will admit to having mixed feelings on the final act. Without giving anything away, I couldn't decide if it was inevitable and accurate, or unnecessary wish fulfillment. I'm sure Stuart was hoping for the first, but I'm not so sure.As an aside, the book makes a fascinating companion piece to the film of the same name, screenplay by Stuart, brilliantly directed by Tim Roth. Rather than a straight-ahead retelling of this story, the film takes a more punishing tone and (perhaps for the best) ends on a different note. Taken together, they both feel oddly essential, neither one negating the other. This isn't a clear case where the book is better than the movie, or vice versa. Both are very good, but not always for the same reasons.I've been careful to rate the novel itself in this review, hence the four-star rating. If I were rating the Kindle edition, however, it would get a single star, and less than that if I could. This is, without a doubt, the single worst e-book I've ever encountered. Paragraph spacing is erratic, words and single letters take up entire lines... It's a mess, plain and simple, and it distracts from the reading experience. The fact that it is so bad, and has yet to be improved, is kind of remarkable. If you do read "The War Zone," buy the physical book, or find it at a library.
