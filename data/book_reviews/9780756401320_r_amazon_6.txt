In "The Gathering Storm" fractured subplots and storylines start coming together, at last, making for a really good story. Sheer perseverance through thousands of (intermittently dull) pages finally feels rewarded. Alas, at excruciatingly slow rate. That old glaring problem of too many characters and too many subplots is particularly pronounced throughout book five. Characters and subplots are divided by literally hundreds of pages. Each time author would revisit this or that character and subplot, I’d forgotten what happened last and, quite honestly, didn’t really care anymore. One of lead protagonists re-emerges four hundred pages into the novel (first reappearance after book four). That cannot be good by any standards. I have my issues with certain characters – to the point of utter disgust and outrage – which I cannot address here without spoiling the plot for some innocent reader.Interestingly, ending of book five felt to me like natural ending to the series. A bit of epilogue could have dealt quickly with plethora of supporting characters though, again, I didn’t really care about them any longer. Knowing there are two more installments to the end of series made me weirdly nervous – since the ending of “The Gathering Storm” had the sense of finality to me, it crossed my mind that Ms. Elliott might want to introduce new storylines and subplots in new installments. Given there are way too many already, I don’t see how that would improve the series. On the other hand, given the author’s propensity for long descriptions and slow conclusions, I don’t see how she can wrap it all up in just two remaining installments. Hence the dilemma…If I hadn’t already purchased books six and seven, I would simply stop here and be (contentedly) done with the “Crown of Stars” series.
