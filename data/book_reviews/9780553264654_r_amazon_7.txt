David Gerrold's When HARLIE was One is a tale of artificial intelligence (AI). Although published in the early 70's and nominated for a Hugo, this version is a re-release in the late 80's and so has about 1.5 more decades of computer culture embedded. Basically, the tale concerns a project to build an AI, named HARLIE, for a corporation. There's the obvious conflict on the bean counters' side who are looking exclusively for ROI versus the unexpected development of the emerging AI trying to understand humanity and what it means to be human.  At the same time, the main architect of the project is a classical nerdy, somewhat Asperger-like geek who learns to be more human by his interactions with HARLIE. While parallels to HAL are evident, this AI is more of scientist. At the same time, there are hints of suggestions that with sophisticated computers, the end of science (in that any question can now be answered) is at hand and will demand a new approach.Clearly in the early 70's, the theme of computers taking over was common. This story take a different angle and creates a truly learning entity that is attempting to understand itself and its relationship with humans. An interesting sidebar is the distinction between morality and ethics in that the computer decides it has no morals, but does posses ethics.
