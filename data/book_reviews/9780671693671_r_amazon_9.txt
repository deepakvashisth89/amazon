Sonny Bono published And The Beat Goes On in 1991 without the help of a ghostwriter. This is the story of many ups and downs. Sonny was the offspring of a lower middle class Italian American family growing up in California. He didn’t have much scholastic or even musical aptitude but somehow forced himself to become a songwriter through cheer willpower while working in odd jobs. From there he rose to A&R man and producer at various LA indie labels before first co-writing "Needles And Pins" with the genial Jack Nietsche before becoming the assistant of the legendary Phil Spector. This gave him a usable level of cred for later endeavors. Sonny’s rise to fame with Cher on the back of "I Got You" is well documented. However, by 1967, the summer of love, the anti-drug and moderate drinkers Sonny and Cher had become cheesy in the eyes of the kids who were looking for the doors to perception that others were keener to provide. Sonny botched up two films and wound up nearly bankrupt. At this time, Cher was eager for more fame and perhaps more of a boost than Sonny could provide. Nevertheless, by the early 1970s shlock was back in fashion and the Sonny And Cher Comedy Hour was a hit on TV. It just masked the disintegrating Las Vegas style relationship. Sonny was the loser in this split, but eventually picked himself up with odd film parts and comedy appearances. Eventually he even became a restaurant owner and cook. That brings him to become mayor of Palm Springs, on a Republican ticket, of all things. Sonny, unfortunately died a very un-Rock N’ Roll death by colliding with a tree while skiing in 1998. We are therefore lucky to have this well thought out, multi-dimensional and in depth document of an exciting era.
