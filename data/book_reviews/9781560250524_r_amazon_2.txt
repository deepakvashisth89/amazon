It seems to me this book is valuable for a number of reasons, and I'm approaching it as an interested citizen. I have no stake in any of the controversies discussed in the other reviews. I give the work a 4 instead of a 5 primarily due to the WAY it is written. I felt that the author was using his narration not only to uncover important details related to why Americans should question official views, but additionally to show his frustration in working for "the last investigation" committee. That is, via the narrative style used, the reader may at times feel frustrated in parallel with the author's frustration due to the study's copious detail and plethora of names etc. These can be difficult to keep track of and somewhat tedious, even though presented in the spirit of thoroughness and honesty. At times I wanted a quicker, clearer jump to conclusions. However, this shortcoming, in my view, is much outweighed by the value of the study overall.A valuable aspect of Fonzi's approach is to make a reader think carefully about official conclusions. He is thus highly convincing there was a conspiracy, one of the book's major emphases. Another strong theme is involvement of the CIA in some way with Oswald prior to the assassination and in the assassination itself. A third major idea is the nature of government investigations as limited by political considerations, including "lookin' good" (Fonzi's language) as well as reluctance to dig too deeply due to pressures from powerful forces in industry and the military.From these main themes the reader can move on into various considerations with a good basis or framework for further thinking and research. His final point is also worth mentioning, that when people are kept confused (aided for example by mainstream press efforts) denial is typical, which also divides a public and prevents significant response.
