I first read this 1970 crime book some years ago (it is the first in the Jemima Shore mystery series) and it is a joy to have rediscovered them on kindle.Jemima Shore discovers a minor story in a newspaper - "Nun Found Dead".  The nun is question was Sister Rosabelle Mary Powerstock, Sister Miriam, of Blessed Eleanor's Convert, who was found dead in a locked building in convent grounds.  Jemima is transported back to the Wartime, when she was a protestant day girl sent to a Catholic boarding convent and Rosa was one of her schoolfriends.  When Reverent Mother Ancilla contacts her, asking for her help, Jemima goes but is resistant to staying and investigating.  Mother Ancilla explains Sister Miriam had a breakdown.  That she owned the convent lands and, after watching one of Jemima's investigative journalism TV shows, she wanted to give the land she owned to the poor.  Jemima had recently done a show on The Powers Estate in London (also owned by Rosa) and had interviewed the fanatical Alexander Skarbeck, who wished to turn prime real estate into a commune.  Was the convent to go the same way?  A note, left by Rosa, said, "Jemima knows", but Jemima doesn't know anything and had lost contact with Rosa.Jemima is having an affair with Tom Amyas MP. Actually, this was the one part of the book I found a little troubling.  Throughout the book, Jemima is presented as having high moral standards and looked up to by everyone, and yet she is constantly slighting of Tom's wife, who is never seen.  I think perhaps the author had a personal axe to grind here!  When Jemima discusses the problem with him though, Tom is critical of the nun', making Jemima defensive. When he cancels a proposed holiday together, she agrees to stay at the convent and investigate.What follows is a joyous cozy crime.  There are mysterious notes, the legend of the 'Black Nun', secret passages, spooky towers and missing wills.  Also a glorious cast of characters, including nuns I remember from my own childhood in a convent school - the driving ability of nuns certainly rang true to me!  Jemima befriends the girls Sister Miriam favoured, creeps around the grounds at night with a torch and, of course, solves the mystery.  This book certainly does read as a novel set in the 1970's, but the story is great and does not appear dated.  I look forward to reading the rest of the series and rediscovering Jemima Shore.  If you only know Antonia Fraser from her (excellent) historical biographies, you might like to give these a try.  She is a great crime writer and it is a shame she never kept writing her crime books alongside her biographies.
