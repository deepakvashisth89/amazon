This slim book was one of the first of a new line of Star Trek novels spawned in the mid 70's, as Paramount belatedly realized that not only would Trekkies shell out cash for all the James Blish-authored books, but would also cheerfully shell out cash for new material. Thus was started the publishing phenomenon that has come to dominate a significant chunk of retail SF shelf space.Unfortunately, `The Starless World' is one of the more limp entries in the Star Trek library.While on a research mission near the center of the galaxy, the Enterprise encounters a wayward shuttlecraft piloted by a deranged man named Thomas Clayton who, it turns out, is Captain Kirk's former classmate at the Star Fleet Academy.  After Clayton is brought aboard, babbling nonsense about a `god' named `Ay-nab', the Enterprise finds itself in the grip of a super-powerful tractor beam. The ship is soon pulled into the interior of an enormous Dyson Sphere, constructed by some long-vanished alien race. The Sphere contains its own solar system, consisting of the planet Lyra; its moons; and a Sun, where Ay-nab presumably resides.The Enterprise landing party discovers that a Starfleet vessel, the Rickover, has been marooned on Lyra, along with a party of (predictably) surly Klingons. The planet sports a modest city called Tumara, whose inhabitants are something like Ewoks. These inhabitants mutter cryptic warnings about `Strangers' who come out at night and do unpleasant things. The natives are unaware that the Dyson Sphere is pelting towards a Black Hole, which will of course remove Lyra and all its people from existence. Much of the narrative is devoted to Kirk's efforts to discover who, or what, Ay-nab is, why he has chosen to maroon spaceships within his Dyson Sphere, and what it will take to get the Enterprise safely out of danger.Even when considering the constraints imposed on licensed fiction, `World' `s plot is thin gruel, and I suspect many readers will see it as a tired rehash of several storylines used in the original TV series. Author Eklund tries to liven things up by introducing some mysterious goings-on in every other chapter, but these bits really don't introduce much in the way of drama or suspense.I suspect that even hard-core Trekkies will probably find `World' one of more underwhelming entries in the Canon.Read moreRead less
