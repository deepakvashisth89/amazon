"Nathan Myhrvold's pudgy fingers whirred across the keyboard as he peered into his 21-inch monitor."The first sentence of this insider's account tells you pretty much all you need to know about the rest of the book: that the author is not a fan of Myhrvold (at the time Chief Technology Officer for Microsoft), that he's going to try and make software development and - well, typing - sound exciting, and that it was written a very long time ago, when the size of your monitor was something noteworthy, and to be envied.  As such, the stories about the battles within Microsoft between the Windows NT and Windows 95 groups, the changing priorities on OS/2 and the relationship between Internet Explorer and Windows 98 can seem arcane (if, like me, you can remember them) or irrelevant at this distance, but they're told in a lively and entertaining fashion.One peculiar aspect of this book is that the main author (who worked at Microsoft, and was embroiled in the technical conflicts described here) chooses to write in the third person, as if he was a character in the narrative.  This doesn't always make for a well-rounded, objective account of the company's fortunes during this period, but it provides an interesting insider's view on how things ended up the way they did.Read moreRead less
