I was a little doubtful about this book because I knew going in that the hero, Matt, was married at the beginning of the book. In fact he was a couple of months off obtaining a divorce from his long estranged wife when he meets Amy, the heroine.He tries to resist her but he's head over heels and so is Amy, who pushes and pushes for a physical relationship. Then a nasty woman reveals the fact that he's married and it's all over.The story picks up three years later when Matt comes to the small village in Cornwall where Amy lives with her grandfather, supporting herself with a small typing, secretarial business she runs from his cottage.  He is all harsh and punishing kisses which is par for the course with these heroes when the heroine mistrusts them and runs.One thing that really got to me was the smoking. It's 1981 and the heroine and hero both smoke like chimneys. She is also a perfect cook. PERFECT. So irritating.Overall this was an okay read. The semi-adultery didn't bother me a great deal because it was clear what his situation was from the start. We get a bit of hero internal thought which was rather nice for a book of that era. The ending was satisfactory considering the heroine was a bit immature and his behaviour flaunting another woman was pretty pointless apart from upping the angst for the heroine.Read moreRead less
