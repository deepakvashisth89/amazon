Having read the other reviews I expected more about the Ilois reaction to being evicted from their home, but it seemed more like a story of how their everyday lives unfolded and there was little if any account of whether they resisted; perhaps the point is that being such uncomplicated people who had never had to defend themselves or deal with challenges to their lifestyle they did not know how to resist and were uncaringly removed and effectively left to fend for themselves in a more complex environment for which they were ill-prepared. The story certainly gets through to you how the lives of the story's characters changed and how they became less happy and had to adapt to a more brutal culture than the simple lifestyle they had known beforehand. They struggled to cope; some coped better than others and some tragically could not cope. To get the most from this book it is necessary to bear in mind the historical context; I think that might have been easier if some of the details and facts that precede and follow the story were woven into the main body, but that is a minor criticism and I'm sure arguments to keep them separate might be equally as valid looking at it from another angle. Well written and vividly portrays life for the Ilois on Diego Garcia and later Mauritius. Quite profound and thought provoking, especially reflecting afterwards.
