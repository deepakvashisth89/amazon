He was a Vietnam vet, and as we were driving back from the Reception station one day I asked him, OK you did time in the field under fire, what is the best book to read on what it is like.  This is the book that he recommended, the year was 1983.When I read this book I came away stunned, here I was joining up, but Mr Rivers and Mr. Hudson really painted the picture of what it was like to be in the jungle, behind enemy lines, infiltrating China via the Ho Chi Minh Trail and then fire fighting their way all the way home.  In many ways as I look back on it, this story reminds me of the Story of the Battle of Cunaxa of 401BC, and the film "The Warriors" that came out the same year as this book was published.Later on I actually entered active duty and by 1985, I had the good fortune to go to Fort Sherman Panama, where with a squad of mostly Ranger qualified infantrymen, I worked the course requirements to achieve the badge of Jungle Warfare Expert.  That training with those men, changed me into a professional soldier.  This book speaks to the story (real life experience is my strong suspicion) of what it means to be that kind of a professional soldier who makes it happen when it needs to happen.If it's fiction, it was written by someone who was there, that much my former recruiter and every other Vietnam vet could agree on who read this book.  As a former soldier, when you read this book, you can feel as though you are a part of the action.  A great read for all who want to know what kind of attitudes make up the professional soldier.
