Mark Twain's trip through the Mediterranean and Middle East was both informative and humorous. A reader needs to be tolerant of Twain's period bigotry, but it's amazing how little has changed for tourists to these areas. Guides still steer you to stores and businesses that surely provide commissions. The conflicts between religions at the Church of the Holy Sepulcher still occur often.His description of those he calls "pilgrims" who go around chipping off chunks of every religious or historical sites is a riot. Also his description of Americans trying to converse in a sort of pigeon French is hilarious. Not much has changed. I actually learned a lot from this book. E.g., even though I had visited Venice twice, I had never become aware of the Committee of Three and theirpower.  Readers who have a historical bent will really enjoy this bookRead moreRead less
