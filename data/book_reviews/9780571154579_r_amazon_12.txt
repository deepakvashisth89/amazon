Spender wrote this novel in 1929/30. Because its central characters (Spender, Auden and Isherwood, all thinly disguised) were in relationships with other men, the book could not be published at the time, even though it is very discreet by today's standards.Spender sold the manuscript to an American university and it was only rediscovered in the 1980s, when he re-wrote parts of it and moved the final section forward to 1932.As with Isherwood's pre-war Berlin novels, Spender evokes powerfully both the atmosphere of sexual liberation in Berlin, as well as the frightening rise of the extremism which was to bring it to a halt and to lead to the horrors of Nazism.As a novel, the book works best as an expression of youthful passion, rebellion and self-discovery. There is relatively little plot as such but the book's characters are intriguing and the portraits of Auden and Isherwood vivid and lifelike.When the book was published in the 1980s, Spender noted in his Journal that it had sold 5,000 copies in France on the strength of its cover.Read moreRead less
