First published in 1952 and last revised in 1974, this remains a superb, and not overlong (234 pages of main text) book about England in the Anglo-Saxon period. Readers looking for a political and military history will be disappointed. This is a book of social, legal, economic, ecclesiastical and cultural history, and it gives a good and interesting insight into the period, with lots of fascinating detail. The style is readable and clear, and next to no previous background knowledge is assumed: the first part of a short preface gives a sufficient narrative outline of the political history for everything else to fall into place.There are passages which refer to the prominent role of women and to marriage and the family, but, if the book were being revised today, more about women would probably be included - and probably also something on leechcraft - traditional medical knowledge and practice, much of it now confirmed as efficacious.Thoroughly recommended for anyone interested in the period.Read moreRead less
