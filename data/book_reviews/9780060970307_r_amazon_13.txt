First published in 1984 and republished with a new foreward by the author in 2017, Janet Morgan's 'Agatha Christie' is a good, solid biography of the crime writer and one which deals very fairly with everyone involved in Mrs Christie's life. This is an authorised biography and one which for her research Ms Morgan was allowed full access to Agatha Christie's letters, diaries and papers and was also assisted ably by Mrs Christie's daughter, Rosalind - who, until 1981, resisted strongly the writing of a biography of her mother as she believed, as did Agatha Christie, that a writer's work should stand by itself and that the context of and the motives for its production should be irrelevant to the readers' enjoyment. Irrelevant or not, Janet Morgan's easily accessible account has provided enough information about her subject to allow the reader to access the life of the very prolific crime writer, who sold millions and millions of her novels featuring the mystery-solvers: Hercule Poirot, Miss Marple and Tommy and Tuppence, but has not delved too deeply into the areas of Mrs Christie's life that no one (other than perhaps the writer's closest family) need know. Ms Morgan does, of course, write about Agatha Christie's famous disappearance in 1926 after her husband revealed he had fallen in love with another woman, but there is nothing here to make the reader feel that they are snooping into areas that the subject, who was a very private woman and one who shunned any kind of publicity, would prefer to be kept private. So, as commented previously, a good, solid biography, and one I would recommend - I haven't read the more recent biography by Laura Thompson but, having read this, I feel no real need to do so.4 Stars.Read moreRead less
