I have read Robert Kaplan for years, mostly in The Atlantic, where I tended read him knowing I would disagree with him. His school of political analysis is of the Darwinian 'dog eat dog' slash Hobbes' life is 'solitary, poor, brutish and short' school, and the US should be a tougher dog. This book is certainly in character, but I have to say I found it a good read. Being nearly completely ignorant about the Balkans when I picked it up, I learned a lot of the basic infrastructure of power and conflict in the region (how accurate it is beyond basic historical facts, I have to wonder, given the other detailed Amazon reviews. Thank you, BTW!). What's important about it for Americans, along with Jerusalem: A Biography, and other detailed regional studies, is just how amazingly little Americans know about centuries of history and how that impinges on the present. (Heck, we don't even have a good grip on the implications of our own civil war.) Then we wade (or swagger or waddle) into the midst of complicated regional conflicts without an f____ing clue. So, the thing about Balkan Ghosts is that it makes you stop and think and question and realize there is a lot more you need to learn.
