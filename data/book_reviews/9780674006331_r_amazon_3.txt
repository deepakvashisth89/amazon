This book is not what you think.  It's not so much a U.S.News-style ranking of public intellectuals, per se.  It's even less another of a never-ending stream of "dumbing-down" theses intended to convince us that things were so much better during the Roman Empire.  No, it's not that.  It IS an indictment of academic specialization.More specifically, Posner uses a greatly oversimplified microeconomic model to show how the "market" for intellectual products forces would-be public intellectuals into the academy.  Within the academy they are encouraged to specialize.  Here's the kicker:  Academic specialization undermines the intellectuals' ability and motivation to make meaningful statements about broader public matters.  The results are a largely academic intellectual debate dominated by esoteric, jargon-ridden theses which fail to engage the general public and are frequently dubious in merit.Worth a read if you're interested in such matters but, beware, the presentation itself is tedious and repetitive.  The best bit is the chapter debunking modern, "jeremiad," decline literature.  The polemical material of Bloom, Rorty, and Berman doesn't hold much credibility with Posner.Read moreRead less
