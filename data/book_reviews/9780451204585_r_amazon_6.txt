...For the lover of mystery and history, "The Case of the Ripper'sRevenge" is almost unreadable. It is teeming with historical errors infact, tone and dialogue -- indeed one might forgive inaccurate perioddialogue if it made up for the lapse with edgy intriguing stuff.Sadly, it does not. The characters are all paper dolls empowered withtrite and cliche ridden dialogue best left to the poorer romancenovels.The mystery plot itself is dull and listless -- announcingits very modest twists pages in advance.  Any avid detective reader who can't figure out the complete story arc and every one of its supposed surprises by page 15 ought to hang up his or her mental deerstalker hat for good.John Darnell is a detective soblandly wholesome and forthright that I'm surprised the author doesn't allow him to take fresh baked goods to local shut-ins. I kept hoping Darnell'struly annoying wife Penny (obviously she is meant to come off the pageas charming, plucky and clever) would contract a severe case of typhoid, waste away with unseemly haste and die.  With real regret I must report that this did not happen.  Poor old George Bernard Shaw is forced into making an appearance as the token real historical figure.  He gets to give ludicrous -- although unintentionally hilarious -- coaching sessions to Darnell's sidekick on how to be working class.  All of Shaw's in-depth social theory later tranlates into Darnell's bait knowing she ought to twirl her little purse as she walks along a Whitechapel street.For concrete evidence of the book's insufferable sloppiness -- please note that the character of Mrs. Lorraine Sheffield is pointedly and specifically described as a wealthy widow when introduced into the story. Yet only a few pages later, that intrepid detective John Darnell is meeting her grief stricken husband Harley.  Like Mark Twain, apparently reports of his death had been greatly exaggerated.Lovers of good historical mysteries would be better servedpurchasing one of Stephanie Barron's far superior Jane Austenmysteries. ...Read moreRead less
