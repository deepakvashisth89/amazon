Thanks to Curtis Sittenfeld for her foreward in the new edition, which helped clarify why I loved this book so much when I read it ten years ago, and why I loved rereading it even more. I also feel this book was written for me, and that Daum is incapable of dull writing. This book has deserved way more credit that it has received, and I suspect the problem is its billing as comic, or chick lit, because then readers feel betrayed by the dark turns, the unflinching observations of humanity. For while TQOLR is sharp, hilarious, and a quick read, it's ha-ha in the way that gives you a stitch, as in, "ow." If Daum were a man and had written about war, everyone would be reading this book in the public schools. But no, she writes about everyday hurt, and how naive expectations of living the pastoral American Dream might lead to a faceful of stallion jizz.Read moreRead less
