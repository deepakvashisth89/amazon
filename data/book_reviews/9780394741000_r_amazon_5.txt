Nearly every Peter Dickinson book is a tour de force, and this is one of my favorites.  Like many of them, it juxtaposes two different times, which reverberate and enrich each other.  In this case,  the first half is set in immediately post-war England, where everything is poised to change (the narrator is a Nancy Mitord-esque debutant); the second half takes events thirty years into the future (she is now shoring up her family's great country house by running it as a tourist attraction and writing bodice-rippers).  There's a lively plot, but the highlight of the book is the intelligence of the narrator-- Dickinson creates great female characters-- and of the narrative structure, which means we are first seeing through the eyes of a twenty-one year old, and then revisiting events, family dynamics, social roles, expectations from life, and so on, as a fifty-year old.Read moreRead less
