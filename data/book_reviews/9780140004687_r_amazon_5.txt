Here we are at the dawning of a new century when we read from an author at the beginning of the previous one, writing about the end of the century before that.Time aside, great characters are timeless when introduced to us with the finely honed craft and art of a masterful writer.The main character is based--loosely or not--on the personage of the artist Paul Gauguin. I've read few accounts of any artist that give such an insight to what a true artist really is. That, in my opinion, is one who creates art without regard to whether it will sell or be accepted, it is one who creates art--regardless of the medium, whether painting, sculpting, composer, writing--become it is necessary, required, or even an obsession from within.Too many artists would simply be or do something else if there were no monetary awards or even teaching positions associated with their art. The true artist creates without the safety of position or approval of anyone else.This is the character of Charles Strickland, stockbroker and family man turned obsessive artist.The descriptions of the art by the first person narrated are quite fascinating and illuminating, especially when comparing those given loosely at the beginning to those at the end.This is one of the great works of the 20th Century!Read moreRead less
