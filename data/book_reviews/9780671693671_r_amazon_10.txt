Sonny Bono can't sing and can barely manage a few chords on the piano; enough to hack out second-rate songs that despite being a producer himself, he hands over to better producers than he to knock into shape. This is Sonny's own appraisal of himself, for in this self-penned autobiography, he eviscerates himself with a clear and painful honesty.It takes him a long while, though, to discover that Cher feels Sonny & Cher are holding her back from the stardom she is capable of achieving without Sonny. A novelty act when they first arrived in the second half of 1965, they had been left behind within two years as pop and rock mutated into drug-fuelled psychedelia.Their long-running Sonny & Cher Comedy Hour brings them a second life, but the bitchy on-camera repartee is a little too real. They make two movies, which bomb. They divorce.What happens next is that Cher becomes a superstar, which she remains to this day. Poor Sonny however doesn't know what to do. His solo tv show bombs. As does his nightclub act. He opens a restaurant in Los Angeles, and quickly discovers that novelty wears off in the dining-out business as quickly as it does in show business.Retreating to Palm Springs, he tries to open another restaurant, but finds himself up against the vested interests of the town, who have the place sewn up very nicely for themselves. This is when Sonny's greatest natural quality, his drive to succeed, comes again into play. He becomes mayor.The book, which is absorbing to read and reveals a very human and engaging man, ends as his first term as mayor ends. He mentions that he might run for the Senate. As we know, that didn't work out. Instead, he was elected, twice, to Congress. At last, he is proving that he doesn't need Cher. But irony hadn't finished with him.As we close the book, we almost hear Sonny's ghostly voice saying, "Finally, I got everything I wanted. But then I skied into the tree."Read moreRead less
