The story was poignant and compelling. However on many occasions I found myself unable to sympathise with the character of Flo's best friend (I forget her name now). Flo was completely in love with a man she had met only a few times. She knows that he is married but he has told her that it is an unhappy marriage and he truly loves Flo. However, when he dies in a tragic accident we-the readers- are introduced to another character. This is a woman who will later become Flo's best friend. We are made to understand that she too had gone out on a date with Tommy (I think this is Flo's young man's name although I am not sure now..it is quite some time since I read this book)and far from being the unlucky man in an unfortunate marriage who truly loves Flo, he is a womaniser who's only motives are to feed gullible young women sob stories about his marriage (btw he IS married, but the only reason for the unhappiness is his own philandering ways because his wife too loves him dearly and desperately) and unlike what Flo felt towards him, he felt nothing more for Flo than lust and the challenge of bedding yet another vulnerable, gullible girl. Now it is understandable why in the shock of the moment this other girl doesn't mention what she knows of Tommy to Flo. But as the years pass on and they become best friends and it becomes more and more clear to everyone that Flo is wasting away her life pining after the inaccurate and idealised memory of a dead man, believing that she lost her soulmate etc. I would think a true best friend would tell her that Tommy was quite far from the image that she had built up in her mind. Yes it is painful initially, but after some time Flo would have made her peace with it and moved on with her life. This concept of telling your friends only what they already believe and keeping them in the dark as to the truth really spoiled the book for me. I found it very irritating.Read moreRead less
