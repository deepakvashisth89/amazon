When Ilan Falk is blown up in a lab explosion, no one doubts it's an accident. Until a beautiful young research assistant tells Alex Kertész that she killed the professor. Simply by wishing him dead.Scores of others wished the professor dead too, and Alex starts wondering if ill wishes really can do bodily harm. The accident begins to intrigue him and invite further study.Alex is a dream - a brilliant, handsome biochemist working in Jerusalem, half-Hungarian, half-French and fluent in several languages. He has plenty of courage - in Israel's war with Lebanon where he's badly wounded, and in the lab where he risks exposure to radioactive materials, poisonous fumes - and violence. With all these dazzling attributes, he somehow comes across as a nice guy, totally likeable.His interactions with his weird wife, his young son and the various attractive women he encounters are charmingly portrayed.The character of Alex is definitely my favorite thing about the novel. But I also loved the authenticity of the lab talk, the lab work, the conference going and the grant chasing. Author Janet Hannah is herself a biochemist, and she brings her world vividly to life in this book.Hannah is adept at creating a sense of place. Readers familiar with Jerusalem (which I'm not) should take pleasure in the author's affectionate snapshots of the various neighborhoods in the city.I liked the easygoing, casual unfolding of events, too. The narrative felt more like real life than a plot. Quite an achievement!The only negative was the mystery itself, which I found far less interesting than the characters, their relationships and the exotic setting. So regardless of some imperfections, and in view of the sheer enjoyment I got from reading this book, I really have to give it five stars.Read moreRead less
