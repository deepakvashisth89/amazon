In common with many short story collections, I found A.S. Byatt’s ’Sugar and Other Stories’, to be a rather mixed bag. I felt the first and last stories (‘Racine and the Tablecloth’ and ’Sugar’) were most probably the best of the collection, particularly ‘Racine and the Tablecloth’ which is set in a girls’ boarding school and looks at the antagonistic relationship between one of the pupils and an elderly teacher; I also found ‘The July Ghost’ an absorbing ( if poignant) tale, and I was rather enjoyably unsettled by ’In the Air’, the story of two elderly ladies, one of them visually impaired, who are preyed upon by a rather disreputable-looking young man - intent on exactly what we are not entirely sure, but whatever it is we know that it does not bode well for either of these two ladies.  And then there were other stories that I thought were less satisfying overall, but all of these pieces of shorter fiction are exquisitely written and I found Ms Byatt’s painterly language very enjoyable to read. A quote from Penelope Lively on the back of my edition states: “…a bright, sensual prose that seems to paint rather than describe” which sums up Ms Byatt’s writing very nicely indeed.4 Stars.Read moreRead less
