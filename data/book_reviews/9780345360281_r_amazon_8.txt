Private detective Thomas Black is good with kids, according to his close friend Kathy, and that helps him in his search for a missing teenage boy, Todd, who, according to his father, is displaying "deviant behavior" and might be suicidal.  Black builds a mentor-type relationship with 14-year-old younger brother, Buzz, who though not completely honest about what he knows does take him to the abandoned Milwaukee Hotel in Chinatown where Todd is squatting.  He also takes him to the home of the cultic author of a lusty fantasy novel whom Todd is investigating.  It all seems to be associated with the suicide of the boys' uncle 6 years ago and missing money, though exactly how is not clear to Thomas as he works the investigation .One thing I really like about this book is that Black's kindness and support of Buzz bring a new dimension--and a heartwarming one--to his character.  It is good to see Thomas less wrapped up in sexy women in this case.  I also like the location of the action which mostly takes place in Seattle's Chinatown, and particularly in the Milwaukee Hotel and tunnels that are under it.In this book Black's friend Kathy has moved back into his basement apartment, which seems to presage a deeper relationship eventually.  In the meantime, Black has an odd sort of romantic relationship going on with a woman he knows little about.A note about Kathy:  I find her an annoying character.  In addition to her just being too beautiful to be true, her constant showing up around Black in states of undress seems rather tartish.  Sometimes Emerson tries too hard to make Kathy outlandish and unique.A note about Emerson's writing.  In this book, as the case progresses, Emerson doesn't always use present tense action.  He describes some of Black's investigative activity in the past tense and occurring over a period of days.
