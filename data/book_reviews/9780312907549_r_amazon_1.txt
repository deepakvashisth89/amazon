I picked up Grinding It Out after watching the movie The Founder, and I’m glad I did. The film gives you the broad strokes, but the book is a deeper dive into the hustle, financial grit, and relentless drive that made Ray Kroc’s story so fascinating. Since it’s written by Kroc himself, you definitely get a biased perspective, especially when it comes to the McDonald brothers, who come across as a lot more like villains here than in the movie. Still, it’s interesting to compare the two and see how the story shifts when told from his own viewpoint.As a business owner, what hit me hardest was learning how many failures Kroc had throughout his life before striking gold. He wasn’t a young hotshot, he took a huge gamble later in life when most people would have been ready to play it safe. That lesson alone is worth the read, because it’s a reminder that it’s never too late to chase something big and not let age or fear convince you that your prime has passed.The financial details and behind-the-scenes of how the franchise deal actually grew were some of my favorite parts. Kroc didn’t just stumble into success, he had to outwork, outmaneuver, and outlast setbacks to get there. It’s a quick and valuable read, especially for entrepreneurs who want to see the less glamorous side of building a brand.
