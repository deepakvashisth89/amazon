This book is what I refer to as a razorblader (that's a good thing). Matt is 15, and new to the ritzy community of Sheldrake, Connecticut. He has been making friends with three local girls-all of whom are 16. One Friday  the girls ask him to go out with them, and he agrees. Tara really likes  Matt, and it was her idea to ask him out with the group. But when she gets  home, she discovers her mother is in labor, and she is expected to go the  hospital with her parents. Matt goes out to a party with the other girls  where they proceed to drink too much, and the driving responsibility falls  on the too-young-to-drive Matt. Tragedy, of course, strikes. This is about  the summer of grief that follows the accident, and how the town &  particular individuals handle it. I truly felt the grief and healing  through Nancy Woodruff's words and phrases.
