Although I felt like This was sort of a slow go for me at the beginning, in the end I felt like the exposition was quite necessary as Marsh puts together the pieces that will later follow. It seemed like Marsh spends quite a good deal of time setting up the subsequent mystery at hand. During this time (at least the first 100 pages or so) for a big chunk of exposition where we learn not only about many of the cast of characters, but also in and outs and background of The Dolphin Theatre and many of the key ingredients to the plot that will set up the murder mystery, namely an amazing artifact, the actual glove of Hamnet as well as some background into two principle characters, playwright Peregrine Jay as well as eccentric owner, Vassily Conducis.My early skepticisms were quite unfounded. I came to really enjoy the reading experience of Death at the Dolphin. I think things were nicely plotted and fell into place quite well, and the pace definitely picks up once the murder is afoot and a certain Roderick Alleyn enters the scene to begin his investigation into said murder (and theft). Clearly Marsh’s love of acting, the stage and theatre is on display and influenced in this book and I found it a fun backdrop for the murder investigation.As far as the various cast of players, I had a difficult time in the beginning understanding who was who, but I think there is enough in the way of dialogue to decipher a little of their personalities by the end of the novel (as some of these personalities really come to light as we head towards the big reveal and unearthing of the crime). The various players offered the right amount of conflict, shenanigans, and drama (of the other sort) to create not only well placed tension, but a smidge of comic relief at times.I’m not very good at figuring out the murderers in mysteries, so suffice to say I was quite intrigued during the latter portions of Death at the Dolphin to find out how the puzzle would come together. In the end, it was quite a fun and entertaining read. This was my first exposure to reading any of Marsh’s novels, so I’m very interested in reading more like this one (with the theatre as the background) or any in the Alleyn series.
