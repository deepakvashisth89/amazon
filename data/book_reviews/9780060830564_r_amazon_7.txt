Black Boy by Richard Wright is a good memoir but it's a little common topic but it's better than other topics, he talks about his interesting life, in a way that attract the reader, but also he uses a lot of unnecessary emotions that will complicate you, in my perspective I didn't like it a lot, because he is repeating his emotions, and that he makes the book very hard to read or to understand what is happening, and like I said he is using a qualified but common topic "Racism" and according to "Virginia Woolf" and Genzlinger the book is fine to be a good memoir This memoir does not follow what Genzlinger states for a good memoir; to be a unique, strange, and inserting story but Richard only talks about his life and wants the readers to pity him. Richard Wright does not write something in which we could learn or something that is not really ordinary, instead, he writes about how his family is poor and doesn't have enough food for him to eat during the time when blacks and whites were still separate. That is an ordinary life of a black person, it's not as if he was the only black person who experiences this. It's nothing important or an unusual experience that we never heard of before.All of the black people during that time all experienced racism, poor, and hunger. An example from the book that even shows that Richard Wright is not the only person who is experiencing all of these tragedies or feelings is when he talks about the kids at the orphan homes. The orphan homes are poor, the some of the orphans living in the orphan homes do not have enough to eat, and furthermore, they don't even have a family or an actual thing to care for in life, and Richard have a family. The only things from Richard Wright's Black Boy that makes a good memoir according to Genslinger's review is that you can say that he is a very good writer because he was able to use strong word, and he was able to make the reader feel as if he experienced the story with Richard in the book.He also lived the whole experience so he know what he is talking about but at the end he is just adding word that doesn't make any sense, he made himself the most important character in the book and ignored the other, he didn't mention his brother at all, he mentioned his mother sometimes, but whenever a new character introduce he involved for little, he has a good perspective that you can't get anything you want and that you have to be thankful but in a strange way, Richard learns and grow smarter and more thoughtful as time passes throughout the book, personally I didn't like it because it was little hard for me, and the ideas wasn't very clear.
