A gripping mesmerizing narrative of the Revolutionary War along the border regions, particularly, New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey.  Also, highlights the ascending colonial morale concerning the "... joy inspired by a momentous declaration made on the fourth of July [1776] ... that proudly bespoke the formation of a brand new country calling itself the United States of America.  A declaration of independence" (Page 81).Sir William Johnson was appointed to serve as the prestigious Indian Supervisor overseeing all the tribes in the King's authority.  He lived for 20 years, until death, with an Iroquois woman named Molly (aka Degonwadonti) and their 8 children.  Joseph Brant (aka Thayendangea) was Molly's younger brother who shared Sir William's British loyalty.  After Sir William's death, Brant was unanimously appointed War Chief of the formidable Iroquois League.  His fervor for the crown was enhanced upon his traveling to England and visiting with the peers.  His allegiance was solidified by uniting his warriors with Colonel John Butler's Tory Rangers, British Regulars, and Royal Green Rangers, whereby they raided, ambushed, and devastated, numerous American homes, settlements, forts, along the border, all in the name of the King.To counter the wanton terrorism, the "... [Continental] Congress passed a resolution authorizing [Commander-In-Chief] General [George] Washington to take the most efficacious measures for protecting the inhabitants of the states and chastizing the Indians" (Page 287).  In that regard, Major General John Sullivan was charged to accomplish that task.  His force was large, in fact, when "... the whole army [was] set in motion  ... [f]rom its head to its rear, the army spread out for over two miles" (Page 327).A brilliant compelling adventure thriller; unlike a boring history textbook.  A warning of instances pertaining to graphic gruesome mayhem.  The maps greatly increased one's comprehension.  Overall, a phenomenal true tale of duty to British King George, or, to the newborn United States.Enthusiastically recommend the preceding volumes comprising "The Winning Of America" series.
