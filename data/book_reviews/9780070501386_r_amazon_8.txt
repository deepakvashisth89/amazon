The Goods:(1)This book is very affordable compare to other books on the same subject.(2) The material contained in this book is as good or even better than other introductory books on abstract algebra.The bad:(1) Somewhat disorganized. The appendix on set theory and mathematical induction should include relations, functions, equivalence class sets, and the peano system. As well as finite sets and infinite sets. Instead, all of this set theoretic topics are scattered throughout the book.(2). The book is not that rigorous. For example, Pinter will talk about mathematical induction. But won't talk about the Peano system. Pinter will say that the intergers satisfy the properties of an integral domain but won't prove it. The recursion theorem is not proved neither. Since the recursion theorem is not proven in this book, Pinter simply grants recursive functions without justifying its existence. For example, the fibonnaci sequence is granted out of thin air.Recommendations:For those who want to learn how to construct the number systems from the axioms of set theory and at the same time learn algebra, then read " Number systems and the ....." by Elliot Mendelson.
