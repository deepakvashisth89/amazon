This is my second reading of this book, separated by 15 years.  I fondly remembered it as an hilarious work, yet this time halfway through I realized that I, and most of my family, lurk in the pages.  We Southerners have families loaded with women who "go to pieces" and men who think they still live in the Middle Ages.  We have legacies of spoiled, sassy belles and proud, wounded gallants still fighting the War Between the States.  Sometimes we leave Mama's house shuttered for decades because "she never wanted her things disturbed". That which would cause hardly a concern in Omaha becomes a major issue in Richmond. Although the names change the cast is the same.  It's all here, and none of it is made up-each character continues to thrive by the thousands in the South.
