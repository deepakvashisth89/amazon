There is nothing more gratifying than reading a well-written novel, such as George Gissing’s  "New Grub Street", which has a rather linear approach to the story; nevertheless, presenting a complexity and symbolic aspect to its characters and essence to the beautifully written plot.  The trials and tribulations of each character in the novel gradually evolved and elicited certain personal emotions for the reader. I’m happy I discovered this book; it’s one of the best I have read; I was immediately engaged. Undoubtedly, there are many abstract nonlinear novels written that have become classics, such as, "Ulysses", "The Sound and the Fury" and others, but those kinds of books don’t evoke similar emotions or personal feelings because it’s not as easy to picture the complexities."New Grub Street" offers a very engaging story with remarkable aesthetic appeal. Gissing’s emphasis on sensory language, elaborate descriptions, and attention to form and style transports readers to a story that is beautifully transcribed as a work of art.I submit an illustration of the aesthetics of his marvelous prose:  “Alone in her room she sat down only to think of Jasper Milvain, and extract from the memory of his words, his looks, new sustenance for her hungry heart. Jasper was the first man who had ever evinced a man’s interest in her….He was as far as possible from representing the lover of her imagination, but from the day of that long talk in the fields near Wattleborough the thought of him had supplanted dreams. On that day she said to herself: I could love him if he cared to seek my love. Premature, perhaps; why yes, but one who is starving is not wont to feel reluctance at the suggestion of food ... .Her womanhood went eagerly to meet him.”The aesthetic of his language as is the language of other authors of his era is obsolete and defined as flowery. But keep in mind that aestheticism in literature, which today we essentially define as flowery literature, is what produced authors such as: Melville, Dickens, Wilde, Austen, Hawthorne, Hardy, Eliot, Brontë, et al.. These are writers that we study and admire because of their style and the beauty of their words–an aesthetic literary style that doesn’t exist with today’s modern authors.
