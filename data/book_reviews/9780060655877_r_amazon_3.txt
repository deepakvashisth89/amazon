I like this book, precisely because a lot of religious people hate it. It approaches the gospel tradition in a wholistic scholarly way that leaves little room for the feelings of modern denominations' particular beliefs about this and that. It addresses the text on the page. It gives important context. I find the footnotes helpful. These aren't the most poetic translations I've ever read of books like the Gospel of Mary or the Gospel of Thomas, but they're solid translations I feel are fairly trustworthy. It's interesting to be able to read the more obscure and fragmentary gospels like the 'Secret Gospel of Mark', the 'Infancy Gospel of Thomas' and other such Jesus movement fan fic. I have a very different perspective on the Jesus movement and Christianity than most of the people reviewing this book. I know I value it for a lot of things other people dislike immensely or are even offended by. Some people will not look outside the canon and consider these other books blasphemous. Some of them are less important than others, but I think the majority have value, even if for only the context they offer on the evolution of the movement and subsequent religion, something more traditional readers might actually fear if it doesn't jive with their programming. So, yes, if you're open minded and want a better understanding of the gospels, ALL the gospels, I think this book is a great reference book to keep on hand.
