Someone once asked a career cartoonist how to make money off of cartoons. The cartoonist answered appropriately: "you can make money off of cartoons?" Today, those who read B. Kliban's third book, "Whack Your Porcupine," may not believe that he made a good living off of cartooning. He submitted cartoons to Playboy from 1962 to his death in 1990 and apparently made good money, or at least good enough. And this remains even more shocking given the esoteric, challenging, confusing and sometimes impenetrable character of his work. Newspapers never welcomed him, nor would they today. And 1977's "Whack Your Porcupine" contains some of his most nebulous, indescribable and mind-boggling creations. The Kliban curious should probably seek safer ground to start on, such as the perennially popular "Cat." Though even that relatively accessible book requires a twisted sense of reality.Though porcupines have a pretty good reason to not like this book, non-porcupines who have survived Kliban's other books will find plenty here to swoon, laugh, guffaw, wince and gape at. The cartoons here don't usually go for a quick and easy elbow jutting laugh. Though some of the puns and rhymes may start stomachs turning, as puns will do. For some reason, this book seems even more out there than Kliban's other collections. Fans will love it, of course, but don't expect anything more than blank, disturbed stares from the uninitiated. Comics such as "Gloria has a visible organism," Monroe is visited by the oral hygiene fairy," "Genitals of the universe," "Pine tree point," "NEVER give a gun to ducks," "The Virgin Mary appears to a foreign car in Denver," "Pantry hose," "The Lewd Sisters soon had Carl aroused," "Dumb fountain with goose," and "Another victim of illiteracy" will either elicit laughter from deep within or a permanently furrowed brow. Some really stick out, such as "Cyril is pursued by savages" in which a black man is chased by white people bearing bats and clubs. The surprisingly poignant "Promenade," featuring a pensive naked man walking past groups of people labeled "Teachers," "Acquaintances," "Enemies," "Dim Memories," "Old Girl Friends," "Living relatives" and "Ancestors" shows the depths to which Kliban could delve. It manages to sum up a good portion of human social experience. "March 6 75" simply displays what looks like a drunken line drawing executed by someone blindfolded. Though it looks like a throwaway at first glance, most people experience the past in this fuzzy indistinct way. An honest pictorial representation of the memory of an average day would probably look exactly like this.Kliban remains an acquired taste and he will likely always remain one. Doubtless his work influenced Gary Larson and Dan Piraro either consciously or subconsciously. Kliban does seem to work better by gradual osmosis. And perhaps that what it takes to finally appreciate the bizarre worlds explored in "Whack Your Porcupine." Quick laughs often burst and fade, but substance seems to have staying power. Kliban's work definitely belongs in the latter category. Sadly, most of it seems out of print.
