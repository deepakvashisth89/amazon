This is the first book of the second Samson trilogy, the forth of the whole series. I recommend whoever is about to embark into this saga to read the books in their chronological order, as the sequence of events all along is highly interconnected. Actually, I would even suggest to think each trilogy as one single book instead of three.Now, about the specific Spy Hook, let's get it straight upfront: this is certainly not the best book of the series I've read until now.With this said, I must add that I read some reviews (some even rather angry...) on this book stating it was awful, very boring and that nothing was happening; that is actually not true, it is not that nothing is happening, actually quite a few things happen, it is just that they happen in the typical way of Deighton's style (that some might not like, I accept that) and that they are happening in the context of a pretty extensive storytelling which unfolds over three trilogies, hence for a total of nine books. So you need to bide your time....So be warned: if you are someone liking the James Bond kind of spy stories, don't even start this, you will be as disappointed as the authors of the above reviews.I then think the whole discussion as to whether Deighton is as good as Le Carré (also the object of many other reviews) is pointless; clearly, the style and the approach to spy storytelling is the same - everything is happening rather slowly, like on a chessboard, as part of a broad intellectual game plus some action here and there. If you like this style (personally I love it and I plan to go through the whole Samson enchilada...), go for it without much worries about comparisons with JLC.
