Stephen states that the single season he discusses in "Fire on the Rim" is really comprised of reflections from the many different seasons he worked for GCNP. I went to the Northern Arizona University School of Forestry from 1968-1971 and fought fires in Northern Arizona during that time period and I worked for the NPS in the South Rim in 1969 as a lookout at Hopi Point and as a smoke chaser. I was aware of Stephen during that summer. For a PhD type, Stephen writes easily for a non-fire audience.  The book is fun to read and pretty accurately depicts fire life on the North Rim.  I think it is a bit of a stretch to make generalizitions about fire on the South Rim from experiences on the North Rim.  The South Rim is 1000 feet lower than the North Rim, has a different cover type, a different microclimate, a different geological basis, and different human impact.  What I experienced as a South Rim firefighter is different from what he experienced on the north.  NPS politics were the same though and he described them well.  I think an example the difference between the two rims is that I was called to chase smokes on two consecutive days and found that the fires on both days were the result of two fatal helicopter crashes.  I don't believe "Fire On the Rim" really represents the body of his work.  As an academic he brings a necessary and welcome study of fire, ecology, and sociality to the fire community.
