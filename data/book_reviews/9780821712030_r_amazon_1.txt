This review is of “Emily’s Destiny”, book #1 in the “American Dream” series by Whitney Faulkner.The book begins in 1773 in Newport, Rhode Island colony. Here, Emily Campbell, the heroine of the book, lives with her father, Thomas, oldest brother Phillip, and twin brother Robert. While the Campbells are outwardly prosperous, they are also privately waging a battle against British rule. It is this quest that leads Emily to meet John McNaughton, the hero of the book, and an Irish emigrant who shares the Campbell’s desire to throw off the yoke of British rule.As Emily and John fall in love, they are separated by John’s missions. On one of his missions, John is shot and seriously wounded but survives. Emily, however, receives news that he is dead, and tries to find out the truth. Her travels, however, lead Emily to being wounded herself and captured by the British.Emily escapes after saving a British soldier’s life, finds that John is very much alive, reunites with and marries him, and they, for a while, are happy.Upside: “Emily’s Destiny” is a well-researched book. Several historical figures make appearances: John and Samuel Adams, John Hancock, Paul Revere, Betsy Ross; all are seamlessly integrated by Ms. Faulkner with her fictional characters.Downside: The desire for American citizens to have freedom from England is the focus of the book. This means that everything else-such as character development-takes a back seat. It’s not that I think Emily and John are unlikeable characters; it’s just that so little is known about them to form an opinion.Sex: One love scene at the end of the book. It’s fairly mild.Violence: Emily is nearly raped by a British soldier; the attack is stopped before anything major happens. A lot of war violence, some of which is fairly graphic.Bottom Line: “Emily’s Destiny” is a decent, historical fiction book. One of the goals for a book series, for me, is to make me want to read the other books in the series. If one doesn’t already own the other three books in the “American Dream” series, as I do, I’m not sure that “Emily’s Destiny” would inspire anyone to seek out the other books.Read moreRead less
