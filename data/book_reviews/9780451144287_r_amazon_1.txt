I read this book probably 20-30 years ago and I remembered it as the best WWII historical fiction book I'd read.  Recently, I'd wanted to reread it, see if it was as good as I remembered it being, but it had gone out of print and there was no Kindle version.  I found a used copy on Amazon--which arrived on time and in perfect condition!  Just finished reading the book and, yes, it was just as good as I had remembered it being.  So much emphasis is--rightfully so--placed on the actual soldiers, sailors, airmen, all of those involved in the actual fighting.  But this book tells a fictionalized version of what happened behind the scenes--the planning, the cunning, the deviousness as well as the courage and sacrifice displayed by so many of the unsung heroes of that war.  The author perhaps encapsulated the the treachery best by a Horace Walpole quote he had spoken by one of the characters: "No country was ever saved by good men, because good men will not go to the length that may be necessary."
