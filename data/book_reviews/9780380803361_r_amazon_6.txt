Celia is a true story about a young African-American woman who was purchased by an elderly Missouri man to be his concubine.  As a resident on his farm in her own cabin, Celia becomes romantically attached to another slave.  She is then forced into a dilemma: either break off her relationship with her own and be failthful to a man of her own race or continue the master-sex slave condition.  She kills her owner and is susbsequently tried, convicted, and hanged.The book covers the owner's beginning, his purchase of Celia, her conflicts, the murder and the trial with a deft hand by the author.  What makes the book so outstanding is the author placing the whole story in the context of the country in the 1850s in such a way that the reader gets insights into the period.
