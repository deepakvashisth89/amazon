That is what I've said to myself so many times while reading this book, usually with a laugh. The first section with the protagonist Professor Kien and his maid-turned-wife and antagonist Therese and their madhouse apartment is just the beginning, and oh boy, buckle up! After that Kien tramps around meeting all sorts of even crazier people and crazy things happen, most of which occurring within a character's mind which is where this book is really interesting. So many digressions that are held together by some sort of logic to the individual's obsession. Each character has some sort of mad obsession, and when their obsession comes in contact or conflicts with another's the bizarre drama unfolds.Other reviewers comment on who Canetti's writing reminds me of, so I will too! I was really reminded of Dostoevsky's hysterical characters (especially in The Idiot & Notes) and all their wild goings-on. Add to that some Beckettian wanderings a la Molloy and absurd black humor, and then some Thomas Bernhard obsessive, monomaniacal rants. Most of this book goes on in "the lower depths" so we meet lots of thieves, whores, blind beggars who aren't blind, ultraviolent ex-policemen, and the pinnacle, Fischerle - the chess-obsessed, hunchback, dwarf pimp. And then there's Kien, oh baby.So please read this great, little known book (I'm 44 with a BA & MA in Eng Lit and have never heard a whisper about it), and enjoy!Read moreRead less
