I was especially interested in Adams' narrative about the political intrigues in Britain and Europe at the time of the American Civil War.  After all, huge amounts of cotton were being shipped to the textile mills of England, and the Confederacy was attempting to use this cotton to manipulate British policy in order to win support - and they almost succeeded.  Henry Adams' father was ambassador to England during the Lincoln administration, but the Confederate states had their own ambassador.  Henry was serving as assistant secretary to the ambassador and lived in England for about 8 years, starting in 1860.  As such, he had access to the highest levels of British society and became intimately acquainted with the social and political leaders.
