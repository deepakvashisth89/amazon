I loved this book. I put it on my night table and returned to New England every night before bed to visit one old/new friend. Books with family trees generally aren't that interesting because the reader can't truly get to know each of the characters. Kitty Burns Florey is brave to span her book across five generations, and I'm frankly surprised she pulled it off so splendidly. Each new chapter begins a new character of the family, each one speaking to us from a different era. But within the first paragraph of the new chapter, I found myself as deeply immersed in the new character as I had been genuinely sorry to leave the last. I still want to know what REALLY happened to Peggy and Ray. But then I remember it's fiction.
