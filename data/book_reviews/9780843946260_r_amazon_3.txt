From the first chapter, in which we see the ringleader of a neo-Nazi gang (interestingly named the "White Eagles," a name rich with evocations both of the stereotyped American Indian names you see in the spaghetti Westerns and American jingoism) thinking of the African-American he has imprisoned in his basement as literally an "alien" from another planet, 99% of the horror in this book comes from the realization of exactly how far some people will go in their hatred of the Foucaultian "other".  Territory well trodden on, but these days, what isn't?Of course, you can just do a surface-level reading of the text as well.  From a purely genre perspective, this novel seems to be one of the somewhat traditional "examine the family relationships of the main characters"...erm...thingies, and those parts do last for quite some time, so if you like your horror quick and nasty, you may want to skip some pages.  And while I don't want to spoil it for anyone (SPOILER ALERT:), I will say that I found the sudden mental about-face of one of the peripheral characters in the end to be a bit too sudden.
