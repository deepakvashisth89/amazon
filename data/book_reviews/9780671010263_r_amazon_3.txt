I read this book because it was referred to by Andrew Musgrave in his book on Make-BelieveMake-Believe Horror & the Supernatural (Children in Films), and after watching the two films made in the 1990s about the topic: Photographing FairiesPhotographing Fairies [DVD][Import][Region 0][1997] and Fairy TaleFairytale: A True Story [DVD] [1998] [Region 1] [US Import] [NTSC].The author outlines the historical circumstances and takes a thorough detective approach in trying to solve the mystery of how the fairy photos taken by two girls duped a generation and even fooled the sleuth Sir Arthur Conan Doyle.The author claims to approach the subject matter from a neutral stance but because the author himself has a Theosophical background (he calls himself a 'sensitive') he can't be completely unbiased.What I find incredible is how the author systematically and convincingly lays out the irrefutable reasons why the photos have to be considered fake, yet he strings us along with his own opinion that despite all the evidence otherwise, he still believes Frances saw fairies at the beck.Even so, this is a remarkable book, and I recommend it as a fascinating and very readable piece of academic research.I am disappointed Cottingley council did not make something out of the folk lore of the beck where the fairies were allegedly spotted - at least a nature trail would have been good, and a blue plaque on No 31 Main Street.
