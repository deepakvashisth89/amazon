After I bought this novel, I resisted reading it for weeks because of the torrent of bigotry and turmoil I knew would rain down from the initial drunken or half-drunken faux pas, in which two male teens kiss at a dance. But what I didn't anticipate was the grace, the saving grace of their love and the strength of their bond, or how transforming and infectious that human bond would shimmer and eventually glow through Joel and Tom. Love conquers Hate, even when that Love is the one that dare not speak its name, even in small towns like Common, New Mexico. The writer paints redemption, despite or because of the naivete and faith of the young lovers. That redemption is hard-scrabble, like the desert landscape, but you believe it.
