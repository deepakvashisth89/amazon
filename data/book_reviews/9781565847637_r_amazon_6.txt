This book is thoroughly misleading. It features Eastwood's "Dirty Harry" on the cover despite the fact that Dirty Harry was released in 1971 and this is supposed to be a book about the sixties.This book is about politics of the U.S. from the late 50s to the early 70s. Yes, this book is about politics, not about movies. Sure, there is some discussion about the movies. But the discussion about movies is noticeably smaller than the discussion relating to JFK, Castro, the Bay of Pigs, Nixon, LBJ, Martin Luther King, Malcolm X, the Nation of Islam, May 1968, and other important political and social events of the sixties. Whenever movies are discussed, they are usually used to discuss how they are related to socio-political events back then. For example, the author talks about how Kirk Douglas's Spartacus was a left-wing movie that had much in line with communist sympathies. Either that or something-something.Listen, I'll be honest, I didn't really analyze a lot of the political stuff he was saying. I was just too incensed that this book was a political history of the late 50s through early 70s, rather than being a history of the movies back then for me to read this book properly. If I wanted to read a political book, I would have gone into the politics section of my library instead of the films section.The writing is good. But I knocked three stars because of the false advertising. Like I said, this is a book about politics of the 60s.
