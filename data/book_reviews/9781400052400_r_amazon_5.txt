Don't read this book if you don't want to find yourself wide-awake at 3 in the morning saying to yourself, "If he can do it, so can I!" and maniacally crafting a plan to turn your own life around (or accelerate the turn you've already started).In Heft on Wheels, Magnuson crafts a vivid picture of his own transformation from hard-partying sofa jockey to hard-riding mountain-slayer.  Along the way, he introduces us -- with grace and wit -- to some of the folks who helped see him through.His keen ear puts his readers in the thick of the action: when he writes about riding, you're right there with him, hammering at the front of the paceline, whipping through a curvy descent, fighting your way up Mount Mitchell (as Magnuson reminds us, the highest point in the United States east of the Mississippi).  When he writes about his friends, you almost feel like you know them.  When he writes about his bar-going days, you're right there with him, too -- even if, like me, you've never actually set foot inside the kind of hard-drinking small-college-town bar he describes.Heft on Wheels won't automatically make you a better person.  No book can do that.  What it might just do, however, is remind you why you want to be a better person -- and you might just even put it down (at 5 in the morning) with a few more ideas about how to get there.Read moreRead less
