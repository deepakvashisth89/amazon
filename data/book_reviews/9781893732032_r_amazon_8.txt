The premise of "God Hunger: Discovering the Mystic in All of Us" -- that at the root of all our longing is a profound hunger for God -- is provocative. But author John Kirvan fails in his task to stay focused and  to satisfy our hunger. The problem with this book is, I think, its  rootlessness. Taking an ecumenical approach to Western spirituality, with  sources as diverse as C.S. Lewis, Rumi, the Kabbalah, and Hildegard of  Bingen, the book fails to fulfill.
