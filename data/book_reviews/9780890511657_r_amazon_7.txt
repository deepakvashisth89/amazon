This book is an expanded version of his earlier "Dinosaurs: Those Terrible Lizards."  This book is highly informative, and you can learn a lot from it if you are brave enough to tolerate all the Creationist foolishness.According to Duane Gish, all of the animals were created in the beginning.  This includes both dinosaurs and people.  All of the animals, even the ferocious tyrannosaurus, were herbivores until the Fall of Man.In this book, he added several very interesting and informative features.  On pages 10-13, he tells us how dinosaur fossils are excavated and restored.  On page 23, he tells us the story of the first dinosaur discovery.  No Evolutionist could take issue with any of this.On pages 62-63, however, he misrepresents Evolutionists as saying that transitional animal species grew useless wings or useless fins on a smaller scale.  He argues that such emerging organs would only hamper their hapless owners.  His illustrators make that point with two amusing illustrations.On pages 64-65, he admittedly does present a challenging point: He tells us that some dinosaurs were "bird-hipped" and some were "lizard-hipped," and that those species which Evolutionists regard as avian forebears happened to be "lizard-hipped."  He even gave the Greek equivalents for those two terms.Gish expands on the subject of the Great Deluge.  He argues that there are Flood legends from all over the world, so it must be true.  I wonder if anyone ever pointed to Gish that there are Cinderella legends from all over the world, and asked him if the Cinderella legend is true.Beginning on page 80, Gish suggests that dinosaurs were dragons.  After all, dragons appear in folkore all over the world, so they must be real.  Again, what about the Cinderella legend?To argue that dragons can breathe fire, he discusses the explosive chemical emitted by the bombardier beetle.  This discussion was also included in his earlier book.Gish was popular with Creationists partly because of his warm and folksy style.
