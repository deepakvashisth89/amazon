In 1924 a boy scout was wandering around Pelham Bay Park in The Bronx, when he encountered a very aged man, an Algonquin Indian, who was living in the park and surviving by foraging and fishing. Over the last months of his life he met frequently with the boy and told him his life story. Many years later, the scout told his son the story, and many years after that, the son wrote the story down in book form. While author Theodore Kazimiroff tells it as he remembers it, many of the details  seem to be based on his knowledge of First Nations culture and were probably not part of the original story.
