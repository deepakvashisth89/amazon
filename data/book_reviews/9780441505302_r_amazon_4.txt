There's magic, romance (periods of interest in the storyline) and great sadness (war, trickery, thirst for power).  Suldrun is a neglected prncess whose only importance to her royal father is to make a marriage that will add to his property and power. Her parents' neglect of her and her rearing by the loving hands of a peasant woman have made her a strong and sympathetic person.  She is able to turn being locked away as her punishment for refusing to marry a well-placed lord to create a beautiful garden that gives her comfort and the freedom to be her own person.  Into her garden comes an almost drowned man, Ailas (prince from an enemy kingdom) whom she rescues and secretly marries.  They attempt to flee and are caught; Suldrun is back in the garden and hangs herself after giving birth to a son, and Ailas is thrown in a pit. Things are downhill from here: the saga of Ailas' long journey to find his son and reclaim his seat on the throne is so overwhelming in detail, description, plot, and magical creatures that it took much perseverance on my part to plow on to the end. You've got to be impressed by Jack Vance's fertile imagination.  However, the saga continues in 2 other books! This one was sufficient for me.Read moreRead less
