I enjoyed reading the details of Magnuson's quest to reform his unhealthy lifestyle and become a better cyclist and a better man.  Some of his experiences rang true for me, as I'm roughly the same age and am going through a similar transformation (though my bad lifestyle habits are nowhere near as debilitating or soul-crushing as Mike's).I truly hope the author can hold to his dream and become the strong-minded and able-bodied person he desires to become.This work's single shortcoming is the manic style of prose and poor copy editing.  Magnuson is (or was) a college creative writing  professor and a published author.  But the stream-of-consciousness style, with its erratic, run-on sentences, disjointed descriptions and creative use of punctuation is a bit of a distraction.  One gets the sense that the author dashed off the manuscript in large chunks, over the course of a dozen, caffeine-fueled, all-night sessions.  The result is unpolished and seems more like a first draft than a finished manuscript.  There are also a lot of typos remaining (which is not surprising, given the unorthodox style of prose).Don't get me wrong.  I enjoyed Heft on Wheels, as would most any man or woman whose life has gotten a little (or a lot) out of hand and feels the need to make some positive and everlasting changes.  I wouldn't recommend the same crazy methodology Mike Magnuson used, but the story drives home the need to climb out of the rut in which we occasionally find ourselves; to set goals large and small; and to commit to those goals as though our very life depends upon it.Read moreRead less
