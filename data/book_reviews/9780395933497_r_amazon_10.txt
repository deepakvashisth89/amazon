Diana Preston's account of Robert Falcon Scott's life, and particularly his two expeditions to Antarctica, was published in 1997, though it was revised in 2012. This review is of the early edition. It is one of many such accounts, and its title is taken from Apsley Cherry-Garrard's reflective comment as a participant in Scott's fatal second expedition.Given the divergent views on Scott as a person, his competence and his worth as a leader, I found this account to be well-written, balanced appraisal which did much to evaluate primary sources. Where these pointed in different directions, Preston has been careful to present all facets and has thankfully refrained from jumping to conclusions by presenting only one side of an argument. She has not shied away from presenting the very real criticisms of Scott's organisation, though has put such criticisms in context alongside his many achievements.Despite having read many accounts of Scott's exploits, I found this one to be fresh with many new insights and well worth readingRead moreRead less
