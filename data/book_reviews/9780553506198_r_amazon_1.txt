Dark Skies is one of those shows that can be described as “brilliant but canceled”. Yet despite its single season run on NBC, it did produce a small amount of tie-in material. This mainly came out of the UK including both a guidebook to the show's first half and this novelization. Written by genre author Stan Nichols, Dark Skies: The Awakening takes the show's first four episodes (including the first episode which gives the book its title) into prose form.As a fan of the series (and someone currently working on a guidebook of my own), there are bits in it that make for interesting reading. Chances are that you already know that The Awakening has two different versions that exist and Nichols draws material from both versions which creates an interesting hybrid of the two versions which makes for interesting reading at times. There are occasions as well where Nichols is able to expand a bit on what happened on screen, filling in gaps that voice over could cover in the TV series but not on screen. Indeed, the novelization ejects the narration from the older John Loengard though traces of it can be found in bits of description throughout. As a fan, it is something that makes the book an interesting read.Beyond that though, the book is a fairly standard novelization. The prose feels a little copy and paste at times with descriptions sometimes feeling like little more than what would have been in various scripts. The mixing up of elements from the two versions of The Awakening also leads to the occasional issue with continuity as well. Further more, Nichols being British does lead to some at times slightly comical issues with idioms as well as factual errors regarding American military ranks and political structure. The errors aren't terribly distracting but the overall prose is a little on the average side.At the end of the day, Dark Skies: The Awakening is an interesting but unessential piece for fans of the short lived TV series. It does have items of interest but on the whole is an average novel that perhaps doesn't serve the material as well it should have.
