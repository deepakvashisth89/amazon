The characters, plot and dialog were on the edge of tedious, not just for being a couple days in the lives of self absorbed upper class twits, but for the inter-character prattle over one drama followed by another.  Ngaoi Marsh clearly invents the 2000s reality TV format and then traps her characters in a building that is at once a bungalo and the mansion from Daphne du Maurier's Rebecca snowed in by a magical weather pattern that snaps from the driving blizzard of a bitter Cornwall winter to a lovely spring rain.  She also expects the reader to have a familiar affection for Inspector Alleyn as she presents him not as a central character, but as a bit of a curmudgeon introduced late in the book to present the solution to the central mystery that seems borrowed from John Dickson Carr without much fanfare.Read moreRead less
