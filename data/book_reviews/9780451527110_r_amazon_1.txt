One of Shakespeare’s best plays. It marks a transition into the sophistication of his later plays. It's really Falstaff that does it. The play is worth reading just to see one of Shakespeare’s most original, vital, and alive characters. Like Harold Bloom says, Falstaff “breaks” the play, and his famous speech on honor distorts our perspective of all the other characters. The play is full of great poetry and speeches, and, again, it’s one of the Shakespeare’s bests.As for this specific edition, I really like the Signet editions of Shakespeare’s plays. They’re compact, and very readable. The footnotes are helpful without being obtrusive. I generally don’t bother with the supplementary material (which includes a thorough introduction, Shakespeare’s sources, and a sampling of commentaries over the years).
