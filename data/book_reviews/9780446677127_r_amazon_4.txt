I had mixed feelings about this novel as I plowed through the 500+ pages. For most of the first half, I was a bit bored by the tedium of the mid-life crisis of Stern, and of the hungry sex-craving widower who had entered the sexual fray only weeks after his wife's suicide. I was also baffled by the white-collar scheme which forms the crisis of the federal investigation. Still, as time proceeded, I became fascinated by the innermost revelations of Stern's emotional evolution, especially as it pertained to Clara, Dixon, Helen, and Peter. The characters are well drawn, and the plot twists are complex and startling. The white-collar investigation was opaque and brilliantly conceived. It may be difficult for the lay reader to understand the legal principals at play-even I, a retired lawyer had to reread sections to fully grasp it.
