I’ve read bad writing before, sometimes it’s just not my cup of tea, sometimes it’s made me cringe but it’s never made me angry like this book has. This collection is so offensive, not just because there is no character development and the stories go nowhere, but because while the prose is great and the description in particular is powerful, this reads less like a collection of short stories and more like a collection of failed chapters in a snooty travel guide to Miseryland. The joyless, brainless characters are just there as a token. And they are offensively drawn. I had to give up a few stories in. It’s not the grimness itself that offends; life can be grim and so can people. It is the laziness of the writing that offends. It is the unbelievable characters. It’s how the author used them, yes used poor people, some with mental health issues, simply to hang pretty prose on. It’s offensive.Why has this book been published? Why is it still in print? Is no one seeing how offensive this is?I can’t forgive the four and five star reviews. But then I can’t understand anyone who has ever paid or considered paying to visit slums when on vacation. Thats what this story collection makes me think of: get a buzz seeing how awful it is for poor unfortunate people, they’re so brainless, they don’t enjoy the finer things, they don’t have finer manners, feelings or thoughts. They absolutely never do any thing heroic or fine. They don’t even think to have a bath.Please, please for the love of god, if you are the writer and read this and especially if aspire to write something like it, can you please do what this writer couldn’t be bothered to do and actually build characters with more than one dimension; credit the less-fortunate character as having some intelligence and heart and joy and courage once in a while. IF only to break the monotony of trash fiction like this. Because this is trash. It is offensive trash dressed up as literature thanks to the author’s literary voice.So many times I was drawn in by the fantastic prose only to behold how dimwitted the characters are yet again. It’s boring and unbelievable; how very strange that the characters observe everything so sensitively and intelligently, (almost like an intelligent writer is intruding in the stories). THen they don’t act anyway like the same sensitive, intelligent characters. All poor people must be thick, eh?I didn’t get the ‘alienation’ theme at all, although I felt more like I was reading about aliens than any one with any humanity. Sometimes the characters suddenly do something random, suddenly change, but don’t be fooled into thinking this is anything resembling character development, it’s just the author didn’t know how to write a believable or interesting character so had them do something random to hide it.Ultimately, this is a shallow attempt at writing about outsiders. I’m horrified to think that people love it without mentioning the biases; it surely plays into many in the industry and literary field’s prejudices and sense of superiority. What does that say about real diversity? That anyone loves these short stories makes me think of that old movie with Audrey Hepburn as a nun teaching Africans how to wash their babies. I’m sure people loved that film, too. What do those poor people know? They are poor. They don’t even know how to care for their young.The character in this book are so cringeworthy and offensive I will never trust another review ever again. It’s theme is poor/unfortunates in society are thick, joyless, spineless and so dumb they actually stink and don’t care. Maybe the book was indicative of views at the time for readers and publishers…would it fly today if someone tried to publish it?
