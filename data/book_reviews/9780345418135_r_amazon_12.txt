Eyes of a Child is the third novel of the Paget series I read; here, a gloomy and rather oppressive atmosphere pervades the whole story. This is what makes it different from the previous two, and other RNP's, novels.Other reviews suggest that anyone can easily guess the culprit in the first quarter of the book; I did not think this was true, what is true is that mystery is not meant to be the central point of the plot here, but you have at least two (if not three) characters who "could have done it". What is pivotal in the story is the combination of the "key social issue" of the book (controversy around child custody after parents separation, potential child abuse, devious manipulation of the situation by one of the parents) and the development of the criminal/legal case around it.Essentially, as the story unfolds, you end up asking yourself the question "how could this have happened/how will it be resolved?" more than "who did it?". Psychological introspection and analysis take a great deal of focus in the book; this is generally something difficult to manage properly in books of this kind - meant to be light entertainment - but I believe RNP does a good job here, dodging the risk of slipping into "pop-psychology". Moreover, I guess the author's ambition is also to try and connect this piece of fictional story with the broader societal issue of child custody and abuse and their effects on involved people.It is a long book indeed, maybe too long; after the 2/3 I started feeling some reading fatigue and I just longed to get to the end. Despite rather powerful descriptions of the protagonists' emotions and states of minds, strong dialogues and ruthlessly realistic situations, the narration is kind of heavy and slow, and at times it struggles to keep up the tension.
