This is no exaggeration: the moment I finished the last word of this book, I wanted to go right back to page one! Having read a few of the reviews, however, dislikes namely, for The Toilers of the Sea, it can only be concluded that the readers today are simply too impatient and are perhaps used to books that are saturated with "story."  What I love about Victor Hugo is that he occasionally takes us away from the story and offers his readers these fascinating historical tangents. If Mr. Hugo feels compelled to describe the details of a rock, or a blade of grass, as he evidently did in real life (and probably to the dismay of those in his presence), I say let him; use an entire page if you need.  But if this is an artistic license that the readers of today cannot embrace, know that even the critics of Hugo's time were just as intolerant. Why? What could have possibly been their hurry?
