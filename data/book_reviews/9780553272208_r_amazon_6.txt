The book itself was too poorly written to be interesting, even as a period piece. Awkard plotting, maudlin characters, long and tedious dialogue to detail the timeline and logistics, and an unbelievably hackneyed resolution. (The drawback of reading on a Kindle is that I can't fling the book across the room in disgust at such a bad ending, not without breaking my Kindle.)Inspector Cockrill himself was quite sketchily drawn and at times seemed incidental rather than central to the mystery. All the red herrings to distract the reader from the obvious murderer barely served the purpose of filling out the central story, which was rather thin.Lastly, the casual antisemitism was probably (distressingly) accurate for that era of the British upper classes, but it was grating to read in a story with no other redeeming qualities.Read moreRead less
