I was an Army Counterintelligence Agent stationed with the 766th MID in Berlin from 1972-1974. Having majored in German Language and Literature in college, I was involved in special operations from the beginning so I am intimately familiar with some of the things that went on in those days and how counterintelligence operations worked. Given that Berlin was still technically an occupied city in those days, you might not believe some of the things we were doing. I won't say anything about what I did, but I will say that Col. Herrington's book was a very interesting and accurate account of some of what went on during those years. It was also very painful to read about fellow American soldiers betraying their country in serious and significant ways for many years.  It was a significant failure by military counterintelligence in general and Col. Herrington in particular to allow this to happen for so long.  In my unit, I  always felt that both the communications people who were responsible for transmitting classified intelligence reports to headquarters and other agencies as well as the classified document custodians would be the best targets for a foreign agent to exploit as they had access to pretty much everything.  The potential problem in any intelligence unit is that people trust each other.  We worked together day in day out, we were friends, we socialized together after hours, we knew each other's families.
