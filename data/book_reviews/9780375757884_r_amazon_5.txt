Nathaniel Hawthorne’s command of the English language, his multifarious sensitivity to the human condition, his playful wit and earthy humor, his scrutiny of the temporal while keeping company with the eternal, all set him apart from those who preceded him like James Fenimore Cooper, those who succeeded him, like Herman Melville. Hawthorne stands alone. In black from crown to heel, with penetrating eye, furtive smile, the air of a man at peace with himself for jobs well done. Sometimes with profound wisdom, sometimes with homely Yankee wisdom. Remaining modest as the book’s title from Shakespeare suggests: ”Life is as tedious as a twice-told tale…” A great writer whose sometimes verbose prose may try contemporary patience.
