To begin, if you are looking for an easy read this isn't it.  If you are seeking knowledge and understanding about the French Revolution there is a wealth of it here but it probably isn't the best place to start, for most at any rate.  I imagine that reading this before the advent of resources such as Wikipedia or Google would require an extensive library and/or knowledge of the people, places and events in question.  When first published in 1837 the names and events were still echoing through society; think WW2 seen from the late seventies. It may be that the internet, by making this accessible to non historians will  help revive interest in this glorious gem.  As John D. Rosenberg notes in the introduction, "It is impossible to 'speed-read' Carlyle..".  Rosenberg quotes J.S.Mill from his 1837 review, "This is not so much a history, as an epic poem; and notwithstanding, or even in consequence of this, the truest of histories."I am currently re-reading it after a short pause.  First time around was a bit difficult for me but still very rewarding.  Carlyle's style must have been revolutionary in itself.  It is very easy to forget that this was written in the early 19th century.  His portrayal of the French people, as excitable or "effervescent"  brings to mind the French as presented by Louis Ferdinand Céline in  Journey to the end of the Night  or Death on the Installment Plan.  Céline and Hunter Thompson came to mind while reading Carlyle, writers outside the bounds of what was considered normal.It can be very disturbing to see how fast civil society can slide into the abyss or rather, the Abyss.  The notion of the Abyss that is beneath the crust of human affairs, always waiting is perhaps the major lesson I take away from this book.  It is a tale of troubled and troubling times but rendered so beautifully, poetry in the truest sense of the word.  I highly recommend this book.  It will give you a view and understanding of the French revolution that isn't available from any other history though access to other sources will help in sorting out the names and events.
