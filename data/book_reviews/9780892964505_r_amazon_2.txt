James Thurber, known for the book Walter Mitty, is one of america's greatest humorist. He expresses a different characteristic in the murder book, Thurber on Crime. This book provides strong entertainment, multiple murder stories, and an overall feeling of satisfaction once done. One story that I specifically liked is The White Rabbit Caper. This short story starts out with a older rabbit saying that her grandchild was missing. She has gone to get help from a so-called private investigator, Fred Fox. They go to a local lounge and investigate more suspects there. But, right after they find the body of the granddaughter, there happens to be another murder as more suspects come. Fox starts to collect clues and finally comes up with a final idea. The grandmother rabbit was actually the granddaughter. She was the one who murdered her grandmother to get her carrot farm! This story had many sudden twists that you will never expect. If enjoyed this little tidbit of this story, you will surely enjoy the rest of Thurber on Crime.I think Thurber On Crime was overall a pleasing read. The aspect of putting multiple small stories into one book gets the reader excited for the next story. Some stories are short, while others are at max 10 pages long. Which is good for a reader who gets annoyed when the story drags. I greatly enjoyed this book because of one stories. One story being "Mr. Preble Gets Rid of His Wife". I enjoyed this story because the author includes a type of humor that is commonly  used in society today. Even though Mr. Preble wants to run away with someone else, he makes a humorous attempts to try to kill his wife by bringing her down to the basement.  However, there were some stories that I did not like as much as others. One story being "The Catbird Seat". This was the very first story in the book and for me it was very hard to pick up what was going on. I don't know if it was just because it was the first story of the book or because I wasn't really interested. In conclusion, I really liked this book because of multiple reasons. I enjoyed the murder aspect of all of the little stories and the fact that there was little stories all captured inside this one book.
