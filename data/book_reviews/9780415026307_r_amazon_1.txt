Preceptive analysis of the " noir style " of filmaking covering the years 1940-50, and the film type, Hard Boiled, by far the most exemplary of this, dare I say it, genre. Whether fim noir is indeed a genre or a film style or both is a never ending debate which author Krutnik aptly covers in chapter 2 " Genre and the problem of film noir ".Other chapters cover Hard Boiled crime fiction, the tough investigative thriller, 1940's crime films, the role of masculinity in noir films and even some Freudian psychoanalysis and of course, fate, are thrown into the mix.Like I said, the main focus here is on the Hard Boiled film, and though I like and admire such films as MILDRED PIERCE, I am far more comfortable watching RAW DEAL, a film I immediately associate with film noir. No Horror-noir, Cowboy-noir, Costumed-Adventure-noir, Neo-noir here. Just private eyes and crooks and dames and night and shadows and fate, 1940-50. The good stuff.And just let me add that this book is well written. You can understand it. You don't have to re-read each page several times to try to decipher what the author is attempting to say, the fault of many academic noir essayists. Krutnik writes in a clear, concise style. He makes his points in a highly readable fashion. Agree or disagree with him, you know where he stands and what he means. I'm really enjoying reading this book.However, there is just one minor drawback. There are no photos. I really like photos in a book on film noir because so much of what the author is saying can be helpfully illustrated by photographs. THE MAGNIFICENT SEVEN needs no visual guide when discussing it as a film, but PHANTOM LADY benefits greatly from photographic illustration. So you really have to have some understanding of film noir and it's look, or having benefited from already seeing most of the films discussed. It's just not a good first book for those getting started with noir films.Photographs would have made this a 6-star book, but in all fairness I can only give it 5-stars, the top rating allowed.Read moreRead less
