This book was written as a history of six critical days about the assassination of President John F. Kennedy from a request of Jacqueline Kennedy to record accurately the events leading up to the death of JFK and the resulting actions after this tragic event.William Manchester is known for his excellent journalistic talents and does not disappoint in this historical treatise which after thousands of interviews and meticulous research resulted in a magnificent look at six days which changed America forever.This book is essentially divided into four major parts.  The first part leads up to the Dallas visit in November of 1963.  Manchester outlines the prevailing attitudes, prejudices, fears and politics of this time period.  It is revealing that Manchester points out the underlying hatred and opposition that was reflected in the attitudes of Texans toward the JFK administration.  What makes this so befuddling is that one of their own in the Vice Presidency held by Lyndon Johnson was an essential part of that much hated administration.Manchester goes into depth as to these attitudes along with other Texan politicians of those times that being Governor John Connally and Senator Ralph Yarborough.  He shows a fractured political terrain that was prevalent in Texas in 1963.  Manchester discusses the warnings given to President Kennedy not to go to Texas for the very fear for one’s own life.  Indeed premonitions of the eerie kind which days later made one for second guessing this major tragedy that in many eyes could have been prevented by taking prudent actions.The second part deals with the President’s trip to Texas which takes us to the motorcade in Dallas and the resulting assassination of JFK by Lee Harvey Oswald.  Manchester shows to all his true due diligence and meticulous research in how Oswald committed this dastardly deed.  He shows to one and all that it was Oswald acting on his own who did the killing.When Manchester wrote this book it was right after the assassination.  Many would consider that Manchester’s insights were made much too quickly after the event without the necessary historical study.  Upon reading this book nearly 50 years later and many theories and arguments later I concur with Manchester’s findings.  When he wrote this he was talking to men and women who were there and the memories were fresh.  Manchester did his due diligence and in my opinion got it right.  The later theories and arguments are second hand theories which are not proven and lack good historical research, that it they cannot be substantiated by secondary sources that would prove them correct.  To me this is what Manchester has brought with this book which will stand as his legacy to this tragic event.The third part of the book deals with the aftermath of the death of JFK.  Manchester centers this part of the book around Jacqueline Kennedy and how she dealt with the aftermath of this tragic event.  The protocol of the funeral dirge, military honors, burial location and how the world reacted to what had happened were the things that Manchester went into great pains in explaining these events.  It is during the events of this part of the book that we learn of the courage and personality of the President’s widow.  Manchester’s take on these events are key into understanding the events over that tragic weekend not only for America but as Manchester relates to the reader it affected the entire world.  Though I remember the watching of this on commercial free TV and thought I knew much of what happened, Manchester gives us the entire picture of these events, warts and all!The fourth part of the book is the epilogue in which Manchester ties up all the loose ends and gives us the world views and what had happened to the main characters after the events of November 22, 1963.
