I've been reading rock biographies which tend to be somewhat depressing, but David Lee Roth's autobiography is different than most. Usually there is a somewhat dry quality to a rock biography, however, Roth's book had a fluid quality. Although he discussed his parents and his childhood, he tends to flit from subject to subject, making his book more like a conversation than a conventional timeline. David Lee Roth isn't shy about voicing his opinions. I was intrigued by what it was like to travel on a tour bus, something I hadn't read about before. He doesn't dwell on negativity or conflicts with his bandmates. It is obvious they didn't always get along, but there are no long passages trashing Eddie Van Halen or Alex, nor does he mention conflicts with Eddie's wife Valerie Bertinelli other than to say they didn't get along that well. His sense of humor is evident throughout the book. He also doesn't get into detail about his many affairs, however, he does state that he only loved three of his girlfriends, but he was gentleman enough to not name them.
