My subject for my summer reading queue was the subject of Progressivism - I just wanted to understand the subject a little better. While I think Reich was insightful I also think that the average man on the street generally knows this stuff.  As I read it I became convinced that Mr Reich sees himself as an elitist and is very full of himself.  For goodness sakes, he has never created a job and in fact has feed at the public trough for most of his life.  I can just imagine him at one of dinner parties attended by other elite progressives pontificating on how labor and society should be rearranged to his liking.  Good Grief!  Read the public choices chapter and it is scary that there are people who think this way.I think I actually like Robert Reich - it is interesting to listen to him and read his stuff.  The problem is not Robert Reich as a person - the problem is that he is actually teaching from a self-imposed arrogance.  Worst, for a short time he was part of the ruling class.  He appears to place a great deal of value on a very narrow part of our economic life - the other elite - almost like hero worship.  I think he looks down of the people who actually make this country work and those who actually create jobs.  He can't possibly be able to assess the world from his vantage point.  He sounds an awful lot like Woodrow Wilson - yikes.I would recommend that you read this book but read it through the proper prism - that he is a elite progressive.  I feel he is a great writer and writes concisely and very directly.  He is not at all secretive like Wilsons writings.  I am not opposed to this book nor Mr Reich.  I just happen to not agree with his elitest views.  So, read the book but know that this man probably considers himself above the vast majority of society.  I am certain that his views are heartfelt.  I just can't understand how someone so smart can believe in statism - that the state can manage society better that the invisible hand of self interest.
