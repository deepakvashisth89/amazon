In a way, all the previous mysteries build up to this one. Nero Wolfe’s favorite waiter from his favorite restaurant is murdered while taking refuge in Wolfe’s house. Archie thought Pierre was being paranoid when he said someone was trying to kill him. Not so.The murder is a blow to Wolfe’s self-esteem because home and hospitality are sacred to him, and Pierre was in his intimate circle. All that makes it a family affair. His three customary investigators join wholeheartedly in Wolfe’s investigation. They’re family too.The investigation is full of obstacles, blind alleys, appalling discoveries — and the murders don’t stop with Pierre. Wolfe behaves erratically and uncharacteristically, even breaking his most cherished rules. Archie’s loyalty is sorely tried. Both of them suffer a stay in jail as uncooperative material witnesses.The denouement is a shocker — with a dash of intriguing moral ambiguity.Rex Stout wrote A Family Affair when he was eighty-eight, a year before his death.  It’s brilliant.  This is my second reading of the Nero Wolfe series, and I intend to re-read the whole thing again in seven years if I survive the pandemic and any other challenges in store.
