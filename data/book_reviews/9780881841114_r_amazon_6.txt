And the case is dark indeed. It involves a pretty young girl who has learned all the tricks of performing séances, a rich old woman who believes in the spirits and owns a fortune in jewels, a handsome young man who's fatally attractive to women, a jealous maidservant, and a gang of hardened criminals.Something horrible happens one night at the Villa Rose in Aix-les-baines. No one knows exactly what, but the result is the body of the rich old woman strangled to death, the maidservant chloroformed in her room, and the charming young Mlle. Celia gone without a trace.Fortunately the young gentleman who loves Celia talks the great detective Monsieur Hanaud into taking on the case. Henaud is vain, secretive and takes sadistic pleasure in daunting amateur detectives. He allows a well meaning, wealthy, middle-aged English tourist named Mr. Ricardo to tag along on the investigation for the fun of ridiculing his deductions. But Hanaud is also shrewd, observant and as devious as the criminals.This novel, originally published in 1919, foreshadows the clever plotting of the Golden Age of mystery writing. It also looks forward to contemporary novels in which the nasty nature of the crime is of as much interest as its solution.According to the short but informative introduction, the author was also paying tribute to the nineteenth-century French masters of the detective story (like my great favorite, Emile Gaboriau). It is rich in French characters and ambiance, and the structure is very Gallic.This book should be a real treat for any lover of vintage mysteries. It was for me.Read moreRead less
