A Room Made of Stone — or Rather, The Court of the Stone Children — ReviewThere are ghost stories designed to frighten readers.And then there are ghost stories that quietly invite readers to listen to history, believing that old buildings sometimes remember more than the people who live in them.The Court of the Stone Children is one of Eleanor Cameron’s finest later novels. Blending historical mystery, gentle supernatural elements, and a profound respect for art and history, it offers a thoughtful adventure that lingers in the imagination long after the final page.I spent a cool, fog-softened afternoon at the Eye of Bodhidharma while Edgar the raven watched the mist gather around Eureka’s Victorian buildings. Their weathered windows and carved stonework seemed exactly the sort of places where forgotten stories might still be waiting.Edgar looked at an old photograph on the wall.“Old buildings are excellent listeners. Fortunately, they also keep very interesting secrets.”The novel follows Nina, an American girl visiting Paris with her family. While exploring an old apartment building, she encounters mysterious children carved in stone and becomes drawn into a centuries-old mystery connected to a lost child, forgotten artwork, and the lingering echoes of the past.What especially impressed me was Eleanor Cameron’s atmosphere.Paris is not merely a backdrop.It becomes another character.Ancient courtyards.Narrow streets.Old staircases.Hidden gardens.Every location feels alive with history.The supernatural elements remain understated.Rather than relying on frightening scenes, Cameron builds wonder through curiosity and compassion.The mystery grows naturally from careful observation.History.Architecture.Art.Memory.Everything works together beautifully.Nina is an intelligent, believable heroine.She succeeds not through extraordinary powers but through patience, kindness, and the willingness to ask questions adults sometimes overlook.Cameron’s prose remains graceful and inviting.She trusts young readers to appreciate subtle emotions and complex ideas without simplifying them.⸻What Works Well✓ Beautiful historical atmosphereParis comes vividly to life.✓ Gentle supernatural mysteryThe ghostly elements
