Kipling created not just a great character but a great crew aboard an 1890s fishing schooner at the opening of the cod fishing season. From late Spring to early Autumn, the crew of the “We’re Here” would criss cross the Grand Banks of the North Atlantic, filling the hold with salted cod for delivery back in Glouster, Massachusetts, luck permitting. What the We’re Here’s didn’t count on was picking up a castaway, a teenage boy, who had fallen unconscious from a steamer bound for Europe. The stranger has to learn the ways of the fishing ship, the moods of the sailors and captain, the trust of the cook, and the allegiance of his one peer on board, the captain’s son. What none but the captain’s son figure out is that Harvey Cheyne, lost at sea, is the son of one of the wealthiest and most powerful robber barons in America. Long used to luxury and command, Harvey must learn to work and play and struggle doubt and hope as real people work and play and struggle and hope.Wonderful story by one of the greatest masters of the English language.
