I first read this book back in 1982 and I remembered that it was quite good.  I am glad to say that my opinion has not changed.  Not only does the story still hold up, it is perhaps Simaks's best book since Way Station written in 1972.  And this is high praise since Simak is a top tier writer from Science Fiction's Golden Age.In Project Pope,  Simak imagines a group of Robots searching for knowledge and hoping it will lead to faith.  They set up their project on the out-of-the-way planet, End of Nothing.  They have built a Robot and feed it all the knowledge they have unearthed via study,  especially the knowledge they have gathered via Human Listeners who gather information from mind exploration of the Galaxy.  In an homage to their human creators they name this super Robot the Pope and their learning complex the Vatican.The main characters in the book are new arrivals on End of Nothing, a doctor named Tennyson and a writer named Jill,  both of whom become deeply involved with the Robot's quest.  Things start to get out-of-hand when a Listener claims she has visited Heaven.There are some great characters is this book including the enigmatic human Decker and his alien friend Whisper whom only a few can see.
