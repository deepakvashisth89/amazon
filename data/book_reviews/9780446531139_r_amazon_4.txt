This was a fascinating book that reminded me a lot of Dateonomics and Make Your Move, two other statistics-driven dating books, except that this book is based on real data on a fairly large sample of people who actually got married, not societal gender-balance data like Dateonomics. Now that I'm in my early 30s, I'm much more efficient at dating than I used to be, cutting men off quickly if I don't think they're marriage material. But I was still making some mistakes that are pretty evident to me now that I've read this book. Coming to terms with the narrowness of my own preferences has been eye-opening to say the least. But the main takeaway from this book is that women actually have a lot more power than we might think we do in influencing the course of a relationship. Based on surveys of women and men conducted shortly after they received their marriage license, Molloy concludes that women who wanted to get married actively discussed marriage with their boyfriends and hinted not-so-subtly that they wanted to be married by a certain time. In past relationships, I felt powerless and felt like I was waiting for the guy to make all the moves, not realizing that things might have turned out differently if I stood up for myself and asked for what I wanted. At the very least, it will eliminate men who are not interested in doing anything to accommodate or please you! Great advice and very practical. A must-read for any woman over 30.
