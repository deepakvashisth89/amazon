The text of this book is bleached by the sun and encrusted with sand and sea water.  And like any story of worth it is the contrasts that highlight the form of the narrative.  We only know what we have when we have lost it, and the pure power of a parent's love for a child is most ably demonstrated when that child is gone.  This book has been likened to Raymond Chandler and Elmore Leonard in it's style, but I don't think it really is ; it has a far more European feel to it and whilst it is dark and violent it does not have the nihilism of a dying empire to inspire it.
