John Buchan's novel THE THIRTY-NINE STEPS has been called one of the best detective mysteries of all time.  I might not go that far but it is very good.An English gentleman, Richard Hannay, reluctantly takes in a mysterious stranger, listens to his tale of political intrigue that portends assassination and war, and then finds the bearer of this unlikely story nailed to his floor with a knife.  What does he do?  He runs, of course, knowing that both the police and the murderers will be looking for him.  What follows is a chase, encounters with characters both suspicious and bizarre, attempts at clearing his name, and finally redemption and satisfaction.  Since this is the scheme of almost every mystery/detective story ever written, I doubt that I've given anything away.Buchan's story is different in that it flows at such a breathless pace through the Scottish moors that the reader gets engulfed in the chase.  There are twists and turns that never seem to go well for Hannay, forcing him to use every scintilla of his wits to develop more escape ideas that also don't seem to work.  It's fortunate for him that he has an agile mind and the physical attributes of a horse, because he spends most of his time running hither and yon across the Scottish heath with his mind working a mile a minute.  All the while he's trying to decipher a code book that carries a deadly secret.The character development by Buchan is well done, as are his descriptive passages of the landscape the runner is traversing.  I say runner but actually Hannay uses many modes of transportation in his frantic attempts to evade detection.  In one escapade he actually sets off a bomb in close proximity to himself in an attempt to blow apart a makeshift prison where he has been placed.  He succeeds but now has to run with a slight brain concussion and sore body parts.  As I said, he's tough.I like this book a lot but there are some far fetched assumptions and unlikely circumstances that occur that make the detective work a little contrived.  The fact is, though, that the storytelling is so superior and the staging is so entrancing that the book is great entertainment and on a near par with the best of the genre.Schuyler T WallaceAuthor of TIN LIZARD TALESRead moreRead less
