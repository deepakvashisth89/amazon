A well balanced comprehensive look into military monastic orders their origins, development, and survival against the odds.  Over the last ten years I have been enamored with monastic knights of the 12th century and have read and visited many of the countries outlined in this text.  Although this book was pricey, it forms the foundation for understanding the where, why, and how these organizations came about.The book is well written, well researched and easy to read by the familiar student.  The reader is immediately introduced to the three prominent major military monastic orders:  Knights Templar, Knights of St. John, and the Teutonic Knights in the first chapter who were given papal charters.  The reader will also be introduced to several additional orders as the Brethren of Santiago, Calatrava, and Alcantana who spearheaded the Reconquista on the Iberian Peninsula.My particular interest is with the latter, Iberian Peninsula, where most of these orders functioned for many years.  I was interested mainly with the Knights Templar entrance in 1128, morphed into the Order of Christ. The Order under Enrique the Navigator, Master of The Order, presided over  a research center at Sages that initiated the expansion of Europe with expeditions which were half-missionary and half-commercial initiating Portugal as a world power.Desmond Seward takes the reader through more than three centuries of time interacting with masters, kings, and nobles of the period many with similar names without major confusion.
