I read this because I had read and enjoyed Willa Cather's O! Pioneers so much, and in the introduction to the novel, Cather writes about what a debt she owes to Sarah Orne Jewett and this book in particular.  I had very high hopes when I started reading it, and I have to say that I was sadly disappointed.  Where Cather's narrative is polished and fluid and grips you right from the beginning, this book is a struggle from start to finish. It is, more than anything, a series of slight vignettes of a people and way of life now long gone.  I imagine it would be interesting to read from an anthropological perspective, but I found it very difficult to read for pleasure. The story, such as it was did not go anywhere, nor did it really say anything and apart from the odd lovely paragraph describing the natural world I found it extremely heavy going.Read moreRead less
