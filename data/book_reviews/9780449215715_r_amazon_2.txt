Over 50 years ago, I read Josephine Tey’s classic suspense story “Brat Farrar.” Now I have finally read Mary Stewart’s “The Ivy Tree,” written as an homage to Tey’s book and handling the same challenges such a novel requires of an author.Both tales involve a stranger appearing and claiming to be a long-lost family member. Inheritances are involved. And intrigue and danger. Stewart manages the convoluted plot, red herrings, and surprising developments masterfully, all while writing in first person.Tey’s book was first published in 1949; Stewart’s arrived in 1961. Cell phones and Google would have ruined both books. Stepping back in time can be a wonderful experience, adding to the atmosphere and mystery.Stewart was married to a geologist and her insider’s knowledge comes into play often here.Some bits of the book are romantic, but the book is not a romance, nor is it a traditional “who done it.” Instead it is a master class in writing a suspenseful tale.Read moreRead less
