After reading the views on Amazon.com, I felt somewhat preturbed about writing this review, as on the whole I would class this book as only average, rather than the brilliant that everyone else says it is. Certainly the books has many good points. Blood writes a very good political thriller, and it is clear that he had significant amounts of insider knowledge, the plot itself is also commendable, especially with the recent relevations of Chinese espionage within the heart of the US government and related departments. The book also gives a good insider view of the true nature of politics, which I think any reader can certainly imagine. Furthermore Blood writes in a very easy style which makes it easy for the reader to understand.However I feel the book falls down in several areas. Most signficantly there appears to be almost incoherent parts tot he story. Take for example chapter one, when the Chief announces how to obtain the PAL's codes via only one source possible - from Langley. Where is there mention of this throughout the rest of the book - nowhere, unless I am mistaken!! At first I thought the author was referring back to the Chief himself, who was ex-CIA, but if you read the chapter, it appears he is referring to someone else. When CeCe is killed, we presume it is the Chief who deos the killing, but is it?There are also parts of the story that for me don't quite add up. Devon's death to say the least is strange. Being forced  to go into the White House grounds and waiting to get shot sounds somewhat far fethced to me. Infact the whole issue of Devon in regard to 'caving' in so quickly just wouldn't make sense, why didn't his bodyguards at any time intervne, infact what happens to them? What happens to the Chinese who are after Hanson, they mysteriously vanish? Why is the US government happy to allow a private citizen to hold a potential nuclear blackmail card? Why is the American Investor so generous in allowing Hanson to live, surely if they were after power, they would have him removed from the equation? Why isn't the US governemnt bothered about finding the nuclear suitcases, indeed throughout the book, all the main characters are private citizens - it just doesn't add up.Finally I'm not convinced with this code being hidden in Masterpieces. Couldn't Devon have found a far more simpler scheme?This are just a few issues I have with an otherwise enjoyable political thriller. Please don't get me wrong, I enjoyed the book, but readers like questions answered, and I feel China Card states many things, but actually never delivers answers.Finally I thought the ending to the book came far too quickly. For about 300 pages, the tension is mountng and yet 90 pages later, every single issue is apparantly answered? Readers like chunky books, you get more value for money that way.Read moreRead less
