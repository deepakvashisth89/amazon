I have one of the Books of Wonder hardcover editions of this work that reproduces the original. In that edition, the formatting of the first edition is restored, and it contains John Neill's wonderful illustrations, both color and ink drawings; and these illustrations are reproduced well--very clear.  It also was faithful to a gimmick used in the original editions; a different colored paper used for each chapter of the book, representing the different lands visited in the story. While admittedly a gimmick, this touch adds much to the fun of reading the book.This edition...well, is very disappointing.  The formatting is changed, the colored pages are of course not used, and the Illustrations are of poor quality; grayscale and very pixelly/grainy.  On the other hand, this is better than another softcover edition I've purchased here on amazon, that says it is illustrated, but does not use the John Neill illustrations at all--rather substituting public domain artwork with no connection to the story whatsover.
