I struggled through this. Ultimately it’s a disappointment. I didn’t learn as much as I thought I would from something this weighty (!) and it isn’t written in a manner that encourages you to press on. However, by reading a few pages at a time I avoided that sort of skipping or speed reading that is unrewarding with large texts. The author is best on the more recent part of the century and ventures more of his own opinions but throughout I found a fault of the book was that it dealt in themes and topics but without facts or detail. For example, frequently it refers to a leader or a president or whatever and doesn’t actually tell you his name. It is fat roo broad brush when it needn’t be. Things in footnotes should be in the main text. Things in brackets should be left out...The author says in his Foreward that the book is longer than he originally intended. That could probably be rectified by chopping a lot of the first few chapters which are an essay in themselves trying (possibly failing) to set the scene on when the century begins and other general broad themes.
