This is one of Atwood's earlier novels, and it shows her talent for writing in terms of her turn of phrase.  But it's not a great novel to stand alongside her later books, mainly because of the humdrum subject matter.  The story is set in the late 1970s and concerns the love lives of several fairly unpleasant and dull middle class people.  The couple at the centre of the story are both unfaithful, and the book starts just after the wife's lover has committed suicide.  I had very little sympathy for any of the three main characters, who seemed stupid, self-destructive and selfish.  How the central couple managed to have so many affairs is beyond me as neither came across as a very interesting or nice person.  I can only assume they were both extremely attractive physically.It's a bit like listening endlessly to the marital woes of a friend - but not a friend you like very much.  For the people involved, it is endlessly significant and interesting and all consuming, but for anyone else it's very boring.  Atwood writes well enough that the book earns a three star rating from me, because even when her subject matter is tedious, the simple way she writes about things offers some reading pleasure.  But I wouldn't really recommend this book unless you are having relationship trouble yourself in which case you may well relate to it and find it worthwhile to read.Read moreRead less
