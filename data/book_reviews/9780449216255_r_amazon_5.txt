I just recently discovered Phyllis Whitney when I read the marvelous "Columbella" --so I was anxious to read another of her books. I selected "Feather on the Moon" - and it was a good read, but a disappointment after Columbella.The book started out with an intriguing concept:  Jennifer's 3-year old daughter Debby was stolen from her, years ago, right after the death of her husband.  She is contacted by a woman, the grand dame of a rich estate. Jenny is told that she believes Debby is staying at her house with parents, who may be con artists. The parents are saying that they have the child who is the woman's son's child, but they may be looking for a piece of the estate, since the son was killed in an accident in South America.Jenny travels to the old woman's estate, hoping that the child in question is actually her daughter, Debby. I followed the book to the point, but it started to slide for me when nearly every character (even the butler) was not who he/she claimed to be.  I studied the cross-hatched identities tried to follow how each character fit into the plot of the kidnapped Debby.  I had to go back a few times.  I found the character confusion very difficult (and wordy) to follow.  I  got to the point where I didn't care who was who as the plot got tangled, and it seemed to convoluted to enjoy.So it was just "OK" for me.  I think fans of Phyllis A. Whitney would probably enjoy the book, but I was too confused most of the time to really enjoy the read.Read moreRead less
