At the beginning of Dombey and Son Paul Dombey is born and his mother dies.  Young Paul is the eagerly awaited son of a father who wants desperately to bring him into his prosperous business.  Unfortunately, Paul is a weak and sickly child who dies early in the novel leaving his father and sister Florence heartbroken.Dickens lets his readers know early in the story that Paul is not going to be with us for long.  Even so, like everyone Paul meets, we come to love him; he is a good child remarkable for his honesty and simplicity.  His older sister Florence, also a truly good and beautiful child, takes care of her brother and is devastated by his loss.  Unaccountably, her father hates Florence for reasons never fully explained by Dickens.  This unreasonable and unexplained hatred is one of the problems I had with the novel.  Florence is good, caring, and loving to her father even as he rejects her and, towards the end of the novel, strikes her, driving her from his home.  Every other character in the novel knows Florence for what she is --a saint on earth.Dombey and Son is Florence's story.  Her father hardly seems human; he is cold and aloof to all who meet him.  If we can take little interest in him, we do care what happens to Florence.  Midway through the novel she finds herself caught between the father she loves and his new wife, Edith, who despises her husband.  Florence becomes associated in the mind of her father with his wife and consequently become even more alienated from him. This dynamic of love rejected by Dombey and hatred openly manifested by him provides many dramatic confrontations and keeps us interested until his marriage unravels and both wife and daughter escape the cold and sterile home Dombey has made for himself and his family.From this point in this 800 plus page novel, Dickens tells a confusing tale of loss and redemption that left me unsatisfied.  Plot twists and turns with characters only marginally related to the central theme of his story of love, loss, rejection, and redemption make the novel needlessly long and often uninteresting.
