As all accomplished artists know how to use colors correctly to shade for effect, Al Martinez knows how to use his rich prose to write an eyewitness description of a doomed newspaper in San Francisco during the '60s. In this novel, William Colfax, a 'Nam veteran, starts as a cub reporter, and during the telling, evolves into an experienced, cynical journalist from his observations of the campus revolution and the corruption in all strata of the city. Colfax introduces the reader to the turbulent Berkeley activists, the scandalous police, the ant-Communist, fanatic power brokers, and the hard-drinking, quirky city room's staff-his family at the San Francisco Herald.  For years I've read Mr. Martinez's column in the LA Times and through those columns have learned a little about the man. He saw action in the Korean "conflict" and was a reporter on a Bay area newspaper, so he wrote a story that he knows well.
