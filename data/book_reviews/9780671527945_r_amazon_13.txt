The 2nd instalment of the Cazalet series begins in September 1939 and the outbreak of WW2. The family saga is seen mainly through the eyes of the three older Cazalet daughters: Clary, Polly and Louise, who are aged 14 - 16 at the start of the book.The girls are at that ‘twixt and ‘tween age (teenagers hadn’t really been invented in England at this point) of being grown up enough to do some things (what they have to) and not grown up enough to do others (what they want to). The context to this awkward age is the uncertainty of war - hence the title of the book. In some ways, there are parallels with girls who were this age during COVID.I did enjoy the book - it’s comfortable, nostalgic reading, evoking an England long gone. But due to the focus on the three girls it feels very much like a book for young teenagers. The long-drawn-out descriptions of clothing and jewellry meant my attention wandered from time to time.An unexpected highlight of the book was actually the “middle” cousins of the family, Lydia and Neville, who age from 9 to 11 in the course of the story. There’s a most amusing description of their game of “operations” with various toys and their younger siblings/cousin as patients.I am sure I’ll continue with the series but may leave a longer pause before embarking on the next book.Read moreRead less
