Jack Vance's Cugel/Dying Earth series is one of my favorites ever, but I hadn't read Lyonesse.When Vance died recently, someone blogged how it really was Game of Thrones before its time, at least in its dark imagery, adult themes and tendency to kill off major characters.  Besides, GRRM is a big enough fan of Vance that he edited a collection of short stories set in the Dying Earth universe (yes, another side project to keep him from completing GoT).Vance's wonderfully whimsical dialogues & tales of trickery beating brawn fit in perfectly with the proto-Arthurian setting of the Elder Isles.  The feeling is much more that of reading a novelized version of ancient European legends than GoT's street-savvy political maneuverings.  It is also fairly slow going, these are NOT action-packed novels by any means.The trilogy covers the rivalry between King Casmir, an unprincipled schemer who wants to the conquer the Elder Isles, and Aillas, an idealistic younger king of a rival kingdom who secretly loathes Casmir for personal reasons.Like Game of Thrones, the military and political maneuverings are subtle and far from the traditional evil wizards beloved of Tolkien imitators.  And like GoT, major characters can meet untimely ends.  The good guys, starting with Aillas, are way more idealized than GoT however.On the gore and blood level, it could not be more different.  Rapes and torture are common but only briefly alluded to.  Vance never gets very graphic, but is chilling in his understatement.  For example, an ogre captures young girls and eats them, but not before "engaging in erotic coupling" with his victims.  How the hero saves the day in this instance is clever and cute.  But, despite the cleverness, elaborate vocabulary, lack of profanity or graphical descriptions, this is most definitely not for the faint of heart.Thinking of it as a clever fairy tale for adults is a pretty good approximation.p.s.
