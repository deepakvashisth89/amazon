Crime and horror are two literary genres that frequently stay over at one another's houses, and the results are often riveting. Whilst this particular novel might be more traditionally categorized as either a crime novel or a thriller, the set pieces and descriptions of events herein will be burned into your mind in much the same way as nerve-shattering horror does.Anyone repulsed by scenes of domestic violence need not apply, as there's plenty of it. Reliably I'm told that the courtroom scenes are highly accurate representations of trial and the family court system - they certainly felt authentic to read through. The character of Arthur Danse is a deplorable, sub-human and evil man, and it becomes a gargantuan effort to put the book down after the first 30 pages as we're along for the ride into his utterly vile brand of madness.Brace yourself for arguably Jack Ketchum's finest hour since "The Girl Next Door". It's about time that he got some additional (and unfortunately posthumous) recognition for his fiendish literary creations.
