I prefer my Who- Done- It’s to be of the locked room variety, about the puzzle rather than the personalities, and this was firmly in the Marple rather than the Poirot camp, but it is still a great read.I was settling in for a pleasant wander through a small village murder in the company of feisty, caring Verity Preston and that would have been a fine book, but it really kicks on when Chief Inspector Alleyn arrives a quarter of the way in.Alleyn adds the seriousness and the humour that make good crime stories into great books. It’s a difficult balancing act for the author. On the one hand the reader needs to feel a sense of jeopardy, that real people are affected, that the deeds committed are truly dreadful, but on the other hand, these are not Thrillers. A light touch is required and in Alleyn, Marsh has a beautifully crafted tiller to steer us through the opposing tides.Alleyn’s relationship with his deputy, Inspector Fox, is a triumph of understatement. Genuine affection and a world of understanding and intelligence is conveyed with a minimum of page space taken up.The plot is good, the final denouement not exactly surprising but distinctive and interesting and the characters are excellently sketched. There are no deep phycological explorations, just well described, disparate and recognisable characters, an enchanting setting and a good plot.Read moreRead less
