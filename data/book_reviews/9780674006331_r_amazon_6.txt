To be blunt, Richard Posner's prose is turgid, tendentious, and sesquipedalian. He writes as if he were a Federal Judge, which he just happens to be.  That said, his targets are deliciously deserving and deserve every word flung at their frontsides, their backsides, and various other directions as occasion permits.  Give his job as a professional observer of Man's folly,  Hizzoner has broken out of the law review mold to draw more generally from what is on his mind such that you'd almost think the intended audience was the general public -- notwithstanding that the prose is way, way past accessibility by Joe Sixpack.  So, to read this you'll hafta wanna, but if you can overlook the bombast and hack the conceptual and linguistic vocabularies it's actually a good (and extended) read.  Or should I just say that there are few others who even address his choices of topics in anything approaching so comprehensive a way, so Judge Posner gets the last word if altogether too many of them.Oh, and good luck trying to buy the edition "with a new preface and epilogue".
