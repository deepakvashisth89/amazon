This was a difficult book to enjoy most of the way through.  We understand that this is a Holocaust survivor who has seen everyone he loved die or disappear.  We want to reach out and understand him.  However, he is so caught up in his past that he is constantly tearing his present to shreads.  Frankly, we begin to lose empathy and/or sympathy with him until he is finally confronted with the cause and solution of his inner turmoil.  Actually, we know the cause but it becomes clearer as to why that cause is so rooted within him.  The solution is brilliant and worth the whole book for the understanding it brings to the reader.  "The Accident" that happens is merely a vehicle in which the resolutions are enabled to happen.  This book is an important addition to Holocaust Literature and is an essential reading for those truly interested in the subject.
