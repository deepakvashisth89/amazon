The work of Farid Ud-Din-Attar, a Persian poet and Sufi mystic of the 12th century. The theme of the work - the need to awaken the heart - is as relevant and vital today as it was then. The story begins with an appeal by the Earth to all the birds of the world, asking them to go on a quest to save her life by bringing back a message from the Great Being who lives in a garden beyond the edge of time. The Hoopoe comes forward and tells the birds that she knows the way and can act their guide across seven valleys, each one guarded by an invisible monster to be overcome before they can reach the Great Being. The journey of the birds is everyone's journey through life, the Hoopoe is everyone's guide.  The monsters represent destructive elements that must be transformed before the soul is free to act on behalf of life.You can read this classic work of Islamic literature as a Penguin Classic, or better yet, read this version.
