Once again, Professor Hilary Tamar leads the barristers of 62 New Square to solve crime in between their day jobs and lots of drinking. Warning: there is a cricket match with a detailed play by play near the end of the book, but you don't have to understand the game to follow the plot, which is good, because I still don't, even after reading Murder Must Advertise several times.The book starts with an estate entailment adjustment that I think Sarah Caudwell made extra complicated to make it all the more ridiculous. Julia, rescued from her erroneous arrest in book one, is a full time character is this book, instead of living only by epistolary. She is still a disaster, but hilariously so. Sarah Caudwell throws out plenty of direct Homer, Euripides, Sir Thomas More, and Shakespeare references but there are plenty of sneaky Dorothy L. Sayers and Mary Stewart ones as well. Not to mention, you better have a passing understanding of 20th century Greek political history and geography.I hadn't a clue whodunit, not until the very, very end. And it was a very clever ending. Maybe a little Agatha Christiesque? Definitely need to know your Golden Age mystery authors. Only two more books. I will probably binge them.Read moreRead less
