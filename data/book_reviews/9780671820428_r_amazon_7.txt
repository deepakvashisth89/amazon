Edith slowly allows her life to be highjacked by others, and the resulting strain shows up in her diary.  In her diary is another Edith, a satisfied and proud mother and citizen.  I really enjoyed this book, never was bored and the story has stayed with me.  This book spans the late fifties to the mid-70s.  What is interesting to a woman (me) in 2017 is that I can see how she is caught between being a good wife and mother a la 1950s and wanting to have more fulfillment.  She doesn't quite know how to enter the "ME" era of the 1970s.  You can argue that a woman in that position today may feel entitled to more, but yet I can't help but appreciate her sense of responsibility and unselfishness.  Or is she a coward?
