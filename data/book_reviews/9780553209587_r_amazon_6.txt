I'm giving this short book five stars because I enjoyed it so much. While not in the same league as Valley of the Dolls, or The Love Machine, there wasn't a page I didn't enjoy in this in-depth look at the life of a first lady who bore more than a passing resemblance to Jackie Kennedy.  The author might have even hinted at JFK's killer in a very subtle way, T. Regan Prescott(I don't have the book in front of me so I'm not sure of the initial). Jacqueline Susann is one of my favorite writers simply because she writes such realist characters, and having such realistic lives.  With her husband being a Hollywood producer and insider, there's no doubt that the author had lots to work with by what she saw around her.I loved being able to guess which characters lined up with the real ones.  I've read several book on Jackie Kennedy's life and this book read like one of them with all the dirty laundry thrown in for good measure.  Whatever flaws Jackie might have had, she loved her kids and raised them well, which is all that matters the end.  The story of Dolores hardly touches the lives of her children, it's more about her search for love and how she was betrayed over and over again in the name of wealth, even by her sister.  Dolores is the story of a woman who had everything the world said was necessary for a perfect life, yet her life was full of loneliness brought on by the very fame that most people would gladly trade her places for.The story ends just when it might have really taken off, but sadly that's because the author died.  Who knows what other books she might have written if she would have lived. I for one, would have read every one of those unwritten books. Maybe I'll just reread everything. Jacqueline Susann deserves nothing less than 5 stars for every one of her books.Read moreRead less
