A fascinating book which not only reminds us of the many social and political upheavals of the 1960's but how those events were portrayed on the screen  and how the movies of that era affected the culture.  Did the violence shown in "Bonny and Clyde" and "The Wild Bunch" reflect the turmoil in America's streets or did it cause it to happen?  Where Hollywood had once sung America's praises, films of the 1960's such as "Seven Days In May" and All The President's Men" cast doubts on the integrity of the military and our political leadership.  Hollywood was out to cover all its bets and the Vietnam war gave us pro-war films like "The Green Berets" and anti-war films such as "Coming Home".  In many ways, Hollywood was both leading and following social trends.  When the 1970's dawned,  public attitudes began to change  and movies reflected a desire for law and order with Clint Eastwood in the "Dirty Harry" movies and Charles Bronson as the avenging citizen in "Death Wish"  If you lived through the 1960's or would just like to learn more about it and see how events of those troubled times were shown on the silver screen,  I can highly recommend this book.Read moreRead less
