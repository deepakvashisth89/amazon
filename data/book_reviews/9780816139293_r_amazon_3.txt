This fourth collection of Father Brown short stories starts out in his old age, on a farm with the now-retired Flambeau. A man comes claiming Father Brown's ability to solve mysteries is due to some occult or supernatural powers, and Father Brown puts him straight. Although reluctant to talk about himself and what he does, he explains that his method was simply to put himself in the shoes of the killer, to "kill the victim" himself and figure out how and why it was done.To illustrate this, he tells the story of several mysteries in the past where he was involved, each one a different type often focusing on alleged occult activities and how Brown solved them.I didn't enjoy these stories quite as much as the first three books, but they still are all very well-written, well-crafted, and clever.Some modern readers, more culturally sheltered, may be dismayed at the frank racism and shocking things that a few of the characters say -- Brown himself has nothing to do with this and clearly disagrees -- but it can be jarring to people who have been isolated from the past and from those who can be awful.Read moreRead less
