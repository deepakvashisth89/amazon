I've just finished reading the three books included in this collection.  The first thing I'd like to say about this collection is that the story flows easily and that it has survived the test of time, seeing that it was written some 98 years ago.  If you've seen any of the movies based on the title story, then you will be pleased.  In my opinion, the book is better, although a lot of the story did make it into the Hitchcock version.  Richard Hanney, the main character is a bit of Sherlock Holmes, a lot of Alan Quartermain, and the progenitor of Indiana Jones, and because of these qualities, the reader quickly identifies with him and willingly tags along as he sets off on his adventures.  If you want a great tale about a man, confident in his capabilities, facing the world on his own, this is the book for you.A slight drawback is the language in the book.  A small percentage of it is dated as are some of the turns of phases used by the principal characters.  As well, since the principal character in all three stories is Scottish there are words and terms that are Scottish in origin.  Those with a wide reading background, grounded in works dating from the early twentieth century shouldn't have to much trouble with the work.  Those who don't might want to have a dictionary at hand.  Still, the narrative is well thought out and the writing style is such that the reader is carried along by the tale, taking little notice of these minor speed bumps.Another minor issue is some racial content.  This collection was written almost a century ago and the author has included sentiments that existed at the time.  I feel that these are not the author's views but plot devices that are integral to the stories.  They aren't intended to insult anyone and after reading the stories, it is clear why they were included.  However, if you are sensitive to such matters, which includes discussion of a 'world Jewish conspiracy theory' and in the 'Greenmantle' Islam and the rise of the religious fanatic in the Middle East as well as touching on homophobia and the use of the 'N' word, then you should avoid this collection.  Also note that `Prester John' is one of the author's earlier works and it is set in South Africa.  This book contains the greatest volume of racism of the three works, however one must remember that it is set in an environment where whites viewed blacks in stereotypical terms.  One should also note that 'Greenmantle' is in a way pertinent to our times, as it does look at the rise of Islamic nationalism in the region and the role of the zealot in changing the balance of power in that part of the world.Finally, I will say one thing about the publication.  Considering what publishing tools are out there for producing EPubs, I think this version of 'The 39 Steps' is a bit of a let down.  While there is a table of contents, you can't just go to it from any part of the book, and as there is three books in this publication, it is disturbing that there is no segregation of the three books.  I went from the last page of 'The 39 Steps' into the the first page of 'Greenmantle'.Overall this is a good set of stories and I recommend it to anyone interested in these types of tales.
