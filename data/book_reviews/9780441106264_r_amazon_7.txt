So I picked this up because it was on sale for $2 and it got good reviews.  I've read a fair bit of SciFi (both classics and modern), but I've never read anything by Simak before and honestly hadn't really heard much about him- he definitely doesn't get as much play as say Asimov.  All I can say is I wish I had read this years ago and I look forward to reading more of he work.So apparently Simak wrote this in response to WWII.  I suppose I see the link (hard not to considering the timing and subject), but as I was born in the 1970's and WWII was a long time ago I can't say I fully appreciate the connection.  That said, I really enjoyed City.  It reminds me a lot of The Martian Chronicles by Bradbury in that it's a series of short stories that become even bigger once you string them together.  The notes preceding each chapter really do add more depth to each story.Often when I read classic Sci-Fi, I often find it hasn't aged well.  Yes, the classics from Heinlein and Asimov are still good stories, but they feel dated and the writing just isn't up to par with people like Dan Simmons or Frank Herbert.  City isn't like that.  He doesn't tell, he shows. I never once felt the story got repetitive (as I did with the Foundation series) or that the story would conveniently jump ahead in a big hand-wave to avoid having to explain away an important turning point (Heinlein in the Moon is a Harsh Mistress).  Even though the stories are more then 60 years old now, it almost feels like it could of been written yesterday- although the opening two stories seem a bit far fetched in their premise today (I still want my flying car!).While I don't see people leaving the city any time soon thanks to the advent of cheap nuclear power and flying cars it never throws me out of the grove and forces me to suspend my disbelief to get through the story.  Instead the use of the Dogs as narrators, the characters and the story pull me in and I quickly leave my concerns behind as I read further along and begin to see the world as Simak envisioned it.
