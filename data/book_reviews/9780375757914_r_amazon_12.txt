I was very surprised at this writer’s attitude that anything can be allowed if it serves for the entertaining purpose, or for the philosophical purpose, or for any other purpose I cannot imagine of.  Restrictions which writers are normally supposed to impose are outraged.  In fact, Chesterton is little or zero concerned with probability or coherency, and the most unbelievable people appear, and the most unlikely events occur, yet he bustles you along with such gusto that  you have hardly time, and in any case little inclination, to protest.But generally speaking, when the limits of plausibility are exceeded, the result has the danger of becoming caricature.  After all, whether you call this book a masterpiece or not depends on how you judge the  plausibility in this book.For my part, I found the story very fantastic, but at the same time I found it hard to follow the story, because the characters were so surreal that I could not regard them as my fellow-creatures and eventually I couldn’t feel enough curiosity to know what they do next or what happens to them next.
