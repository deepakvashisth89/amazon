They say to write about what you know, and for many authors, this means writing about happenings in the place they were raised. Stephen King writes about spooky stuff in Maine. Lydia Davis writes about depressed people in France. Bobbie Ann Mason writes about the working class folk in Kentucky being swept along reluctantly by the tides of change.The last few stories within this collection are a breath of fresh air as they demonstrate less emphasis placed on regional mannerisms and inter-generational mingling. A sentimental undercurrent persists, but isn't applied quite so heavily.I'm not sure how well a collection of stories like this would age today, even with the current pop culture trend of basking within nostalgia. The N-word appears a few times, so fair warning there as I'm not sure just how much any young reader today would be willing to dive into the politically incorrect side of 20th century Dixieland.
