Having previously read several of MacLean's WW2 novels, I decided to try this one too as we were going on holiday to California. I liked the premise of nuclear warheads being in the hands of terrorists and reading how the authorities would fight back. I liked the main character, Ryder, and seeing how he dished out punishment to corrupt magistrates and senior police officials. However, this novel began with a huge amount of information about earthquake fault lines and later a lot of information about nuclear warheads. Clearly MacLean knew his stuff, but it diminished the paving of the narrative. The ending felt rushed too. So, overall, rather uneven in terms of structure and pacing, but still worth a read.
