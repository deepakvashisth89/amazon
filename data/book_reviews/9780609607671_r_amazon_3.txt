This book is a follow-up to Timothy Freke & Peter Gandy's 2000 (excellent) book - The Jesus Mysteries. If you enjoyed that book, you will definitely want to read this one. If you didn't read the Mysteries book, I strongly suggest you read that one first, and then this one. The book is well written and thoroughly documented, and it covers an impressive eclectic range of subjects and time periods.The authors continue the theme that Jesus was a composite of the myths and legends of the pagan world, especially the gnostic tradition which drew from the Greeks (especially Pathagoras and Plato). Then they go into the philosophy of gnosticism, often comparing and contrasting the gnostic view with the competing orthodox Christian view. Quite frankly, gnostic philosophy is not an easy read, and while Freke & Gandy do a good job trying to explain it, readers unaccustomed to depth philosophies may find the going a little tough.
