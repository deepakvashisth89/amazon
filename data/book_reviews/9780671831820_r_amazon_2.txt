Although the book is a fair read and entertaining, IT IS FICTION!  Too many of the premises used are unbelievable.  First, such a large reactor being built as a single unit WILL NOT BE DONE for exactly the reasons explained in the book.  That is why there are two and three reactor complexes for the generation of such massive amounts of power--and nowhere is there a single complex that could generate anywhere near eight million kilowatts of power as surmised in the book.Second, the concept of piping radioactive coolant some thirty or forty miles away, BEYOND THE STRICT SECURITY AND CONTAINMENT OF A REACTOR COMPLEX is absurd.  Heat exchangers are used to prevent the escape of ANY radioactive materials/coolant beyond the containment area of such a plant.Finally, the concept that a nuclear reactor could 'blow up', as the author surmises, is fantasy.  The amounts of nuclear materials in a reactor IS large, as put forth in the book, but it is not enriched to the point of being able to actually explode.  Even the worst nuclear accident in the world, at Chernobyl, did not cause an explosion--it was heat and insufficiently reinforced construction that cause the failure of the reactor containment building.  After the fact, the area WAS contained in a more heavily reinforced building.It is irresponcible bending of facts and further, alarmist conclusions presented as fact that should not be allowed into print without being thouroughly and prominently labeled as FICTION.
