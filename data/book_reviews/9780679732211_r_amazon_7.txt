Because I've read some of A.M. Homes' other work, I was interested in this early novel of hers written in 1989.  It doesn't have the punch that either "The Safety of Objects" or "The End of Alice" has, but it clearly shows the promise of a young writer.Written as a first-person narrative by a Jack, 15-year old boy in the suburbs, which is Ms. Homes' favorite landscape, we experience all of this boy's highs and lows.  His father and mother have recently divorced and his father has just told Jack that he is gay.  This, of course, has a tremendous effect on the boy who has conflicting allegiance to each of his parents.  Jack is also dealing with adolescence.  He is starting to date girls and take driving lessons.  His best friend's parents have problems too.  And someone has written some cruel words on Jack's locker at school.The book is merely 218 pages long and a fast read and drew me right into the mind of this young man and his conflicts.  If you are a fan of A.M. Homes, you might like it.  But the theme is simpler than some of her other work and didn't have the same kind of depth.  But I did enjoy it for what it was.Read moreRead less
