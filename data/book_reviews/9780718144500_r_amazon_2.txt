Nicci Gerard and Sean French are the husband and wife writing team, who write under the pseudonym, Nicci French. This book, “Beneath the Skin”, is the first of their work that I’ve read. The psychological thriller centers around three characters, Zoe, Jennifer and Nadia, who are each being stalked by a man who terrorizes them with threatening letters.Each macabre note indicates that the young women will soon die. Each story is told separately, and then eventually linked. Zoe is a struggling grade school teacher, who lives in a dismal flat in London. She gains brief and sudden local fame when she thwarts a thief by brandishing a watermelon that she tosses gamely at the would-be assailant. Her life is depicted as bleak, tedious and predictable.The description of her home was so desolate and grime-filled that I loathed where she lived and couldn’t understand why she’d continue to stay there another minute, however financially strapped she was. Additionally, her friends were a grim lot as well, a cadre of hapless men, who befriended her boyfriend, Fred, also a distasteful sod. Only Louise was an empathetic and warm character. I kept thinking, “Where did she find these awful people?” When Zoe began receiving the ominous letters, it just seemed more of the same, something else to be added to her already sad life. While I sympathized with her fear and panic, the presence of a potential murderer, was yet another horrific thing for Zoe to cope with. Needless to say, Zoe’s portion of the book was off-putting to me. She was earnest and somewhat endearing, but her circumstances were so prevailingly depressing, that I often put the book down.I wanted her to have self agency, and leave her horrible home and her scary environment for a safer, happier existence, but she seemed resigned to a dark fate.When next we meet Jennifer, she’s a more self- directed character, a busy wife and mother of three boys. She’s renovating their crumbling, new home, juggling contractors and bent on keeping everything running smoothly. Her husband, Clive, is frequently absent, so Jennifer handles the daily household tasks with the help of a nanny and a sporadic housekeeper. Jennifer spends her time supervising the work of those around her, envisioning the day when the house will finally look like what she imagines. Carpenters, painters, decorators and gardeners frequently traipse through the unfinished main floor, and there are always strangers milling about. When Jennifer opens the stark death threat that shows up in her stack of mail, her first response is to discard it with a laugh. She has far too much to do and to manage, than to take the words of a random lunatic seriously. She calls the police reluctantly, only at the insistence of one of her contractors. She apologizes for even bothering them, and is content to get back to wrangling the chaos of her home. When investigators immediately respond to the threat she receives, Jennifer thinks they’re overreacting. When she’s provided in-home police protection, instead of feeling grateful, she feels trapped and smothered. She’s used to the independence andautonomy that her wealth provides, now feeling uncharacteristically caged and stifled. Jennifer huffs about in not-so-quiet fury, unaware of the danger she’s truly facing. Only when she’s isolated and alone does she begin to grasp her actual peril.When Nadia enters the story, we find a confident, chronically disorganized woman, who is grappling with a breakup and a cobbled together career as an entertainer for children’s parties. When she, too, receives the disturbing notes threatening her life, she experiences the same obvious fear and trepidation of Zoe and Jennifer, but she vows to fight back. Unlike the others, she surreptitiously obtains confidential police files for the burgeoning case, delving into the psychological profile of her tormentor and the backstories of the other two women. She becomes immersed in the information she uncovers, hyper-vigilant, but also careless.The book contains some frightening moments,  as the women each confront the man who has watched and tortured  them for many agonizing weeks. The reader can feel the palpable dread and paralyzing horror that they endured. This is the primary fear of most women, a known or unknown attacker lurking in the shadows, someone obsessed and fixated with dark intentions in mind.The book is a gritty crime drama involving three very different protagonists, who are relentlessly besieged by the same man. The story arc from victim to survivor is clearly apparent, and I appreciated the efforts to depict Nadia’s personal empowerment as the narrative progressed, without denigrating what Zoe and Jennifer experienced.The book is well-paced and often engrossing.Each character is finely-drawn and distinct, yet lacks the inherent, intrinsic likability that one would expect. They are mired in unhappy lives and linked by their mutual trauma. The story held my attention, but the word that I kept coming back to was, grim. There is pervasive grimness everywhere. It permeates the tale, despite fleeting moments of triumph and hope.The lives of these women were already bleak beforehand, and then they faced potential death when they were arbitrarily targeted. Again, predominantly a grim read. Well-constructed, but not a favorite.Read moreRead less
