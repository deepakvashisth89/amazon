This is NOT the best dream book out there! First of all, I was put off by the disrespect Nacson showed toward dreams by actually including in his list of dreams "eating something that doesn't agree with you!"  Really? I don't think so. That lends dreams a haphazard air that does not match up with what we know about them.Second, thumbing through the dictionary whose layout did not impress me -- in fact, it made it harder to find certain symbols one is looking for, as it's all alphabetical, thereby losing the connection one needs when one looks under headings such as "Animals" or "Buildings" -- I found some of his "meanings" to be glib and intrusive of his own ideas. For example, "jar" lists ideas as to whether it is half-full or empty, but does not mention that it could be an obvious reference to being "jarred."If you love your dreams and have a true curiosity about them, and if you know just how sacred they are, you can find no better book out there than Wilda B. Tanner's "The Mystical Magical Marvelous World of Dreams." Not only have I read them all, I've studied dreams for 30 years. Particularly if you consider yourself to be a spiritual person or on a spiritual path, this is the book for you. I have sat with her book crying and laughing through her suggestions and interpretations, eventually becoming enlightened about my inner life -- and you will too.Tanner's tone is warm, loving, kind and encouraging, while I found Nacson's tone to be flip, glib and a little too sure of himself.
