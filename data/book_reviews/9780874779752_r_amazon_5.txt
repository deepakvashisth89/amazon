I am not a trekkie.  I have never been interested in science fiction.  I came across this book in my recent endeavor to divine the future of humanity (by understanding the past and the present to extrapolate the  future).  It was obvious to me that life's goal is to perpetuate life, and  part of this is by expanding its territory.  It made further sense that  humans, with their history of colonizing frontiers, should and must find  new frontiers.My purpose in reading this book was to ascertain the  feasibility of space colonization based on current technology.Though I  half expected rigmarole, Zubrin's book was lucid and thorough.  He has the  patience and ability to explain the mathematics and technology to the  layman so that the reader doesn't feel left behind.  Zubrin also has the  supreme teacher's gift of taking you step by step through his thesis,  beginning with man's first steps out of his homeland, a slight overview of  colonization in human history, a history of our aeronautical endeavors, an  understanding of our current space program (its problems and what can be  done), the near-term commercial possibilities of space colonization, how we  get to mars, why we avoid the moon, how we master this solar system, and  what we can do to get to others.  Along the journey you will be amazed at  the information presented.  Beyond your newfound knowledge of aerospace  technology: there is also an illuminating entry on asteroids (and their  history of pummeling earth as well as when they might strike next and what  can be done about it), a comprehensive discussion on the possibility of  extra terrestrial life (of which I am still skeptical), as well as a lesson  on terraforming, a means by which we may make inhospitable worlds  habitable.Zubrin is a compelling writer, and this is an excellent  addition to any collection of literate science.  In the end, the argument  seems to be that humans will colonize space not for commercial gain, but  because of the inherent human desire to explore and adapt and colonize.  Man cannot continue long without frontiers.  We are a technological  society, but cannot guarantee this will be such forever.  There are myriad  threats, including global warfare and large asteroids (eventually certain  to collide with earth).  We owe to humanity, to life, to begin colonizing  space.Read moreRead less
