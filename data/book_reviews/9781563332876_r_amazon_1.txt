Stan Leventhal's novel gives a fairly accurate depiction of what it was like to be a gay man of the Baby Boomer/Sexual Revolution generation in  Manhattan in the early 1990s. Stuck in a going-nowhere relationship and  living in a tiny apartment in the West Village, the narrator (whose name is  never mentioned, or at least it is not mentioned often) has to come to  terms with being HIV+, after having lost many close friends himself. The  book is an interesting glimpse that should have been a lot longer. But, it  is the last writing Leventhal did before his own death. Some aspects of the  story seem awfully abbreviated, especially the narrators very quick  acceptance of his HIV+ status (there are many other better books on that  topic). But, Leventhal touches on a lot of challenges facing a gay New  Yorker of his generation in the early 1990s: bad bosses, alienation from  heterosexual siblings, issues with parents, gentrification, and losses on  many levels.
