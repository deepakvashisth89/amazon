The setting of the novel takes place after the events of the ST TOS movie "The Undiscovered Country." The pompous Klingon Ambassador Kamarag, who warned the Federation Council in the TOS movie "The Voyage Home" that there won't be peace while Kirk lives has kidnapped Kirk's nephew Peter, whom people might recall as an orphaned kid whose parents were killed in the TOS episode "Operation Annihilate!", is now an adult and attending Starfleet Academy. Peter is captured and taken hostage to the Kiingon homeworld Qonos.Meanwhile, Sarek's wife Amanda is dying, and she asks Sarek to read her journals to remember how she and Sarek first met each other, courted, married, had Spock, and had many fights regarding Spock's upbringing. While reading the journals, Sarek is assigned to handle a difficult diplomatic situation involving abducted Vulcans and their ships perpetrated by Romulans and kept hostage on a frozen world in the Romulan Neutral Zone.All of these storylines come together at some point, and Kirk learns that Stockholm Syndrome still exists, even in the 23rd century: Peter Kirk is in love with one of his jailers, a Klingon woman, and declares her as his mate, while he fights for her affection against her suitor, one of his jailers following his rescue by his Uncle Jim, Spock and McCoy at the Klingon Ambassador's home.Meanwhile, Sarek has discovered the Romulan's evil plot against the Federation using the Vulcan hostages on that frozen world, and goes mano-a-mano against the Romulan leader using the ancient challenge Toriatal and poisonous knives. Sarek wins, and ask the Romulan leader, who's really a kidnapped Vulcan, to release the hostages. Two last things happen in the novel: 1: readers are treated to a Kiingon battle against the Klingon Ambassador that resembles the one shown in "The Undiscovered Country" and 2. Peter is a starship captain undergoing the no-win Kobayashi Maru scenario shown in the TOS movie "The Vengence of Khan;" he uses the ancient Toriatal challenge against the Romulan commander in the scenario to save time and evacuate the Kobayashi Maru's occupants to safety aboard the Enterprise.The novel has excellent storylines that are all tied together by the TOS characters and Amanda's journals. That said, the novel uses the tiresome device of Romulan abduction of Vulcans and their ships to advance their plots. How many novels or ST episodes can one read or watch where this storytelling method is used? I can name several: the four part Rihannsu novel series by Diane Duane, starting with My Enemy, My Ally; The Pandora Principle, by Carolyn Clowes; TNG episode "Unification, Parts 1 and 2", and this novel.All this said, what really gets me going is learning how Amanda meets and courts Sarek, a story that also gets told in two other ST TOS novels: "The Vulcan Academy Murders" by Jean Lorrah, and "Spock's World" by Diane Duane. Another enthralling thing that kept me reading and turning pages was learning about the ancient challenge Toriatal between champions, and the use of senapas, the poisonous knives.
