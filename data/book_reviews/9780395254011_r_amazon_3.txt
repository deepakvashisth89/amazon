Suddenly you are just given glimpses of people's lives. And people that I'm having trouble keeping track of - who's Trader? Who's Monkey? I can keep track of the mute boy, but that's about it. I was really looking forward to the setting of this book in Turkey 50+ years ago and sort of wish I had looked to see if there's a Kindle edition; this way,  I could search for Turkish things like borek and raki rather than come across them like little rewards only after reading pages of, well, boring text. I know she writes in a special way but boring is boring- if youre not enjoying it, what's the point? It's uneven and parts like the brother of the mute boy lost in the cave are striking. She's really good at giving vivid visual images. I saw a NYT review of her very first book that says she clearly had writing talent but the book didn't come together as art. This one is her 8th book and for me that's close to how I would describe it. This is the first of her books I have ever read and I guess if all the books are like this, I can see why she's a "neglected author". I know it won an award, but perhaps this is the kind of can't miss book if you study literature. So what i'm doing to get thru it is just to skip around - like I do for a magazine - doesn't seem to hurt the story any and maybe i'll stumble on more bits that will justify the expense (which dropped $1.50 right after -becasue?
