(*while Henry loves women, the sexual content is tame compared to most modern romance novels)This was my first (and likely only) Edward Abbey book, this book was chosen for a group reading. I personally felt Henry Lightcap was difficult to engage with as a character.The story opens with an older Henry, who has just been left by his third and last wife. In the progress of a drunken night, Henry decided it is time to make the 3000 mile trek back home from Arizona to West Virginia. Throughout the book Henry reminisces about his childhood (primarily his relationship with his brother, Will, and his father), and his lady loves (and there are many, but we only read about the significant ones). Throughout his travels to his childhood home Henry is accompanied by his faithful hound Solstice, who is dying from parasites from tick infestation, (and a likely metaphor for our protagonist himself based on our group discussions) by way of his beat up, aging (and also dying) pickup.Henry tends to be a dichotomy between his world of psuedo-intellectualism, and a more simplistic and archetypal world influenced by his childhood.Henry self sabotages when it comes to love, women, work, school, etc. The connective thread and driving force for Henry seems to be his brother, Will.All in all, this book is a good examination of the human condition, but has a bleakness that may not appeal to some readers. The are moments of humor, tenderness, and pain that engender at least sympathy if not empathy. If unreliable characters are your kryptonite, I doubt you will like this book; if you are up for a challenge (or you like narcissistic unreliable characters) then give this one a try.Read moreRead less
