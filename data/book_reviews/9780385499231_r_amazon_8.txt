Hemon has a great eye for detail -- there is no doubting that. And he is obviously well-read and intelligent.However, Hemon gets a great deal of mileage out of the fact that he is a Bosnian, writing in English. Readers seem quite willing to overlook his often awkward, unidiomatic phrasing, and contrived metaphors. I almost laughed when I picked up the book and the first sentence contained the expression "yokey sun". Later, I chuckled at "prostrate towel". Hemon tries too hard to leave a mark with every image.The other criticism I have is that Hemon borrows (imitates?) many of the techniques of Kis, Kundera et. al, but his work does not have the intellectual scope of those authors. If Hemon were just a guy from Chicago, and not from a war-torn country, one wonders whether people would be heaping the same amount of praise on him. Undoubtedly he has tapped into the West's obsession with the former Yugoslavia.That said, I too am waiting to see what he does next. Hemon writes neatly, and he is very readable, that alone makes him someone to follow.This collection is worth a read, but it is not entirely satisfying. Kis's Encyclopedia of the Dead is a far far better work of fiction and hopefully readers will move from Hemon and get to the really good innovative stuff.Read moreRead less
