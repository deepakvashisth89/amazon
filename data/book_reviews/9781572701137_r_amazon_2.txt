This is a really good book. I have never seen the original Hitchcock film, which kind of suprises me as I thought I'd seen most of his movies. Even though I was aware of this classic, I really had no idea what it was about which provided a great reading experience. Du Maurier is a wonderfully descriptive writer. You can just visualize Manderley and all of the gardens and property and smell the flowers and grass.I did think the book was a bit slow and repetitive in a few places which is why I gave it 4 stars. The narrator is a bit too vapid in places as well, but du Maurier bails her out so that you don't give up on her. It is easy to be preturbed with her, but in the context of time the story takes place and the fact that she is in her early 20s, her frustrating lack of a spine is understandable.I actually loved the ending. It leaves multiple unfinished threads allowing the reader to ponder the possibilities.My Kindle version included an Afterword by Sally Beauman dated 2002. I enjoyed her thoughts and interpretations of the book but disagreed wholeheartedly of her characterization of Rebecca and her similarites to the narrator. It is worth reading, though.Overall a highly recommended book which would make for a good bookclub discussion.Read moreRead less
