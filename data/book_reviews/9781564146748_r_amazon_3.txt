Various books on crop circles have been published. What attracted me to this one was the interview with Colin Andrews that Jon King included in his book Cosmic Top Secret. (This interview is also on the internet, in three parts.) Unfortunately, in Crop Circles Signs of Contact there is no reference to that interview. That interview was significant because in it Andrews related the pressure that he said was being exerted on him to declare that crop circles were predominantly made by hoaxers. He said there had been threats on his family, but also that he had been offered a considerable sum of money to be deposited in a Swiss bank account, so that he need never think about working again. That interview was conducted in 1995 and Andrews told Jon King that he was resisting attempts to make him declare that crop circles were made mostly by hoaxers. But then, in Aug 2000 he appeared on TV and declared that 80% of circles were man-made and that only simple circles were natural and that these contained magnetic anomalies. Andrews's statement was accompanied by a BBC segment that indicated that natural, simple crop circles could be explained by "elaborated animated computer graphics illustrating how magnetism may play a role in the creation of simple circles".In his interview with Jon King, Andrews was adamant about the role of the CIA in spreading disinformation about crop circles. The Signs of Contact book has a list of people involved in crop circles and the entry for Jim Schnabel reads, "Author of Round in Circles, a book that purports to prove that almost all crop circles in existence are hoaxes." And that's it! No other mention of Schnabel. In his interview with Jon King, however, Andrews was adamant that Schnabel worked for the CIA (UFO Reality magazine, issue 3) and concerning Operation Blackbird, he said that he was spied on and that "my EVERY movement, had to be precisely known by them. And the information they eavesdropped was given to Jim Schnabel of the CIA."However, in an appendix there is an entry for the CIA in which he writes of how he was approached by someone who claimed to be a CIA agent, but, "The alleged 'agent' was discredited by individuals who I consulted who had knowledge of the standard tactics and methods used by the CIA." However, he doesn't say who these individuals were and how he knew that they were telling him the truth.The question remains, did Andrews accept a large sum of money to declare that most circles were hoaxes and that the remainder could be explained naturally?Another area of controversy is the Oliver's Castle video. Andrews says it was a hoax and that the maker was John Wabe, who "came forward, admitted his involvement and then signed a deal with Nippon TV to participate in a documentary about the Oliver's Castle video." He doesn't say if the documentary was ever made. However, Andy Thomas discusses the Oliver's Castle video controversy in depth in his book Swirled Harvest. Thomas, writing in 2002, states that "a television crew from Nippon TV tracked down the man they believed was Wade, and even chased him across a car park in an attempt to interview him, but nothing was proven. An apparent 'confession' video, long rumoured to exist, has never publicly seen the light of day."I found the first two-thirds of the book to be interesting. It appeared to me that Andrews was attempting to repair his reputation by describing how crop circles were in fact not hoaxes. The Operation Blackbird chapter included the revelation that 440 yards from the site of the hoaxed formation, a real circle appeared and that its formation was captured on film, but that the tape was immediately removed from the site, whereabouts unknown.So, overall, it was OK.
