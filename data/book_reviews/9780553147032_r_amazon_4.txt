On a diplomatic mission to carry a number of Federation ambassadors to a detente with the Romulan Empire, the Enterprise is placed in peril when one of the ambassadors dies under mysterious circumstances. After the death of a second ambassador, an SSD colonel (Special Security Division...a never before seen branch of Star Fleet) is called out to the Enterprise to attempt to solve the mysterious visitations by "Death's Angel" before any more ambassadors are killed.So what we've got here is essentially a (poor) "who done it" style mystery which, as far as I can tell, has been inexplicably dropped into the Star Trek universe. There's a lot to stuff wrong here, some of it badly so, and honestly I'm only giving it two stars because I'm reserving my one-star reviews for TOS books that were "Fate of the Phoenix" bad. This is a (small) step up from that, so, two stars. The SSD officer is a walking bombshell, who's beauty instantly seems to incapacitate any male character from Kirk and Spock to the lowest redshirt. The ambassadors are an assortment of ludicrous aliens that no one's ever heard of. Even the SSD is completely fabricated.The main Trek characters are the biggest issue here and miss the mark so badly that they're unrecognizable. Spock is crippled by his emotions throughout the book, and he and his father, Sarek, regularly have pity parties for themselves. Kirk vacillates between unusual levels of anger and adoration for the SSD officer. McCoy is at least recognizable as McCoy. Beyond the issue of characterization is the fact that Kathleen Sky can't write a mystery. The SSD officer essentially goes about interviewing all the ambassadors, which leads to a number of quick dead ends, gets crazy drunk with a humanoid-crocodile alien with a fetish for human novel memes and as the book runs out of pages, McCoy essentially provides the answer to the mystery - an answer so obvious that a police cadet with two brain cells together would have started with that particular lead.One last minor quibble - admittedly a personal one - the ranking system for SSD officers involves different colored collars with different colored piping on the seems. Why are they the only division in Star Fleet that uses this style to define their ranks? How would a color blind person tell them apart?
