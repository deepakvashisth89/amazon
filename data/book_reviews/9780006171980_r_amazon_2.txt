The Richard Sharpe series is not to be considered great literature but it is rollicking good fun.  Mixing historical accuracy with graphic depictions of real events during the Napoleonic wars, along with a healthy dose of wry humor, these books become habit-forming for lovers of historical “faction.”  Not at the level of the Patrick O’Bryan “Master and Commander” series, which I highly recommend, but a jolly read where the bad guys are bad and the good guys are good, if a little naughty sometimes. Be aware that some of the descriptions of atrocities by the French in Portugal and Spain are gruesome, as they no doubt were in reality, but one can skip over without losing the story line.
