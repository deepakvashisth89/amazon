I read the excerpt of this book in an MBA career magazine recently and I have to say it's ridiculous. I am born and raised in Hong Kong and have worked in an investment bank there, so you bet I know a thing or two about  the Chinese culture. Look at what the author's advice is for doing business  in Hong Kong: 1)"Acknowledge the most senior person first by bowing.  Always bow lower than a person who outranks you, and raise yourself only  after the other person has done so..." -- Pretty conplex set of rules,  eh? Wonder if they're true? The truth is, we don't bow at all!! 2)'Touching  and patting are considered taboo' -- never heard of it. 3)'Men should avoid  crossing their legs while sitting' -- Not true. Never heard of it. 4)'Avoid  giving a clock. The noise it makes signifies death.'-- Imaginative.  Actually, it's true, but for the wrong reason. It's not the noise it makes,  but the pronunciation of 'give clock' in Chinese sounds like a Chinese  custom that is related to death. This shows how much an 'expert' the author  is. 5)Now this is the best one: "You will not be offered a napkin. It  is appropriate to blot your mouth on the tablecloth." -- First you  WILL be offered a napkin. And if you follow the author's advice and blot  your mouth on the tablecloth, you will receive the same look from everyone  at the table as you would if you do it in the western world. My impression  is that the book plays a lot of little tricks with Chinese customs the same  way Hollywood directors do, which is far from the truth.
