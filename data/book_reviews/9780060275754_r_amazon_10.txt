I've been meaning to read this collection for years - it's been waiting on my kindle for longer than I'd care to admit - but I've been in the mood for audiobooks lately and, luckily for me, this one was available from my library. I'm so glad I decided to listen to this one because Maggie Mash is a fantastic narrator, giving the heroine of each of these tales such a distinctive voice without any of them ever sounding cartoonish.Kissing the Witch is a collection of fairy tale reimaginings, each one leading into the next so that it becomes one big story within a story within a story within a story and so on. It could have become repetitive so easily and yet I spent the entire collection absolutely enamoured. Reimagined fairy tales are nothing new, but this is the first collection I've come across that's felt like a true reimagining; it's easy enough to guess the inspiration behind each of the stories, but Donoghue handles them in such a way that they simultaneously feel older than time and incredibly fresh.I enjoyed the entire collection, but the standouts for me were "The Tale of the Handkerchief", inspired by "The Goose Girl", and "The Tale of the Kiss", which is the only tale in the collection not inspired by a particular story. Anyone who knows me shouldn't be surprised that these two are my favourites, though, considering who their narrators are.I'm mad at myself for taking so long to read this - I loved it - but I'm already excited to reread it, and I'll be recommending it to everyone who hasn't read it already.Read moreRead less
