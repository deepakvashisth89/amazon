This is not just a personal (warm and affectionate but in no way reverential) account of how Springsteen's work impacted on the author's life - it is also a brilliant study on the historical, social, cultural and political background to Springsteen's work. It discusses the role history and class have in Springsteen's ongoing development and the significance of his work in the wider context of contemporary America. At long last an author that understands Springsteen because he understands his politics and consequently doesn't leave anything out. You won't find the level of biographical detail you get by the biographers, but you do get deep insights and a thought-provoking, very well written argument on why exactly Springsteen is so special.
