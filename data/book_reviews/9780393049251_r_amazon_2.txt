I picked up The Sleep-Over Artist after seeing it on an earlier version of the NYT Notable Books list.  It is certainly notable in the sense that it is written more like a series of short stories and less like a novel.  The book starts off with a profile of a young man named Alex Fader. He has what you may call a very basic life with absentee parents-and the eventual death of his father-- and he spends a lot of time sleeping over at friends' houses--hence the title of the book. We learn about his schooling which also doesn't go well until he picks up filmmaking which he very much enjoys and becomes the focus of his life.  Although in his earlier years, one would not think he would be the romantic type, he has an almost insatiable desire for sex and has many girlfriends throughout his life.  The one we learn most about is his relationship with a upper crust English woman who has a young boy.  It is the least obvious romance one could imagine but the one that perhaps defines Alex's life and really forms the backbone of the book.
