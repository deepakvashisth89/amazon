Spellfire tells a story of a young kitchen maid, Shandril, who longs for a life that is more than drudgery working in the kitchen, and with the crowds in the tavern telling tales of high adventure, it is just that adventure that appeals to her.So one-day she takes the plunge and runs off from the tavern to join a group of adventurers that happened to be passing through, little knowing the dramatic and traumatic events this would cause in her life.As the title of the book suggests, Shandril, goes on to wield the magical enigma that is Spellfire.  This form of magic is so powerful, and so dangerous to magic users that any person known to be wielding it is hunted down, either to be used as a weapon, or destroyed totally if they're found to be not compliant to their wishes.The characters in this book are strong and interesting, combined with a good plot, which is always a good combination.The only thing that stopped this being an excellent mixture is the fact that some of the dialogue I found to be quite nauseating.For the most part its good, however the interaction between the two main characters of, Shandril, and Narm, I often found unbelievable and overwritten.It's not their general conversation that's the problem, but their unfolding love story.  I won't go into detail as that might affect the plot, but I will say that I think that even most Mills & Boon writers would be reaching for the sick bucket over some of the love dialogue.Combine that with the fact the dialogue seems to get repeated a little too often in my opinion, and you are left with a weak area of the book.It may be I'm being a little over critical, as this is a good book with some excellent dialogue, but some parts really did grate on my nerves.I do recommend it, but with a little reservation.
