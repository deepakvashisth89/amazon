This is a good resource for learning about LTC.  However it refers the reader to the nolo.com website for state-specific rules.  I haven't had a chance to check out the web resources.  Years ago, when going through Nolo's 6th edition (2006), it seemed to be better than the newer 12th edition (2018).  To be fair, maybe I'm just being impatient as state-specific information is what I need most at this time.One other observation:  Nolo appears to take the position that LTC insurance is not a good use of money in most cases.  My USAA financial advisor thinks otherwise.  In as much as my stay-at-home wife has virtually no retirement income other than 1/2 of my Social Security benefit, should I require LTC without insurance to pay for it, she would be financially devastated.  For the same reason, I carry a Term Life policy to protect her should I pass away early in retirement.  For similar reasons, I also carry a very inexpensive Umbrella policy to protect the retirement assets we do have from being wiped out should we, for example, be sued for a large amount of money due to an automobile accident.Recommendation:  Read the book and learn.  Next, using what you learned talk to a financial advisor who can assess the risks to your retirement assets.  If you don't have much net worth to lose, and would be happy surviving on Medicaid and the restrictions it may place on you, Nolo's bent against LTC insurance may be justified.
