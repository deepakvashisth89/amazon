I just reread this book, 25 years after reading it the first time, and 25 years after graduating from Brown -- and a few months after going back to Divine Providence for the first time since graduating.Why on earth did I wait so long?  Memories.  They're back.  And they're wonderful.This is a superb read even if you don't know much about Providence or New England or the early to mid-1980s there; it's a blindingly great read if you do, and even better (if that's possible) if you lived through it.  No need for me to give away the plot: this is a fast read (a day or two), and a gripping one.And for those of us, like me, who lived in that town at that time?  Believable, and unbelievably so.  It's all there: Brown and RISD (and Yale) and College Hill; Benevolent and Power and Thayer Streets; the Avon and the Cable Car and Lupo's and the Civic Center and Kennedy Plaza and the Turk's Head Building and the Biltmore and the strip joints downtown and the Italian joints on Atwell and the cheap Chinese on North Main; Buddy Cianci and Raymond Patriarca and the Portuguese and the French (Canadians) and the Wasp establishment; even Roomful of Blues!  Wow.  Talk about a madeleine.  But Wolff is a superb craftsman in any event and for any reader, and this easily ranks with James Cain or Chandler or Spillane, but more literate.Read this book.  Now.
