I was referred to Publish & Perish as an example of the more recent `campus novels'. Although the three stories that comprise this work have university lecturers or professors as their characters, and a university setting in Casting the Runes, they really do not tell the reader that much about academia. One gets some idea about the importance and difficulty of publishing in Queen of the Jungle and some sense of the all-too-frequent problem of the appropriation of the work of doctoral students by their dissertation advisors in Casting the Runes, but nothing much beyond that.There is elegance to the writing and a few interesting academic and cultural observations and insights. But, I found the horror or supernatural elements in all of these stories to be unappealing and quite a distraction.Not what I would call a good recent example of the `campus novel' genre compared to Straight Man by Richard Russo or Lorenzostein by Mary Smetley, or the first half of Hynes's The Lecturer's Tale.Read moreRead less
