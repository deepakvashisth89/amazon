Initial D tells the tale of a highschool student who is not particularly clever or popular. He's not super strong, super good looking and he has no superhuman abilities. All he has going for him is that every night for several years he has had to deliver tofu for his father's business, in his father's Toyota Trueno AE86. The delivery route involves driving up a tall mountain, making the delivery and then driving back down again. He hates having to do this and has found ways to complete the delivery quickly, drifting down the hill on the perfect line to speed things up. As a result he's an expert level racer without realising, and quickly finds himself being roped into the racing scene (against his will) and 'battling' stronger and stronger opponents while also juggling his delivery duties, high school and even a potential romance with a classmate.Initial D is good for many reasons, professional racers were called in to offer their opinion and while a lot of the racing is heavily exaggerated there is a lot of actual logic to how it works. Technical details are brought up, explained and often used to turn the tides of a race. If you have even the slightest interest in cars, Initial D is a worthy read just for that.Initial D is quite a journey. It focuses mainly on the driving but a large aspect of the earlier chapters is about growing up and deciding what you want to do with your life. The main character is as indecisive as I was, so I enjoyed those parts the most.I would recommend Initial D to anybody.
