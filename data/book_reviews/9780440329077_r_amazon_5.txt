This was a first rate war memoir by a first rate writer.  I cannot recommend this book enough.  It will be a part of my library for the rest of my life.  Wm Manchester was an acclaimed historian who wrote excellent biographies on MacArthur, Churchill, JFK etc.  In the late 1970s he returned "to the scene of the crime," the Pacific theater of WW II.  WM himself served as a sgt in the USMC and he led a unit who called themselves the "Raggedy Ass Marines."  It was mainly a group of enlisted men who were college grads like WM.  They were scouts, mappers, message runners etc.  But every marine is also a rifleman and WM was an expert shot.  WM weaves past and present including tales about his father's WW I war experience, his own early life as a "nerd" and bookworm, his experiences at boot camp, his experience killing his first man (a Japanese sniper at Okinawa) and he vividly describes the beaches of Guadacanal, Tarawa, Iwo Jima, Peleliu and others.  He imagines and writes about the nightmare of the invasions.  Suicide missions for some of the first waves of marines.  WM brings his intelligence and writing skills to craft a very interesting and engaging book.  His ego is also fully on display.  By book's end I was almost in tears, I wanted to shake his hand, pat him on the back.  WM, in his late 50s, attempts to exorcise some of the demons created by his war experience.  All the marines who fought toe to toe against the Japanese deserve much credit.  I think about the horrors they witnessed and how those memories will never go away.  He was not a "gung ho" Chesty Puller type of marine but he was also no fool on the battlefield.  WM, in the end, comes to the conclusion that men fight for one another, for the love of one another.  This is not a book that glorifies war.  He writes about the horror of war.  There is drama, humor, sadness, friendship and more.  There are stories of men who were alive and well one moment and seconds later they were unidentifiable bloody pulps.  This is not an x's and o's book.  There is not great detail about battle plans and battle results (plenty of KIAs and wounded) but enough for me.  An excellent memoir, you won't be disappointed.Read moreRead less
