This is an excellent read if you are interested in how one of the most famous American playwrights wrote his plays, among them "Death of a Salesman" and "The Crucible."  It was written in 1987, and he died in 2005, so the book has been around for quite a while.  The scope is amazing, covering the Depression, the Spanish Civil War, World War II, McCarthyism and the HUAC blacklistings, Viet Nam and the protest movement, Miller's three marriages (including the second one to Marilyn Monroe), and his fight for the freedom of writers around the world to write as their consciences dictated.  The book is heavy on his philosopy, and it is fascinating to see how it changes over the years as he ages and becomes disillusioned, especially with Communism.  Through his own words, we come to know this complex, intelligent, creative and caring man.  Read it slowly and enjoy the ride!Read moreRead less
