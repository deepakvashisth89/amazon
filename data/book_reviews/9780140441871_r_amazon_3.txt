Although my high school Latin teacher made me read this for a huge final project, I was surprised by how much I actually enjoyed this book. Going in, I expected something dry and overly dense, but this translation made Caesar’s account feel much more accessible and personable. The translation is clear, and the message of Caesar’s writing stays consistent. This book really puts Caesar’s actions and thoughts into historical context, making it a mindblowing read. The content is fascinating, especially if you are interested in Roman history or ancient politics. Caesar makes subtle arguments and justifications for waging war against Pompey and the Senate. In a way, he forms his message into propaganda, but he makes you think critically about how history is written. The narrative is a true explanation for why Caesar is regarded as one of the greatest military leaders ever, as it dives deep into his strategies and thought process, something you would not know without a firsthand account. I am only giving this four stars instead of five because the novel assumes you have a bit of background knowledge. There are many names and terms that the majority of people would not know, which would lead you to look at the glossary a lot. Even I, a Latin student for 5 years now, needed to look in the glossary relatively often. In addition, the book had some areas where the text was unclear and somewhat difficult to understand. For the most part, the book was flowing and understandable, but some sections threw me off. But, for a book written over 2,000 years ago and translated, it is very impressive. If you’re a student working hard in Latin as a junior in high school (like me) or someone curious about how power worked in ancient Rome, this is an extremely good and readable choice.Read moreRead less
