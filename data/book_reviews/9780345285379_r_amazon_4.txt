If there's one thing I've learned about Marilyn Harris, it's that she likes her goods damaged. A character existing in her fictional universe will more likely than not have severely messed-up things happen to them, with the scars to show for it. It's the price of inhabiting such a magnificent place. That fact alone makes me glad that Marilyn Harris didn't invent me-- although I'll gladly be a spectator to the action. I'm guessing our authoress subscribes to the theory that suffering builds character. Either that or she's a sadist.As we begin our story, John Murrey Eden, the bastard's bastard, has just entered Eden Castle with the swagger of a pimp, but is soon made to know his place: at the top of a dung heap. Given a reprieve, the future Master Mason proves himself a chip off the old block and soon has Daddy's lover (his mother!),Lady Harriet, knowing her place: his bed. Once everyone knows who they are DNA-wise the consequences send Johnny-boy on the road and mother-dear into her chambers, never to come out, but not before she gives herself a Sophocles-inspired makeover. If only she and her son had been discussing Erskine Caldwell immediately prior to getting the bad news that she was in a smokin'-hot love affair with the result of her Secret Pregnancy. Perhaps she'd have embraced the incest, not reacted with horror. Heck, John was an open-minded person and he would've been cool with it, had circumstances allowed. Evidently the sex was that good.And this is where things get patchy. By no means is this a bad or even an average book. At times it was so awesome I could barely believe it, but then there were other parts where it was good, but good in a way that lacked the Harris touch, and for that reason it disappointed. Coming from another pen sure, well done, props. When it's Harris I need to smell money in the air, or it ain't right. Living as a clerk, building railways, living modestly in India, with the drama that those life situations can produce-- not the ideal material to use Harris' talent to its potential. Let's go where the cash is, please. Which is where we do go in the end, so I have hope for book four. Biggie & Diddy were right "mo' money, mo' problems"-- and better ones, too, in Harris' case.
