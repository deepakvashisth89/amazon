This book has absolutely revolutionized my relationship with Jesus and the way I read my Bible. I have been studying Theology, and Apologetics with the avid hunger of someone who had been starving in the wilderness for 34 years, as a former atheist. When I first started believing, I fell in love with Jesus first as a human being. In time, as I deepened my knowledge and matured in the faith, I slowly lost the joy of my salvation, as Jesus became the exact distant God the author sadly describes. I have been struggling to get back to my Jesus for years unsuccessfully, and this book was just what I needed, and a big answer to prayer.I don’t think this book intends to summarize Jesus. Most of the negative reviews posted here I believe may have misunderstood the point of the book, which is described in the subtitle. The author noticed that there was something lacking in the way Jesus is perceived by a big number (if not the majority) of people, as well as in what churches in general teach about Jesus, and decided to step in to bridge the gap. It is written in a very easy, colloquial style, not your typical theological, academic writing style, which I think also caused some confusion with the readers who perhaps were expecting this to be a theological treaty.My only disagreement with the author is in his choice of the word “forgive” when he tells us some of us need to forgive Jesus. What I think he really meant is that we need to trust Jesus and let go of our bitterness toward him when we experience deep suffering. “Trust” would have been a much better choice of word, in my opinion.In sum, this book achieves exactly what it sets out to do. Therefore it is a successful book! It is an honest, courageous, down to earth book which has helped me immensely, and for which I am very grateful.I would absolutely recommend it to people who have trouble experiencing closeness with God, and I will most likely order copies for the 2 young ladies I mentor.
