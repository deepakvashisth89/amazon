This volume by Lucia A. Salemme, `Compositional Exercises for the Painter,' along with his previous work, `Color Exercises for the Painter,' are two of the most unique and helpful books of this genre I have in my collection.  They are actually quite different than anything I have or have run across over the years.In this current volume, Salemme has used the same tools as in his first volume but rather than the focus being on color and color values, the thrust of this particular work is on pure composition; an aspect of painting and drawing that many, myself included, find perplexing at best, extremely difficult at worse.The author has broken his work down into seven parts, each addressing a different aspect of composition.  (see breakdown later in this review).  He has used the work of the master painters of the past and present to illustrate the problem being addressed.  As an example, he has used the work of Rembrandt van Ryn (Self-Portrait) to illustrate how a mood of mystery can be added to the work using pure halftones - shifting shades all blending to make a whole, and a whole statement.  The author encourages the student to attempt to replicate the work (not to copy it per se, but to use the method to create their own work).  On a personal note, I have found it extremely useful to indeed first try to copy the original work with as much detail as I am able, and then, once a `feel' is obtained, to go ahead and try to be more original.  Now the author does not give a step-by-step breakdown as to how the painting was created but rather concentrates more on why the painter, in this case Rembrandt, did what he did...what was the logic used.This books is 168 pages and features 62 black and white illustrations and 24 full color plates.  The book is broken down into seven areas of interest which include:Composition BasicsPerspectiveVolume and DimensionValuesSpatial RelationshipsMovement and TensionColor and CompositionThe text, while technical in certain aspects, is not TOO technical and most students with a elementary working knowledge of terms will find it comprehensible. Now the question is always asked, "Is this book meant for beginners or for the advanced student?"  I simply do not know what to tell you other than the more training you have the more you will get out of the book.  But that being said, I see no reason for the beginning student not to benefit from reading and trying the techniques addressed here because knowledge is knowledge, and small amounts of it are just as viable as large amounts.You can find this book here on Amazon, in the library or at a used book store at a very reasonable price and it is worth every cent you spend and every hour you study it...in my opinion.This book came from my private collection.Don BlankenshipThe OzarksRead moreRead less
