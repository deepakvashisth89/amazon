I could read Swain's Tony Valentine books all day, every day.  Swan's got to be one of the most original, creative and clever authors writing today.  His books reek with reality, from the "Pulp Fiction" -style dialog to Swain's incredible knowledge of casino gambling.  Some of the plot turns and characters are way way outrageous, but in Swain's hands they don't insult the reader.  You'll guffaw in more than a few places and you'll learn a lot about casinos and the people who inhabit them.  Unlike his other books, Swain sets this one in Valentine's home town of Atlantic City.  He gives us a nice clean, simple plot that develops linearally and is not cluttered with a whole lot of psychology and intrigue.  But from the outset, where Valentine's old cop partner gets killed, until the ending, where once again we find that crime doesn't pay, you'll enjoy a book that is a treat to read.
