Despite the dated style of the cover art, which is representative of the period of its publication, the story is actually very engaging and I found myself liking it, despite my personal preconceptions.  Being a rapacious reader of White Wolf publications and a connoisseur (sp?) of the vampire lore / canon laid down through numerous gaming supplements and fiction series, I initially began reading this book with an arched brow and snobbish smirk on my face.  I am happy to admit that the pace, content, and overall style presented by the author was smooth and subtley provocative, plausible, and elicits an effortless assimilation and acceptance of vampires, their abilities, and the application of these concepts in moving the story forward through the next installments (which I am not done reading at the time of this review!).Despite what you might initially think about the concept of vampires in popular fiction, make a deliberate effort to suppress that and pick up this book.
