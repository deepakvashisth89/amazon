From Spion Kop to the 1973 Yom Kippur War, there are 28 different conflicts covered -- six of which have several chapters --  by sundry military historians, including the redoubtable Alistair Horne, among many other talented writers.There are multiple compelling accounts, my favorite of which are those brief but powerful essays on Verdun (written by  Horne), the Somme, Stalingrad (which integrates several fragments of  "Last Letters from Stalingrad"), Kursk, and the Battle of the Bulge.Over the years, I've literally read the cover off of this book. It's chock-full of fantastic glossy photographs, maps, illustrations and breakouts with timelines and other details, along with others which suggest further recommended reading -- something not often found in modern books. It is very well-designed.I've read military history books for the last 40 years, and this was one of the first dozen I studied. And through the years, for school papers or other forms of writing, I've consulted it many times, and on other occasions decided to re-read the whole book or just certain battles.I've rarely read such chilling accounts as those on Verdun and Stalingrad. They've stuck with me for more than three decades. This book led me to read many other volumes on these battles as well as several others within its pages. Some battles that never seemed to get the same attention of others, such as the British campaign in Imphal, or for that matter, El Alamein, are nicely done, as is the Battle of Dien Bien Phu.Without creating an extensive list of the chapters, suffice it say that, considering its size, the book also contains detailed accounts of D-Day, the Battle of Midway, Iwo Jima and several others in both World War I and WW II, not to mention Tsushima, the air war in Korea and the battle of Tet.I'd highly recommend this book for someone who is developing an interest in military history and either likes variety and/or is looking for a particular conflict or era to focus on. The many photos and illustrations make it very easy on the eyes and the comparatively short accounts (as opposed to reading entire tomes on each battle) makes it perfect for this attention span-challenged generation of readers.Read moreRead less
