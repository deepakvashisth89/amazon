This is the seventh or eighth book in the Nolo Press series that I've read and they have all been outstanding, comprehensive and very well written.  My understanding of Nolo is that they are a legal organization dedicated to providing details about complex legal issues in laymen's terms. They are, in my experience, thorough, smart and well organized. It doesn't matter what you do or don't know when you open a NOLO book, you're pretty likely to find out everything you need to know and then some.This book is about Long Term Care, which is a misunderstood and often maligned product that is absolutely essential in a financial plan.  If you can obtain LTC insurance, you should. If you don't, by the time you need it, you won't be able to get it.  Many people believe long term care is covered by someone (though they're not sure who) and although Social Security has a long term care component, you have to be pretty far gone to get the benefit and the monthly allowance is than would be required in most cases for a day or two.  This is important.  If you are considering purchasing LTC insurance, this book is a good guide. If you have a parent who has assets and you are concerned if they get sick about protecting those assets, there is guidance here too. With the baby boomers growing older, LTC coverage is going to be of increasing importance and this book is an excellent guide.Whether you are reading it for general information (I am a financial planner looking to learn more to help my clients) or as a reference, you will be able to easily find answers to your questions and questions you should be asking.Last, in closing, there are a number of legislative changes that are up and coming based on the Affordable Health Care Act.  The details are not in this book (which was published before the Act was created).  Please use this book and double check the key facts in case there are relevant changes to the laws. I'm certain an update will be forthcoming from NOLO press.  This review is based upon the 8th edition which was published in November 2010.
