Although I'm probably older than the target audience, I found this book truly mesmerizing.  Cults and their effects on people have always fascinated me (which may account for a good deal of my interest in this book!) and Leaving Fishers is a chilling account of how things work and how easy it is for a lonely, friendless girl like Dorry to be sucked into them.We get to see things from both sides, as Dorry first joins and is overwhelmed by the love-bombing tactics, then rises in the cult (although not very far!) and becomes one of the love-bombers herself.  Leaving the cult is very difficult and the writer does an excellent job of portraying Dorry's conflict, as well as the tactics used by Fishers to try and lure her back in.Where this book really shines for me, though, is in the sensitive way it tackles the question of religion in general.  It would have been so easy to turn this into a book that condemns all forms of religion, yet the writer manages to walk the line between religion and religious cults without being judgmental.I thoroughly recommend this book to anyone!Read moreRead less
