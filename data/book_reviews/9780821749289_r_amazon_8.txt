Three and a half stars actually.While this book did not create nearly the fuss among romance readers asDaggers of Gold, her first Medieval-- this relatively short novel does an excellent job of appealing to those who enjoy a little romance in their history.It opens with two men appraising, in the frankest terms, the likely virility of a young man who they hope to hire to alleviate one of their mistress' problems, i.e., to provide her elderly and infirm merchant husband with an heir.Of course he does, to the satisfaction of all, and of course we know exactly what is going to happen in ten years when a new lord comes to Morlaix to claim the estate as vassal of King Henry II.  But that by no means is all of the story.Emmeline is a strong no-nonsense heroine,  much in the vein of Roberta Gellis'Alinor. She is competent to run her deceased husband's business and to make her own decisions about her future-- As a widow she had much more freedom than she would have done as wife or an unmarried woman.(The traveling matchmaker isn't particularly historical, but it does allow Emmeline to show her mettle.  Nor did the classes mix as much as Deauxville suggests at this particular time.  While a very rich merchant might have more ready money than a landed knight, the knight would always take precedent.  It is the landless knights who would be more likely to marry into the merchant class.However, these are relatively minor nits in an overall enjoyable (if not great)book.Read moreRead less
