This novel is hard to quantify. It seems from its blurb and title to be a fantasy novel about a woman who changes into something not quite human. But it’s mainly a psychological thriller, a feminist screed about the tyranny of men fought silently against the backdrop of one of the most threatening natural disasters there is.While the language and plot manage to be coldly chilling and mystifying enough to draw the reader like a helplessly hooked fish from one line to another, it’s hard to get a grip on reality. The novel starts with poor Jessie Waterman hogtied hand and foot while her rapist leisurely goes through her home looking for valuables. The subsequent paragraphs wring chuckles yet make you squirm as Jessie’s mind careens from the terror of her situation to wondering whether this robber will take valuables like the toaster or record player.This same rapist/homebreaker crops up intermittently throughout the story, serving as catalyst for Jessie’s inner wrestling to be free of that hated helplessness and fantasy other. The author keeps us guessing: does B.G. really exist? If he isn’t real, is he meant to be a stand-in for Roy, Jessie’s adoring yet constantly unfaithful and misogynistic husband?Jessie isn’t the only feminine influence writ large in the book. There is her daughter, Diane. Diane is struggling with her nascent sexuality, while Robin (one of her husband’s children by a former marriage) explores feminine power through dolls based after historical and fictional women of power. Kate initially starts off as being Roy’s lover…then comes to realize that it’s not Roy she truly wants.When a monstrous hurricane, named after a warlike goddess, threatens their safety, the novel takes a frantic turn. Perversely, it is now Jessie who is calm, certain that all the precautions she has taken will protect her and those under her care. Her nervous, dithering manner gives way to an inner hardness. Was it there all along or has the storm triggered it into being?Jessie does achieve control but by becoming a person who is nearly as unlikeable as her husband was. It’s a sobering lesson to learn. It’s as if the author would have us believe that women can’t seize control like men without taking on their worse qualities.While I didn’t care for this story in the end, I can’t deny its eerie, almost otherworldly pull.Read moreRead less
