Kirby Frye is the young, new deputy sheriff in the twon of Randado. While he's away, a pair of Mexicans are caught rustling. Several town leaders, egged on by wealthy rancher Phil Sundeen, form a kangaroo court and condemn the Mexicans to hang. When Frye returns to town, he objects to this. Sundeen's men beat him up and march him out of town without his boots on.But Frye won't stay down. The story that follows is written with Leonard's usual sparse but engrossing prose, telling a fascinating story about a man determined to do what is right. It's similar in some ways to the plot of Leonard's "Valdez is Coming," though Frye acquires more allies than Valdez did. In both cases, its about a man who won't back down due--not to pride--but to his strict moral code.All the characterizations are excellent, with both good guys, bad guys and those many inbetween having fully developed personalities and realistic motivations. The story plays out in sometimes unforseen ways, with the final confrontation between Frye and Sundeen bringing the novel to an unexpected but satisfying conclusion.Read moreRead less
