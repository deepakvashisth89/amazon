Read this. It's REALLY GOOD. I'm about half way through this book. I find it hard to put it down. It's subject, the times, the historical background, the characters and the way they are portrayed are all just amazing. De Courcy is such a gifted writer - the narrative is sweeping and flows effortlessly. So much to sink your teeth into: The strength and sophistication of the personalities, their courage and non-conformism against the victorian cultural backdrop. Personally reading this I became ambivalent about the main character (Diana), who was exceptionally charismatic, beautiful, authentic, smart, brutally honest, bohemian; but at the same time she supported some appalling politics, was generally egotistical, ruthless towards her surroundings in her pursuit of her own thrills (sexual, intellectual, social), and was totally oblivious to the consequences of her own morals (it could be argued that she contributed to the heartbreak which led to the death of Cimmie, the 1st wife of her second husband, with whom she had an affair prior to Cimmie's death). The consequences of the anti-semitism which she failed to oppose are too obvious to even mention. My favorite characters in this biography are so far Bryan (Diana's sensitive, gentle, poetic first husband, which she divorced for the fascist, muscular Mosley) and the witty Nancy Mitford who came to be at odds with fascist Diana over the politics of Diana's husband (and Diana herself). The fact that Diana, the subject of this biography, comes across as a three-dimentional, layered, captivating yet not entirely likable figure - is attestation to De Courcy's skill in sketching an honest, well-informed, perceptive depiction of the characters, the times, and the events.Read moreRead less
