As a traveler to Florence, I wanted a better understanding of Renaissance Italian art, so I bought this book based on the positive Amazon reviews.  This book was the wrong book to buy.  As the title states, it is a social history, not art appreciation.  But I like social and economic history, and the first few pages are indeed highly engaging.Since I normally read academic works in theology, history, and economics, I am used to dense material, but this I found  to be pedantic and pompous.  The material on Renaissance contracts, choreography, geometry, proportional reasoning, and appropriate gesticulation in preaching, are often worth reading, but when the author finally makes his point, I think he earns a roll of the eyes.  He could have made his point in far fewer words.Here is a sampling of points made (in nonacademic English):1. Renaissance art was a business.The book includes excerpts from contracts and business correspondence.Compensation varied: for example, some patrons paid for paintings by the square foot, others paid by the hour,and others paid by the piece.2.  Art was a luxury good that patrons (customers) often commissioned to impress others, as well as to please themselves.3. Taste in art changes over time.Example:  The book covers the change that occurred in this century from valuing a picture for the costly pigmentsand gold used in its manufacture, to valuing the skill of the artist.4. Art is a product for and of its era.Since we do not share the culture and education of Renaissance men,we cannot understand the pictures in the same way that they would have.  The past is a foreign country.The author provides examples of the knowledge, skills, and beliefs possibly prevalent in Renaissance Italyin order to show  how a Renaissance person  might have understood a painting.Be prepared to leap over gaps in the evidence.In addition, the book has low readability, with tiny font size and mediocre plates that are often on a different page from the relevant text.Look up "period eye" on wikipedia.
