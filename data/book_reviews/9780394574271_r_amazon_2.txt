Edgar Allan Poe has long been a major influence in the life of the late Sir Simon Marsden, and this book is the perfect complement between the two — Poe’s visionary writings of the eerie and macabre and Marsden’s uniquely haunting and atmospheric photography based around his infrared film and specialised printing techniques. Poe is best known for his darker works of the uncanny so needs illustrating by someone who deeply understands the genre, and few photographers do more than Simon Marsden.The works included are: Alone, The Masque of the Red Death, Mesmeric Revelation, William Wilson, The Oval Portrait, To One in Paradise, The Angel of the Odd, The Colloquy of Monos and Una, The Raven, The Black Cat, Berenice, The Fall of the House of Usher, The Sphinx, Annabel Lee, The Imp of the Perverse, Metzengerstein, The Sleeper, The Premature Burial, The Bells, The System of Doctor Tarr and Professor Fether, Silence — A Fable, Ligeia, and A dream Within a Dream.As befitting Simon’s photography, all the photos are monochrome, with the usual array of gothic imagery of memento mori, decaying buildings, deserted landscapes and the like, with a list of illustrations stating what and where at the back.It’s a large coffee-table book of 128 pages, with an introduction by Simon, which offers a fascinating insight into Poe’s life and character, and why he has so influenced him the way he has.
