In "Dutch" Edmond Morris has written what readers have dreamed about and writers have striven to accomplish for generations,the Great Americsn Novel.  In no other way than by creating a fictional character whose life parallels Reagan's and every one of whose interactions with him is based on fact, could he have given us this remarkable image of a man who was a magnificent mystery while at the same time an assemblage of everything distinctively American in the 20th century - meager Midwestern beginnings, a hymn singing mother and an amiable unfulfilled alcoholic father, handsome, ambitious, a lifeguard who projected his voice and looks onto the screen of  the Depression stricken national longing - a radio sportscaster, a movie star, a combination union leader and corporate spokesman, and, finally, the lead role in the great political and international relations drama of the times.  Morris captures this elusive man, so public and so hidden, so ordinary and so extraordinary,using fiction to find the truth.  As Saul Bellow once said to me,"Only in a novel can you tell the truth about our times."  That is exactly what Morris does, writing with skill that makes him one of the country's truly great writers.  Miss this book and you will miss meeting the 40th president who, in his plain folks manner, his ambition, and his actor's readiness to shoot life in discrete scenes, out of order if necessary, and whose simple message carried him through to undeniable achievements.  Morris give us Reagan and the 20th century American Dream in a great and hugely enjoyable book.Eugene Cullen Kennedy, Ph.D., Emeritus Professor of Psychology, Loyola University of ChicagoRead moreRead less
