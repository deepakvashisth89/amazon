I was hesitant about getting into this novel. I have several of Rosnak's nonfiction works, but from what I'd read about Flicker, it sounded pretty trivial. It's not. The book covers a lot of ground both about films & the film industry & about our culture in general. It's a good introduction to several different approaches to film criticism, & in that way has made films a lot more interesting to me. But aside from that, it's a good, fast moving drama with a lot of suspense & interest, but very little or none of the usual drivel: murders, romance, etc.  (There are some sex scenes, but not like any others I've ever read! I don't want to spoil it by trying to explain.) It's just a good thought-provoker that left me with more understanding of what goes on in movies & some good chuckles about the incredible foolishness that people seem to waste their lives on.
