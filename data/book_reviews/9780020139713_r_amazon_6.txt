Harry Mathews’ novel Cigarettes resembles a chamber play in its finely structured repetition of pairs of characters. Revolving around crisply defined chapters, thirteen characters are assorted in a complex web of affairs, frauds, and tragedies. Focalizing around Walter’s portrait of Elizabeth (which in turn generates a counterfeit), this world of money and art brings us in to a dense prism, where each character is experienced and then refracted through the eyes of the next. At times depressing, if irritating, readers will nonetheless be surprised to find this brilliant novel hilarious and finely wrought. The coda section is especially masterful.Read moreRead less
