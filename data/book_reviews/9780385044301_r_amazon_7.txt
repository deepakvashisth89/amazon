It is a Senegalese novel about a railroad strike in Africa in 1947. The events are fictitious, but based on another strike in Africa about the same time. The novel is written by a Senegalese native, a member of the French Communist Party and as such contains much propaganda and ideology. It is also an angry novel of the whites oppressing the blacks in the name of racial imperialism. Several times I was upset at the brutality but Sembene intended the reader to get angry, for he wished to educate the outsiders on the injustice of the idea of white controlling the Africans. The Africans wished to run the railroad with dignity, fair pay, and respect of their customs (Islam, polygamy, native languages). They did not wish to be like the whites. The novel serves as a landscape for the chasm of the whites and blacks, but it is good on pointing out that the various people of West Africa cannot be lumped into the same culture or tribe.The whites are not shown sympathetically except for one main character -- a former anthropologist -- and Sembene spends a considerable amount of time on fleshing out the various African characters, showing the roles of women and men and how each played their roles in the strike. An ensemble of characters, too many to keep track of, some were well educated in Western ways and some knew only the ancient ways. Thus he is also sketching a portrait of old versus new Africa, and how a community works in spite of the evils of the white man.On the whole, a powerful book of a brutal time and a book to remember. There was nobility in the Old Man, Fa Keita, a dignity similar to Gandhi, even though Fa Keita did not support the strike. Yet he became part of it by an arbitrary arrest. Some of the organizers were not quite so noble and the leader Bakayoko realized that hate was a necessary factor. The book hints that though the strike was won, the struggle for dignity for the African will continue. On the other hand, I see the book for the politically correct crowd: dignity for the African, white colonialism being evil.
