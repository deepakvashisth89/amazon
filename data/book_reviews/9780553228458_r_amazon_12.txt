When I first saw ZEMINDAR by Valerie Fitzgerald while browsing for historical romances, I thought the title resembled some old Bollywood masala movie, and expected nothing less than a novel in the line of Jane Eyre [a comparison that most reviewers found irresistible], altogether devoid of any sense and reason, written by someone whose daydreams followed whiny, superfluous girls who bicker constantly about their independence and self-will, but allow themselves most gladly to be seduced, dominated, exploited and made ‘useful’ by svengali men; a novel that would only satisfy those who mistake abuse for love. Had it been just that...!!! But this un-put-down-able book has hardly allowed me any sleep for the two days following the evening I picked it to read.ZEMINDAR IS A MUST-READ. It is a timeless tale, a howling [mark that word] historical romance, more historical than romantic, that will definitely stay with you forever!Zemindar’s magnificent prose is a stunning reminder of Victorian classics. Though most people relate Valerie Fitzgerald’s writing with Charlotte Bronte’s, I felt more of George Eliot in it – that keen observation, that no-nonsense approach, writing without romanticising anything, and characters that we all know and see around us! It still is hard for me to believe that Zemindar was written in 1980. In spite of being the first person narrative of Laura, it accurately picturises both sides of the story, British and Indian; an indifferent observation of the myriad of mistakes made by vain, ignorant people resulting in one of the most brutal struggles mankind has ever seen. It has been an eye-opener to me in many parts of history - particularly in the popular belief the history textbooks taught me - the British purposefully destroyed India, which probably and more accurately was because of their lack of understanding than malevolence. I was sad learning that those Europeans who understood and loved the real India were never allowed by foolish and pompous officers in rising and helping the country. Also, our Indians were no less than British, whatever we preach regarding the struggles for Independence. Certain descriptions are very brutal and upsetting that I stopped reading at times and went through other resources to check their accuracy; I was stunned by the scrupulous attention given by the author in describing them..!!! Besides, both Indians and British people had been utterly foolish at time. I laughed many times at the lack of common sense in leaders of both sides, though nothing less of a serious nature was found anywhere. Given the vivid, stunning descriptions of political and natural weather and landscape of Northern India, I was not surprised learning that Valerie Fitzgerald was born and brought up in Lucknow, before our Independence. Her grandmother, who was the inspiration for Laura, had lived through the siege of Lucknow which is the central event of the story.The love-story of Laura and Oliver is not anything exceptional. Some people may not like it – an old school romance, very chaste, very basic, and very simple, devoid of any big twist and melodrama, and almost predictable. Anyhow, I loved it, because their love develops in a slow, leisurely manner, more like the flowering of a bud, beautifully and sensibly, sensibly because Laura doesn’t allow herself be swept off her feet for petty emotions, and Oliver is not the typical bad boy who yearns to be redeemed through the blood and love of a virgin! But the character I loved most is Charles. He is very much inexperienced in life, thus makes mistakes after mistakes until he learns from them. It is a sad fact that he is explored less than adequate though he is a central character who is constant throughout the story unlike Oliver. Also, I felt that some scenes, very trivial ones, are there only to show him and Mr. Roberts inferior to Oliver. There are too many characters and details to be included here, a lot of them real persons. Misfortunes many other characters face during the siege are more saddening than the experiences of Laura and her friends.I have always disliked stories in which penniless girls fall for mysterious strangers who are rich beyond dreams. But this one, this timeless saga, like a powerful earthquake, destroyed the minarets of my expectations. It is so perfect, so beautiful, so magnificent...!!! No sentence in Zemindar is unnecessary, no word is misplaced, nothing in it can be replaced with something better – and that is a towering achievement for a first time novelist.  Yet it is not a novel for everyone. It is 830 pages long, takes a lot of time to develop its characters and events, and those who like more action oriented, fast-moving stories are to be disappointed.
