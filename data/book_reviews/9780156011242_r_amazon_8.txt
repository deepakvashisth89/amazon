For me the appeal of this book was twofold, the writing of William F. Buckley Jr (as I have previously enjoyed his spy fiction) and the subject of James Jesus Angleton (that I wanted to find out more about). Especially in a fictional setting that Angleton mostly shows up in from other authors depictions of him.On both of those counts I wasn't disappointed. Buckley's writing has always been both erudite and eloquent, and surprisingly touching and affecting with some scenes. I don't remember the disastrous sex scenes or descriptions that others have mentioned, but I do remember a portrayal of a man, vulnerable and lonely in self-isolation that made him memorable as a character. I don't purchase Buckley for the sex, if I wanted that I would pick up an erotic novel, but to me mostly Buckley handles sex in an intellectual manner that elicits sympathy for his character rather than disgust. I appreciate his delicate handling of sex without being vulgar, and it actually makes the emotion more powerful and exciting that way for me.The only time I had some trouble with it was how it kept switching back and forth from Angleton's POV and to a more third person POV, from Angleton's diary to the events happening in the story. Also a nitpicking note: From another reviewer who said that this book had to be written by a woman instead because (to quote them): "How can I be sure that the author was female? Consider: the male characters have an adolescent, romanticized, and idealized view of women, while the women are either prostitutes or opportunists. Only a woman would be so naive about men and so cynical about women." I disagree with that assessment. As a woman myself I am not that naive about men or that cynical about women. That opinion seems to me more male chauvanistic than real proof whether the author was a woman or not.I wouldn't call this a perfect book, or perfect Buckley book, but I am glad I purchased it for the sympathetic telling of a man in an unusual position in history, in the CIA, and in fiction.Read moreRead less
