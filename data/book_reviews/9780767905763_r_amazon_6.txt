I think I heard an interview of Pelton on Bob Edward's radio program. I thought they were pitching a new book. The interview wasn't compelling enough to cause me to buy that new book (it must have been "Licensed to Kill") without first buying a cheap book (this biography) to learn about the author. I usually buy Kindle so I can do searches and apply annotations. But the Kindle version of this book was more expensive than a used paperback ... so I read the cheap paperback.On the plus side, this book is easy to read in short sittings. It's just one short anecdote after another so you can put it down and get back to it with little trouble. It is Pelton's biographical anecdotes interspersed with his travel experience anecdotes. Both proceed sort of chronologically but don't seem to be related to each other (i.e. childhood experiences only loosely relate to the travel experience at hand).I read this book with the quick reference resource of Google and Wikipedia. To me it was essential to understanding context, both of his youth and of his travel experience. I had little knowledge of the places he was visiting or the people he was meeting. I was able to read about the geography, history, politics, etc. of each. This proved to make the reading more interesting ... but slightly less believable.Amazon's prompting for me to write this review prompted me to revisit my reading of it in the first place. Going back and looking at Pelton's books, I find his last new book ("Licensed to Kill") was written in 2007 (thus not new)  leaving me wondering why Edwards was interviewing him in the first place.I have higher priority reading so I'm putting pursuit of Pelton on the shelf.Read moreRead less
