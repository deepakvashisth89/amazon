5-7-5 is not the standard for most of the best haiku practitioners in English. The only place where 5-7-5 is the majority is in the classroom when taught by English teachers who've made no study of the form beyond: "5-7-5, season word." For one thing 5-7-5 onji in Japanese is not the same as that number of syllables in English. Some aim for 3-5-3, as closer to the length of the Japanese poem, or want a poem that can be said in one breath. A rigid adherence to 5-7-5 often leads to poems that are stretced and squeezed in unnatural ways to fit, and also to poems being considered haiku because of the syllable count.
