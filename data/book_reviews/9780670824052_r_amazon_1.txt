Whether Graham Greene's intended this to be his final literary work, or if it just was, it nevertheless displays the author's lifelong personal exploration and struggles with the mixed bag of true humanity amid life's conflicts and ambiguities. In other words, Graham Greene doing what he does best.Reprised again are themes from previous Greene works, detailing the hell we create for ourselves and others, while pursuing our vain and foolish ambitions as flawed humans, sometimes finding our better selves, yet always seemingly falling short.This poignant tale is set in three locations: rural England, greater London, and around the Panama Canal. The story covers selected experiences of Victor Baxtor (also known as Jim), bridging the post-World-War II period to the Central American crises surrounding the final negotiations to return the canal from the United States to Panama in 1977.Rejected by his schoolmates and sold by his father to "the Captain," Victor's life becomes entangled in the lives of two people victimized by the boy's absentee father.As with most of Greene's works, this short, focused literary novel can be taken literally or allegorically, as the reader wishes. Greene's understated plotlines, each imposing its own motives, collide through ill-timed and incomplete fragments, twisting the storyline into a convoluted and tortuous tale of human woe, dispair, and sad consequences of flawed wishful thinking.Most insightful to me was how skillfully the author's storytelling used vignettes ranging over two or three decades to shape heartwrenching glimpses into the struggles of the human soul. Perhaps that is what appeals most to me in Graham Greene's works is that the author truly has a soul that values sparks of sacrifice and loyalty among common human beings.To me, this final publication reveals missing pieces about the author and his previous works, completing Graham Greene's contributions to our common human experience. Hidden in these pages are wise insights, observations, and lessons learn from the nearly 90 years of the author's troubled life.I found this a most satisfying and informative read and a meaningful valedictory expression from one of the best authors of 20th century.
