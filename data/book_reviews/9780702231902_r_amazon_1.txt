When I started this book there was a point, very early on, where the pain of this author's childhood was almost too much to take ... But I decided to read a little further since the book was so highly praised in Australia.  I very quickly saw beyond the pain and really loved it as an exquisitely written childhood memoir, and it then became so much more: her love of books and penchant to dream, World War II and her love for her returned soldier, making her own family while not forsaking those who came before.  Above all I am amazed at the tamimg of the Australian country, especially when I located the places she was referring to on Google maps.  Wow.  The untamed land she lived on and cultivated now resembles the very beauty and straight lines of the Paris countryside!  She looked on the hard work taken on by herself and her husband as a matter of course, her only complaints echoing all complaints of any and all ways of life.  Most especially I related to her interest in books and all things educational.  I also love books and it is a shame for those who don't because they don't have the constant comfort and distractions of books.By the way, I couldn't find the roundabout in Bangalow of her title on the Google map ... and I really wish she could have included some photos of the people and places she writes about.  But, I always want more of everything when I find a book as good as this one!  WHY aren't these types of books published and/or publicized is America?
