George Stewart was a Professor at UC Berkeley who wrote novels on many topics. He's best known for "Earth Abides," a post-apocalyptic plague story that does not moan and woe about loss of electronic media after an EMF pulse. "Storm" and "Fire" are realistic novels of environmental events, where the event itself becomes a character in the plot. "Storm" (1941) looks at a winter storm, dubbed "Maria," that hits Northern California, how it arises in the Pacific, how it grows, makes landfall, devastates individuals and communities in its path directly or indirectly, and the weather forecasters and road crews attempt to 'control' and 'fight it', humans vs "Maria," a force of nature. Guess how that goes? 'Maria' is the protagonist throughout. The technology only seems dated, but the action familiar as any Sierra snowstorm in the 21st century: how do you keep two-lane US40 or four-lane I-80 highway at Donner Pass open?
