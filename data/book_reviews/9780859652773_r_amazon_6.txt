First things first, while I have long been a fan of metal, Marilyn Manson's music is not my cup of tea. But having been impressed by his manner in some interviews over the years, I approached this book in the hope of a decent insight into the man. And the first chapter- detailing the bizarre sexual perversions of his grandfather- was amazing, awkward, unflinching and brilliantly told. And then after that, it seems that the ghostwriter left Manson to his own devices, leaving a few notes for the structure of the remainder of the book. I found that Manson steered away from the type of introspection and self analysis that would have interested me, and instead started pointing fingers.Teachers, Religion, intact, all the usual authority figures get a serve. But it gets tiring quickly. He'll attack someone for their hypocrisy and I would think...'Well fair enough.' Then paragraphs later he would gloatingly recount some wrongdoing of his own, but with no effort at regret or even self awareness. Now to a jaded old codger like me, I can find my critiques of the modern world in more appetising fare, and better written and researched as well. If you are a young fan, then you'll possibly be impressed at Manson's vehement attacks on 'the man.' But if you've been through that phase of life already, then you'll find little of interest here.I feel that the book never really explores who Manson really is, it's just some more publicity, cocaine, goth chicks, knives, self harm blah blah blah, the usual rock n' roll  excesses are here in the usual detail. But that's not interesting. Marilyn Manson has always sold himself as an interesting product of modern society, yet he describes the most banal rock story that any fan of music bio's has already read...and written far more convincingly than this, I might add. You'll find more suitable reading fromThe Heroin Diaries: A Year in the Life of a Shattered Rock Staror better yet Anthony Keidis' story inScar Tissue. And those artists never claimed to engage in any great social discourse, the way Manson has sold himself to the media and fans all these years. An image that isn't addressed much at all here, and it feels like that's because it is only an image, not a true passion.It isn't all bad, that first chapter was utterly brilliant, but it all ends up like some myspace page by the end, with lists of rumours cribbed from the Web, standard drawings of internal organs and things embellished with some suitably 'Goth' font and poetry. Oh god, the poetry. That almost loses one star for me, but that's really a matter of personal taste so I'll be generous and leave it at that.One for the fans only, perhaps.Read moreRead less
