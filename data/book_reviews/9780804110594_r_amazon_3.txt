An elaborately written chronicle of America's sexuality revolution. I learnt of the author Gay Talese in one of this year's New Yorker issues, in which he published The Voyeur's Motel. Likewise, a non-fiction, this book threads through the dispassionate, unblinking narrative a literary finesse, rendering personal observations, dialogues and emotional interaction in the most intimate, reflective way. The book is an outgrowth of extensive research on history, modern society and people, as well as of the author's singleminded exploration of the sexual labyrinth in 20th century American. It took me nearly three months to finish -- and reading gets dull sometimes -- but it's a book very worth reading: it simply changes your mind.Read moreRead less
