It would be difficult to say something new about a seminal work like Fear of Flying. In short: Jong's writing is brilliant. She was able to evoke all the desperation of the protagonist through an intimate first person narration without demanding pity, or scorn, or that feeling of helplessness that a reader has when a character they care about begins making careless choices. Isadora Wing's strength is evident through the offhanded comments she makes in her internal dialog drawing from eight hundred years of literary figures and quotes. She is at home nowhere, so she's equally ill at ease in New York, or Paris, or Florence, or Lebanon, whereupon she relates her experiences in French, or Italian, or ancient texts.Of the many remarkable things about the work is her inexhaustible ability to find exactly the right word. I was sent to the dictionary more than once and each time I found myself saying, "Perfect. That's the perfect word." Make no mistake. The book would have been a masterpiece even without Jong's exceptional diction. But, her word choices, cadence, drawing upon just the right literary allusion gives the prose a richness that any lover of language could lavish in.I read the book 40 years ago, stealthily borrowing it from my parent's bookshelf. As a teen I found it confusing. Forty years later I found it spellbinding. Jong's work is lightning in a bottle that has lasted 40 years, and will likely last at least 40 more.Read moreRead less
