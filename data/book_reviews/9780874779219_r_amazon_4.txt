Ernest Holmes, founder of the Church of Religious Science, wrote this epic book as a "textbook" for those interested in a practical approach to metaphysical and New Thought ideals.  I was first introduced to many of the concepts back in 1994, when I read "Creative Visualization" by Shakti Gawain (another book I highly recommend), when I was going through a particularly dark period of my life, and seeking answers I could not find in traditional orthodox religion.  While "Creative Visualization" was an adequate introduction, "The Science of Mind" really expands upon and explains these concepts more thoroughly.  It is intimidating at 600+ pages, but once you get into it, you really don't think about it's length!  Since I first acquired this book in 2007, I've read it 3 times straight through, and will read it again, I'm sure.  Since I have applied these concepts to my own life, I've noticed definite improvements, and notice more every day!  The underlying message that everything is One, and that we are all an integral part of the Divine Creative Principle is both comforting and empowering.  "It is done unto you as you believe" is a truth for all ages.  I really feel that Ernest Holmes was ahead of his time when he wrote this book, and the truths it contain transcend time - in fact, a lot of this is more relevant now than it was when it was originally written.  I've long since collected every book by Ernest Holmes I can get my hands on, and I find all of his work very illuminating and spiritually liberating.  His books always make me feel good about life, and give me the realization that I do, in fact, have control over my life.  If you can get them, I also recommend the "Change Your Thinking, Change Your Life" 6 volume home study course in "The Science of Mind", which gives an even deeper understanding of the concepts explained in this book.On a sidenote, this is the 1938 revised and expanded volume of "The Science of Mind".  The original 1926 version was shorter and, in my opinion, not as well organized as far as subject matter.  I have tried to read it, but find this 1938 version much easier to navigate.  However, the 1926 version does contain an entire section on psychic phenomena, which was intentionally omitted from the 1938 revision, as Ernest Holmes (according to his biography "That Was Ernest" by Reginald C. Armor) felt that much of it just couldn't be proven.  Still, it is an interesting read for those interested in the field of psychic phenomena.
