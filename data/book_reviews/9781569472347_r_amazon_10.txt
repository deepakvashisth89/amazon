I have been waiting for this book for about ten years... it was ten years ago that I first went to Japan and ever since then I have longed for someone to write about their experiences of Japan and the Japanese - not as  a traveller merely passing through, but as a person who has lived and  worked with the Japanese, who both can empathise with them and yet find  them even more enigmatic as time goes on.  This book made me laugh a lot -  but knowing Will from Kumamoto days, I had expected that, but it also made  me cry which shocked me to the core.This is the first book that has  articulated the feelings I have held about Japan and haven't quite been  able to express as Will has: that love-hate relationship, the wabi-sabi,  and for all who have lived there for any amount of time, it should strike  deep chords.  For those yet to go there, it is a wonderful introduction to  (and preparation for) the "real" Japan, the Japan of the rice padis and red  lantern restaurants, of kindness, of hypocrisy, of gaijin vs gaijin-san of  the bizarre and poignant.What I also appreciated was the background  history that was entwined within the book.  I certainly learnt a lot and  wished I been aware of all those cultural snippets of information before I  went.I would thoroughly recommend this book to all who have had some  Japan experience whole-heartedly and for those who have yet to go - take  note of the konnayku reference and it will stand you in good stead for the  trip!Read moreRead less
