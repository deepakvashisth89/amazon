Borrow this your local library if you feel the need to learn more about this person. Please don't waste your money. The purple prose used in some reviews here is not merited. The book is too densely detailed and could have benefited from competent editor. This life does not earn a two volume set. The subject of the book (IMO) was the Kim Kardashian of her time.*spoiler alerts* Her 'rise' to infamy was based largely on marriages to two millionaires and numerous liaisons before, during and after both. Her jealousy towards both husbands' infidelity was laughable, in the face of her behavior. She managed to write one decent play (The Women) with the heavy assistance of a professional writer and her then husband's money. She was the 'mother' of a daughter who was shipped off to boarding schools when her presence got in the way of Ms. Luce's full time social agenda. Her racism (could not find decent domestic help for her Southern manse because of FDR's social programs - wrote a letter to him scolding him for his safety net financial programs) and so-called feminist activities (less than one month in DC) are cringe worthy, even seen through the prism of more modern thought. So much for cutting edge and ahead of her time. Her globe trotting journalistic trips were funded by Mr. Luce's money and some of the articles she wrote were not published due quality issues. Her purchased political win is not worthy of the brief discussion in the book's final chapter.Personal footnote: if your affairs are so frequent that you need to remember them by circling a page number in your diary, something is off in your emotional makeup. A human being who can face, embrace and accept their true nature is a life worth writing about. A professional B-girl with a big wallet who lies to everyone including herself (and both of her therapists - that's in her diary, too) is not.Two stars - this does not deserve the energy to hate it. I do hate that trees died to make this book though.Update: I am returning the sequel book (Price of Fame) that I bought. I have better things to spend my money on.
