I have always found Robert Barnard's books enjoyable, and this one is good enough to read once and then, 10 years later, still findit fresh on a second reading.  The subject of the evacuation of British children in the early years of World War II has alwaysfascinated me - Americans could not imagine sending their children away to strangers living in relatively safe places, but itwas a widespread event in 1940 when London was being bombed nightly.  What effect did this "exile" have on these children,at the time and in later life?  Read this book and see how one story develops - you will be amazed.
