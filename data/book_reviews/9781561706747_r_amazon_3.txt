First, I must disclose I've met the author on several occasions. I watched him anchor the news while he did his stint in Phoenix.  After reading the book, it's clear I incorrectly assumed this talented, handsome newsman probably had the perfect family and the perfect life. His honest and engaging narrative of a man, trying to locate his father living on the streets  is a reminder that no matter how people appear, their true stories may shock, surprise and inspire you. Du Pre's story does that with unrestrained honesty. The abundance of his childhood and family begins to slowly crumble as his lawyer father's broken vows and habits led to the emotional and sometimes literal famine that plagued Du Pre's growing years. His search, sparked by seeing a homeless man shivering in the snow, begins an odyssey of seeking what we've all so generically termed "closure" with our pasts.
