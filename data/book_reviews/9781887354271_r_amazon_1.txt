Anatomy of the Lighthouse is a carefully crafted celebration of maritime architecture that reads like both a field guide and an elegy. The book’s physical presence matches its subject: the binding is superbly executed, weighty, and tactile in a way that suggests permanence while inviting repeated handling. The text moves confidently between technical explanation and human story without losing clarity or momentum.Photography and designThe photography is rich and inventive, treating lighthouses less as static monuments and more as layered objects—detail shots that read like close-up anatomy, wide landscapes that restore context, and inventive angles that reveal how these structures meet wind, salt, and light. Layout choices support the narrative: plates and captions are integrated so the images extend the text rather than interrupt it. The result is a visual essay that both instructs and lingers.The Cape Flattery lighthouse: a case study in careCape Flattery Lighthouse sits on Tatoosh Island at the entrance to the Strait of Juan de Fuca and is the northwesternmost lighthouse in the contiguous United States. Built in the 1850s and first lit in 1857, its stone dwelling and 65-foot tower served as a vital guide for mariners for more than a century. The station’s history is layered with cultural contact and contestation: Tatoosh Island was long used by the Makah people for seasonal fishing and whaling, and the lighthouse’s construction and later ownership touched on complex relationships between the tribe and the federal government.The island’s isolation created chronic maintenance challenges: limited fresh water for the fog whistle, leaking roofs, harsh weather that accelerated decay, and periods when the station fell into near dereliction until emergency or restoration work occurred. Automation and later replacement of the original lens and light with modern beacons reduced the human presence, but did not remove the need for active stewardship; by the early 21st century the buildings required significant stabilization and repair, and the lighthouse was designated a National Treasure to draw attention and funding for preservation.As Anatomy of the Lighthouse illustrates, Cape Flattery’s story is a textbook example of why lighthouses require continuous, informed care: material vulnerability from salt, wind, and waves; the technical obsolescence of lighting apparatus that shifts responsibility for upkeep; and the social dimension of stewardship when cultural ownership, federal agencies, and local communities must coordinate to save historic fabric.Read moreRead less
