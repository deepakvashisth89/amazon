Taught myself pretty well with this.  Used some supplemental readers for additional practice, but this will teach you all the grammar you need, say, to hack your way through Caesar's Gallic War.Forget immersion courses, except possibly as supplement to Wheelock. As a baseline course they are for lazy teachers or else for bona fide language savants.  Stick with the centuries-old approach that taught all the best educated men of days gone by.When my children suffering through Lingua Latina homework ask for help, I can take one look at the worksheet and rattle off a quick lesson on what they are supposed to be learning.  Recently such lessons included indirect questions and jussive noun clauses.  Sometimes  I write out a table describing what should have just been presented to them in plain English.  Wheelock prepares you to recognize and understand these things from the ground up. Lingua Latina just leaves you guessing.  If I am fuzzy myself, then we just turn to the Wheelock lesson on the topic (and there is *always* a Wheelock lesson on the topic). In a matter of minutes they are able to complete their homework with better understanding than if they had been left to guess their way through.I suppose if you are the kind of person who enjoys doing things the hard way or driving 10d nails through you sensitive parts, then go ahead and jump on the Lingua Latina craze.
