I really like Chief Inspector Rostnikov, whose nickname is Washtub. His formidable shape comes from weightlifting. He has a Jewish wife, which is a political disadvantage. But he's very good at his job.In this book the KBG saddles him with a case of potential terrorist attacks, which should be their responsibility. The timing is sensitive. The Moscow Film Festival is in progress, and foreigners have flooded the city. Rostnikov is being set up as a scapegoat, in case the terrorists succeed.The Chief Inspector has a dedicated team, each with their own cases, which they are obsessively pursuing. Tkach is hunting down a gang of rapists. Karpo is after a thief of paintings at the Tretyakov Gallery. Tkach has an interesting family life.  Karpo, a fanatical socialist, has no life outside his burning desire to serve the State.The urgent need to stop the terrorists before they strike is, of course, the priority. But the other cases enrich the plot.I found this book a pleasure to read. The characters are engaging, the time and place are fun, and there’s plenty of action.
