I read this book when I was just beginning to turn into one of those hormone wrecked teens. Maybe that's why it made such a huge impact on me. I found myself wondering what I would do in the same circumstances and sympathizing with the characters. The author does not say which option is right or wrong, she doesn't preach, but merely makes the reader understand that no matter what the character chose - mistake or not - it was a very difficult and important choice. She does not make the mistake of a happily ever after ending either, but acknowledges the many obstacles ahead for all of them. In the end, it is the characters' determination to make it work out that give the book a hopeful ending.It was my first look into abortion. Afterwards, I was still interested, and for several school papers I chose this topic to explore.(Several spoilers beneath)The stories of Diedre and Treva hit harder than Pepper's. Perhaps it's because of the fact that Pepper has already had an abortion before. I know that if I had one, I would never want to repeat the experience (especially since the author describes the painful process).The best and most heart-wrenching part of the book is when Mitch - the man who got Diedre pregnant, later goes to an abortion clinic to try and understand why and how it felt for Diedre to get an abortion on her own. It is with this new understanding that he meets Diedre with that makes me believe that they might have a second chance.After all these years, I can still remember the character's names and believe me, that is saying something. I read so many that usually they blur together! (Well, maybe that's my bad memory's fault too...) Anyone could learn from this book, regardless of race, gender, or age.
