This is an elliptical and bizarre journey through the minds, emotions and affairs of Djuna and Lillian. There are other people involved but the novel centers mainly on their attempts to make sense of themselves and each other.Anaïs Nin played with language in a way that baffles the mind to make coherent sense of it. You get the impression that the two women are traveling through an odd kind of dreamscape, one informed and deformed by the myriad characters that encounter them. They try to find happiness in sexual affair only to wind up anxious and lonely as the men disappoint them or keep them at an emotional distance.But you don’t care about the men so much as you do about the women. You don’t yearn for happy endings because they care for none. The author wrote them to be read in no particular order. So Djuna and Lillian are both attached to the present and detached from the future.If you like reading with more than a touch of the illusory and surreal, the novels herein will be the things for you.
