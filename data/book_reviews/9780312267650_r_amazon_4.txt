I love Brooks Hansen's work. 'The Chess Garden' is probably my all-time favorite book. I really wanted to love his newest one, 'Perlman's Ordeal'. I really did. There are so many minor parallels between this and TCG, and I found myself slipping comfortably into his beautiful prose many times. Unfortunately, There are some major differences between this novel and TCG. First, Perlman's a dilletant. There's really no other word to describe him. He has his office, his snootier-than-thou thoughts on music (which he never shares except for once), and his grandiose plan on healing those in need. Within his own little world, he is Lord and Master.... then Sylvie Blum enters his life. On a whole, I kept turning pages, thinking 'When is all this going to come together?' When it finally did, it *was* beautiful, and harmonius, but from the picture to the final scene, the narrative wandered, was fuzzy, and most of all disjointed. The 'story' Nina (with help) tells is broken up into a few tellings, each becoming more complex. The final telling, for a dinner audience, is further broken up into 3 'scenes' and in the end, it becomes hard to tell where the motive force is coming from: Mme. Barret, Nina/Oona/Sylvie, or Perlman himself. It is beautiful to see the sense of 'reality' dissolve into the 'Nonsense' Rolls and a sandstone crescent. It is frustrating in the fact that Dr. Perlman is merely a conduit for all this, being basically unchanged, or at least the novel leaves us before we can get that sense.
