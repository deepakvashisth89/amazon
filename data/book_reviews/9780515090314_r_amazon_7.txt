This was an Anisfield-Wolf award winner (1961), so there's a certain amount of prestige attached to this book.Despite its award-winning status, I disliked the first third of the book. I found it to be rather slow and disconcerting. I don't think that it stood up to the passage of time the way that some books do. There were a lot of prejudices expressed, which may have been tolerated back then, but that aren't widely acceptable nowadays: body shaming, misogyny, classism, etc. – all things cringey.To the book's benefit, it did pick up a bit about 30% in. The last 2/3 of the book was much more upbeat and hopeful, as everyone began growing and learning from one another. There was also a lot of good use of descriptive detail throughout the book.The chief selling-point, however, is that it is a good read from an educational standpoint – giving the reader insight into the prevailing racism of the time. Coming from an insider's perspective (this book is an autobiography), it's an invaluable lesson in that period of black history in the UK.Read moreRead less
