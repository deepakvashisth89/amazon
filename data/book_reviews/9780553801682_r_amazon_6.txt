As in all the Ian Rutledge books this is a very good book. The combo writers who are Charles Todd do a usual marvelous job immersing us into the complex emotions of WWI and post-war UK. I learned much about Rutledge's relationship with Hamish which provides a richer understanding of the characters.But the ending...was...so...abrupt, I searched the list of Rutledge books to read excerpts to see if the story continued into the next book, which, from what I can surmise, it does not.It was as if the authors (not any of the characters) died after penning the final chapter. In the past I have been disappointed in how books have ended, but never one that would be so easily fixed. If I went to a good movie and the theatre lost electrical power with 15 minutes remaining and I never saw the ending, I would feel the same as I did at the book's ending.But it is worth the read, especially if you like the series and intend to read on. The only reason I could see for not tying up all (or most) of the loose ends is that some of these characters will appear in other books and the authors are not sure how they are to be used (but that is a kind reprieve that I am providing.)Read moreRead less
