Before diving into “Lights Out” the reader would be well advised to be acquainted with Coleridge’s “The Rime of the Ancient Mariner.” To not would be to miss an integral component of the story and the protagonist’s interpretation of life around him, first as it was in prison where he’s been living for 15 years and then of the explosive situations he encounters in quick succession after his release without much more than the clothes on his back and a few clues to what happened before he was incarcerated at age 19.Except for a teenaged boy working in a NYC used and rare bookstore, with whom Eddie develops a trusting bond, and Eddie himself, there are no truly likeable characters in this tale of crime, punishment and redemption.  Eddie “Nails” Nye is admirable for his sense of honor, his dogged determination and the survival skills developed in prison as well as those created on the run as he pursues the elusive truth about his long imprisonment for a crime he didn’t commit.Although he vows to leave prison absolutely behind, he finds it impossible to escape it completely. Influences from behind bars and locked doors reach out to manipulate him time after time and at nearly every turn he takes upon his re-emergence into the world. Old friends, and there are very few, are not trustworthy. Neither are the new people he meets. Even worse, his beloved big brother, Jack, is no better. Yet, Eddie  remains true to him in spite of what he learns of Jack’s involvement in the event that landed Eddie in deep trouble.Listen to a bit of “Poinciana” and “Malaguena.” Sip a little Planter’s Punch Think of the Bahamas, clear turquoise-blue seas, soft sandy beaches, coral reefs, sweet breezes; Boston, ice, snow, bitter winds;  NYC, financial wheeling and dealing, high-rolling night life, grim streets, rain, rain, rain.
