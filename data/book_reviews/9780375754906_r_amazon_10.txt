Arnold Bennett has slipped into obscurity. Hugely successful in his day - a century or so ago - the majority of his fiction is no longer in print. The Old Wives' Tale is the exception - and what a fine novel it is. The story of two sisters as they move through life, it is beautifully written, extremely readable, and filled with well defined characters, it is a masterly piece of work. On the evidence of this novel, Bennett was a superior writer to  his still in print contemporary Somerset Maugham - and I say that as a Maugham fan. Of Human Bondage is good novel, but there are areas of human observation in Bennett, plus felicities of expression, that are outside Maugham's range.
