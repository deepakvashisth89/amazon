I know Henry James is considered a Great Author, but I found this book almost unreadable. Having read and disliked James years ago, I thought I'd give him another chance with this short novel. But it seems I was right the first time.  This is the story of the child of divorce, who is bounced between households of mostly absent adults.  Maisie's parents and step-parents  are unsympathetic and self-absorbed, and they treat the hapless Maisie as a burden and a weapon against each other.  Her governess is appalled by the promiscuous lifestyle of these adults(only implied; there's nothing remotely sexy here), and wants to "save" Maisie from them.  This depressingly scenario constitutes the whole plot.  James's writing style did nothing to relieve the tedium. I'm not sure whether the book was intended as a satire.  Except for short bursts of welcome dialogue, it consists of  sentences so convoluted that they must be deciphered by figuring out where the subject and predicate are and then omitting all the intervening clauses.
