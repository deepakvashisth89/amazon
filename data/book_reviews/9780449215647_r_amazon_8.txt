Mary Stewart is one of my favorite authors from the 60s and 70s. Literate, suspenseful, with intriguing characters, how could anyone be better? I bought this classic Stewart hoping to recapture those days of reading magic only to find the scenes I loved as a teenager are hopelessly dated. Not only does our heroine constantly defer to her husband, a professional spy, but also to a teenaged boy, simply because he is male. Despite being a qualified veterinarian, and a grown woman, she seems uncertain of herself, simply because she keeps looking to the male characters for confirmation or rescue.  The magic of the story line was overwhelmed by her self-deprecating statements and actions from pre-lib 1962.I kept updating the story line in my head: the movie newsreel becomes a YouTube video, the lack of communication is solved by cellphones.  But it is the constant little comments by the "poor little me" heroin that really flattened what had been a wonderful book 50 years ago. This story simply hasn't aged well.
