For obvious reasons, we don't study religious philosophy in school--it's based on a set of rules that were not agreed upon--but I definitely think this one is at least as valuable as Plato. While Augustine's discussion is primarily based on religious principles, he also brings up relevant points, like, "Why do we spend so much time fearing death when it's inevitable and we should instead focus on living?"In truth, I'm just happy this book exists. I've been researching the 5th/6th century and have discovered there's not a lot written around that time. The Romans kept good records, but this is about the time their Empire fell. The City of God was written before the time period I'm researching, but it's one of the books they would've read during the time period I'm researching, and that's super cool. Reading this lets me step back into time and see more of how the world was back then.So if you like philosophy and history, I recommend this book.
