Two Years Before The Mast  by Richard Henry Dana, jr.This is for the Signet Classics edition, with the nice picture on the cover.Every book can be considered a voyage, and this one even more so. And a good voyage it is.So many stages are sailed and walked upon in this story: notable are the icebergs and ice flows encountered on the return trip, glowing like immense crystal islands. Snow, sleet, rain and wet clothing that never has a chance to dry out. A mast touched by strange corposant light, raising superstitions. Or a man so ancient that he has to keep his eyes open with his fingers. Calm nights, ablaze with stars, with creakings high aloft in the sails.Throughout the journey, the author remains down to earth; this makes the narrative genuine.  He claims no great heroics, never says he’s better than the more experienced sea hands, and never assumes a condescending stance by virtue of his schooling. In fact, he often admits that he has no understanding of certain things, but he is willing to learn. And he does.Thus, his departure on the PILGRIM, and subsequent return on the ALERT, creates a tale of two Danas: the  untried, inexperienced Dana that leaves port, and the roughened and confident seafarer that returns. And so the voyage has changed him; there’s no denying it; a certain aspect of innocence has departed forever. This loss seems to manifest suddenly, in an unforeseen rise of apathy at the final approach into Boston harbor. Things, once familiar, now look different from the water.
