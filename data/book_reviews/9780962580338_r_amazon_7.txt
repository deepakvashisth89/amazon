I had wanted to read this book for a long time. Eventually, I bought it 2nd hand... and I have to admit, I struggled with it. Written in the aftermath of the furore of MZB's 'Mists of Avalon', it seemed that every other book written by an American female author was, shall I say politely, a homage to MoA'. Whilst this deals with another British legend, that of Robin Hood, there are definate hints of 'MoA' here. That said, it isn't a bad story, the characterisations are quite good, although the name 'Maerin' is just annoying! Robin does rather better, though as I went on, it became clear that the book was somewhat influenced by 'Prince of Thieves' (Loxley's father being accused of devil worship & the family home being burned down)and then the 'Alan a Dale' character's noble lady being married off to an old git, was pure Robin of Sherwood! Neither of these incidents are part of the mainstream legend but were added on in more modern times, so more research please. That said, the author spins a good tale, though she does get a bit carried away with her own prose - there were whole sentences that should have been struck out, and the book could have lost about 30 pages with no interruption of the plot. The usual US slang slipped through before I'd read more than a few pages, execrable but expected, but the worst was the New Age USA Wicca replacing good ol' Trad Brit Witchcraft. Oh deary me! The funny thing - in spite of all the mistakes, it could have been a great book, which is why I gave it 4 stars. There has been no sequel, which was rather a shame, because given half a chance, I think the author would have improved. This though was written like a movie, which is another set of skills altogether. But I willread the book again it just might grow on me.Read moreRead less
