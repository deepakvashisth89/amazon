This Widowed Land (Paperback)by Kathleen O'Neal GearI was surprised when I cracked the cover of this book how current it still reads. The books were written in 1993, but it still proves today that people have not changed. In the news just before I started the books a religious group had just pasted the deadline of the end of the world, with the leaders claiming that they read the signs wrong, that their predictions were off. As I began the book I was surprised that the contact between North America and Europe in the 16th century was also seen as the end of the world. The Jesuit's believe that finding the Native American tribes in the north east were the sign of at last finding the lost tribes of Israel. I am amazed to find out that writings of Geronimo De Mendieta wrote in 1554 " who knows whether we are not so close to the end of the world that the conversion of the Indians is fulfilling the prophecies for which we pray that the Jews may be converted in our time? They believed that because if the Indians descend from the Jews, then prophecy is fulfilled."Besides the historical characters shows in the book the personal struggles of the fictional characters give this book a characteristic that would bring us back to this story over and over. I love the use of Historical characters to base how the interpersonal relations between traders, and the "black robes" and the Indian populations. The story of love found and lost by living life and the choices marked mark the path of your future.It struck a key note in the story about the inter relation between the two belief systems between Christianity and native Americans, and the echoes of similar between the two, and that they both do not truly cancel each other out, especially if you step back and remove the symbolic nature of religion but put the aspect of what is the meaning of religion, that is the same.This widowed land is a great story.Read moreRead less
