This is a complete rewrite of Fowler's Modern English Usage. Unlike the second edition from the 1960s, it has virtually none of Fowler's original prose and does not claim to follow his thinking. In other words, it is Burchfield's Modern English Usage, trying to cash in on the popularity of its illustrious predecessor.There is no doubt that Fowler needed an update, language has changed a lot in the last 50 years. The elegance of Fowler as a tool was that, although it was prescriptive, it was rarely hidebound - it did not insist that it was exclusively right other than where that was obvious. In fact, the original was written with such wit and understanding of where English was at the time, that one read it as much for pleasure as for information. I'm afraid Burchfield's revision has none of its predecessor's charm and very little of its conviction.I bought the Burchfield edition and then rapidly searched for a replacement for my second edition of Fowler - the Gower revision - which had long ago collapsed. This is still available and, although I use the Burchfield sometimes for corroboration, my main port of call is the second edition. In fact, I enjoy it so much that I bought a facsimile of Fowler's first edition - lovely!
