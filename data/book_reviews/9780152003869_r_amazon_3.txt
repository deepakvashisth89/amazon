I just read this book to my daughter yesterday. She rarely stays for the entire reading of a book (usually wanting to stop the story and draw parts of it or stop the story and act out parts of it), but she stayed until the very last page on this one. Erika Tamar and Barbara Lambase complement each other wonderfully here. Tamar's story is about a young girl's trip to the beach and how, in a style that perfectly captures a child's complete immersion into their own wonderful imagination, she fully realizes the world of a mermaid before having to be interrupted from the dream by the trip's end, the less than wonderful ride back home that is so sadly different from the undersea world she imagined; but the lived dream becomes the dream to be dreamed again in sleep that night. The writing is poetic, and call me crazy, but I hear echoes of Yeats when the girl keeps repeating a line that becomes more powerful as the story goes on:  "I will be called Donnatalee." She imagines beautifully descriptive scenarios of what different animals will do for her in an aquamarine world, the scenes both domestic and fantastic are all described with words that evoke the blaise boredom or the enlightening brilliance of it all, and if ever a book justified letting children have their imaginative life unimpeded (which a parent truly cannot stop or control anyway), this is it. And Lambase's illustrations are soft and rounded and uniquely captivating. I wish I had better words to describe it. There is a slightly Impressionistic quality to it. Hair moves up and in directions it shouldn't and gets our attention that way; cityscapes focus on the lives of the people that live there, seen in the immediacy of their lives on a hot summer day; and then the scenes of Kate's imagined life under the sea are drawn with a brilliance, beauty, and fascination that is not over the top, that is not Disney-ish but is exactly the dreamy happiness we would like it to be. This is a large book, so the pictures are nicely sized, the pages are completely painted, pictures often framed by soft colors reminscent of what is going on in the story at that moment, a perfect complement to the main illustration. This is an absolutely wonderful storybook, and a very nice publication. And it made my daughter happy. What else is there to say?Read moreRead less
