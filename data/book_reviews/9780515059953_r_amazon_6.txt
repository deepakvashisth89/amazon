The victim is Sommita, an Italian diva with the voice of an angel the temperament of a devil.  The murder scene is arranged in the style of grand opera. The setting is far from Italy, on a remote island in New Zealand — but those present include several Italians.There are murmurings of a connection to a Sicilian vendetta. There is also a malevolent photographer in the vicinity who has been stalking La Sommita globally.Happily, Inspector Alleyn is on the island on a vague assignment, and so is his wife Troy.  Troy has been commissioned to paint Sommita’s portrait. House guests and staff are as varied and intriguing as you’d expect in a mystery in the tradition of the Golden Age.A storm cuts the island from communication (and escape), so Alleyn is on his own with an operatic murder case. He has no real authority and no helpers but a sympatico doctor and his wife. The classic “closed room” aspect of the case places this book all the more firmly on the Golden Age, despite its original publication date of 1980.
