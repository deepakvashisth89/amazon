This is a fine historical novel based on the lives of French Catholic missionaries  Jean -  Baptiste Lamy, who became the first Archbishop of Santa Fe, New Mexico, and Joseph Projectus Machebeuf, who became the first Bishop of Denver, Colorado.  They were sent to their mission in 1848, shortly after the annexation of that area by the United States as spoils of the Mexican War, and the book episodically chronicles their work, their brotherhood, and the history of the place over the next four decades.  Also included are events that came before and provide a backdrop for the story: the old Spanish missions, the indigenous uprisings against Spanish rule, the story of the Virgin of Guadalupe.The Western Writers ' Association names this as one of the great westerns, and it is a western with the usual cast of characters (Kit Carson and the Navajo chief Mañuelito make cameo appearances) but with a distinctly different story line.  In its own way, it tells the familiar story of old ways and new ways in that crossroads of multiple cultures, how they clashed, how some vanished, how some remained.Willa Cather once again has a great feel for the the time, the place, and the people.  Given the subject matter, her calm but exquisite prose make this almost a devotional work.   Certainly readers with an active religious life can appreciate its spiritual themes.The one curiosity is the title.  The archbishop does die in the end, and there are other deaths that affect him, but that's not what the title really refers to .  I think it is more about the death to self required by the missionaries in their work; the death to the Old World necessary for rebirth in the New World; the death and loss of old ways and connections-- many of them good and irreplaceable -- as history marches on.Read moreRead less
