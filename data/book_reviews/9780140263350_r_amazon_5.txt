Akiko was born under another name but was given a new name while in captivity during the war. With the war at its end, she was taken in by missionaries where she met the man she would marry and later give birth to a daughter they would name Rebecca.Painful to digest and difficult to read. The story is focused around the horrific abuse of the Comfort Women by Japanese soldiers during World War II.  Although Keller is a skilled writer, the ragged time jumps forced me to reread sections and flip back and forth through the book. There are also several instances where she changes language and I felt it wasn’t always clear what she was attempting to convey. Akiko’s past is one long nightmare that becomes her fortune and her downfall as she converses with the dead. Young Beccah is Akiko’s ferocious defender but is also frightened and ashamed of her mother’s afflictions.In the finale. Beccah discovers the truth about the woman she is realizing she never really knew.
