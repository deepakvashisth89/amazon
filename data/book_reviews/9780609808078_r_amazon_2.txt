Ali Smith is an exceptionally talented photographer and has photographed 35 women who are edgy downtown artists, sports figures, politicians and activists. If you like photography, you may agree that these stack up against the best work in Rolling Stone and Vanity Fair.Some of the "laws" are profound, others somewhat constructed. What's refreshing about them is that they aren't all pretty-face new-age sweetness and light. Some of the life lessons come from women born to power and prestige, but more from those who've struggled with economic disadvantage, physical handicap, emotional scars and artistic iconoclasm. Mentoring an underprivileged girl? Get her the book. Counseling a confused adolescent? Give her a copy. Your daughter doesn't think you're cool? Well, she probably still won't, but there's a chance that one of the women profiled might spark her interest in that person, or her field, sport, business or art. Fabulous high-school, college or grad school graduation present. I can also see this as a book group selection (probably a nice break after reading some 500-page tome).While I personally prefer portraits isolated on a page, this book juxtaposes them with text and interview dialog. However, the author's writing is clean and unpretentious, and the layout is appropriate to the visual style of many of her subjects, so the organization of the book has integrity.I think we can expect a lot more good work from Ali Smith in the future.
