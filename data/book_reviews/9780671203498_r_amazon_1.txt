I first received a copy of "The Most of P.G. Wodehouse" as a teenager.  Possibly a birthday, but then again, Christmas?  In any case I devoured it.  It was the mana that purged that dark romantic period that afflicts so many teens.  Bertie Wooster.  Aunt Agatha.  Jeeves, of course.  But those are just the most known of the dozens of quirky characters.  The "Golf Stories" are worth the price of the book, just by themselves.  Even if golf is not your thing.That gift copy was a fortunate First Edition, since this was way back in my "back in the day" period, that is, the 60s.  The stories brought so much joy that I lent that much-loved First Edition to a friend in dire need, who of course needs it more than I.  Still.  To this day.  Thus I sprang for a fresh copy.  Wodehouse is not sufficiently appreciated in America, it seems, as my fresh copy arrived from England.  As perhaps has been said, "From any shore, English smells the same."  The humor (or humour) lives on.I few years back I was fortunate to publish one of my little dramas.  Sadly it is not currently available on Amazon.  Happily, I can say without reservation that the characters and dialog owed much to a recent dose of P. G. Wodehouse.  If you are not laughing by page 3, press on.  If you reach page 33 without a few snorts or guffaws then at least you'll have clarity on that great question of philosophy, "How do I know if I'm alive?"
