[The actual subtitle is "A treasury of Yiddish folk SAYINGS"]There are several collection of Yiddish proverbs that have been available in recent years. Kogos and Ayalti come to mind. For my own purposes,when I was learning Yiddish about twenty-five years ago, this book was the best. It includes the proverb in normal Yiddish orthography (Hebrew letters with modifications typical of Yiddish), Roman transliteration, and a plain translation. It's distressing that this excellent book is out of print; that appears to be the case as well for a second volume by the same author, Shirley Kumove. This latter book was published by Wayne State University Press.Ms. Kumove derived her Sayings from a small number of sources; these are indicated after each entry. Some were drawn from personal acquaintance; the speaking world was once a good source of information; it is a world that is dying out.I suppose that I shouldn't be surprised that collections of Yiddish proverbs haven't been a hot item of late. Constant use of proverbs is something that was quite common in a world that still existed not so long ago. Not being a native speaker of Yiddish I have no insight into whether such linguistic behavior has persisted among the shrinking number of native Yiddish speakers. These are now likely to be adherents of Ultra-Orthodoxy, so their world-view is much more limited than that of the conglomerate of Yiddish speakers in former eras.There has been a sequel, every bit as good: "More words, more arrows".Read moreRead less
