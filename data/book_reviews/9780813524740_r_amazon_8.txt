I knew little of Gertrude Stein, other than her portrayal by Kathy Bates in the Woody Allen movie, Midnight in Paris. Ms. Bates made her come into my radar as an interesting woman, but Linda Wagner-Martin has made her fascinating to me. This superbly researched biography takes readers deep into Gertrude's childhood and adolescence, allowing the reader to draw many conclusions about how those early relationships and experiences shaped her writing and actions in later years. We, of course, get to be the fly on the wall during her time in Paris as The Ultimate Hostess and perhaps, the Dominant Force in so many artists and writers lives. We get to experience two wars and what Gertrude did to survive. At times I felt the book drag a bit, when the sheer number of her acquaintances and impact became almost overwhelming. Her relationship with Alice B Toklas is explored, and we get insight into her relationships with Matisse, Hemingway, Picasso,Fitzgerald and Wilder, among others.
