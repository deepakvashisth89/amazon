This is a review of the Audible version:  I love Edith Wharton, Summer being one of my favorite books, and Ethan Frome also on my Very Sppecial list, but although I had seen the Scorsese movie, I had never actually read Age of Innocence. For some reason I cannot articulate, the many long descriptive passages seemed somewhat off-putting each time I picked it up. I didn't know why I was having such a hard time, but I was. I thought the Audible version might be just the solution, and it, in fact, did the trick!  The narrator, David Horovitch, is remarkable, and more than does this work justice, a true treat: he is able to give the French quotes and 19th century surnames their proper pronunciation. He also manages to uniquely voice each different character.  I was more than impressed.  This is an important work, given that it was the first novel by a female author to receive the Pulitzer Prize, but maybe more importantly now, it shows what day to day life was like in a world we no longer have access to.  Like most of Wharton's work, it opens a window of insight into how 19th century souls, trapped in the social conventions of the time found their way through (or not through) the obstacles in search of something genuine.  Even when it means bucking against society, which it often does in Wharton's work, a love or a freedom of sorts can be found. I thank the narrator and Audible for opening this impressive work to me--I finished it last week, and it remains on my mind, worthy of contemplation.Read moreRead less
