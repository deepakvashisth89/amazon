This review is of “Desire’s Promise”, Karen A. Bale’s final romance novel, according to FictionDB.The Story: “Desire’s Promise” begins in Fort Worth, Texas. It is here that we meet the heroine of the book, Cassidy Rae Raines. Cassidy is 12 at the beginning of the book. She lives with her mother, Kate (Cassidy’s father, a gambler, was killed earlier.) Kate co-owns a boarding house, but also works in a house of ill repute. Also living at the boarding house is the hero of the book, Dalton Granger, who is 16 at the beginning. Dalton was rescued by Kate and he does odd jobs around the area. Kate is sending him to college so that he can better himself. As Cassidy begins to develop feelings for Dalton, Kate is having issues with one of her customers, William Radford, who tries to control her life.Fast forward several years. Both Cassidy and Dalton have graduated college; Dalton is working on a steamboat and has a fiance’, Emily Stadenhurst. Cassidy buys into a detective agency as part of her plan to ruin Radford, who killed Kate years earlier. As the book goes on, Cassidy and Dalton fall in love, but their love is threatened by Radford discovering-through scorned woman Emily-that Cassidy plans to destroy him. Despite being warned about Radford and his behavior, Cassidy stupidly falls into a trap Radford sets, which Dalton and others have to extricate her from. After giving Radford his comeuppance, Cassidy and Dalton agree to get married and have their Happily Ever After.Upside: Not much upside here. Cassidy and Dalton are fairly nice characters.Downside: There is zero character development. The passion level between Cassidy and Dalton can be summed up thusly: slim and none. The way Radford-and Emily, his accomplice-are dealt with for their behavior is completely unsatisfying. Ms. Bale spends the first third of the book focusing on Cassidy and Dalton as teenagers; while I believe it's important to establish a backstory, I felt Ms. Bale spent too much time there and didn't develop the characters at all.Sex: Two mild scenes. I suspect Ms. Bale was a fan of the Harlequin Romances of the 1970’s; her love scenes are pretty much the same as found in those books.Violence: In addition to Kate’s murder, there are other assaults and shootings. None of the violence is graphic.Bottom Line: If one is a long-time Karen A. Bale fan and wants to add “Desire’s Promise to their collection simply to say they have it, then it’s a good book to have.
