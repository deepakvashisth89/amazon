My headline is no exaggeration.  I first picked up L.A. Morse's "Video Trash & Treasures Volume I" at a book store at a local mall in the early '90s and to this day I still refer to the thing constantly.  First published in 1989, it has stood the test of time despite the VCR and traditional video stores now being things of the past.This book reviews all the key low budget slasher, zombie, post apocalyptic and supernatural flicks that came out between 1980 and '89, and the '80s were undoubtedly the golden age for this kind of thing. Each section is devoted to a specific genre of video "trash" and all are equipped with highly informative introductions that give the history and background of any given style of low budget film.And the reviews are concise, brilliantly written and very often hilarious.  When Morse doesn't like a movie, he doesn't mince any words about it, such as in his review of the 1984 slasher entry "Splatter University," where he plainly states the film contains "no bimbo skin; no suspense; no humor; no talent; no intelligence; no point."  The book's absolute funniest section though may be the bonus chapter, where he lists movies that are so astonishingly bad that he considers them to be descendants of noted filmmaker Edward D. Wood's works, which of course included the all-time late '50s golden turkey "Plan 9 from Outer Space."  One film he includes in this section is 1981's "Don't Go in the Woods," which Morse states has "a budget apparently culled from the refunds on bottles found beside the road during filming."It's refreshing that over two decades later, many of these at-one-time ultra obscure VHS titles from what Morse calls the "video fringe" that were back then only available for rental at your local mom and pop store, have now been given full-fledged DVD releases.  I am a lover of low budget video trash from the '80s, and as I said, this is the book I still refer to all the time as the ultimate authority on the subject.
