Coulter Langston is a wealthy rancher, and a widower with a young daughter he doesn’t know quite what to do with. He’s also a soulless jerk through most of the book. His only redeeming quality is that he feels some responsibility for his daughter and the ranch and its people.Coulter’s daughter, Missy introduces herself to a lost little boy named Ricky at a festival, and meets his aunt who has been caring for him since his parents died. Natalie Crane has been working herself to half to death to support herself and her nephew and there never seems to be enough. When Coulter offers to marry her as an answer to both of their problems, Natalie accepts and marries the cold man before she has a chance to think too hard.Now this is the start of what could be quite a romance novel, but it lost me for awhile after this part. They went to this lovely ranch, settle in, and she meets a handsome ranch hand that she likes instantly, and he likes her. Then, instead of the handsome, but cold Coulter seducing his new bride, he demands sex, she refuses, and he rapes her. I get that they’re married, but this wasn’t in the least bit romantic. They suggest that she likes his forced attention every night, but he is cold and indifferent to her and even cruel.There are a few moments where you think he’s human, but you don’t see any real sign of affection on either end of this relationship until the end. This is the most dysfunctional romance I believe I have ever read. I gave it a four star, not for the romance value, but for the writing. It was well done, if bizarre.
