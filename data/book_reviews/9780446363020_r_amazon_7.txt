As good as this book is and as many people as Randy Kraft killed in as populated as a place as Southern California... it begs the question: How come Randy Kraft is not well known?  I did see a couple of the theories by some of Oregon's law enforcement agents and I happen to agree with them.  Many of the victims were gay men as was the killer and the victims, being men, were not considered as vulnerable as women are; hence if you ended up dead then it was partly your fault.Leaving that aside, the book was a very good read.  It wasn't bogged down with just times and dates and it is apparent that Dennis McDougal did considerable research.  Granted, this is probably the goriest and most descriptive true crime book I've read, but it is all necessary in conveying the brutality of the crimes.  At times the book was more biographical about Randy Kraft than it was an account of the crimes itself and the pursuit of the criminal, but that helped give a view of what the killer was like.This book was also heavily laden with references to other killers.  Dennis brought up the likes of Charles Manson, Ted Bundy, John Wayne Gacy and even lesser known killers such as Dean Corll, Juan Corona and Bluebeard.  But one of the more interesting threads in this book was the mention of Patrick Kearney (the Trash Bag Killer) and William Bonin (the mistaken Freeway Killer).  These two eerily similar serial killers were operating at the same time as Kraft and in the same locale!!  So it was almost a bonus to read about their crimes and perversions so as to get an even more complete picture of the atmosphere in Southern California at that time.Ultimately, I liked McDougal's style and his delivery.  This is a worthwhile book for true crime fans who would like to know about the most prolific serial killer in American History.Read moreRead less
