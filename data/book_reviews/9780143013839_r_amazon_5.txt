I knew the original "Slades" had parted ways, and I have to say how sorry I am that they did. I wish they'd reunite, because the writing of this book is amateurish and clumsy. The characters are about as cardboard as they can be. The historical contexts endemic to Slade novels seems to have been cut and pasted without reference to the plot action. The original "Slades" were scary, gripping, well-written. I'm rereading "Headhunter" now just to get "good" Slade, and the contrast between it and "Bed of Nails," which I just read, is staggering. One is great; the other was written to take advantage of the Slade name and make money off it. It's unworthy of the series, which should have ended when the "Slades" broke up. Then it would be a worthy series, even if short. To keep writing bad novels under the Slade name is avaricious and disappointing to those of us who appreciate good writing, good plotting, intelligent dialog, and real-seeming characters.The book is misnamed. "Bed of Nails" refers to a film in the making that's slogging along, until a producer comes to see what's wrong. His fate is sealed, but no one seems to question anyone in the film crew. Poor, poor, poor. There's a separate reference to a real bed of nails, late in the novel, but the book says little about it and it's not, like the film name, sufficient to justify the title. The novel is not about beds of nails at all, and so the title is misleading and silly.Protagonists have names. This book "stars" RCMP Special X's Zinc Chandler, but he is referred to as "Zinc," "Zinc Chandler," "Chandler," and "the Mountie," not by characters but in the narrative. Writers should pick one and stick to it. After introducing him as Zinc Chandler, he should be referred to in the narrative as Zinc or Chandler. Two major characters are referred to alternately by their first and last names. Two other major characters don't seem to have last names at all.
