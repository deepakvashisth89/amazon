"Industrially produced fiction..." begins this book, and there you have what you need to know, because at no point is Dorfman talking about critical darlings' writing bestsellers or auteur films being "industrially produced." No, he means things he doesn't care for, things that don't entertain him. Comics, television programs, genres he doesn't enjoy or mediums he does not peruse except for research. And, by research, I mean: apparently glances at the covers and quits.Ariel Dorfman has some good points, in this volume, about the effort that should be put into the ramifications of entertainment works, that the author/creator should make an effort to be aware of what is being said by complicity as well as what is explicitly presented. However, the rest of the volume is given to slipshod, selfish research (the focuses appear to be motivated by personal interest, while areas that do not interest the author or entertain them, are glossed over or grotesquely stereotyped), and a classist air is maintained over everything. The book's best value may be in a reader's chance to see vague research explicated with thorough conviction, with broad generalizations expressed as concrete, inescapable fact, and with entire genres or mediums dismissed or heralded based on the assumption of the class, the monetary, gender, or social status of the intended audience.Read moreRead less
