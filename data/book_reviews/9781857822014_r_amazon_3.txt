This is a riveting account of a young man's double life as a British infiltrator of the Irish Republican Army. Compared to the many other histories I've read of The Troubles, this one excels in showing how both sides of the long fight have heroic and cowardly aspects. Here in the U.S. I can't begin to fathom what life must be like in Belfast, where during recent decades innocents were senselessly killed by both sides. I doubt anyone not steeped in the culture can truly understand the issues, but it seems to me that a fundamental breakdown in English and Irish society began fifty years ago, with the abandonment of God and individual liberties by the respective governments -- and I consider the IRA to be a defacto, if unrecognized, government. Both the UK and IRA demonstrate total corruption and greed, steeped in vengeance and self-absorbed materialism.
