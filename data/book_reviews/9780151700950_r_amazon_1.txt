I should have assumed at first that this was going to be a much darker book than I anticipated. This book was reference in another book called " waiting for Godot in Sarajevo" which is a book about finding meaning in suffering via different philosophies. so I got this book thinking. "wow I can't wait to read another deep delve into philosophies on finding meaning in suffering". I did not get that out of this book. sure the central story surrounding the Jesuit priest who sacrifices himself for another person in the concentration camp is compelling and the best part of the book, but literally every other short story within this book has no redeeming qualities. All of them are a bleak and realistic look from different perspectives of people during WWII. from people witnessing a Jewish wedding being slaughtered by Nazis, to eastern European partisans, to German soldiers fleeing after they have lost. Every story is as depressing as they possibly can be. Redemption is found only in the chapters about the Jesuit priest and even then the reprieve is very short. I would say it is a good book, you just need to know what you are getting into prior to reading.Read moreRead less
