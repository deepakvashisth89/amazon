Don't look for nuanced character development, or an inspirational story. They just are not here. The characters all have something of a comic-book quality about them, entirely predictable good-guy/bad-guy type of stuff, and quite a few pages spent on a narrow story line.I am no history buff, but I am a native New Englander, and the capture of the Ticonderoga arsenal is a part of our local lore. As I recall, the actual taking of the fort was embarassingly easy (as goes the story in this book, also). However, the real drama of this group of people was in what happened after the capture: a number of related military activities in the primitive American and Canadian colonies, overlapping intrigues as various groups and individuals vied for power in a fast-changing political and military situation, the hardships of moving major weaponry through two hundred miles of rocky forest, and the triumph of their arrival and use on a hilltop overlooking a Boston Harbor full of British troops. For an author building fiction of the future on the fact of the past, these subsequent events would have added considerably more texture to the novel than what is here. This book could have been the first 100 pages of something far more interesting.That being said, wait until summer, and take it to the beach. It's OK.Read moreRead less
