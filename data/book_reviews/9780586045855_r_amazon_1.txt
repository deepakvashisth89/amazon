I've seen a number of comments describing the book as "hilarious" and know that's not my view of the book.  It has funny moments, moments that are satirical when the figures in the book are portrayed in caricature, however, my overall impression was sadness and the futility of it all.  Basically I think it gets to the baseline of what did it all mean for India to be ruled by Britain for centuries and where did it leave both groups of people.  I saw wasted lives.  People who wasted their lives living a certain way -- the British in a military and civil "caste" system that was as oppressive as the caste system that restricted the movement of the Indians who were born to castes and were expected to be resigned to their fate in the confines of their own caste system and then had the extra burden of the British coming in and imposing another caste system on top the homegrown model.  I didn't see happy or satisfied people instead I saw people who on some level eventually recognized the futility of the lives they'd led, the rigidity of their lives, and how meaningless it all was ultimately.I'm glad Scott finally won the Booker that he deserved but IMO, he deserved the prize for the Raj Quartet -- his masterpiece which throughout led inexorably to the characters knowing in their hearts the futility of their presence in India and their counterparts -- the Indians recognizing it also.
