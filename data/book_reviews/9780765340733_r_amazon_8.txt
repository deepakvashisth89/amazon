There are strengths in Kevin McLeod’s Cosmonaut Keep novel, Act I of the writer’s “Engine of Lights” trilogy. There are sentient kraken, nictitating membrane types of reptile aliens and an Earth descendant colony in the far side of the galaxy which has beauty and depth and demands more than a superficial level of reading.There are also glaring weaknesses, mostly having to do with the failed idea that never dies (socialism) which makes a glorious comeback in a speculative mid 21st century Russian-invaded United Kingdom and European Union.This reviewer is not opposed to exploring political ideas, but the Cosmonaut Keep background is so laughable to the point of being barely readable. Not to mention that the amount of pot smoked in every other page by humans, aliens (but mercifully, not the kraken) is outright silly for a novel written almost half a century after the 1960's.There could be something more fleshed out and memorable than what we have with this Hugo Award nominated novel, which basically sends out a warning to all decent science fiction writers (among whom Kevin McLeod is definitely one): science fiction and socialism do not mix well.More kraken, less Trotsky please.Read moreRead less
