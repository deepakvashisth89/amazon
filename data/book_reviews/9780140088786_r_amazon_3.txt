Having read this book, I feel I have embarked on one of the greatest of journeys in history.Robin Lane Fox hits all the key highlights on one of the most remarkable and influential men in history, although I found his decision to not name the chapters as a curious mistake since each chapter focuses on one or two aspects of the enormity of Alexander's brief life but one cannot easily go back to the book and look at the Table of Contents to locate the events and discussions.  For that, I removed one star.Who was Alexander?  He was extremely brave to the point of rashness (injured 9 times).  He was a born leader that was not only the greatest military leader in history because he was undefeated (about 20 battles) but because he managed to hold together his army and his multi-cultural, polyglot empire for a decade while far from his home base.  He was often ingenious in how he won but was always decisive - cruel when he needed to be and magnanimous when he did not need to be.  He was a man ahead of his time with his desire for a brotherhood of mankind without concern for race or ethnicity and his desire for a unified worldwide empire.  Had he not died at 32, he may well have succeeded in creating such an empire (he had plans to go west when he died).  The fact that his empire imploded almost the day he died is testament to his power.  He was also more than a military man, but a builder, an explorer, a man of piety and a patron of the arts and sciences.He was far from a saint or a perfect man as his murder of his friend Cleitus and his own biographer proved. Likewise his unnecessary destruction of the magnificent Persepolis palace, his extravagance, his drunkenness, and his foolish decision to cross through the Makran desert for indubitably self-centered reasons.  He was a man prone to extreme megalomania as witnessed by his naming of over a dozen cities by his name Alexander (of which Alexandria Egypt is the greatest).  He knew he was a man of destiny but arrogantly saw himself as Son of Zeus, the Second Achilles and King of Kings and Pharoah, increasingly taking on the regalia and pomp to go with such aspirations.There is more to be said about this remarkable man but Robin Lane Fox is terrific at extracting much thinking about his importance, his uniqueness, and his epochal journeys.
