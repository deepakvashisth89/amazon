I had already considered Jack Lemmon one of my favorite actors, but I have even more respect for him (if that’s even possible) after reading Don Widener’s Lemmon. Mr. Widener brings to vivid life the personality, sense of humor and dignity of Mr. Jack Lemmon, who made acting look so easy. Mr. Widener relays the stories of Jack’s mischievous youth and accounts at Harvard, not being a Grade-A student, having his mind on music. Being in the right place, coasting on charm and having unbelievable luck, Jack managed to get through military service (seeing no action) and to New York, for his “starving and struggling days,” which weren’t that long. His first wife, Cynthia, his early tv career, making films with Judy Holliday, Billy Wilder and Walter Matthau, winning an Oscar for “Mister Roberts” and Felicia Farr, his second wife serve as highlights in this loving yet honest depiction of his life. Not without controversies, his seriousness and dedication to his craft earned him respect and admiration from his peers. Many felt he was a born showman, a clown who put on the face of his character and was do-or-die for the sake of the camera. When his character has a nervous breakdown, he had one. He won a well-deserved Oscar for “Save the Tiger,” doing so. Widener explains that he was born with an innate desire of exploration, especially the commonality of people. You’ll love this ode to one of America’s greatest actors, an everyman who could be anyone. Despite the fact, some of his movies were not worthy of him, Widener show us how there was never a bad Lemmon performance. And, I can attest to that, as I have seen most of his movies.
