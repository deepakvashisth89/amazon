Finally beginning my dive into Stephen King and I figured "Hey, what better way to read King than to read in publication order?", so Carrie was the natural starting point.I have to say, what an absolutely WEIRD book this is. 5 parts horror, 3 parts documentary, 2 parts fantasy/science-fiction, and 100% absolutely freaking crazy. I've never seen a book written in this fashion before, and I really can't tell exactly what it's trying to be, except for an absolutely whiplashing story.The moral of the story is honestly extremely plain to see: Don't be a bully, if you do, you might just FAFO, and boy oh boy do so many characters in this book F around. Carrie herself is a very tragic character. Whether her social awkwardness stems from an underlying neurodivergence, the years of abuse she endured, or some combination of both, it's impossible not to feel sorry for her. Socially outcast as a "freak" or as being "weird", the book makes very sure you know, this is not a girl who has many friends. And then for her to have THAT happen to her for the first time so late in her teenage life, AND it's in front of a crowd of people that already loved to torment her? Dear God this poor child was going through.And basically nobody had her back, a couple of characters here and there speak up for her, but they're few and far between, their voices lost in the noise of the bullying and "mean girl" energy that permeates throughout the first half of this novel in opposition to Carrie. It's really messed up stuff.But the kicker? Most people who struggle in school with bullying or awkward coming of age questions, can rely on their mother being there to help them. No matter what's going on, many of us seek comfort in our mothers. Carrie? Absolutely the hell not, it could be argued that nobody was a bigger bully to Carrie, nobody inflicted more damage to Carrie, and nobody made Carrie feel evil for just existing, than her own mother. What a hell of a choice, and it's not exactly a spoiler either, it's established so early in the book. For one's own mom to be their biggest enemy is heart wrenching stuff. Hell, it almost makes me feel like I couldn't blame Carrie for becoming what she becomes. Almost.The book reads very weird. King's narrative voice, at least with this book, is very hard to put into description. It's not that his prose is a problem, in fact they're quite simple and easy to follow, but his formatting is bizarre. We don't really have chapters, just 3 parts, with the unfolding plot itself being constantly interrupted with excerpts from interviews that exist within the world the story takes place in, as well as snippets from news reports and magazine articles. They're peppered in mostly at times that are relevant to the current goings-on of that particular section of the story, but it's a bit jarring to go from plot, to interview, to plot, to magazine, to plot, all without chapter breaks to separate those shifts.I enjoyed my time with this book, I look forward to seeing how King's future writing is, and it certainly feels like Carrie could be an early example of the kind of psychic abilities (the Shine?) King would explore more deeply in later novels.The unusual structure and constant shifts between narrative and documentary excerpts kept me from becoming fully immersed, even though I appreciated what King was trying to do. I didn't love it, but I certainly didn't hate it. It's a perfectly weird 3.5 out of 5 stars, and it's worth your time for sure!Read moreRead less
