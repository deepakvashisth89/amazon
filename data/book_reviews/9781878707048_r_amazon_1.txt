What a pleasant reading book. The author, Harry Helms, is/was a technical writer but also a writer who is trying to let his reader learn and grow from their reading experience. Mr. Helms died of cancer in 2009 at the age of 57 according to the internet. The first book I read of his was titled 'Shortwave Listening Guide Book' and it taught me so much I looked for other radio related books he had written. Both books were easy to read, enjoyable, educational and often fun.I purchased this book right after I got my Technician license and became a radio amateur. It inspired me to keep on learning and reading; which I did. That same year I got my General class radio amateur license. I can tell you that studying the license exam books was not nearly as fun as reading Harry Helms' books. Thank you Harry for the gifts you left us.A short list of the subjects covered in the book include: Hams and their radios (which has 19 sub-categories); Ham radio customs, folklore, habits, and [as Harry puts it] other good stuff; Signals, sidebands, and other good stuff; Ham radio meets the personal computer; Propagation and simple antennas; Electrons, capacitor and other stuff that makes your radio work; Beam antennas, feedlines, test and measurement gear, and safety; Hey! we're getting near the finish line (which has topics of interest to a future ham).A short paragraph about the author tells how he had his ham license since 1988 (AA6FW) and held an Extra Class license. He also wrote the Shortwave Listener's Guidebook and numerous articles for magazines like Popular Communications, Popular Electronics, 73 magazine and four others. He is also the author of some 20 other books. He is already missed.Read moreRead less
