I love this book.  It's a series of historic battles discussed in a reader-friendly way that familiarizes the reader with major historic battles without burdening the reader with a mountain of statistics, terrain features, unit designations, etc.  Those facts are important to understanding military history, but this book offers a different approach and that's fine.  If you want to spend countless hours reading Livy's The War With Hannibal, be my guest, but you can save time by reading this author's account of Hannibal's battle at Cannae.  The author gives you a personal description of the leaders involved, discusses the major issues, what led up to the battle, the battle itself, and the long-term results of the battle.  This is important, since this is a discussion of fatal victories, in which the winner was ultimately the loser.  Pearl Harbor, for instance, was a clear victory for Japan which ultimately doomed them.  The Tet Offensive was a major victory for the US, but in terms of perception, it wrecked credibility at home for the military and ended in US defeat.  This is a snack-size offering of major Pyrrhic battles and I have enjoyed reading it off and on for many years.Read moreRead less
