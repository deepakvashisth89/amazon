As I write this review, the book currently has two reviews posted on Amazon - one of 5 stars, and one of 1 star - both posted in 1999.  Quite a long time ago; and obviously very disparate views of this book.The current edition is the tenth; the book appears to have been fairly continuously published in new editions from 1964 through to 2006.  Clearly many revisions have taken place over that time; and in this newest edition Judith M Bennett has taken over the editing and revision as C Warren Hollister passed away in 1997.  So the 1999 reviews may perhaps have to be taken with a slight sideways glance as to their relevance to this current edition of this book.The tenth edition highlights the following changes from earlier editions, including- Medieval Myths - little sidebars in some of the topics on topics that may be of interest to the students- Maps which have been redrawn and improved- Chapter 2 has been reworked to focus less on Rome and more on barbarian settlements- Coverage of Byzantium and Islam has been reworked to be more balanced throughout the book- Citations to all quotations are now listed- Suggested primary sources are includedThe current edition has the following chapters:1. Rome becomes Christian, c. 31 B.C.E.-430 C.E.2. Barbarian settlement in the West, c. 400-5003. Early Western Christendom, c. 500-7004. Neighbours: Byzantium and Islam, c. 500-10005. Carolingian Europe, c. 700-8506. Division, invasion and reorganisation, c. 800-10007. Economic takeoff and social change, c. 1000-13008. New paths to God, c. 1000-12509. Conquests, Crusades and persecutions, c. 1100-130010. Worlds in collision: Papacy and Holy Roman Empire, 3. 1125-130011. States in the making: England and France, c. 1050-130012. Diversity and dynamism in late Medieval Culture, c. 1300-1500This book, it would appear, is widely used in the American education system, and perhaps the book suffers a little from being geared towards students who are in the classroom reading the book `because they have to', not specifically `because they want to', which is what you might expect of a more mature, driven audience who approach the subject out of a desire to learn more on the topic.However, the book does what it says on the cover.  It is an introductory book on a wide geographical and chronological spectrum, and it covers it well for those with little or no prior knowledge of the places or times, particularly students coming to the topic for the first time.  New terms are highlighted for the reader to be able to check in the Glossary, and further books for reading are included at the end of each chapter.A good and highly recommended book for the period, very well suited to a student new to the subject.Read moreRead less
