This is a very well-researched book, with the downside of it being dry at times. I had a 30-year interest in Jungian topics before my psychotherapy experiences. Started with MAN AND HIS SYMBOLS, then of course Joseph Campbell et al. I was a little surprised that what with Jung's life, exploring mediumship and naturally his broken-off friendship with Freud, he as a man himself just wasn't very interesting. This book did hold my interest though. Somehow at front and center is the Sun-cult or cult of Mithras. I knew a little about that from my Mircea Eliade studies though. More interesting to me was Noll's insight on the mass levels of marketing involved in today's Jungian analysis business. And it IS a business!In the book, there is a story where Freud turns to Jung as they enter New York harbor and says: "Don't they know that we're bringing them the plague?" Oh very funny, gentlemen! It sounds like therapy is somehow an "initiation" and no wonder I've now heard stories of people being in it for 15 years, meanwhile suffering from obvious ailments that needed a Rx. I certainly did not have a good experience and that was only for six months.The book also mentions an "erotic relationship" Jung had with one of his in-patients, Sabina Spielrein. I mean, this just does not sound like someone who's advice I need to hear. I've managed to lose all interest in anything Jungian now. I don't know why or how it ever appealed to me!Read moreRead less
