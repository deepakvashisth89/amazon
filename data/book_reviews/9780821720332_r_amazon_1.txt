Read for answer.This one was a bottom-heavy novel, meaning the best bits were in the second half. Cooper Cohen always gives good plot; always puts her women and men through hell, and while this isn't her most hellish work, it's still no picnic. (Although if you're not familiar with her other novels and you read this one, you'd probably have trouble imagining what it is that happens in the other ones that's worse than this. Just trust me. It's worse.)Besides marital affairs, this book puts the focus on parental affairs, specifically the damage that parents, unwittingly or not, can do to the minds of their children, causing Issues that will need to be dealt with and overcome to have a happy adult life. Cooper Cohen writes about these Issues in a way that shows a solid understanding of psychology. I was impressed.Lastly, I must make note of the few hilarious and brilliant Brill and Howard scenes. Their problems are portrayed in a serious way for most of the book, but there are a handful of chapters that are straight out of June Flaum Singer, another Double-Barrelled Wonder.
