“Julian” is an enjoyable historical fiction of medium length.  It is about a Roman Emperor of the fourth century A.D.  As with other of the mature novels of Gore Vidal, it is readable, but thought provoking and does demand one’s attention.  Although comprehendible, I would not consider it a fun, light reading experience.  I enjoyed it very much and consider it a “thinking person’s” novel.The format of this novel is unusual. The novel has three ever shifting narrators and involves correspondence between two of the narrators.  In that regard, the novel resembles other novels that I have read that are termed “epistolary novels”.  I am formally uncredentialed in regard to literary theory, but I am not sure that this is a true epistolary novel, but shares similarities to one.The novel is based on much actual history.  In that sense it provided to me an opportunity for parallel study.  In that context I learned a good deal which adds to my,personal enjoyment.  A different reader looking for a simple enjoyable light reading experience might not enjoy that aspect of this novel.In the event one chooses to read this novel and does enjoy it, one might consider “I, Claudius” by Richard Graves.  “I, Claudius” was published in 1934, predating “Julian” by three decades.  I found “I, Claudius” to be very humorous and as much as I like Gore Vidal, I enjoyed “I, Claudius” at least as much.  In any event I feel the two novels create a great opportunity for a reader to compare and contrast the two books and authors.In summary I completely enjoyed this novel.  I read the novel while listening to an audiobook and enjoyed both the audiobook and the hard copy equally.  Audiobooks often add emotion and emphasis that my personal “inner narrator” lacks.
