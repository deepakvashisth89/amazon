I would like to address the idea that this is a "beginner" book. I feel some of the reviews are misleading. It might be a beginner to them, or perhaps they are not sitting down trying to recreate the examples on the page.My main gripe in this area is the fact that many times in the book the author(s) do not spell out the examples verbosely, they assume a lot of knowledge to begin with, so if you are like me you will struggle with it: me, meaning somebody who will not move on without getting the same results as the author suggests with the example.Sometimes, based on your version of Korn: the examples just might not be supported, but sometimes it is a matter of telling you x=something and have to infer the rest based on what you have learned so far.I don't necessarily think this is a bad thing because when you are forced to apply the knowledge you have learned, it is way more satisfying once you figure it out.So you should probably know that going in if you want the most out of the book, you will have to think a lot and come up with the answers on your own.Also, if you are using say: anything but ksh93, you will have to figure out what applies and what doesn't apply making it that more difficult.
