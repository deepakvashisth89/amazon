I've been reading the books in the Laser series and was not excited to be reading another by Tofte. His earlier book, about a family crash landing on an alien planet, was quite simple in its plot and characters. Like a book written for kids.Walls Within Walls is more complex and interesting, but still ends up feeling like something for young adults... except... there are some elements that are a bit creepy, in all the wrong ways.The story starts off with the protagonist as a 6yr old... a 6yr old who enjoys watching the girl next door as a voyeuristic hobby. He's also a mutant and was supposed to be killed at birth.He's got a 'normal' twin brother... but that character seems only to allow for later scenes when the protagonist can use his shared resemblance to gain access in the outer city.The whole story of the mutants living underground and planning their revolt, of the upper city living in ignorance of the true nature of the world (and who their masters are), is done well enough and kept me going for most of the book... but then that storyline resolves itself VERY quickly and the protagonist finds himself in a sort of agrarian utopia, similar to the characters of Knofte's previous book.The preceding story is dropped in favor of a final few chapters about unrequited love.Was this crammed in because of the publisher (Harlequin)? Hard to know.What I do know is that the behavior of the protagonist toward the woman he loves is... really kind of ugly and offputing. I'd also question how he could 'love' this woman who he doesn't really know... based entirely on a lifetime of longing for her based on his memory of her as a 6yr old.It's quite weird and spoils what is otherwise an enjoyable, but light, read.Read moreRead less
