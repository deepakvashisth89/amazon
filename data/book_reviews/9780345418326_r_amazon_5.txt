John Le Carre's reprise of the wisdom of George Smiley was a fun, but less intense read of a series of reflections from the experienced and well worn intelligence officer to the eager and uninitiated future spys. This novel's context was an excellent venue to gather many previously withheld stories and insights. Perhaps George had a few more secrets to share, or possibly Le Carre had some prior editorial cuts or undeveloped ideas from previous books he finally chose to tell. Regardless of the motives, this was a delightful journey with an old friend filled with sage observations and counsel from a grandfathered spy whom we come to trust.This installment reflects well the author's own misgivings of the intelligence "industry" that has become a life unto itself, as a Frankenstein-like monster, cobbled together into is current dysfunctional, uncontrollable, and self-perpetuating form. An industry built on betrayals, lies, half truths, and misinformation, these charlatans of reality seek to protect us from ourselves, while creating deception and disruptions to further their own professional careers.Le Carre makes us wonder if there is such a thing as a honest or ethical spy, all the while we have learned to trust and adore George Smiley. He was our heroic representation of someone good whose integrity made us feel safer in a world of incompetent and self-serving bureaucrats.Perhaps too, this book serves well as our warning of how like Smiley, we have an unfulfilled marriage with our governments, who constantly take our trust and money, only to the betray us again and again, while our lives lengthen and our hopes shrink.Well done again, John Le Carre!Enjoyed the supportive friendship too of Audible's capable narrations.Read moreRead less
