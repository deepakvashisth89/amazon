This book, along with the book "A Death in Jerusalem" by Kati Marton provides insights into the activities of the Zionist para-military underground organizations know as the Irgun and Lehi.However, J Boyer Bell's book has some limitations. His book says almost nothing about Irgun activities during the early-mid 1930s. It begins with violent Arab attacks against Jews in British Mandate Palestine. But why didn't the author begin with the Balfour Declaration of 1917 and the Zionist objective of taking over Palestine to create a Jewish nation and what that would mean for the existing non-Jewish population? Also, there is very little specificity about the role of the Irgun's last commander Menachem Begin during the Irgun’s extensive bombing campaign and military raids against the British in Palestine (and elsewhere) beginning in early 1944. For example, there is much detail about the July 1946 bombing of the King David Hotel in Jerusalem, but Mr Begin is mentioned almost as an afterthought when the author briefly discusses how he had to go deeper into hiding after the attack. Are the actions of the commander not central to an organizations’ operations?Another limitation of this book is the absence of detailed documentation of sources. One gets the impression that the revelation of sources may have been problematic for any number of reasons given the nature and sensitivity of the events being described.However, despite these limitations, the book is a good read and fills in many of the gaps in coverage that are almost never mentioned by American media or Washington DC politicians.Also, one reviewer of this book states that Mr Begin replaced Vladimir Ze’ev Jabotinsky as Irgun commander. This is not true since the Irgun had several commanders that fought for the Zionist cause after Jabotinsky including: Avraham Tehomi, Robert Bitker, Moshe Rosenberg, David Raziel, Hanoch Kalai and Yaakov Meridor.Read moreRead less
