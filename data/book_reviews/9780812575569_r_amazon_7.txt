A richly woven tapestry of plot, character, and setting. I stumbled upon Norman Bogner's previous book by accident and liked it so much that I scrambled to get the new one as soon as I heard it was out. What a  surprise. Honor Thy Wife is very different from To Die in Provence: no  detective, no lurid crimes, and a strictly American setting. But it wasn't  a disappointment. It is as rich in characterization and intrigue, though on  a quieter and more familiar scale. I also felt I got a good sense of what  life was like in the 1960s for the regular people who lived through a  decade that's often portrayed as nothing but sex and drugs and student  protests.
