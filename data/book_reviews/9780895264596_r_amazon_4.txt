This book was published in 1995 and it primarily a transcription of JFK's journal notations from his time touring Germany in the summer of 1945. Although he was working technically as a journalist, his father's wealth and social position allowed him to travel in circles and access areas often barred to the average American. Although Kennedy was a young man and still wrote like one (the book cover makes him look borderline boyish), I occasionally had to remind myself that by this point he was also a decorated war veteran who encountered combat in the Pacific.The entries rarely last for more than a page or two, but they do contain some interesting observations. While visiting one German woman's bedroom, he notes that she is extremely underfed and the risks of coming winter. In line with some of his father's thinking, he also described FDR's New Deal as having at its roots totalitarianism, something that may shock his liberal admirers today. Unlike many of his other published works which were either ghostwritten or substantially polished by others, this work is authored solely by the future president and thus purely in his voice. In tone, he was, as Hugh Sidey says in the introduction, "a serious man on a serious mission." In that regard, this book, while contributing little new to our perspective of post-war Europe, is a valuable glimpse of the man who would one day be president.Read moreRead less
