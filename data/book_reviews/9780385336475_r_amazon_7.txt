There is a lot to absorb here...John's colorful childhood with his eccentric friends, then terrible grief and loss, then his even more magical relationship with an African Bushman. When poor John loses this beloved mentor too, a whole cascade of torment and drama ensues. We get to see years of John's married life, which actually feels like padding that could have been cut. Eventually John goes to America to search for his missing friend. Then comes a completely different storyline in the American slavery times. About 3/4 of the way through the book, a new character emerges (Midnight's daughter). At this point the author kind of lost me. I did not really believe the storyline about the 15-year old slave girl "Memory" and her plot to get weapons and stage an escape. It felt like something pasted on. Was this a novel about Portuguese Marranos, a man's tragic relationships, or the evils of slavery? Pack too much in and it is hard to digest. This is what knocked it from a 5 to a 4-star. On the plus side, there wasn't as much violence and horror as I had feared there would be. It was a good read, well written and absorbing, and it didn't put me to sleep.Read moreRead less
