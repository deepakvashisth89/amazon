I suspect that many Japan-lovers have a hard time dealing with this not at all subtle book.  Kerr's arguments are driven home so hard that he not only knocks the rose-colored glasses off one's face, he stomps on them a few times for good measure.  And, as someone who has watched the death of beauty in Japan for 20 years, I can say that it is about time!  Those hoping to save what little remains here to be loved owe Alex Kerr an immense debt.The truth is painful, but until Japan confronts it there will not even be a diminishing of the uglification of this country.  I am an avid mountain climber and, from my window on the 9th floor of a Tokyo apartment I can see a panarama of mountains, almost all of which I have climbed.  And not one is unscarred.  Ill thought out concrete damns are lined up like dominos even in previously pristine mountain areas such as the South or North Alps.  Mt. Buko, one of Japan's sacred mountains and listed as one of the top 200 by the Japanese (who love ranking things) can be seen on the horizon from here, only it is now little more than a shaved pile of chalk with a few trees on top and a barrier to stop people from falling into the concrete mines below.  Chichibu City under the mountain depends on tourism and the mountain is one of its symbols, but now chalky dust storms blow off this stripped vertical wasteland.  Look towards the mountain from Chichibu and you see an immense smelter with conveyer belts, ugly to a degree that HR Giger could not have designed, in front of a huge pile of stepped chalk where a 1,200 meter mountain used to be.I can't believe that some reviewers of this book truly believe that this book overstates the case and that Japan will find a way for the damage to be undone.  How many hundreds of years will it take to restore Mt. Buko? Not even top soil remains, and there are hundreds upon hudreds of Buko-scale disasters happening in Japan right now.  Ancient temples have been destroyed all around Tokyo and replaced by concrete temples and even concrete tori.  Ancient things CANNOT be rebuilt.  When they are gone, they are gone.  Even if rebuilt they are no longer ancient.I have travelled the world over, and that has been enough to confirm that Alex Kerr has it right and Japan presents a unique case scenario of disaster.Read moreRead less
