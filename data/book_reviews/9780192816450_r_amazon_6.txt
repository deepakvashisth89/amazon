VATHEK is considered a gothic novel, but there it little gothic about it. There are no churches or castles, and the maiden does not run from the caliph for long. However, there are a great many supernatural elements taken from Arab mythology, at least as they were understood by William Beckford. These elements may make this tale worth reading some 230 or so years after it was written. Indeed, I found it delightful despite the digressions from the main narrative involving Vathek and his mother's quest for hidden knowledge and power in the hands of the forces of evil.
