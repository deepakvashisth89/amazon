On the basis of the actual content itself, I consider this book to be the single most important book ever written, hands down, it’s not even close.  Therefore, I recommend the “Critique of Pure Reason” to every human being, so it is worth giving it a few tries in understanding it, no matter who you are.  There is hardly a forum of intellectual thought with an idea that is neither a reaction against or a reaction in accord with Kant’s critical project, particularly the first “Critique.”  With that, I move on to this particular edition.If this book contained only the introduction, which is about 100 pages, I would rate it as a five-star book and suggest it to everyone.  The fact of the matter is that the structure of the book is so finely laid out, that it is impossible to read it and not have an exact idea of why it is set up as it is, or what Kant’s general thinking is.  The introduction to this book is among the best introductions I have ever seen for any book, and so I recommend taking a look at it, if you are first beginning study of Kant’s KrV.The translation seems good enough, for the most part, but I have my concerns, as one of the one-star reviewer noted.  I think in some places the German is not translated in an idiomatic fashion, and makes the text incomprehensible, unless you have read another translation (and get the idea that way) or have read the German.  For the most part, I think people are fine reading this edition, and, on some points, it is a bit stronger, because there is a slightly rougher translation that allows for deeper understanding, which folks who have translated texts will understand.  My understanding is that Wood and Guyer do not have the philological pedigree and training to translate the KrV as well as some others (e.g., Pluhar or Smith).Over all, I think this is a fine edition of the KrV, but others, such as the N.K. Smith translations, are better.  I would definitely give this one a read, if not for the sake of the introduction, then for a supplementary translation, especially if you don’t read German.
