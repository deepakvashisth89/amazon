I read all of this series as they came out and have now begun to reread them in  the kindle editions which allow me to hear them as well. I am now so accustomed to Mr. Robot that he seems quite human most of the time although never matching the fine voice actors to be found in the Amazon-owned Audible editions which I accumulated before they bought that New Jersey company. (Recent generations of Kindle e-book readers have dispensed with text reading but the latest inexpensive Fire does possess it but I have not yet had a chance to try out the voice used.) I have found the authors three other series and the relatively few non-series book pleasurable but not as consistently good as the Sarah Kelling & Max Bittersohn one (consisting of a dozen volumes in all). This is the first, and for the reader who has dipped into the series with one or two volumes and liked it, the necessary preliminary for the rest. Here we meet the heroine and learn about her strange marriage to an older man whom she has known since she was a child. The book revolves about the troubles which follow upon her finding that the entrance to the family tomb is bricked up. As her problems mount, she meets among other young men with whom she makes contact for the first time, an author for whose book she agrees to make sketches. He is Max Bittersohn. No, it is not love nor even friendship at first sight, she is about to enter a new period of happiness in her marriage and he has a habit of  popping up at unexpected times in unexpected places. We are treated to some quite interesting (or appalling) characters among the wealthy elite of Boston and to a good deal of what used to be called in the Mystery Trade, Lady in Distress situations. All in all, I continue to find it a good read which reminded me how good the series is and is leading me to turn immediately to the second volume in its Kindle edition.Aside from those familiar with other volumes, I would think that entirely new readers with a taste for well written mysteries not of the blood and guts, police procedural or overtly sexual types are likely to find the book good reading as well. It is not the best of the series nor is it among the less appealing (there are no 'worst', in the sense of bad, books among those she has written).
