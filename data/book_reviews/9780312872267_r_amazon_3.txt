I like to read books in a series in chronological order of the story line.  For most series, that's fine and easy.  For this series (and some from a few other authors, such as Katherine Kurtz), it's not quite so simple.  Since each book is independent, it's just as easy to read this way as in publication order.  At least I can fill things in as I go along.  However, I did start with the first published, "The Magic of Recluce"It was so clear in "The Magic of Recluce" that "chaos magic" was evil and "order magic" was good.  It's not so clear in this book.  Although "order magic" is associated with healing, that seems to mainly be confined to women.  As "advanced" as Cyador appears to be, even calling the neighbors "barbarians", it's rather primitive in it's treatment of women.It's about 200 years after "the firstborn" and a lot seems to have already been lost and they can see the end coming.The "hero" of the book, Lorn, is a bit out of place and not sure where he fits.  However, he has strong feelings and is not afraid to do what he feels is needed to protect people -- including killing several people and leaving all sorts of confusion behind him.For someone who is planned to be put into a position where he has to be killed, he's extremely agile, flexible and frustrating to his enemies who find themselves on the wrong side of his strong feelings.There is a great deal of violence in the book as is expected with the level of the culture included.  Some is rather different using such as Lorn learning to use his own "Chaos Energy" but it's still violent.  It also has a great deal on intrigue and politics that make some of our modern politics pale in comparison.
