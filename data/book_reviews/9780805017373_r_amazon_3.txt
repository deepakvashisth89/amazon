This book reminds me of the book about Gloria Vanderbilt as a young girl called POOR LITTLE RICH GIRL, only this one would of course be POOR LITTLE RICH BOY, and I mean that in no way derogatory!!  Both of these innocent little children had tough, hard, unloving lives amongst all the wealth America is supposed to be famous for, and for which all Americans appear to be striving.  I read this book years ago; in fact, I remember reading it twice because it was so moving.  The reason it comes to mind today is that I just finished THE HELP by Kathryn Stockett, and in that book she portrays the loving bonds between the children and the Black maids/the help of the 1960s.  In THE CLASS the litle boy is also comforted time and time again by his Black maid; in fact, she was pretty much the only source of love and comfort he had as a child.  Very sad!  His parents, of course, were too busy living the American dream with all their money and rich friends...... and even when they lost their wealth they still had no time for their son.  I know there is more to the book but I have forgotten, so I will go find it in my bookshelves and re-read it.  Highly recommended!  It is of course a true story and takes place several decades ago in New York and from what I read, echos of this lifestyle are still there in certain neighborhoods.
