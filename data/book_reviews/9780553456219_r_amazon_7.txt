I have read or listened to a number of Bill Bryson's books and always find his voice and humorous rants about people, places and situations to be amusing and even sometimes laugh-out-loud funny. "Neither Here Nor There" is definitely not a travelogue or travel guide, just the jottings and remembrances of someone trying to recapture the experience of backpacking through Europe as a youth in the 1970's. The wisdom he has gained in the ensuing 20+ years (this was originally written in the 90's and only just recently published for Kindle), changes his perspective and also makes him somewhat more judgmental of some of the peculiar habits of the locals. He endures the full range of accommodations and service, and in general makes no attempt to fit in or learn any of the local language - in other words, just like most Americans travel in Europe (though he actually reside in England). As an author, he seeks out ands stores up experiences to fuel his writing. If every bus ride, restaurant meal, hotel and museum were as perfect as those pictures in travel guides, he wouldn't have anything to write about.I highlighted some of the more amusing comments and opinions rendered by Bryson:"The best thing that could be said for Norwegian television is that it gives you the sensation of a coma without the worry and inconvenience.""Italians are entirely without any commitment to order. They live their lives in a kind of pandemonium, which I find very attractive."In Yugoslavia, "I sat beside a man whose concern for personal hygiene was rather less than obsessive..."He sums up the business of travelling by saying that "You fly off to a strange land, eagerly abandoning all the comforts of home, and then expend vast quantities of time and money in a largely futile effort to recapture the comforts that you wouldn't have lost if you hadn't left home in the first place."Traveling along with Bryson through Europe probably won't convince you to dust off the suitcase if you're not already a veteran traveller; but for those of us who start planning the next trip before the suitcases are even put away, "Neither Here Nor There" will have you nodding your head in agreement or checking out TripAdvisor for the best place to stay in Sorrento or Capri or Istanbul.Read moreRead less
