Taken as a work of its time this is without doubt a ground-breaking piece of historiography. This is a robust look at Francis' life and something of his times in that it seeks to push aside the weighty curtains of mythology and to see him as part of a specific moment in history. For a contemporary reader though it can be rather frustrating to read, sadly, as quantity of that fog is left unchallenged! Rather than clear and lucid prose Sabatier himself is not above waxing poetical and air-brushing the material. We learn that Francis went to the 'Holy Land' for instance, but this is covered in a mere matter of lines whereas a few telling facts would have created a more meaty- concrete picture. We are assured that Francis was 'the gentlest of people' and then in the next page he is in fact being thoroughly unpleasant to one of his friars. This is a St Francis for Protestants, (the pope is exposed as a temporal dissimulator while divine and demonic reality is unquestioned) and thats great as far as that goes. But I wanted a more penetrating and vigorous work, one less misty and wooly, where the problems and stresses of his times, as well as a critical analysis of their ideals and drives sprang from the page.
