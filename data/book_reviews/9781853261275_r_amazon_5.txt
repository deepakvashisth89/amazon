This book is an absolute breath of fresh air.  Many would say this is not an easy read for young children and they would be right; but why is this a necessity when choosing books for children?  This is not a criticism of the text.  Gilbert weaves a truly joyful tale of a hero and his many adventures.  We walk alongside our hero with a growing pride as we witness his wit and intelligence, his honour and humanity, his courage and his determination.  We will him on and hope for his timely escape from many a close call.  This book contains beautiful and powerful descriptive passages that evoke the colours and scents of the Greenwood with complete ease.  There is a lot of historical understanding to be had from this text: the social structure, the customs, dress and way of life of people in those times as well as bringing a sound understanding of the hardships and struggles of the people.  The language used is also of that time, and how wonderful to acquaint ourselves with the words of old whilst engaged in the hilarious banter between friend and foe in this book. Some reviewers say it is too complicated for the young to understand.  Having a grown up to read this book with will easily help a younger reader to grasp the meaning of 'thou' and 'thy', and the same words crop up repeatedly.  It is not that hard, and is not a book intended to help us broaden our minds?  It is a book quite unlike those written today.  Read it slowly, with care, and you will have the pleasure of a story that is gripping, witty, tragic and bold, and you'll have a good deal more understanding of that period of history too.  This is not a book for children alone, I would encourage adults to read it too.  However, if you are looking for a book that will be read independently by a young child and that you need not provide any help or guidance with, something that gratifies quickly and does not require any effort to get the mind around, the Gangsta Granny might be more appropriate - simplistic fun.
