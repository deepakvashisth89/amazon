I cannot ever remember reading a romance book I have disliked more.  First of all, the main character, Nick, pretty much gives off a stalker vibe that was just too much:  he would not take no for an answer and pursued Tibbie far past the point of what is reasonable.  He just did not come across as a kind or caring individual.  I know that romance heroes are usually supposed to be a bit of a bad boy, but this was just too much.  Elaine Coffman's writing is trite:  I felt like I was reading a mash-up of a book that started as an old-time Americana romance (e.g.  the goose chasing Tibbie down Main Street in the opening scene), which is why I bought the book in the first place.  But that mood was not sustained as there were too many jarring elements that got in the way, like bits of dialogue that was not true to the times, or elaborate descriptions of a pet pig and the food mash that he eats (e.g.  shades of Charlotte's Web).  Even the descriptions of the clothes were annoying:  Tibbie starts off wearing nothing but drab nondescript baggy dresses then somehow for no discernable reason is wearing more fashionable dresses with a bustle (hello, those dresses were not introduced until much later into the century).  Even the cover photo is out of whack:  why is Tibbie shown wearing cosmetics and a very low-cut red gown that only a prostitute would have worn back then?  Does not go with the book.  When I bought the book I was looking forward to losing myself in a sweet, well-written old-time Americana romance, but this book is not it.Read moreRead less
