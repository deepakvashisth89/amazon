I bought this little book and paid for it and I must say that after going through it I was not very happy.PRECISION: this book describes only the large destroyers of Kriegsmarine, namely:- 22 units of "Leberecht Maas" classes, with 127 mm guns (Z21 to 22),- 15 units of so-called "Narvik" classes, with 150 mm guns (Z23 to Z34 and Z37 to Z39)- 5 units of 1936B class (including the two unfinished) with 127 mm guns (Z 35, Z36 and Z43 to Z45)It does NOT treat smaller "Elbing" class destroyers and I think it was the right decisions, for lack of space. "Elbings" are, somehow strangely, treated in the book about "Kriegsmarine Coastal Forces".I was not happy with this book for following reasons:1) Information given is superficial. Osprey New Vanguard series are short (48 pages) and therefore the use of space must be optimal - here it is definitely NOT the case. Author made an extensive use of lists and enumerations which left many pages half empty. As the result, information about German destroyers is indeed very basic and especially the description of their operational history is short.2) The ZH1 (ex-Dutch "Gerard Callenburg"), ZG3 "Hermes" (ex-Greek "Vasilefs Georgios") and TA32 (ex-Yugoslavian "Dubrovnik"), large destroyers which all actually served in Kriegsmarine and fired many a shot in anger are mentioned IN ONE FREAKING LINE each!3) Once I went through this thing, I realised that EVERY single bit of information this book contains can be found for free on internet.4) Pictures included can also be almost all found on internet.5) Colour plates are not terrible. The only one having real value is the central two pager showing the interior of one destroyers. There is only one plate showing actual fighting, it shows of course the battle of Narvik (how original...) and even if it is honest, it's hardly worth paying actual money for it...6) Even in such short publication editing was poor and as result there are some really basic errors. It is said that Rear-Admiral Leberech Maas died in action in August 1915, when if fact it was in 1914 - and considering that it happened during the quite famous British victory at Helgoland, well, shame on you Osprey guys... In one place in the book we learn that Z28 was sunk on 6 April 1945 by British aircraft - just to read couple of pages later that she was sunk on 6 April 1945 by Soviet aircraft... From what I could find in my sources the first version is actually true... Honestly, I dont think that a serious editing effort was made on this book.For all those reasons I think that this book is only for terminal military history freaks who ALSO make a collection of Osprey books. I plead guilty on both counts. For anybody else, it makes much more sense to simply look up German World War II destroyers on internet and save your money for something else.Read moreRead less
