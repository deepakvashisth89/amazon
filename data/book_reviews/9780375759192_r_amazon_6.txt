It was hard work reading this book but I stuck to the end because of the reviews that termed it a "masterpiece".  That such a "perfect" Lord of England will persevere to love a woman he hadn't even "dated" for 6 long years even after her marriage to someone else sounds a little far-fetched.  Goodwood seems to be almost a stalker.  At first It was hard to imagine a spirited woman like Isabel going back to Osmond.  But on further reflection, maybe because she is what she is that she goes back to Rome.  An ordinary woman wouldn't have gone back.  But she goes back to face her reality.  Maybe she needs someone like Osmond to challenge her spirit.  She also is a person who will have satisfaction in taking care of her stepdaughter.  Another interesting detail is the stark difference between Pansy and her natural mother.  What is the ending trying to say?
