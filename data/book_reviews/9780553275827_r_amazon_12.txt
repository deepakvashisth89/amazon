As an avid consumer of Vietnam war memoirs, I'm amazed this one took me so long to come across. It was mentioned in David Ballentine's "Gunbird Driver" and I bought it.It follows a young West Point graduate as he joins an experienced, battle-hardened platoon already out in the field. It describes how he won their respect, and his thinking behind the actions that earned him that respect. It gives honest accounts of mistakes, candidly describes events where he contradicted the opinions of his men and was proven wrong. And both the corrupting and civilising influence of his men. It describes the frustration and fatigue from relentless patrolling and the battle against the VC and the constant threat of booby traps.I would highly commend this book to anyone in a leadership position, or aspiring to one, men and women in the military, or aspiring to be, or anyone interested in the Vietnam war and the psychology of the men who fought it.Read moreRead less
