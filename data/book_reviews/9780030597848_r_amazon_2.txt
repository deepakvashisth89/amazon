I’ve enjoyed every his Inspector Tibbett mystery so far (I’m reading  them in order). But this book captivated me most of all because of the fun character of the protagonist. Anthony Crombe-Peters (everyone calls him Pudge) is a stout young aristocrat with a pink and white complexion. Pudge has money, which he’s invested it in a the making of a film. As a condition for financing, he has insisted on being in charge of the budget. This makes him Executive Producer.As the money man in the realm of creatives, Pudge gets no respect. The director shuts him up. The cast puts him down. Alliances, friendships, and hostilities shift kaleidoscopically among key people on the set, and poor Pudge is always on the outside. Yet he’s quite good at his job, and often useful in other ways. He stays relatively calm in a sea of temperament. He’s also an innocent, and he accidentally interests his friend Henry Tibbett in the film house, when an ex-employee kills herself under odd circumstances.No one thanks Pudge for bring Scotland Yard onto the set. But as accidents and deaths plague the project, there’s no getting quit of Inspector Tibbett.  The case is complex, and every one of the many suspects is all too an good actor — except Pudge, of course.Patricia Noyes is a crisp, engaging writer, and her characters are vibrant with individuality.
