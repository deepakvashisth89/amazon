[i]The idea for this play came from an event that Elie Wiesel witnessed as a boy in Auschwitz: “Three rabbis - all erudite and pious men - decided one evening to indict God for allowing His children to be massacred. I remember: I was there, and I felt like crying. But there nobody cried.”[/i]Taken from the blurb on the back cover of the book.Set in Shamgorod, Ukraine, on Purim eve 1649, this tragic farce follows two Jewish survivors after a savage pogrom. Three Jewish minstrels arrive to perform a Purim play but instead, the two survivors stage a Din Torah (trial of God), questioning divine silence amid their suffering. This act highlights human dignity and moral courage, akin to Job’s biblical struggle.With humor and irony, the play reveals raw anguish as no one initially defends God. It becomes a testament to enduring faith through honest questioning, transforming into a powerful expression of resilience and hope.Elie Wiesel’s work honors the indomitable human spirit that defies despair, reminding us that questioning deepens faith, art and community affirm life, and even in darkness, the choice to protest, remember, and hope persists, urging us to confront suffering while embracing creativity, solidarity, and life itself.Read moreRead less
