I've been reading "Hope Against Hope" by Nadezhda Mandelstam in which she writes of her life with Osip Mandelstam. She also writes of many of her encounters with Anna Akhmatova and others in the post revolution Soviet union . Mostly she writes concerning their lives while trying to survive under Stalin. Their lives seem a living Hell especially after Osip Mandelstam is arresed for writing a poem critical of Stalin and is sent to prison and then sent to Siberia in 1934. Interestingly Nadezhda voluntarily follows Osip to Siberia so as to be with her husband until his death in 1938. This is a long and extraordinary story; well written and speaks of the many fascinating aspects of their lives, with much emphasis on the processes by which Osip wrote his poetry.  "Hope against Hope" is a long read at 400 pages, but that Nadezhda (which means Hope in Russian) and Osip are able to keep going and writing Poetry, as well as helping other people when they can, in spite of their often extreme suffering is a miracle of love.
