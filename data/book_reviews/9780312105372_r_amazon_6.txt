It all starts out as Trevor is looking for his constable uncle because his mother was beat up by someone living with her....he then stops Jack the ripper from murdering a whore, but gets shanghaied by him and taken to America, where he manages to escape him.  He meets up with a general who gives him room and board and sets off for Arizona with his daughter.  There is lots of typical wild west scenarios: train robberies, Indians, horse thieves.I just didn't like the unbelievability of the story.  Trevor is one of the unluckiest guys in the universe.  When they have an opportunity to obtain a gun and kill Jack the ripper, they decide not to, and some characters are murdered.  When he gets to America, he doesn't go immediately to the authorities and report a noseless man who is killing women.  Think about it:  if they knew they were looking for a noseless man, they would have caught him very quickly because...how many noseless men are there in society?  Also, how did a posse with guns not capture the ripper?  That's impossible.  I don't have patience for unlikely scenarios.There's plenty of adventure, violence, and sex as is typical in Laymon novels.  The girl in the story begins to speak British even though she's American (she says "bully!).  It's as if Laymon got confused while he was writing dialogue.  Just my thoughts.Read moreRead less
