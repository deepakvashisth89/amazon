Cold Steal — ReviewThere are detective novels that begin with a crime.And then there are detective novels that begin with Leonidas Witherall minding his own business and somehow finding himself trapped in another whirlwind of murder, confusion, and comic disaster.Cold Steal is one of the most enjoyable entries in the Leonidas Witherall series and a wonderful example of how Phoebe Atwood Taylor could blend a genuine mystery with laugh-out-loud humor.I spent a fog-softened Eureka afternoon reading it at the Eye of Bodhidharma while Edgar the raven occupied his customary fence post outside. The fog drifted through Old Town like a thief attempting to steal visibility itself.Edgar considered the title carefully.His position was simple:“A cold steal suggests someone planned poorly. Hot pastries disappear much faster.”Leonidas Witherall remains one of the most distinctive detectives of the Golden Age.A retired schoolmaster who bears a remarkable resemblance to Shakespeare, he repeatedly finds himself surrounded by crimes despite possessing no particular desire to become involved.What especially impressed me was the pace.The story races forward.Complications multiply.Suspects appear.Alibis collapse.And somehow Witherall remains calm while everyone around him becomes increasingly bewildered.The humor is superb.Not slapstick.Not parody.Rather, it grows naturally from eccentric personalities and absurd circumstances.The dialogue sparkles throughout.The supporting cast is full of colorful characters who seem incapable of making life easier.Beneath the comedy lies a solid mystery.The clues matter.The puzzle is fair.The solution is satisfying.Like the best Leonidas Witherall books, Cold Steal never sacrifices mystery for humor or humor for mystery.It manages both.⸻What Works Well✓ Wonderful comic mystery blendHumor and detection work together perfectly.✓ Leonidas Witherall himselfOne of the most charming detectives in mystery fiction.✓ Fast-moving
