I found this slow going. I did like the background: the author does a good job researching life in medieval monasteries and the technology and medicine of the times. The personalities of the characters are decently developed but not grippingly lively.The story surrounds a former love interest of Cadfael's, married to a local lord who deeds his property to the monastery in exchange for a lifetime of ease, essentially allowed to live in the surrounds of the monastery and receive cooked foods and medicine for himself, his wife and his servants. At his death, his large estate passes to the religious order. But soon after the new abbot sends him a delicious dish of special food, the master succumbs and Cadfael knows--it's poison. But who did it? His stepson, who had every reason to murder his stepfather or, someone else? The obvious is never the actual, and Cadfael has to hold off the authorities while solving the mystery.As usual, the mystery novel meme of the incompetent and impatient police is employed, and plenty of red herrings. But I found it a bit slow going for a mystery. My friends tell me the dramatized series on television is far better. I believe it.Read moreRead less
