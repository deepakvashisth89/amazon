I love this book! It's a great introduction to Nero Wolfe, and he doesn't make the mistake of putting too many characters to give them any depth in this one (which he sometimes does in later books). The plot is clever. The mystery is one that you probably will be able to solve before the end, but the story is still very engaging. The personalities of the characters are great, and you really feel you get to know them, whether they're stubborn and furtive, loyal but insistent upon justice, etc. There are only two things is change. One is a sign of the times but I wish they'd edit it out, which is that Archie makes racist remarks about so one because they are from South America (and he also assumes that everyone from Central or South America can be lumped into one, very racist category). The other thing I don't understand is that when it becomes obvious to Wolfe & Archie who the murderer is and that the murderer will strike again (and they know the intended victim), they do nothing to stop it. In other books, they've had operatives like Saul and Fred cover a victim or suspect to try to make sure they weren't harmed, or they could have told the police about their conclusions (they had evidence by this point, plus Cramer would have listened, since one of the murders was a high profile one). It's a fun read and does a great job introducing you to Nero Wolfe & Archie.
