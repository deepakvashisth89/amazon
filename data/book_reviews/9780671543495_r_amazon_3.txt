Some people might find this book to be a bit technical, but that does seem to be the point.  It lays out the rules for proper Klingon in concise, well-organized chapters.  It starts with a guide to letters and pronunciation.  Then it moves on to noun and verb prefixes and suffixes.  The rules do an excellent job of contrasting the language patterns with English.  While we speak more commonly with an active voice (He hits the ball.), Klingon sentence structure translates most directly to what we would call passive voice (The ball is hit by him.).  The book also notes that while the rules presented are the ones for proper Klingon, it is common to leave out some of the unnecessary or implied words for normal speech.  After all, "Go!" is a bit faster than "Go right now!" in a battle situation.  And this shortened military slang is said to carry over into normal daily speech quite often.There is a large translation vocabulary section, going both from Klingon to English and English to Klingon.  Following that is a short section of common phrases (like "Where is the bathroom?")The book itself is a normal quality paperback.  I see it getting well-used over time, and I'm sure it will hold up just fine.If you're interested in learning Klingon, you can't do any better than this book.  It is well-organized, simple to understand, and provides a lot of good examples.  I have only been reading the book for about a week, but I can fairly easily spell Klingon I hear on TV or in a movie, and I understand the occasional word.
