This place in Boston and Provincetown of 1994 and 1995.  The narrator of the book is Jeff O'Brien--he's 33, gay, a writer and going through a variety of crises.  He's starting to feel he's too old for the youth-obsessed gay world; his lover of many years (Lloyd) has just told him the "passion" has gone out of their relationship; his first lover Javitz is dying of AIDS and he gets seriously involved with 18 year old Eduardo.When I first read this in 1997 it blew me away.  I live in a suburb of Boston and I can tell you--this caught the gay life of Boston in the late 1990s perfectly!  The characters, situations and locales were all familar with me.  It was almost like reading a non-fiction book!  Any gay men who were living in or around Boston in the late 1990s will probably enjoy this.  I've read it two or three times since then and loved it each time.  Of course it IS dated now but it's so accurate.  Some people have complained that the main character is obnoxious and annoying.  That's true--but that's just like real life. (Actually I find his lover Lloyd a hundred times worse than Jeff!)  The characters talk (and act) like real people and the situations are realistic.  It's also kind of sad reading this.  Two places listed (Mildreds and a bookstore--We Think the World of You) are long gone and this was written when getting AIDS was an automatic death sentence.  This book does have some problems however.  Some characters get on your nerves (more than once I wanted to hit Lloyd) and Eduardo was WAY too intelligent for any 18 year old.  Also I was never part of the partying and hook-up crowd this book shows--but those guys DID (and still do) exist.  Still this is a truthful, insighful book that is a classic of gay literature.  This was so popular that author Mann went and wrote a sequel (Where the Boys Are) in 2003 and a few years later did ANOTHER (which I think is the end) called "Men Who Love Men".  The second one was as good as this (if much longer) and the third was the shortest--and the worst.  Still this is well worth reading as an accurate picture of gay men in Boston in the late 1990s.
