Although I found this book a little less compelling and appealing than Thunder and Roses, which I stayed up all night to finish in one sitting, it was still very enjoyable.  Kit and Luce were both likeable and sympathetic characters.  Kit took rather longer to overcome her trust issues than I thought necessary.  It was somewhat predictable but that wasn't a serious shortcoming, in my opinion.  Putney explained the meaning of Dancing on the Wind as an allusion to hanging, so I rather expected someone to end up on the gallows--no one did.  She did not as explicitly make the connection to the dancing abilities of the twins, but I think that was her intent.  I am a reader who reads the end of the book to determine if it will be worth the effort to complete--I like happy ending and/or, at the very least, redemption.  I find reading the end more cumbersome with e-books, so for those with similar foibles, this book was worth the read and had a satisfying denouement.  I look forward to meeting Kit and Luce again as the stories of Rafe and Michael are explored--I wouldn't mind running across Kira and Jason again.
