This, above else, is the central lesson of this sprawling novel:  “The one great principle of the English law is to make business for itself. There is no other principle distinctly, certainly, and consistently maintained through all its narrow turnings."  The case, Jarndyce  and Jarndyce exemplifies this "great principle" and forms the sun around which the various the novel's host of characters, revolve.  The case "has passed into a joke. That is the only good that has come of it."  Jarndyce and Jarndyce is said to "drone on"; it is long in the tooth and has involved intricacies that no one, least of the litigants, understand, or more precisely, can reach agreement on.  Wandering into this cavernous case are the novel's central characters:  Richard and Ada, and the unforgettable Esther.  And, of course, let us not forget the magnanimous guardian.The novel, at over 900 pages, is no walk in the park.  Yet, it's occasional discourses on the state of the law can be amusing and at times downright hilarious.  I was unaware before I started this epic tale that Bleak House is considered by many to be Dickens' best novel.  I am in no position to offer an opinion on that, as I have only read a couple of his novels, but it would not in the least surprise me.  Has the law as it seen from the eyes of countless layman ever known a better description.  After all, it involves numerous difficulties, contingencies, masterly fictions, and forms of procedure in this great cause (Jarndyce and Jarncyce), there has been expended study, ability, eloquence, knowledge, intellect...high intellect.
