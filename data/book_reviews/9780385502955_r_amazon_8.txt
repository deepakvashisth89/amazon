When I pay full price for the work the of a well known author, I expect a lot more than I would expect from the ninety-nine cents a pop e-crew that I also read here on Amazon. Using that criterion, THE QUEEN OF HARLEM doesn't deliver for me. I didn't find the writing or the story engaging.After CATCHER IN THE RYE, there has never been a need for another novel about a spoiled anti-hero rich kid nursing silly high-maintenance issues and obsessed with an older woman. And even if there were a need for another such novel, THE QUEEN OF HARLEM ain't it. The best thing about this book is its O'Henry-esque ending, but the ending isn't worth wading through the mediocrity preceding it. Maybe it's just me, but while I might find interesting the travails of well-to-do black kid from the South who changes his name and chucks it all to go and find himself in Harlem, I'm not much interested in a well-to-do black kid from the South who changes his name and, while still suckling at the tits of his rich parents' credit cards, fakes struggling to find himself in Harlem. I was never drawn into the pretentious world of Mason/Malik and Carmen. Nothing happens in this novel. Not really. I got no sense that Mason/Malik was growing up or evolving as a person. As Malik, Mason has no more depth than a Halloween costume. He just flits from one unnecessary subterfuge to another. He can't even fall for a girl of his newly adopted class.
