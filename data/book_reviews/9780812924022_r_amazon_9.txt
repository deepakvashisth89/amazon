This is a wonderful book. While ostensibly a memoir of the author's year as a clerk to U.S. Supreme Court justice, Harry Blackmun, it is a much richer work than that. It is, in fact, a deeply scholarly and extraordinarily well-written analysis of the Supreme Court and its  work, taking in, along the way, some very perceptive analsyses of leading Supreme Court deciions. If I were teaching a course on comparative constitutional law, I would certainly have this as a key item of reading  material. However, another of its great strengths is that it can be read and enjoyed  by anyone, whether or not they have a background in law or in American politics.Read moreRead less
