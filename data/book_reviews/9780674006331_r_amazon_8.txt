A very lengthy and detailed examination of what Posner refers to as the public intellectual market, which in his opinion is in decline.  Posner takes the market framework to make various points - that there are very low costs to being a bad public intellectual and therefore the product is shoddy, for example.  He gives many examples of public intellectuals who often comment outside of their expertise and expose their ignorance, which does not discourage them from pontificating and often making erroneous predictions.  There is no price to be paid, however, for this failure as often the public intellectual is mostly serving the function of building solidarity among the like-minded.  At almost 400 pages long, the book covers a lot of ground, sometimes too much.  The more interesting sections are the latter chapters where Posner goes into individual sub-fields, so to speak, of public intellectual activity, like public philosophers and legal minds.  Posner concludes with a series of recommendations which are mostly pie-in-the-sky.  I think his analysis suffers from being a construct that does not fit with concrete proposals so that in some ways framing the work of public intellectuals as a market is a bit of a stretch.  Interestingly, Posner, a public intellectual in his own right, provides an example of one who exposes his own out-of-field ignorance when he comments on the confession in the Catholic Church.
