I'm so glad I randomly stumbled upon this book. I had no idea LeVar Burton had written a fiction novel, and now I'm sad to see he hasn't written more (unless I'm looking in the wrong places).This was a really great and engaging read. I don't think I've read a post-apoc book before this one, not one that wasn't mixed with fantasy or urban fantasy, but LeVar has made me a fan.Written back in '97, this tells the story of a near-future (2019) America ravaged by civil war. The first African American president is assassinated by an extremist, which ultimately leads to a race war whose effects pretty much cripple the nation. People are left homeless amidst the wreckage, creating tent cities where they can; illness runs rampant, including a surge of skin cancer amongst Caucasian and fair-skinned people due to the ozone being diminished, which leads the wealthier citizens to undergo skin grafts with darker pigmented skin, creating a somewhat patchwork effect -- the main antagonist is one of these, nicknamed the Zebra, and is actually a doctor who specializes in it.There's a group of protagonists, but one which everything kind of revolves around. That's Rene, a scientist who invents a device that could pretty much cure all illness in someone, including cancer. She ends up being taken captive and, through telepathy (a very rare side effect from using her device (it can increase brain function)) calls out for help.This is where the rest of the protagonists come into play, the ones who hear her plea and decide to answer the call: an African American man who used to work for NASA but is now homeless like so many others; a little girl who is all alone looking for her mother; and a Native American who believes the call is from a great spirit who will prevent the end of the world.Will their paths join? Will they reach Rene in time? Or will they end up just another victim to the racist Skinners or other bloodthirsty vagabonds that roam the dying Earth?I'll let you read it for yourself and find out.I'm truly surprised I've never seen this book mentioned or talked about, it's really good and I believe would make a terrific show. It's a strong drama focused around people and their connections while they struggle to live in a post-apocalyptic nation (sorta like TWD, but without zombies).Read moreRead less
