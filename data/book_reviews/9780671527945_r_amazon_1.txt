The more I read by Elizabeth Jane Howard, the more impressed I grow.  She's supposed to be kind of a middle-brow female writer, and it's true that anybody who enjoys Rosamunde Pilcher or Anne Tyler or other similar authors whose primary terrain is family life will also enjoy Howard.  But she's so subtle and clever - I don't think she gets enough credit.  This novel is set in the early part of World War II, and continues the saga of the multi-generational Cazalet family which was begin in The Light Years.  The chapters are each told from the perspective of a different family member, with a special emphasis on the points of view of the children.  Howard is especially deft writing children, and what impresses me most is how unique each is - a fully defined individual, even if not fully mature.  She is marvelous at capturing what they understand, and what they don't, and manages to convey what's going on even when telling the story from the perspective of a child who doesn't fully understand what's going on.  That takes some doing.  And all along there are rich insights about human nature, families, relationships, growing up, and the impossibilities of fully knowing another person, even if you love them.
