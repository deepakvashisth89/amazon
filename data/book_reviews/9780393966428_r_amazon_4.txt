I had read, and really enjoyed "War and Peace" on my Kindle. I wanted to read "Anna" before seeing the new movie. I'm a fast reader but it's taken a couple of weeks to get to 75% on the Kindle - which just means that I feel immersed in the experience of the book. One thing I've enjoyed is that the translation seems to me to be in the modern English, even American vernacular: it's somewhat casual. I do not know Russian so I have to assume that I'm getting the story the way that Tolstoy had intended. It has been very readable. I don't read much French, either and it seems as though the farther along I get, the more French phrases there are. The first time I tried to read "War and Peace," the French is what caused me to put the (analog) book down and to take French in college. The way the French phrases are annotated in this Kindle version, with links to the translation, is a little awkward. I've read other Kindle books that had the translated phrase nearer the original, not requiring clicking back and forth. Better yet - if you are going to translate the Russian into English, why leave French phrases in? There are several places in which it's mentioned that French is spoken in order to keep servants from understanding so I think it's obvious that the upper classes switch languages frequently. As far as the book goes - it's character-driven, like "War and Peace;" there are several main characters to care about and to follow. There's lots of descriptive detail about farming and everyday life, along with some 19th-century Russian political philosophy, that help to paint a picture of time and place.
