When I was but a naive and shy young boy in elementary school I latched onto books as a means of escape. I didn’t comprehend it at the time, but I really struggled with the home life I had been thrust back in to. And believe it or not, but I probably secretly wished that I had been left in foster care.Anyway, books in the school library were one of my only means of escape, or at least as a short reprieve from events at home. And while I don’t recall 99.9% of the titles I read, one title in particular made an indent in my nascent imagination. That was C.S.Forester’s “Sink The Bismarck”.Years later, sometime in my twenties, I probably saw the film adaptation for the first time, most likely on a Saturday afternoon when one of the broadcast channels showed old movies… And while it further stimulated my interest regarding the hunt for the ship, I was prisoner to the whims of the local affiliate stations as to when I might be able to watch it again.Remember, this was the dawn of the VCR and the VHS tape, and studios had just begun to realize that there was a growing appetite for content, and that it was possible for studios to broaden their revenue streams. This was also the age when Blockbuster and its competitors seemed unstoppable in their reach… Before Redbox and back when Netflix had vending machines.Anyway, years later I found a DVD copy of Sink The Bismark, and I watch it about every 8-10 months. But it was Robert Ballard, fresh off of his discovery of the Titanic…Shameful that ghoulish grave robbers were granted exclusive rights to remove artifacts, and profit off their display and sale, but even more shame on the courts that allowed it…Anyway, I was excited to learn that Doctor Ballard had also discovered the wreck of the Bismarck. And I’m planning on getting the National Geographic documentary on the discovery, but in the meantime I had this wonderful book to affix my attention.And the book does not disappoint, not in the least. I love the format, where the actual events of the brief life of the ship are chronicled, both from official sources and from eyewitnesses to the events. And it sheds new light and insights into the lives and choices made by people from the lowest rank on up to the leaders.The images I have kindled regarding the British admirals on one side, and of Admiral Lutjens and Captain Lindemann on board the Bismarck, were heavily influenced by the portrayals in the movie, Sink The Bismarck. So I was surprised to read of the apparent disdain that Lindemann had for his superior. And of the growing indecisiveness and of self-doubt that affected Lutjens from the moment they sailed out of the relative safety of the Baltic right up until the ship’s fate was sealed.And I was surprised to learn that there were in fact U-boats in the area. Two of them that are mentioned. The U-556, especially, had forged a bond with the Bismarck before leaving port, but now found itself low on fuel and out of torpedoes, while it had a British aircraft carrier and cruiser in its periscope that were joining the hunt. And the other U-boat, also low on supplies, was ordered to stand by and retrieve the Bismarck’s log books… But apparently the only thing they managed to do was pick up a handful of survivors after the sinking.These historical accounts from witnesses and survivors on both sides tell the tale of what happened in the North Atlantic in May of 1941. And interspersed between those chapters is the story of the two expeditions that Robert Ballard mounted in 1988 & 1989. But at the end of the book he mentions that there are still a number of unanswered questions. Especially regarding the whereabouts of the missing turrets (they only found one of the four). And Ballard talked about wanting to return with a better equipped UAV to take color still and video images of the wreck.The book did encourage me to get the Nat Geo DVD, and I already had the similar documentary of Ballard’s search for the 4 Japanese aircraft carriers lost at the Battle of Midway, where all he found was the one American carrier lost, the USS Yorktown. So maybe some day he’ll finish that search.The ocean is a deep and dark graveyard that is fickle about surrendering its secrets.
