Kung, with his well-publicized history of dissent from the Roman Catholic magisterium, was indeed a provocative choice to pen the Roman Catholic edition of the excellent 'Short History' series, but he was in many ways a superb candidate to write it. Still a priest in good standing, a scholar of great insight when it comes to the economic perspective of broad and complex historical realities and, indeed, one of the foremost theologians of world Christianity, Kung would never be tempted to gloss-over the more unsavory moments in the story of the Roman Church -- many of which (the Donation of Constantine, anyone?) changed the course not only of ecclesiastical history but global history. What you get with Kung's amazing gift for forensic, penetrating scholarship is exactly what is advertised: a no apologies, no frills, and no obfuscating short history of Western Civilization's most ancient, most enduring and --for many-- most mysterious and at times irascible institution. All the high points, low points, saints and serpents are here, from the first decidedly non-papal house-churches in Rome to the pseudo-conversion of Constantine, to the horrific split from Constantinople and Orthodoxy to the Reformation and the first creaking windows thrown open for Vatican II.
