This review is of “Amber Passion”, the final book in Elaine Barbieri’s “Amber Trilogy”.“Amber Passion” begins four years after “Amber Treasure” ended. The heroine of the “Amber” series, Melanie Morganfield Young Randolph, co-owner of Young Shipping Enterprises, and her husband, Captain Worth Randolph, co-owner of the same shipping concern, are very happy together. Their happiness is short-lived, however, when news comes that one of their ships has been attacked and sunk by Chinese pirates. Equal parts angry and concerned, Melanie and Worth board another of their ships, the Mary Lowe, and set sail for China.In Chinese waters, Melanie and Worth’s ship is captured by the notorious pirate Sie-Seh, who they are shocked to discover is a woman. They are even more angered by the identity of her helper; Jason Marsh, an Englishman who tricks his way onto their ship by claiming he is the only survivor of a ship attacked by Sie-Seh. Jason eventually redeems himself by helping Melanie and Worth reclaim  their freedom and the Mary Lowe from Sie-Seh, but at a heavy price; Worth is seriously injured fighting Sie-Seh’s pirates. Worth does, with the help of a Chinese acupuncturist, return to full health. Meanwhile, back at home, Melanie’s daughter, Sara, and longtime family friend Michael Searle, are realizing that they are in love with each other.A series of events shakes the Randolph family, but they persevere and eventually, Michael and Sara and Melanie and Worth finally have their Happily Ever Afters.Upside: I can pretty much cut and paste from every review I do of Ms. Barbieri’s books; the characters feel their emotions very deeply, both male and female. That remains the strongest part of Ms. Barbieri’s books for me.Downside: Again, cutting and pasting; the emotional depth comes at a cost of character development. I was also very uncomfortable with the relationship between Michael and Sara. From the first time he was introduced, Michael was in love with Melanie. Even though Ms. Barbieri tried to move away from this, I feel very strongly that Michael’s love for Sara was, at a very basic level, inspired by the fact that she is Melanie’s daughter and looks like Melanie. I found this-plus the disparity in their ages: Sara is 16 and Michael 34-very uncomfortable for me. The ending of the book-which cried out for excitement-ended with a thud.Sex: The scenes are a little racier than before in Ms. Barbieri’s books, but they are more focused on the feeling of the act rather than the mechanics.Violence: Multiple assaults. Two of Melanie and Worth’s workers die due to sabotage at their shipyard. The violence is not overly graphic.Bottom Line: “Amber Passion” might be my favorite book in the “Amber Trilogy”.
