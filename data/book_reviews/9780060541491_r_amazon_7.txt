Firstly I just want to say a big thank you to Joyce Carol Oates for writing this beautiful, honest work of fiction.  I read a lot in high school and was always rolling my eyes when teen books skirted around profanity or blurred when something mature was happening.  If the narrator is a true teenager or describing a real teenager's life, they're not going to be able to avoid big topics like sex or profanity like the f-bomb.  Joyce knows this, and tells the story truthfully, not caring that some sheltering parents won't want to buy it for their children.Now, about Sexy.  There's a lot going on in this story, and I think it's sad that some people are defining it as a "gay" book or a "pedophile" book because it's very much neither.  What we see is a 16 year old boy who does very well for the swim team, and has grown incredibly gorgeous.  He's discovering a sense of popularity and perhaps unfair favors being provided for him because of his looks, but being the shy type he doesn't go around abusing his powers.  There is a pivotal moment when his male English teacher gives him a ride home and acts a little flirty with him.  Though nothing sexual happens, Darren feels embarrassed and recalls what his father told him about homosexuality and wants to forget all about it.  In the long run, he's able to forget the incident as nothing important, but when his friends want to get even with the English teacher for failing them for plagiarism, he is faced with more tough choices.The book is dedicated to the "Darrens" of the world, and I think that's so appropriate.  I know many Darrens, and it's quite heartbreaking to see a gay teenager who also just so happens to be a jock and what they go through.  Or maybe they're not gay at all, but just don't see why people treat them differently.  I've seen children grow into beautiful, adult like bodies and often that comes with a lot of responsibilities that maybe they aren't prepared for.  This book touches on all of those things, and it does it seriously.  At no point does it feel edited or abridged to fit the morals of clueless parents.  It's paced perfectly that I couldn't put it down and read it all in one sitting.  It's a short book, but nonetheless that's rare even for a book-lover like myself.If you're a parent and are worried this book is too "risqué" or "inappropriate" you need to get a life.  Your teenagers go through a lot of tough stuff and if you can't appreciate a book aimed for them that talks about it honestly then you don't understand what it's like to be young.Though I am only 3 years out of high school, I think I can also say that this book should appeal to adults as well, even if childless.
