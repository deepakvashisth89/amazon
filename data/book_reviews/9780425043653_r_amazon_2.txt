“The solar system seethed with activity…fighting, feeding, and breeding, learning the new technologies that spewed forth almost before the old had been mastered, girding, itself for the first exploration of the far stars in deep space…”This is one of those books where I couldn’t decide on how I would feel about it until the very final paragraph of the very final page. I wanted to see how everything leading up would play out. In the end, I ended up really liking this novel, even though I had some reservations early on.There is a definite uniqueness in Alfred Bester’s The Stars My Destination, something that gives it a quality that is groundbreaking, edgy, and modern. It is very dark, violent, and has a quick-paced and frenzied feel to it. It has its action-packed moments, yet is also ripe with a brand of literary elements as well, important themes and symbolism.Although it is often billed as “The Count of Monte Cristo in Space,” there are other elements that are noteworthy and make it an engaging read, such as the innovative technological aspects and the character transformation of one antihero, Gulliver Foyle.Left for dead on board the spacecraft Nomad, mechanic Gulliver Foyle has spent nearly six months waiting for help. When another craft, the Vorga, passes him by without as much as offering help, he makes it a personal vendetta to go after those who were aboard this other ship.One of the more innovative features is the method of jaunting, or teleporting to a specific location, as a means of travelling. This method becomes a prominent player in key moments in the novel.“He was Gully Foyle, the oiler, wiper, bunkerman; too easy for trouble, too slow for fun, too empty for friendship, too lazy for love.”When we meet Foyle early in the novel, he is a big, dumb brute, and there is unrestrained savagery about him that is off putting. His quest to find those responsible creates a rage inside him, and this will open up complications as we proceed further in the book. One of the more remarkable aspects of the novel is the character transformation of Foyle, actually forming a conscience and moral compass later on.This was a fast-paced and uniquely presented science fiction. It is dark at times, as I mentioned earlier, but it was entertaining and engrossing. I had never actually heard of this author before, so I’m interested in looking at some other of his works.
