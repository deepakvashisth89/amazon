The funny thing about Alfred Bester's The Stars My Destination is the fact that you would never know it was published in 1956 unless you read the copyright date in the front of the book.  The novel reads like a modern sci-fi novel with all of the qualities you want from the genre: action, intrigue, and imagination.The plot is basically a revenge story and the comparisons to the Dumas The Count of Monte Christo are easily and often made.  What's fascinating about this book is where it falls in the science fiction timeline.  Usually books half a century old feed dated and tame but the pace of this book is relentless while at the same time introducing concepts that have since been replicated into science fiction norms.The main protagonist Gully Foyle is an interesting character that is fueled by revenge but is more then just a one-dimensional character.  His strength is his ability to relate to the reader as an everyday man and you can't help but root for him despite his many faults.  Unfortunately pretty much everyone else comes off as cardboard cutouts except for some of the female characters who don't play into the story as much as they could have.The Stars My Destination is considered to be a masterpiece of sconce fiction and ranked 6th in the 1998 Locus all time SF poll.  Interestingly enough it moved up a few spaces from the 1988 poll, replacing some 70's sci-fi novels that apparently felt more dated in 98' then this mid 50's book.  For me it's lacking in scope and doesn't transcend the genre like some of the other books in the top 20 but I can appreciate this for being what it is and it's importance to science fiction.Bottom Line:  Unlike the boring LOTR trilogy, this is a great influential novel that rocks for more then just the generic "it was the first of it's kind" reasoning.Read moreRead less
