You can read "Seven Wild Sisters" without having read "The Cats of Tanglewood Forest" first. But it's probably better to read Charles de Lint's and Charles Vess' previous book. Both stories take place in the countryside outside Newford, but they're separated by many decades.Lillian Kindred, the young girl who was turned into a kitten in "Cats" is, in "Sisters," now an elderly woman living alone in a cabin way back in the woods. She's befriended by Sarah Jane Dillard, the fourth of seven redhaired sisters, who comes to appreciate the simple way Lillian lives. When Sarah Jane finds a very small man who has been shot with many tiny arrows, she brings him to Lillian. This involves them in a faerie war, with both fae sides taking the other six sisters captive. Sarah Jane, Lillian, and the Apple Tree Man must find a way to solve the crisis.Charles de Lint's writing is captivating, as usual. Charles Vess' wonderful illustrations fit the narrative perfectly.Buy the hardcover edition instead of the Kindle edition. I really love e-readers, but illustrated books should be viewed as paper editions as the author and illustrator intended. It will be well worth the extra $4.00. Besides, if you ever get the opportunity to meet the two Charleses, as I did recently, how are they going to sign an e-book?According to Amazon, the book is written for children in grades 3-7.
