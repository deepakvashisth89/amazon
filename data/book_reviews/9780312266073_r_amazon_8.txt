I looked forward to the arrival of this book as in the past most writers tend to to focus on the relationship between Jacqueline Kennedy and her father or her in-laws.I thought that this would explore new avenues in the life of a woman we have all read so much about....How wrong I was.The book is filled with superficial facts that you might find in a copy of a woman's magazine throughout the sixties and seventies. The relationship between the two woman is not delt with in any depth at all.I feel it would have been more interesting if the author had dealt with the relationship between the two woman and other family members...much as sister Lee Radziwell and half brother and sister ,James and Janet Auchincloss.It would appear from the writing that she has gleaned most of her information from previously published books.The author seems to have an  annoying obession with monetary issues and its equivalent value in todays currency, and rambles on and on in the first couple of chapters about everyones ancestors...lee,Bouvier,kennedy and Auchincloss which serves only to fill in space .The illustrations were disappointing, the only one with any interest that hasn't been seen before is the one on Janet's 80th birthday supplied by Hugh D. Auchincloss Jnr.If your a Kennedy enthusiast then you may find something worthwhile in this slight volume,but I seriously doubt it.Read moreRead less
