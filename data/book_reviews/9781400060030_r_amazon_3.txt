I am a Thomas Perry fan, and I get that his readers hold him to a very high standard, which he has met many times.  But I don't understand why some of these fans are disappointed in this book.  It chronicles well the easy path any of us could take into evil, and the accidents of fate that might lead any of us to encounter it (and end up in mortal danger).  Robert Mallon may not be the most glistening of Perry's protagonists, but his path from the easy ennui of wealth to putting himself on the line to stop evil is well-drawn and worthy.  This book is a substantial allegory for the moral dilemmas of our times.  Well done, Mr. Perry.Read moreRead less
