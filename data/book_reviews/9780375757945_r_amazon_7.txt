This was a really great book.  A little tedious at times, maybe because of my short attention span, but thoroughly enjoyable.  He is an excellent descriptive writer, and paints wonderful portraits of early California, the Mexican culture, the three main ports (San Diego, Monterey, and San Francisco, as well as Dana Point, which was named after him. He was an upperclassman at Harvard University when he dropped out due to failing eyesight, and decided to sign onto a merchant ship in Boston.  For many years his book was the main source of information about early California.  The reader is also treated to a description of life on a small merchant sailing ship from a common sailor's point a view.  The "before the mast" in the title refers to his being a common sailor (in spite of his wealthy background, this was his choice) as the sailors, as opposed to the officers,  were berthed in the bow of the ship, in front of the masts.  The latter part of the volume, "And 24 years after," is a disappointing narrative of a return trip he made back to California, not nearly as rich in it's descriptions as the main book.
