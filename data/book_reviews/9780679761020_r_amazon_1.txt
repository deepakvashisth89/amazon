Thanks to my introduction by way of the sublime "So Long, See You Tomorrow," I recently read a collection of William Maxwell's short stories.  The title of the collection is "All the Days and Nights" -- a phrase sublime in its own right.My favorite story is "Love," about the author's fifth-grade teacher.  "Her voice was as gentle as her beautiful dark-brown eyes.  She reminded me of pansies. ...  We meant to have her for our teacher forever.  We intended to pass right up through sixth, seventh, and eighth grades and on into high school taking her with us."My favorite passage, from another story, is this: "The view after seventy is breathtaking.  What is lacking is someone, anyone, from the older generation to whom you can turn when you want to satisfy your curiosity  about some detail of the landscape of the past,.  There is no longer any older generation.  You have become it, while your mind was mostly on other matters."Maxwell's stories are gems of remembrance.
