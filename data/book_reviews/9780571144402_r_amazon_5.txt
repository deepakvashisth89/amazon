This book has dated somewhat since I read it 10 years ago.  The prose was obsessive and repetitive, labouring too many points, fully forgivable in the circumstances it was written.  But after Havel, then just a playwright and political prisoner, later Czech president, the book serves only as historical interest in Havel's biography.  I have not read Letters to Olga, but from the references to that work in this book, I would say that must hold more resonance still than Living in Truth.  There are some interesting appendices by a host of other writers worth looking at.  But if it is a philosophy of living under (post-)totalitarianism you are looking for, there are better places to go than here.
