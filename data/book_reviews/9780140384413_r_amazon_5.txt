Leo has "borrowed" his parent's car and is driving his rather self-centered friend Tim to the local busline so that Tim can take his drawing portfolio to an interested publisher.  However, they just aren't going to get there.Leo is getting more and more annoyed at Tim, as Tim is getting more and more anxious that he will miss his bus if Leo doesn't get a hustle on, but Leo is having less and less control over his car.  Leo's car is losing more and more of its power, until it stalls out completely.Then Leo and Tim start seeing strange lights, and then even stranger creatures appear by their car.  Then they are abducted and taken aboard a flying craft where they are experimented on by grotesque looking disconnected heads.  After various tests, the heads decide to release Leo, but to keep Tim, while mentioning another group called "The Others".  But just before they release Leo they wipe his memory of his experience about the abduction.  The disappearance of Leo, and the new hole in his memory causes Leo no ending of problems when he tries to explain why Tim didn’t come home with him.Of course, Tim’s father pitches a fit and tries to convince local yokel Captain Kroll to arrest Leo, insinuating that Leo may have robbed, or worse, Tim for the money he was carrying.  But Kroll isn’t convinced, and he eventually refers Leo to a regressive psychologist Dr. Viridian, who will hypnotize Leo in an effort to recover Leo’s lost memories, which he does.  This is something that he does, but then Dr. Viridian replaces these memories with some unbelievable cartoonesque memories.  False memories which then gets Leo in even more trouble, and then Dr. Viridian disappears, and when Leo attends a meeting of Dr. Viridian's patients, he finds out that a Dr. Pierce, who specialized in the same area as Viridian, has also disappeared.  Then on the way home Leo is followed, and then he is placed under control and made to go to a rendezvous point where he finds Tim, who has been returned, but who is now two years older than he was when he was abducted.What makes matters worse is that when Leo gets home, Tim's father shows up, and while still claiming that Leo is responsible for Tim's disappearance, and makes very vocal declaimers that the returned Tim is not his real son, but a look-alike double.Then as a last resort Tim and Leo decide that publicity would be a good thing, and so they publish one of the pictures to national sensation, and then the action in the novel picks up, and we get another kidnapping, another abduction, or two, and a rescue, and our world is saved, and the bad guys are sent running.On the positive side, the book is a breezy read, the novel has some nice twists and the characters are, for the most part, likable.  On-the-other-hand, there are plotholes you could drive my bother’s semi through; Tim keeps being dumber that a box of rocks; and the novel is clearly meant for younger, unsophisticated readers, as older readers will have seen it all before.  This is because this novel reads like a Goosebumps crossed with an X-Files episode, with the abducting heads meaning to gross-out more than anything.  What's worse is the cynical cyclical ending that screams "sequel".But still, my much younger self probably would have loved it, but I can only give the novel three stars, and I'm being generous.
