Cape Cod has its share of quirky residents, and Bartholomew "Bart" Paget of Quanomet Bluffs is certainly one of them.  He's been described as suffering from "sullegomania" -- "a mania just for collecting."  His yard and his house are crammed full of his collections.  But now what he's really looking to buy is a full suit of armor.  He calls on Asey Mayo to tell him this news and to show off the fact that he's got the old Porter "Century" buggy running.  Asey intends to follow Bart and to make sure he gets home in that contraption, but other things come up.  The next morning, Bart is found dead in his library, with claw marks on his neck.  Asey feels an obligation to investigate the murder, since Bart very well might still be alive if Asey had just followed him home.  Bart had friends in the community.  Who would have wanted him dead?Well, maybe Susan Remington, for one.  She has two wild cats, Smith and Wesson, who attacked the Bigelow boy just recently.  And at the time of Bart's death, someone had let the cats out of their cage.  But where is Sue, anyway?  She was supposed to visit Bart that night to speculate on buying his set of chessmen.  An empty case was found near the man's body.  What about Elly Allenby, her son Scott, and their housemate Lucy (the one with the crystal ball)?  One of them might have had reason to eliminate Bart, since he had recently reneged on getting a job for Scott.  There's Webby Betts, the one-armed man who has created his own sacred golf course on his property and who gets irked anytime anyone else steps on it.  And Asey keeps hearing about one Noel Nash, who is said to have had crushes on just about every man on the Cape, and who may be seeking revenge on the women who eventually landed those beaus.  Suspects abound.  And in the midst of an unrelenting Spring rain, Asey must figure out what's what.  As usual, his friend Doc Cummings helps the skillful sleuth in the course of the investigation.  They even find the murder weapon, the wagh nakh.  Yes, it looks as if Bart was killed by something on display in his own house."Spring Harrowing" is one of the better episodes in the series, with enough odd people and events to keep even modern mystery lovers in suspense.
