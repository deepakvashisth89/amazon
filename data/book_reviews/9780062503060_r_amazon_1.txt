I am reviewing with pleasure the 1990 edition (ISBN 9-780060-623393) of Dr. Marcia Falk's "The Song of Songs."The attached photo shows a typical poem in this edition with the original Hebrew text, the poetic English translation and a beautiful illustration.  Marcia Falk's translation is not free verse.  Rather, she adopts a modern English poetic style to enhance the reader's experience.  Adding to that experience is her use of different fonts to indicate a woman's voice, a man's voice and the voice of a chorus.  It should be noted that half of the verses are from a woman's voice.  Hebrew women found their voice in the Song of Songs!Modern scholars do not believe that the Song of Songs, aka Song of Solomon, was written by King Solomon.  While Dr. Falk presents many "origin theories" for the Song of Songs, she makes the case that it is an anthology or collection of poetry that was primarily written by Hebrew women.For students of the Bible or Tanakh, Dr. Falk includes a rich set of commentaries and analyses of the Song of Songs and describes the journey of providing a faithful translation of an ancient text that can be fully appreciated by a modern reader.  For each poem, she includes revealing notes to help the reader understand its original cultural context.Of the four editions of "The Song of Songs" that I have, the 1990 edition is my favorite.  It contains illustrations that are not present in the 1982 edition.  It contains commentaries and notes missing from the 1993 and 2004 abridged editions.  If you are looking for an edition with only the Hebrew text, the English poems and the beautiful illustrations, I recommend the 1993 edition.
