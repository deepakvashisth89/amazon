The first few stories belong with the earlier “Bech:  A  Book”.  In fact Updike almost parodies himself when he interweaves two travels, two separate stories, into one, even interweaving sentences from each story in one paragraph without confusing the reader (“Australia and Canada”).  The most entertaining of the opening stories is the segment of “Three Illuminations” in which he is sent to a Caribbean resort – Norma Latchett, accompanies him, very much Bech’s equal for once.  Bech becomes totally outrageous in the “Holy Land” in a sentence in which he generalizes about the female of our species.The heart of this publication, 76 pages in paperback, is “Bech Wed”.  Here we find a conflicted, sympathetic Bech, who even relates to children.  The conflicts are both of personality and cultural background, as Bech tries to make monogamy work in the foreign world of Wasp suburbia.  Since the writing is wonderful as usual, this is a 5 star piece which could be enjoyed extracted from the rest of “Bech”, with possibly a brief introduction which would almost certainly mention that Bech won “the Melville Medal, awarded every five years to the American author who has maintained the most meaningful silence”.Read moreRead less
