The first in Kaminsky's rereleased series of 24 Hollywood-themed thrillers, and it's a good one. The character's more human than many grizzled ex-cop detectives (he's not much good with his gun and stands just 5'9 in his socks), and use of language is more poetically thought-out than in many hardboiled thrillers ("dotted his eye with a bullet").The fun part, though, is the name-dropping: actual actors and producers from the golden age of cinema wander in and out of shot, often at times in their careers before they were famous. (In this one, Errol Flynn when he was still on his way up.) In addition, the sense of "being there" is very strong: he describes backlots, sound stages, little details like lighting rigs and camera crews without labouring.Kaminsky, of course, was a film professor, and lived and breathed this stuff. His love for the big screen is clear on every page, and it gives this series a twist that makes it different to the average thriller. One BIG FLAW though: these books are TOO EXPENSIVE. The first is taster-priced, but in the UK the Kindle editions (after the first) start at £5.39 and go as high as £8.99! Not even top names like Lee Child price so high, and a Reacher thriller has up to three times the page count. (Kaminsky's books are about 50-70,000 words, short novels not epics.) At £3 I'd buy the lot, but at over a fiver a pop I'll be waiting until the price point falls.Read moreRead less
