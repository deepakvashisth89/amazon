If you thought generational theorizing began with the Baby Boomers, Lawrence will prove you wrong.An epic stretching three generations, Lawrence tries to show how the ancient agricultural lifestyle changed to that of Edwardian England. While at first I balked at Lawrence’s use of quasi mythological language to depict his protagonists, I noticed that its omission in the latter third of the novel is supposed to express the change from archetypal types to modern interpersonal life.It’s an impressive effort to depict in a novel the transition, almost like Thomas Hardy, to modernity. And while the eponymous rainbow that ends the novel as a sign that future generations can be filled with hope proved completely fallacious with the coming of WWI, the Great Depression and WWII, it doesn’t detract from the interest of the text.For those who like Lawrence’s curious blend of spirituality, socialism and anti authoritarian attitude the novel will probably succeed.
