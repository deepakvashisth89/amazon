When critics describe a book as a thriller, I expect to be thrilled. A thriller this is not, although it has its merits. I read the text in translation but was able to appreciate the language, which is poetic and graceful. This is certainly a fine coming-of-age piece, well-done, certainly, filled with a marvelous sense of the reality of childhood. I was especially pleased with the author's gutsy treatment of the reality of childhood, the harsh capacity of children to be brutal and cruel to each other. The title suggests a level of involvement that I never experienced. I was never gripped by a compelling story. Rather I thought that the story moved slowly. The strength of the writing is not in the plot, which is true of most thrillers, but in the descriptions. I thought the childhood relations were spot on, especially between the younger and older children, who use their strength and size to intimidate and harass the smaller, more vulnerable kids. The kids are smacked around a lot by their parents, too, which I think is both realistic, and marvelously non-PC. (In modern America, the greatest country in the world, the kids would call the police and the parents would be locked up.) The story around the kidnapped kid struck me as thoroughly disappointing. There was little pay-off emotionaly, and I didn't think the experience was explored well or fully.
