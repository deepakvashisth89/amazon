'God's Bits of Wood' is a classic novel based around the true facts of a railroad strike in 1940s French colonised West Africa (the modern nations of Senegal and Mali).  There are multiple characters based at three cities along the railroad: Bamako, Thies, and Dakar.  The Black railway workers go out on strike in an attempt to gain equal working conditions and pay with their white colleagues.  The consequences are severe - starvation, violence and imprisonment, but the workers persist and ultimately the women in their lives also take action.There's a diverse range of characters, all interesting and generally likeable (the latter not being true of the racist white managers however).  It highlights again how wrong colonialism was and it is uncomfortable reading for anyone from a nation that practiced colonialism.  Reading stories like this helps build understanding of the ongoing resentment of colonial powers.  But to say it's just a story about colonialism would be to simplify things too much.  There's such a lot of meaty subjects involved here and I can see why it's on some exam syllabi and a reading group favourite.It's remarkable how a story with so many characters can manage to give them all development and the pacing is done really well so you never fall behind and forget too much about each before they reappear.  Often with a narrative that shares its time between multiple character groups some shine to the detriment of others, but Ousane manages to give a fair hearing to each of the fictional 'bits of wood' in his novel.I was left with a deep respect for the real people who undertook the strike and what they suffered in order to obtain rights that should have been theirs automatically.  As well as highlighting a piece of history, it is also a really fine novel full of great characters and touching stories.
