As an avid reader of adventure fiction, and as a person who has lived on and around the sea all my life, I can only say that I am appalled by the depth and extent of Mr. Stroud's ignorance of things nautical, natural and biological. This book is fraught with what we used to call "howlers"---inconsistencies and errors which are so bad, they're inadvertently funny. First and foremost, Stroud should just call the back end of a boat "the stern" or "the transom". Seemingly endlessly, he refers to the "stern boards", as if lacking a thesaurus. Then he describes a huge tiger shark as "yellow and black"! The boat commandeered for some of the chase scenes is described as an older 1941 Chris Craft some 60 feet long and very narrow. Now, admittedly, CC did make a 40-foot and a 48-foot "commuter" type (both fairly narrow) in the 1920's but most of the later, longer boats were quite lofty and beamy, being cruisers. Be that as it may, the boat seemingly had gas turbines and/or diesels which seemed to both be available for propulsion. Then we learn that the boat has a "chain of portholes along her lapstrake keel". For viewing the bottom? I didn't laugh---I shouted.At this point I was ready to put this ridiculous book down, but I persisted long enough to find out that tropical storms no longer circulate counter-clockwise north of the equator and can sneak up on Floridians with no warning whatsoever; jet liners frequently fly through hurricanes; to rescue someone lying to the west of you, you turn to the east; to kill someone from behind with a knife to the heart, you stab them under the right shoulder blade----wait, maybe that's in the northern hemisphere(!); the Gulfstream can move at 20 kts.; whales ingest porpoises and tuna (even sperm whales don't do that and orcas don't frequent the Keys); kelp and ferns festoon the Bahamas; helicopter heavy machine guns emit 20mm slugs---and just an endless array of similarly stupifying errors which rob the book of the last vestige of realism.I honestly cannot say who should be more embarrassed: Mr. Stroud, for revealing himself to be a true hack with no fundamental knowledge of those things about which he writes, Simon and Schuster, for publishing this ludicrous slop or the general public, such as I, who were induced to buy this by the glowing reviews. Bait and switch indeed. If this is the state of current fiction, God help us. Ditto reviewing and criticism. If we are this uncritical as a public, we have progressed not a whit since Mark Twain (was it not?) sent a "winged esophagus" across one of his lectures. I will allow that Mr. Stroud did alright with Sumner-class destroyers-----I served as a Weapons Officer on that class-----but the rest is just flat moronic. There was a book published a few years back, dealing with errors in Hollywood films. I believe it was called "Gladiators Don't Wear Wristwatches". Should the readers be sticklers for accuracy? Only if they have self-respect. As for writers: talk (or write) about what you know; ask about what you don't.  I have a couple of other books by Mr. Stroud; I don't know if I can bring myself to read them, or, if I do, if I can restrain myself from writing another review. Mr. Stroud, if you read this (which I doubt), I stand ready to proof your future work for similar slips.Fred DouglassRead moreRead less
