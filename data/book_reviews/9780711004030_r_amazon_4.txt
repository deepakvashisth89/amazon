If you have the volumes covering the USA fleet, the Japanese Imperial Navy, and the German Fleet. You realise just how big our fleet was purely from the thickness of those volumes.  The UK had more battleships, battlecruisers and Cruisers than anyone else and were on par with the USA for aircraft carriers during the early part of the war.  The only ships we were short of were the escort destroyers for the North Atlantic convoys, hence the large number of dated US destroyers we had to procure through land lease.  The book is not as detailed as say the Conway volume covering WW2 ships, but it is a good second.  When you looked at the ships we scrapped straight after the war it was a shame that some of the larger ships like HMS Rodney and HMS King George V were not saved and turned into floating museums as some of the US Navy ships have been.
