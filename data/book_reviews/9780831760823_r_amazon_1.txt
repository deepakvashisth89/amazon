Although it provides a decent overview of the state of naval architecture in the late 70s, this book presents Norman Friedman at his earliest and least focused. Much of what it says about warship design, naval weapons and electronics, and the problems facing ship designers, still ring true today. Unfortunately, each of the 12 chapters are rather sketchy, and the book as a whole isn't anywhere as full-blooded and detailed as Friedman's work since 1980. Some of the concept warships in the last chapter are quite fascinating, especially the large hydrofoils and Surface Effect Ships, but the pre-AEGIS, pre-Falklands, publication date renders much of it rather dated. John Roberts' plans and drawings are decent, but his impressive talent as a draughtsman are largely wasted here.I'm a fan of Dr. Friedman, and tend to make excuses for some of his indulgences as a writer. However, I recommend this one for completionists only.
