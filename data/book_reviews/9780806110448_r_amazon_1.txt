This is a Non-Fiction Auto-Biography written by Tom Horn who was born November 21st 1860 in Memphis, Missouri on a family farm. Were he leaves as a young boy for the west and a life of adventure. He writes a fascinating narrative about himself from leaving the family with a good portrail of early western history that is not taught in our schools. The mere mention of Apache Indians usually makes most people think of wagon trains being attacked. But Tom Horn introduces the many sides and issues of the Native Americans of the South West both in Mexico and the USA, to his the final chapter of his life and hanging. He introduces the reader to the Apache Chiricahua Native Americans who roamed the deserts and mountains of the South West in Mexico and the USA.He [Tom Horn] then introduces us to Al Sieber a man who was respected by the Native Americans. Through Mr. Sieber's tutorage Tom Horn becomes well respected by the Native Americans too. He later goes into his years living with Apaches while learning their language, tracking skills, and ability to survive in such a harsh climate as the Deserts and Mountains of the South West adding onto the skills he was learning from Al Sieber. The author [Tom Horn] later explains the reasons for the last Chiricahua out brake from the "Indian" Reservation at San Carlos Arizona. And then the final capture and to the "civilizing" of Geronimo.The book then goes into Tom Horn's later adventures and then to the final chapter of his life in the Cattle Wars throughout Wyoming. Were he was hired by the "Cattle Barons" to stop the cattle rustlers. But as Tom Horn's methods begin to get noticed by the Government and the Press the Cattle Barons turn on Tom Horn. He was later hanged as a common criminal and many to this day still think he was either set up or mistaken for the charges against him. Tom Horn writes his book while in jail during the trail time and while waiting for his hanging on November 20th 1903.At the end of the book are many Letters and Statements of Character to and of Tom Horn during his Trial. Including one of note from his mentor Al Sieber who taught him the skills he used during the Cattle Wars while tracking down "rustlers" just as he had before with the Chiricahua Apaches.Over all I found this Book on Auto-Biography by Tom Horn to be a great book to own by anyone interested in Western Stories/Facts, the Outdoors, the West, Native Americans, and finally just a great book on American History. I learned many things about Native Americans and the History of the early west, more specifically the Apaches and the Chiricahua.
