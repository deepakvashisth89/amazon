The Perfect Storm by Sebastian Junger tells the story of an extremely ferocious storm in the North Atlantic Ocean, October 30-31, 1991. The common assessment of meteorologists is that this was the third worst storm in the North Atlantic during the entire twentieth century. The two worse storms in the North Atlantic were: (1) the September 21, 1938 storm, the "Great New England Hurricane," and (2) the August 19-20, 1991 storm, "Hurricane Bob." Thus, 2 of these 3 storms occurred in 1991.The author deserves credit for learning whatever he could about people who had been lost at sea and for interviewing survivors whose lives had been shattered by the catastrophe. In detail, he writes about (1) fishing for swordfish, (2) meteorology, and (3) the application of meteorology to weather prediction.In doing so, the author makes extensive use of maritime and nautical terms. The scientific data and vocabulary provide a context for the story. However, the same data and vocabulary may make it difficult and/or irritating for some readers to appreciate how the people involved in this story dealt with such a massive storm. Some readers may simply dislike this book because of the “data dump.”Before deciding to stop reading this book, I recommend reading two sections in particular: pages 153-163 and pages 180-201. Those sections elicit “page-turning while holding one’s breath.”The author uses an important acronym “PJ” but does not inform the reader of its meaning “pararescue jumper” until a couple paragraphs later. More significantly, throughout the book the author frequently uses the present tense while describing something that took place in the past. Most glaringly, the author fails very often to use quotation marks while actually quoting people.
