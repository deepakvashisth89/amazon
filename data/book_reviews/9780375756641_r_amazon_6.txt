This book was originally published in 1943; this edition, newly edited by the daughter of the author was published in 1988. Clementine is a delightful story of an American family in pre World War II France who hire a marvelous French cook, Clementine, and  learn about French cuisine from an amazing master of Burgundian cuisine. When they are called home to the Boston area, they sail home with their French Cordon Bleu  cook in tow. Clementine learns to find her desired ingredients in American supermarkets and butcher shops and with the help of her American employers, decent cheeses and cuts of particular meats; American and French butchers don't do the same kind of cuts. What I loved is Clementine's record keeping about menus and recipes so you can depend on following hers and managing to make a lovely dish.This edition includes the dry point illustrations of various illustrations from the original edition, and line drawing illustrations of the various characters in locales that are described in the book.
