Nancy Cartwright points out in her book that she has many of the advantages of celebrity without many of the disadvantages.  Even if you knew that Bart Simpson was voiced by a woman, you obviously wouldn't be able to pick her out of a crowd.  Cartwright brings this "stealth" celebrity to her autobiography "My Life as a 10-Year-Old Boy".  The "Boy" is, obviously, Bart Simpson.  This book is written for fans of the Simpsons, in that there is very little ground covered that does not involve the show.  There are anecdotes, technical details, and descriptions of the celebrity guests of the show's first several seasons (6 maybe?).It is in the personal vignettes that the book works best.  Stories about Tom Jones inviting Nancy onstage at his Vegas show; how she can't help but yell "Marty!" when she first meets Earnest Borgnine; Mel Gibson asking about a stage direction ("what's a 'Curly Nyuck'?"); the reaction at the show to news of Phil Hartman's murder.  There are no hidden skeletons, no National Enquirer moments for the tabloids - she is very respectful of her co- and guest stars, and presumably anyone she didn't like, she didn't write about.Unfortunately, there is a lot of technical detail thrown in that dilutes the fun and the impact of the book.  She talks a lot about the technical aspects of the show that are not directly her responsibility (about how the cartoons are inked, shipped to Korea, timelines on the rewrites, etc.).  All this information is available (and more entertaining) on the DVD extras of the show.  True, the information on the early session for the Tracy Ullman show are much more detailed, but most of the descriptions of the day-to-day production of the 1/2-hour show are uninteresting here.  I also would have liked to know more about Nancy's background.  She had extensive work in other cartoon series.  How did the average Saturday morning "My Little Pony" session compare to a "Simpsons" recording session?  What about commercial voice-overs?Some reviewers have claimed that the audio book is the way to go.  Although I haven't heard the audio book, it stands to reason that a voice-actor's narration of her own autobiography would be much more interesting than reading her words.  This might be especially true in this case, since the book has an almost amateur prose that sounds more like a transcript of a chat at the grocery store than an editted hard-cover book.  Some people will find this charming.  I do not.Overall, it's not a hard read, and you'll learn some new things.  You'll laugh in places.
