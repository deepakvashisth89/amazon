The basic theme of this book is that a group of space travelers return to Earth after a mission to another star system, and the world they encounter has changed dramatically from Earth as they remember it. While they were gone, about 80 years have gone by and society no longer values or even understands why we would travel in space.I thought this premise would make an interesting read, as it gets at what is best for society - to have a sense of exploration, adventure, and personal drive - along with violence and cruelty -  or to create a world of peace and calm (via medication) that also strips humans of the desire to do anything other than exist.The book is full of lengthy descriptions of the new Earth environment, but it is worded in a way that completely fails to leave the reader with an actual sense of what this world looks like. It is like a bunch of descriptive adjectives are strung together yet fail to help me "see" it. I kept reading hoping it would get better, but it never did. I did not have a real sense of the world and the people, and I never grew to care enough about the main character to have it matter to me what happened to him.I believe this book was written in the 1960's, so the most interesting part of this was that despite the author imagining a world with dissolving walls and spray-on clothing, he still had characters accessing telephones that were land lines (so he had not imagined anything like a cell phone), and he pictured books being available as crystals that are obtained by going to a library and checking them out and having them put on a reading device - so there was no idea of a Kindle capable of loading a book from any location.All in all, though, I was extremely disappointed in this book, and would consider it one of the worst books I have read in a long time.
