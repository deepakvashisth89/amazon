Polly dies in childbirth giving life to her son Tip. As a ghost she is able to follow Tip as he grows older. I loved the story's originality and I thought Abby's prose was rich and so eloquent. I believe many of the reasons I was so impressed with the author's writing style was because I'm intimately familiar with the location where the story takes place. For over half a century I've visited Oshkosh, Wisconsin on Lake Winnebago--every year and many times two and three times a year. Places like Asylum Point, George's Gaslight Inn, and  the old "defunct lighthouse", even the tiny island where the story opens, I've been to. So the novel was a fun read for me. I was especially enthralled with some of the descriptions and phrases--gauging temperatures by the chirruping crickets and the "tadpole in the biologists mouth" are just two examples.
