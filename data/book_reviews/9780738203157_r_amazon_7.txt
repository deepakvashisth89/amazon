This book has an enormous quality, which is to propose a convincing alternative approach to violence. To say it grossly, he does nors believe that violence is a social construction or a by-product of our culture, but that it is rather innate and that it developed out of natural selection.I find it rather convinving, especially because many of our violent features also exist in other primat species such as chimps.But :- Ghiglieri should not start and end each chapter with an anecdot. These little stories are of no use. They would be better in a trash magazine than in a self-pretending scientific book.- Ghiglieri does not make all the necessary conclusions from his analysis. His one and only answer to violence appears to be more repression and more jail. It is true that impunity is a major factor of violence, but one of the most interesting aspects of this book is that it emphasizes n the social status of criminals.As an example, when Ghiglieri deals with rape, he founds out that rapers are, most of the times, "losers" from an economic and social viewpoint. I am not trying to excuse rapers, but we could start from this fact to try and see if there is any correlation between inequality in a given society on one side, and the level of violence of the same society on the other side. I would not be surprised to learn that the more homogeneous the society, the less violent the people. So, along with a more efficient repression, maybe we should try to reduce inequalities so as to diminish one important factor of violence. I regret that this hypothesis is not at all addressed in the book.
