As an old school 1st/2nd ed. AD & D gamer who likes low-level, low-powered characters in a game that's closer to realism than to high fantasy, I found this to be a great addition to my game.  (Some of the aspects of it I will never use, only because I've never been involved in siege warfare or anything high-level like that.) Player's Option: Combat & Tactics adds several different elements to the core rules, all seamlessly optional and designed to be cherry-picked without unbalancing the game.1: Rules for miniatures combatThis seems like a pretty streamlined way to handle tabletop combat within a traditional RPG setting.  Personally, I feel too old to want to change the feel of my game that much, but if I were still 15 and picking this up now (instead of 37, with much less time to play games), I would definitely bring that element in to clarify combat without unnecessarily complicating it.  None of the rest of the rules rely on adopting this, though, and the book is very consistent about providing rules for miniature or non-miniature play.2: More combat maneuvers à la Complete Fighter's HandbookThis is what I was hoping to find, and it came in spades.  Tactics, attack maneuvers, trapping and breaking weapons, fighting styles, unarmed combat (for martial arts, though, I'd recommend the rules in the Complete Ninja's Handbook, as cheesy as that book sounds), and new dueling rules ... all good stuff, all usable à la carte.  You can play with new people or less creative fighters who just want to hack and slash, all the while trying more robust techniques of parrying, sapping, disarming, shield bashing, etc.3: More types of weapons and weapon proficienciesWeapon expertise, specialization, mastery, grand mastery, and all of the stuff from the CFH (which, if you own Combat & Tactics, becomes reduced to the kits, since it's all here now).  Historical groupings of equipment, in tables, with significantly new things added.  I'm kind of an equipment junkie, so I loved this.  All weapons now also come with an optional knockdown value, for the chance to knock down opponents, depending on their size.  Going to try those rules out soon. Some pretty interesting new Nonweapon Proficiencies added as well, such as Iron Will, Fine Balance, Ambush, Steady Hand, and others in that vein.Table of Contents:1 - Player's Option Combat System2 - Combat Options3 - The Battlefield4 - Weapon Specialization & Mastery5 - Unarmed Combat6 - Critical Hits7 - Weapons & Armor8 - Siege Warfare9 - MonstersUnfortunately, most of the art in this book is particularly bad.  One decent painting, plus the cover, and the rest is crappy line art, often with a chumpy comical tone.
