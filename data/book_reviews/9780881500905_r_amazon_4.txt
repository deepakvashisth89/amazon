On the surface, it seems as though nothing but good times have come to Weesit, Ms. Taylor's fictitious Cape Cod town.  Phineas Banbury, a successful Midwestern business man with a national chain of bakeries, has moved into the old Bog House with his wife Lu and daughter Jane.  They renovate the place, which had fallen into disrepair.  Phineas then uses his new-found pull to make some improvements throughout the community:  traffic lights where needed; new sidewalks, trash cans and signs; a new whaling museum; a new yacht club; and the reopening of The Weesit Inn and the Weesit Country Club.  Yes, Weesit has come to life and is bustling again, with many thanks attributed to the Banburys.  Until Phineas and Lu bake and serve some of their famous tarts at a church function, and 200 people get sick from arsenic poisoning.  Who could have tampered with the Banbury tarts?  Is someone less than thrilled with the newcomers' contributions to the community?  Almost simultaneously, Abner Grove, one of the Weesit selectmen, is found dead in the Banbury Welcome Wagon.  Somehow, he'd been drowned and then propped back up in the driver's seat.  What in the world happened to him?Avid mystery readers won't be surprised when the local authorities come to the wrong conclusion and arrest Phineas Banbury himself for both crimes.  Why would the man poison his own product and then kill his closest contact with town government?  Local amateur detective Asey Mayo knows just how ridiculous that assumption is.  So he of course begins his own investigation into the situation.Soon it becomes obvious that someone's looking for something, and Asey keeps getting in the person's way.  Eventually Asey figures out that the murderer is looking for Abner's gold watch, which was not found on his body.  But why?  Then Phineas somehow escapes from the jail, which adds to the confusion.  And everything is made more difficult by the arrival of a real Cape Cod "Stayer," a thick fog that settles in to hamper and dampen the day.  It adds further surrealism to the town atmosphere.And as usual in any kind of plot, Perception is everything.  Even Mary, Tabitha Sparrow's cook, knows how important it is.  "Asey, doesn't it beat all, what people think, and why they think it?"  Yep.  And that's the key to solving this mystery.
