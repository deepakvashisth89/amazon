For me, this was one of those rare discoveries: a title that's been around for decades by an author I'd long heard of but never sampled, featuring delightful characters, both credible and surprising.The main character's evolution was, in fact, so surprising that upon finishing it I am rereading this novel to fully appreciate how Powers prepared me for his finale while distracting me with humor. The ending was unexpected and true, and completely satisfying.Maybe it's just me, but I'd avoided this book for years mainly due to its odd, off-putting title...about a death (when I prefer to read of a life). And was Urban a person or a landscape?Urban is the main character, a priest, who has to die to himself to be the human he was meant to be. (Don't we all?) Father Urban is so witty, smart, and easy to identify with, that he seems solid from the git-go. To say more gets into Spoiler Alert territory, so I won't. A worthy winner of the National Book Award in 1963, this novel needs to be read, not read about. Enjoy.Read moreRead less
