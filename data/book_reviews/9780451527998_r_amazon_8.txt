This action classic, which I think I first read 60 years ago, still packs a punch on re-reading.  It has thrilling accounts of individual combat, medieval dress and manners, heroes, villains, fools, serfs, clerics, religious beliefs, superstition, dungeons and castles.  One could argue the title, but since Daphne du Maurier subsequently co-opted the heroine’s name for his best-seller, ‘Ivanhoe’ serves just as well.  Sir Walter Scott uses calculations regarding the relative value of tangibles and intangibles, which include ransom, vows, armor, chivalry, land, honor, power, religion, provender and eternal salvation, to characterize the individuals and groups involved—all of whom vary in the degree to which they are willing to risk and undergo risks for them.  The book ends happily for those engaged by romance; the remaining character, who from necessity penetrates the illusions of the age, ultimately finds in those dark times, a refuge in a more tolerant society.
