The author deliberately chose to write in the voice of a Deaf man, Laurent Clerc, who was educated in France and then emigrated with Thomas Gallaudet to America to found institutions for deaf education in the US in the early-mid 1800s. The introduction explains this choice and does an excellent job of setting up the scene so to speak. When we enter Clerc's experience in Chapter 1, the book reads almost like a novel rather than a history. It does get a little confusing when he divides chapters by person being described rather than directly chronologically but once you understand that, it isn't distracting anymore. He provides fully drawn and realized visions of other famous educators of the deaf in France and the US such as Sicaud, l'Epee, and Gallaudet - the book reads like a directory of famous people you've never heard of because you aren't deaf.Furthermore, this is a fascinating and necessary introduction to the history of the Deaf which has been sadly ignored for many years.
