This book is an excellent read. Many of us are acquainted with the work of Dian Fossey through the film Gorillas in the Mist, but, as so often happens when Hollywood becomes involved, the real story is far removed from that of the big screen. Ms Fossey is a complex character to understand and Harold Hayes interviewed many people who interacted with her. The person revealed by these interviewees is far removed from the portrayal on screen by Sigourney Weaver.Ms Fossey became totally obsessed with the gorillas that she was supposed to be scientifically aloof from, and regarded anyone who visited her remote research station with hostility and suspicion. In the latter years of her stay in Rwanda she was hermit like and was continually at odds with her sponsors, Government officials and her own staff. All of this is revealed in meticulous detail in this book, but it does not become bogged down in minutiae but cracks on at a brisk pace.The book starts with the details of her murder and then looks back at her life through the many interviews that the author conducted.Whatever the circumstances of her death and the way that she treated people it cannot be denied that she succeeded in halting the decline in numbers of the endangered gorillas on "her" mountain . This book is a serious work that is extremely well written and gives the reader an insight into the mind of one of the truly great naturalists of the 20th century.If you are looking for a biography that is also a murder mystery then this is definitely the book for you.Read moreRead less
