Yeah, this one's still a barn-burner of a page-turner and a half. As a work of influential Gothic horror literature (and it's certainly a horror novel), it's fascinating to revisit, as its own object, a text that stands in stark contrast to how it's come to live in the collective memory, in relation to adaptations/Rice's own ret-cons in how she later developed her vampire mythos/what evolved/devolved out of its genre influence/etc. Hits differently for sure, profoundly more bleak, psychologically disturbing, and all around more harrowing than I remembered it from the last time I read it over twenty years ago. Much like the interviewer's response at the end, every "paranormal romance" vampire novel since somehow both missed the point yet is all too understandable/relatable. Don't call this a "paranormal romance", for Louis and Lestat (the original L&L, as we meet them here) are many things, but a "love story" they ain't! And literally everything to do with Claudia is especially skin-crawly. Rice once said of it, "It's not just about vampires. It's about us."
