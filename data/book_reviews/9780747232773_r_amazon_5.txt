As with all her many later books, this very early work by Edith Pargeter is beautifully written, almost poetically in places.Perhaps a little too slowly as she describes the boredom on the WRNS work amongst the bombing at home.But in the final pages, where in the words of letters purportedly received from her lover fighting and retreating from Greece and expecting to die during the evacuation of Crete, Pargeter describes the brutality of war and “stupidity and arrogance and lack of imagination of our own strategy”.These descriptions of the fighting in Greece and then Crete in 1941 are remarkable. Places that were never immortalised in war films or later books. Fighting that I’d never known about.Yet this was written contemporaneously with those events and published in 1942.It was very brave, I think, at that time to express thoughts such as: “To die in front of the enemy’s guns is war, but to die strangled by the ideas of your own leaders is murder”  or:“It was not we who failed Britain; it was she - some rottenness in the present form of her - that failed us. And we, who make up the sound heart of her, how can we forgive those who cheapened and despoiled our enthusiasm, and squandered her proper splendour and our lives, which are her life-blood?”Read moreRead less
