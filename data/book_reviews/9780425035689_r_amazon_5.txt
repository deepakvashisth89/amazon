This book deals with race from the perspective of the 50s (at best). It also has an unflattering view of the traditional place of women. Be warned: these are now very old attitudes.Our typical Heinlein hero, a sane, practical man, finds his home full of people just as the Bomb hits. Fortunately, he has a shelter ready. Unfortunately, the third bomb manages to project it a long time into the future.Long enough for the land to have completely recovered; indeed, to them it is as if it were an alternate world in which Man had never set foot. The first half of the book details how they work together to survive.But this is our world, and they are trespassing on a Great Lord's private property. Fortunately, their servant not only knows French but is African-American and so can talk to the Chosen person in charge, who knows the long-dead language French because he is a scholar.The society is basically the inverse of our own. How the mother adapts, and how the son is treated given his very open racism, is a major part of the book. But the fate of the hero, his new wife, and their sons is what leads to the end of the book.This is the second time I have read it, but the first time was so long ago that I only recognized bits and pieces of it. It was as if I were reading it again for the very first time.I would recommend this to anyone who likes Heinlein, especially the early Heinlein.
