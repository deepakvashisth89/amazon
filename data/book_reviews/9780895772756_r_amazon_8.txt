Huckleberry Finn was one of my first "real" books and it has remained a favorite. Life on the Mississippi is a biographical work and  provides a fascinating background for the writing of Mark Twain's classic. He describes with affection the challenge of learning the trade of river-boat pilot in the 1850's, with many an incident that had me guffawing out loud! He then goes on to reflect on the changes he observed, on re-visiting the river post-Civil War, including the near disappearance of the river-boat pilots, driven out of business by the steam train and by their own short-sighted arrogance. Twain leaves no doubts about his own views on "Southern chivalry" and the pretentiousness and hypocrisy which characterized it.I read Life on the Mississippi in the course of an ocean voyage and I looked forward to every page of it.
