This novel kept me from reading so much of the daily news for which I'm eternally grateful to the author. The daily news is bad enough but during this COVID-19 lockdown, it's too disheartening.What makes this story of mutual redemption interesting is Alexis Harrington's attention to the thinking process of each character.  I've always been interested in how real people think and I've learned more about it in novels like these than the dark, tragic novels that we were forced to read, study, and take exams on, in school.Speaking of school, the typos were a definite drawback because the double words leftover after changing the structure of a sentence dragged my mind's eye back to the words on the page. All good readers know this; your eyes "see" and comprehend the words but your imagination provides the "movie" in your mind and typos interrupt the movie. Not only the same word doubled, but the wrong homophones such as, "your" and "you're." So many reviewers of so many novels by different authors have complained about typographical errors that you'd think the publishers could pay people to read the final draft and check for typos, grammatical mistakes, and continuity.Getting back to the story, I found the added "trial" at the end a nice twist. However, the easily made confession was less satisfying than seeing the deceitfullness of the antagonist recounted and laid bare. I don't want to spoil the ending for those who are reading this to see if they  want to buy the book.One other thing bothered me and it related  to the "suspension of disbelief." Where did their income come from? A farmer working alone would not have left his two daughters with enough money to live on for over three years without making money on their own. If they had lots of beehives and sold honey all over the territory, the novel would have been more believable, especially when the other sister had money to spend. Where did she get it?I'm only mentioning this as a way to help the author with future books.If you've read all of this, you WILL enjoy, "Allie's Moon."Read moreRead less
