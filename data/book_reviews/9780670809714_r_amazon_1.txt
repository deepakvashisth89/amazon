I have really enjoyed some of A. N. Wilson's novels and was pleased to discover that more have become available on Kindle.This is a story of life in the upper classes in late-nineteenth-century England. The main characters include Maudie Nettleship, a teenaged girl beloved by all who know her; Maudie's father, Professor Nettleship, whose Christian faith was destroyed by his study of geology; Maudie's mother, Charlotte, who never speaks directly to her husband, except when putting on a pretense of normality in front of others; Maudie's brother, Lionel, who is away at Oxford and has decided he wants to be a priest; a young painter who can't entirely give up the life of a gentleman to devote himself to his art; and the Nettleships' worldly and affable though excessively talkative family friend Mr. Chatterway, who has returned to England after many years on the Continent.This is not one of my favorite novels by Wilson--in particular, there wasn't much of an ending, and a misunderstanding at the heart of the plot was drawn out for too long for my taste--but the story was diverting enough, and I always enjoy Wilson's writing style and his observations about faith and about adulthood.I'd recommend Wilson's "The Vicar of Sorrows," "Incline our Hearts," or "The Healing Art" (all set in the twentieth century) over "Gentlemen in England."Read moreRead less
