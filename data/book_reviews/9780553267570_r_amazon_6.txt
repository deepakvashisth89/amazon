Disclaimer.  I've read all Le Carré's novels, the first 'The Looking Glass War' when I was in junior high school all those years ago in the late 60s.  I recently reread it and, in spite of negative reviews, it holds up in my opinion.  I had started 'The Little Drummer Girl' several times and could not slog through it.  Perhaps, too much interior dialogue and not enough action...unsure, but one doesn't read Le Carré for plot and action; rather, it's the dialogue and acute descriptions of events and the times.  He captures it completely.  So, I finally bought the Kindle version and decided to read it.  That was some time ago, and I had several false starts.  While on vacation, I made the commitment and just finished it.Several comments.  For the patient reader there are so many rewarding moments of recognition - foreshadowing, isn't that the literary word?  And, there were passages of sheer intensity, edge-of-the-seat tension.  Most Le Carré's books end in a climatic crescendo with the protagonist usually suffering some fateful consequence.  This ending reminded me, dare I say it, of Charles Dickens' 'Great Expectations' where there are two endings, one with no hope for Pip and Estella getting together and the other where Dickens was forced to rewrite a new ending leaving hope of a future reconciliation.  Charlie and Joseph together.  Lastly, of note, all the characters, whether major or minor, are drawn in such fine detail that I know them.I've often wondered and asked myself who the next Le Carré is.  I've always been a fan of spy fiction, and I'm usually disappointed with most offerings - more thrillers than literature.  Over the years, I read hundreds and there are some good series but Le Carré remains at the top, to be copied but never surpassed.  I recently discovered the Slough House Series by Mick Herron, which I highly recommend.  Herron is a good writer who can capture a moment and his stories are taunt with twists and turns.  It's not Le Carré  but I highly recommend this new talent.Read moreRead less
