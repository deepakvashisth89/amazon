Here's an interesting book to both assure and inspire those of us who struggle with the confines imposed by traditional Society, including the 9-to-5 job.Brooke Williams and his wife, writer Terry Tempest Williams, seemed to be on their way to realizing the stereotypical American dream after they got married in 1975.  They'd both grown up in Salt Lake City and in Mormon families.  Brooke was even a descendent of Brigham Young.  Generations of Williams men had developed a successful plumbing business, and it was assumed that Brooke would follow in those footsteps.  The trouble was, he hated the desk job and the field calls.  He would rather have been hiking or running or exploring the natural world "out in the field" than selling or repairing fixtures, no matter how secure the job was or how much he got paid.  Family, work, and church became a tangled knot with interwoven demands and expectations.  Finally in 1989, Brooke made the decision to quit the business and proposed a three-year transition period to train his successor.  By 1992, he was out completely, and ready for the next stage of life.  What would it be?We follow Brooke throughout this process -- mentally and physically -- as he figures out an alternative and carves his own niche.  The issues here are not only Work versus Wildness, but also Organized Religion versus Spirituality for both Brooke and Terry, and are always set against the backdrop of the American West.  Without ever mentioning the man himself, the memoir embodies a process that Henry David Thoreau might have approved of.
