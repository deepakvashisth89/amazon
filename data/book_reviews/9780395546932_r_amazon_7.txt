This is a personal story of one leaders life that was easy reading and that I enjoyed.This is not a history or an analysis of the 1989 Tiananmen Square Protests and subsequent massacre.  It is a rather standard autobiography told in strict chronological order.  The first part deals with life growing up in China in the 1970s and 1980s, including the struggles that his college-educated parents went through as seen by a young boy.  The second part is Shen Tong's life at university, especially his first steps into political activism, his steps out of activism, and then his full involvement in the Tiananmen Square protests, ending with his eyewitness accounts of the massacre and an epilogue about the few days leading to his escape from China.  There is nothing about his experience in exile.I enjoyed both parts. The first was a glimpse of one's person's boyhood in Beijing after the Cultural Revolution but before the recent economic expansion. What did a boy think about the embalmed body of Mao lying on display?  How did he date?  How did he study for college entrance exams?  For this very casual China watcher, I gleamed new insights.  The second part about how he became a student activist in college and his experience and frustrations led to me thinking about my own involving in activism.  Having watched the protests and massacre unfold on my TV in Minnesota, I learned more about how it looked from someone on the ground with access to the different meetings and venues.  What went through his head? If I was there, would my reactions be the same?Neither part was very different from other autobiographies that I have read.  It was about average for the genre.  There never is and there was not here much detailed analysis of his actions or the events in question.  Shen Tong is just relating his experiences as he remembers them.  He has left it for others to write the histories and analyses. I would have given it 3-stars but world-significant topic -- the Tiananmen Square Protests and massacre -- earns it another star.If you want a history or an analysis of the Tiananmen Square Protests and subsequent massacre, read another book.
