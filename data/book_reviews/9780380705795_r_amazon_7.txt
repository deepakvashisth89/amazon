This is a collection of 13 problems, all brought to a satisfactory conclusion by Albert Campion, with the aid of 'others', ranging from Supt Oates, through a former police officer now turned hotel security, to a pick-pocket; the network of Mr Campion's friends is cast over a broad spectrum of society. Mr Campion is now middle-aged, but still attended by his 'man', although Lugg's name is never mentioned. The beautiful, thinking-themselves-sophisticated young women and the young and earnest young men who require his assistance are a generation removed from Albert's own youth, although lots of old, familiar names appear - but not Amanda's.  The stories are very clever - although there is an amazing amount of gullability amongst the young (but isn't there still?) - and Mr Campion's deductive powers and observational skills are as acute as ever behind his spectacles. This is an enjoyable wander back into what is called the 'Golden Age' of detective fiction, where people may be blackmailed, kidnapped, hit on the head, or simply defrauded in one way or another, but without the need for graphic descriptions, or blood and gore.
