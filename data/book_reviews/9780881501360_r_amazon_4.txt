It's 1940, the world is at war, and the women of Cape Cod are ready.  They've organized a branch of the Woman's League to Defend America at all Costs with Action.  They've scoured the neighborhoods and have armed themselves with as many guns as they could borrow.  The trouble is, very few of them know how to correctly fire the weapons.  And before Asey Mayo can turn around once or twice, someone has accidentally taken a few pot shots at him.The group is led by Mrs. "Picklepuss" Belcher.  Oh, that's just what people call her behind her back, since she runs her own pickle business.  Her real first name is a diminutive based on the last syllable of that nickname, and it's a word that is no longer acceptable when used in polite 21st century conversation, even if you add "cat" to the end of it.  So I won't repeat it here, even though it's mentioned numerous times in the book.In any event:  Asey has other business to attend to.  He's got an appointment to meet with Commodore Humphrey Bunting at the Sketicket Bay Yacht Club, to discuss the club's finances.  Some things just don't add up.  But before he can iron out those differences, Asey's attention is drawn to Miss Lucia Newell, who's sitting on the private beach under a nice big umbrella.  She's dead.  Poisoned.  And just that morning, she'd called and left a message for Asey, claiming that she was in "dire peril."  Usually that was her way of saying that something was wrong with her car, and that she wanted Asey to fix it.  But now that she's dead and her car checks out fine, Asey's not so sure that Lucia didn't have some important information to pass along to him instead.  It's possible that someone wanted to stop her before she could convey it.  And if any of that is true, then maybe those pot shots aimed at Asey weren't coming from a member of Mrs. Belcher's homeland security squad after all.While the noted sleuth is aided in his subsequent investigation by his old pal Doc Cummings, Asey is also getting some advice from Ross Ward, a sardonic journalist who's in town to write pieces about the "quaint Cape."  Both men are annoyed by the ranting and ravings of Lucia's overweight and overbearing cousin, Dudley French, another member of the club.  Also intrusive is the war news that emanates from just about every radio.  It gets to be too much after a while, all that talk of enemy ships and submarines and such.  Asey can't be bothered listening to that; he's got a murder to solve.  Ward becomes so impressed with his new friend's abilities that he calls him "Yogi Mayo.  Sees all, knows all, figgers the rest." (p. 192)  Can Asey really determine the murderer's identity, with or without the help of the armed women running around the town?An interesting episode in the series, in which 98% of the storyline takes place in just one 24-hour period.
