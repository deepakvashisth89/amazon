God's Bits of Wood is a historical novel depicting the individual struggle of many people during the infamous Railroad Strike of 1947.  The story revolves around Ibrahim Bakayoko, although he does not appear in person until the second half of the book.  He is a railroad conductor who leads the strike with his intelligence and courage.  All of the characters are somehow affected by Bakayoko.  His spirit is reflected in all of the African characters.Sembene's novel is not a historical account.  He is not even a historian; however, his narrative lies on a deep foundation of historical events.  Sembene is deeply versed in labor history, being an activist throughout his life, and God's Bits of Wood explores the rhetoric of Marxism and Communism through his characters.  Sembene could be called the Herodotus of Post-World War II Africa.  He tells a rich and detailed narrative of a historical event.God's Bits of Wood is full of symbols and metaphors.  Some are superficial like Bakayoko being a train conductor while also leading the strike.  Others are more subtle.  For example, the watchman, Sounkare, is an old man who remembers when the repair machines were not available.  Upon their installation, he knew that he had become obsolete.  He later died a gruesome death when he fell off a balcony and was eaten by rats.Gods Bits of Wood has a huge amount of characters, each with their own values and stories.  Their stories often overlap as they fight French oppression.  Sembene's characters are courageous and unique.  The use of several points of view gives a vivid image of Africa post World War II.
