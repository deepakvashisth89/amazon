I have now read 4 of James Carroll's books.  I would rate "The Cloister" and "Warburg in Rome" as the best of them and "Secret Father" on par with "Memorial Bridge".  Carroll is generally strong on plot and historical setting and he's an expert on the inner monologues and motivations of his Catholic characters.  I believe this book is partially semi autobiographical drawing on the career of his father and some of his potential relationship with him.The story is best when tracking the ascending career of Sean Dillon from working in the meat packing business in pre WWII Chicago while attending law school at night to a career in prosecution and law enforcement and military intelligence.  It's much weaker when describing his relationship with his wife Cass and the conflicts and relationship with is son is germaine to the plot but isn't particularly deep or credibleThis is consistent with all of Carroll's books which are quite clunky and awkward as he attempts to portray intimacy and as he strays from the main characters there is weak definition.  Frankly I wish he'd give on intimacy as it generally adds little to his stories which are otherwise filled with conflict, passion and explore topics unfound elsewhere.I skip over the love scenes and enjoy the story.Read moreRead less
