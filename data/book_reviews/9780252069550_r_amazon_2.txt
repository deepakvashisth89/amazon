As a woman I found parts of this novel difficult to enjoy. I was expecting a science fiction novel similar to H.G. Wells', but the focus of this story is not nearly so much on the gadgets (though they are described in fascinating detail) as it is on their impact on humans. And since the biggest gadget in the book is a robotic woman ("Android"), the majority of the philosophical and sociological content is about love, sex, and gender. To dismiss the material as simply sexist and wrong would be a discredit to the author's insight, but it would be an understatement to say that his point of view conflicts with modern opinion. L'Eve Future might never be widely read for this reason alone. For those who are willing to risk being a little offended, however, I would certainly recommend this - it is a story of a noble man of society and a hero of science, and their ambitious common goal.The translation was decent, though it was a little awkward sometimes. Still, for a first English edition I was pleased. The descriptions were long and detailed enough to be engrossing, but not so long as to be tedious. The dialogue was okay; the characters' reactions to one another were the most poignant part of the book. The story as a whole was interesting and suspenseful, and I often picked this up when I had planned on doing something else.
