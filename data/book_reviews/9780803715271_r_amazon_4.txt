My title will irk some. Better than 'Little House on the Prairie' books? While I believe most of the classics by L.I. Wilder are worthwhile reading, I see 'Bound for Oregon' as a step-up.  The story sometimes has a 'Little House' flavor to it (it was written for around the same era), but the characters are stronger, sometimes more believable than Mrs. Wilder's. The faith of the Todd family is a step up too. These people were human and had their failings; but their Christianity affected their everyday lives. They weren't just 'Sunday Christians.'  It is a step-up in who it is written for, as well. Young people up to about 13 or 14 would find this an interesting read. 'Bound for Oregon' is excellent for home schoolers who need a story of the mid-19th century to accompany their studies of the Westerward Movement. It is excellent for Christian schools for the same reason. For young people attending public schools...it probably wouldn't be used there because of its distinctive Christian flavor...but perhaps a young person would like to do a book report on it.
