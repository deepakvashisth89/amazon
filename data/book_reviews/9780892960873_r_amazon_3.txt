Beautiful Outlaw made me realize that my life had been filled with a very sneaky idolatry. Jesus said "I am the vine; you are the branches. If a man remains in me and I in him, he will bear much fruit; apart from me you can do nothing." For most of my Christian life, my main goal has been to bear fruit. It wasn't until reading this book that I realized my main focus should be abiding in Christ. Bearing fruit is important, but is not the main focus. This would be like skydiving and focusing mainly on landing- the first and primary focus should be deploying the parachute. Once you get that going, landing becomes much easier.Elderedge does at times make some illogical claims unsupported by the Bible. They are nothing I would label as necessarily wrong, but they are assumptions made without evidence. One example is where he assuming an interesting mindset of Jesus relating to the rich man on p 103. He also uses the example of a seminary graduate who lost his faith and an attendee of Christian school who hated the Bible to claim that these institutions are negative by saying "you shall know them by their fruits." (p169) Maybe this is more true than I realize, but using an example of one person is not enough to throw out an entire institution. Also on p 170 he makes the claim that "religious talk is an immediate sign of religious infection." I see nothing wrong with using the words "quiet time" or "Praise the Lord." I do agree that around non-believers we should not annoyingly throw out Christian jargon all of the time, but it seems like he says we should not use them at all. I am confused by his heart on this issue.Do not let these mistakes keep you from the book though- I simply pointed these out to encourage readers to carefully analyze what he says. Remember that if a point is not Biblically supported it is not authoritative, and if it is not Biblically or evidently supported, it is just an opinion.Reading this book has opened my eyes to some lies I had been believing, and more importantly it has further opened my eyes to the beauty of Jesus Christ.
