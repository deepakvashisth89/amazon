I picked up Gravitation as a tween and through time it has never stopped being one of my all-time favorite manga. Despite lacking the more serious romantic style of similar titles (FAKE, etc), Gravitation is an extremely enjoyable read, and I can honestly say I've never come across anything quite like it. The author, Murakami, clearly does not take herself too seriously, and her personality comes through in her work. Our protagonist, Shuichi, is a great kid, really; he's got passion, guts, ambition, and absolutely no rational brainparts to speak of. The storyline hurtles along at breakneck speed, too fast to let you catch your breath, or tear your eyes away from the pages. Every chapter brings ridiculous new ups, downs, and corkscrews that alternately drop your jaw and make you fall out of your chair laughing.The actual artwork is decent; it's kind of ugly, out-dated and 90's-looking in the first few volumes, but eventually transforms into something sleek, glossy, and sexy by volume 7, with volumes 4-6 being a slightly awkward in-between as Murakami gets her bearings and tries to figure out her own style.Murakami's lack of planning may have somewhat flawed an otherwise awesome 12 volumes, but like her protagonist, she's got balls and she makes it work on the fly. The storyline is 90% coherent, with a colorful and quirky supporting cast. There's K, the gun-toting American; Sakano, the uber-polite, high strung Japanese producer; Tohma, the feminine-looking boss who smiles serenely while pushing enemies in front of moving cars...The list goes on. Every character adds another touch of insanity to the story, in the best way possible. Gravitation is full of hilarious gags, crazy love shenanigans, bazookas, and giant robot battle pandas thrown in along the way.Although I love Gravitation primarily for its humor (it really makes me laugh until I suffocate, it's that good), there is some seriousness and plot woven in. The story isn't going to win any laurels for depth, and the characters don't get huge amounts of development (other than Eiri Yuki, who has a dark past, and lots of psychological problems that he works through), yet Gravitation still makes me want to dance with girlish joy.This is a manga I would recommend to anybody with an open mind, not just BL-fans and slashers. The sexual aspect of Eiri and Shuichi's relationship is sidelined quickly, discarded in favor of focusing on Shuichi's career and Eiri's inner demons. Ultimately, Gravitation's appeal lies not in a great romantic tale, but in sheer, unapologetic, fantastic entertainment that doesn't pretend to be anything more than what it is.
