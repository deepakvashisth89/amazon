Spoilers ahead!I just can't make it through this book. It keeps making me sick to my stomach. It seemed good at first--it felt like some kind of fantasy style Little House on the Prairie with fantasy flavored domestic type drama. The writing sucked on the basis of craploads of exposition and VERY FEW scenes! The story was told in summary for the first quarter, though I forgave it that. But it's first major twist was awful! I was horrified to find that she had to go to the mortal realm to act like a slut to concieve elven children, but I was still willing to hear the story out. I really wanted the story to redeem itself somehow. Here's my problem: I loved Aulaudin, Jerlayne's husband, I thought he was hot and was looking forward to some relationship development and a nice good first love making scene between them. I was disappointed by the rush of all that--but that's not what the book was about, was it? If someone out there reading this would like to write a fantasy-homestead-drama, I'll gladly give it a try. Anyway, I never liked Jerlayne, I thought she was a brat on page one. A brat who CHOSE Aulaudin for her husband and got him pretty easily. Then we get the nauseating story twist and I'd be with her if she were angry and unwilling for a few chapters, or at least pages. But boy how fast she got infatuated with some random gross prostitute! He was only, like, the SECOND man she saw since entering the mortal realm! After what? Almost 100 years of being married to the "love of her life?" There was absolutely no guilt apparent there and if there was mention of guilt, it didn't convince me. She even snuck back into the mortal realm to screw the guy TWICE! Nonetheless, I kept reading because that, at least, was necessary for the survival of her species. I kept reading because I wanted to see the lies all come tumbling out and I wanted some relationship development with Aulaudin. She barely interacts with him throughout this book. I was hoping he'd be part of the plot development, but we barely see him and never get any hints of what he knows (keep in mind I only read to the start of the last quarter). I wanted deeper conversations with him and didn't get them. ...And then she screws the goblin, pretty randomly. I'm done. This is just nasty. I want to take a shower. I want to puke.If you like stories about spoiled little brats who cheat on their husbands la-dee-da, and books with serious mechanical issues, then look no further. Ugh. Let me tell ya, I've read some horrible books in my day, but I've never read one that's made me so angry--and ill! Congrats, Abbey.Further thoughts: I don't want you all to get the wrong impression about me: I do like fantasy that is dark and dirty, but there's something definitely wrong with this book and I've pondered it a little bit since I read this. I did not finish reading it--I couldn't because it made me sick. With this book, I may be a victim of "uncategorizable genre," the cover looked like a fantasy romance and the back cover blurb sounded like epic fantasy--both of which are good. I tend to avoid fantasy worlds that cross with the real world--I simply don't like that, and I can't remember if the blurb hints at a cross-worlds theme. Maybe it did and I did not notice when I picked it up in a used book store.My best guess as to what frustrated me so badly was the development of the character, Jerlayne. I don't recall much talk as to why she was sleeping with these other people. Some books' characters are loose, that's fine, I get it, but I hated this one for some reason, possibly because there was a lack of communication on the character from the author. Her tryst with the goblin came out of nowhere and it threw gasoline on the frustration I was already feeling. Without the much needed communication as to why Jerlayne acted the way she did, it was extremely painful to have to watch those actions happen, especially after I had invested my time and interest in her relationship with her "hot" husband lol. I just didn't get what I was reading.After the lack of communication, one of the other major pitfalls, to me, was the book's number 1 theme: secrets. Abbey goes far out of her way to tell us about the secrets and I just can't wrap my head around the "why?" There seemed to be no point in these secrets. This is what makes me wish I could hear a full book report on this story and why the heck it was written! Everyone in this fantasy world is well aware of the secrets they all keep from each other and for some reason that's ok with everyone (except Jerlayne)? It just doesn't make sense. This thing with the secrets seems like a forced mechanic. But, without all these layered secrets the book wouldn't have a plot, I guess. A book needs a plot. :/This book was bad.Read moreRead less
