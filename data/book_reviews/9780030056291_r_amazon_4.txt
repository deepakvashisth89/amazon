The 16th in the Henry Tibbett series features his lovely wife, Emmy, in a supporting role. Tibbett is a Chief Superintendent of Scotland Yard, and the story begins with Tibbett receiving a mysterious crossword puzzle with some very peculiar cues. Not being a crossword enthusiast himself, Tibbett turns to his friend the Bishop, a crossword fanatic, for help. When the solved puzzle proves to be the names of persons involved in old deaths--deaths from which they personally profited--Tibbett begins to see a link between the anonymous puzzle and a talk he is scheduled to give to a group of murder mystery writers. Are they trying to test his mettle? Or is someone trying to point to some murders that have gone unsolved?This book was very cleverly plotted, the clues and characters intricately placed to confuse and intrigue the reader. I do think the book bogged down in the middle and was a bit padded--a good editor could have tightened this up and made it a better read. But if one is looking for a plot with twists and double-back turns, Moyes certainly delivers.Read moreRead less
