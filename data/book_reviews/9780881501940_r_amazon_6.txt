AMBERGRIS.  Ever hear of it?  Well, it's a major focus of this book.  Multiple characters hide it, find it, spirit it away from others, and hide it again.  And unfortunately, a few murders take place as a result.According to the dictionary, ambergris is "a grayish, waxy substance from the intestinal canal of sperm whales, often found floating in tropical seas; it is used in some perfumes."  It sounds icky and at the very least, smelly.  And the waters around Cape Cod could hardly be considered tropical seas.  But twenty-three-year-old Pamela Frye of Quanomet indeed finds a hundred pound mass of ambergris along the nearby shoreline.  Her sister Marina helps transport it back home.  Pam shares the news of her good fortune with a quick visit to old friend Asey Mayo, who taught her sailing when she was a child.  The fifty thousand dollars that the ambergris is worth would go a long way toward improving life at the Octagon House.  It would at least give them electricity and running water.  Asey is both amazed and supportive of Pam's discovery.But when Pam goes back home to reclaim her prize, she finds that Marina has been murdered.  Pam has enough presence of mind to take the ambergris and hide it, and she also knows that she'll be a suspect. After all, she and her sister weren't exactly the best of friends.  And Pam recognizes her knife as being the murder weapon.  She beseeches Asey to help her clear her name and to uncover the real culprit behind the dastardly deed.But that's not the only news that's got the fictitious Cape Cod town a-hoppin'.  Marina's husband Jack Lorne was commissioned to paint a mural for the interior of the new town post office.  (Remember the WPA murals of the 1930s?)  Its unveiling has caused quite a stir.  The mural depicts the "History and Customs of Cape Cod."  But the artist used real and noteworthy faces of the Quanomet residents as key characters in the piece ... and not necessarily in a good way.  Tempers flare, all around.  Was Marina the brains behind Jack's artwork?  Did her involvement give someone a reason to get her out of the picture, so to speak?  Or did someone have another ax to grind with Marina?  What about some of Pam's boarders at the Octagon House?  They seem to know more than they're letting on.  And local bad boy Roddy Strutt, that young but rich ne'er-do-well, seems to be in the thick of things as well.  Even worse is old Nettie Hobbs, the perennial town busybody.  She's gotten everything so blown out of proportion that the town is overrun with tourists.  What a mess!Asey's got his work cut out for him this time, trying to figure out who had the means, motive and opportunity to kill Marina, as well as making an attempt to turn up the missing ambergris.  This book is one of the better episodes in the series, featuring a good story line and a lot of Cape Cod quaintness and quirkiness.  I was a tad disappointed in the identity of the murderer, though.  I didn't suspect that person at all.  Still, this installment makes for good mystery reading.Read moreRead less
