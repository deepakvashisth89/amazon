In an era when advocacy for and against guns has reached a startling shrillness,  Chris Bird's "The Concealed Handgun Manual" is a refreshing respite.  Bird gives us a chapter on gun safety, including ten safety rules.  He starts off with the caution, "guns are dangerous."  Safe handling of guns is the primary responsibility of any handgun owner.  He also presents a section on "Children and Guns."  The section on gun safes and gun storage is excellent.  During the long life of a gun which may someday save your life, it is exposed to potential misuse, and it is, again, the gun owner's responsibility to control access to and use of the gun.  Bird gives us gun accident stories to illustrate the downside of gun ownership.Okay, now the gun is out of storage and you are carrying it concealed.  Do you believe that you are mentally prepared to kill another human being?  If the answer isn't "yes," then you should put that gun back into secure storage, because guns are very dangerous.  You need to be ready, willing and able to kill someone if you are going to carry a gun.  You need to have made that decision well before the moment it is needed.  You need to know how you are going to react.  And you need to know how to shoot to kill.  Don't expect the danger to disappear just because you draw the gun.There is also a section on when to use deadly force and what to expect when you do.  Bird relates the story of Ralph Williams, of Texas, who defended himself from an unprovoked attack, killing his assailant.  Williams ended up spending tens of thousands of dollars to stay out of jail, even though he was one hundred percent in the right.The section on state-by-state carry laws and reciprocity between states is excellent, though Bird wants you to know that the laws are constantly changing and you need to check out your state's regs for yourself.Guns can enhance your safety and the safety of those around you or they can make your world more dangerous yet.
