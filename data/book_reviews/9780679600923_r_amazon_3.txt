This is classic reading, but difficult to stay with. It's like fifty or eighty years of newspaper journals from one county in Mississippi, with memories from the Civil War and action until late 1940s. If you like serials, you'll love it. If you like civic philosophy, you'll love it. I don't know how many statements respecting personal liberty are in the three books. Two ideas occurred to me, perhaps from the combination of the book and my own experience. First, a woman does not care where the facts came from; she just wants to know the facts. Second, behind every great woman there's a person trying to be a man. I can't find references for these ideas, so may have read them into Faulkner's writing. However, this direct quote represents one character. ". . . every Snopes will make it his private and personal aim to have the whole world recognize him as THE son of a bitch's son of a bitch."
