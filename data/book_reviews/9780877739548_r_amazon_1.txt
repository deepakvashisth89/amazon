Of all the books I've read on Buddhist practice, this is the one that I come back to over and over again.  Its directness, precision, clarity and immediate usefulness is stunning, profound and impactful.  Reading this book for me was like getting kicked in the face, in terms of its powerful call to wake up and just DO what is being suggested, stop playing around with philosophies and start growing up.  You don't even have to be a Buddhist in order to do this stuff, because there is no doctrine whatsoever.I somewhat paraphrase the author's own introduction when I say from my own heart, that this book profoundly affected my own understanding of Buddhism, in a way that other texts have not.  You find that you can just DO IT and it works.  The core teachings here have no frills.  No pop psychology, no touchy-feely, bare bones.  No esoteric language, no beating around the bush, no stroking your ego.  Just straight in to *what you can do right now to train your mind and cultivate loving kindness*.There are some terms that require explanations which are given quite well by Trungpa.  Furthermore, this book may not be accessible to somebody with no prior exposure to Buddhism at all.  Those individuals may benefit from beginning with some books by the author of the foreword here, who herself was a student of Trungpa, and who incorporates these teachings in all of her many books, each of which is simultaneously accessible to all while being invaluable to even long-time practitioners.In summary, I am VERY grateful that this text exists, and for the expert commentary provided by Trungpa.  The first reading changed my life, and I am never disappointed when I return to the book for further study.
