The 5 stars is for Kant’s work itself. The rest of this review is about the Kindle edition.If you are indifferent between the hard copy and the Kindle edition, buy the hard copy. The hard copy has a few errors, but very few, as you would expect in an extremely high-profile work with very talented people involved. In contrast, the Kindle edition has a number of errors. It seems likely that the Kindle edition was computer-generated, and then checked by an editor, but the level of care taken is lower than that for the hard copy. There are places, for example, where an A-pagination or B-pagination appears in the middle of text without brackets, an obvious glitch, since these indications duplicate bracketed indications elsewhere, and the vast majority of page indications are in brackets. I noticed a couple of places where editorial footnotes are italicized, even though the convention in the Kindle edition is for translator’s footnotes to be italicized. There are a few places where the text makes no sense, one must go to the hard copy to discover the correct text. An example, from the Kindle edition:Now since the proposition I think (taken problematically) contains the form of every judgment of understanding whatever, and accompanies all categories as their vehicle, it is clear that the inferences from this proposition can contain a merely transcendental use of the understanding, excluding every admixture of experience; and of such a procedure, after what we have shown above, we cannot at the outset for many very favorable concept.The error here is in the words “for many” near the end of the sentence. In the hard copy the text reads “form any”, and that makes sense.In general, the number of errors in the Kindle edition is far from shocking, and the edition is definitely serviceable.
