What a poignant, all-consuming story of the tumultuous 60s and 70s and about a man who truly represents the zeitgeist of that entire period.  This is not only a compelling story of one of the most colorful and vocal leaders of the political-cultural revolution of that time, but also a family saga:  The price and toll upon the Hoffman family as a result of Abbie's role in what I see as "the American Spring" is also a cautionary tale about how our government tends to come down very hard on those who disagree with its policies even when those policies are deemed illegal and in violation of our Constitution.  And who knew that Abbie Hoffman was a savior of Woodstock!  Or that towards the twilight of his life his debut as a stand-up comic was, in fact, very promising.  His loving brother Jack Hoffman's story is at once heartening as well a gut-wrenching and without a doubt clarifies and solidifies Abbie Hoffman's courageous role as a seminal anti-establishment hero who inspired millions to stand-up for affirmative change while sacrificing and risking his life in the process of a higher cause.
