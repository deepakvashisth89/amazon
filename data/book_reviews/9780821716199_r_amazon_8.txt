As best I can tell, there is a gap of some years between this story and the previous one I read, The Mask of Fu-Manchu.  In between that and this there is The Bride of Fu-Manchu, which I have not read (but understand to be set just prior to this opening).  We also get the current date as being the mid-30's, and best of all, we are back in London (or at least its environs) for yet another showdown with Fu Manchu and his daughter.Its pretty clear that these books are somewhat formulaic, but also that the formula works.  Its especially obvious when you read them one after the other rather than over the 20 years these seven books were written in (and we are not at, or even particularly close, to the finish yet).  Once again we have Nayland Smith, older but wiser, with yet another young sidekick (American this time) along for the ride.  There are femme fatales and feminine betrayals, and we see that Fu Manchu has in fact discovered the secret of eternal life - he is basically a supernatural entity these days: certainly he is a supervillian in full.  Up against that, its all the forces - of, well, not good necessarily, but say for the sake of argument the Western world - can do to hold him to a draw, and whittle away his networks and supports.And yet, for all you think you know what is coming, there is still a shocking death and you do really feel that our Heroes can in fact die: there is real tension here.Yes, with 5 plus books to go, its obvious Fu Manchu will not finally win or lose in this volume (if he ever does - and I suspect not).  He's simply too great a villain to kill off and to do so would almost create an anti-climax.  However, I do plan to keep reading to see what does become of him…in the end.Read moreRead less
