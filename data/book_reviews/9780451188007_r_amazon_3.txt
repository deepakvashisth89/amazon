And you can't get her work in e-format, which is a shame.  Under the pen name Patricia Oliver, Patricia de la Fuente wrote Signet Regencies from 1993-2002.  They weren't to all tastes.  Some of the rakes were quite dissolute, not just boys who want to have fun.  Some of the heroes were jerks, a few of the women too willing to allow them to act like jerks.  But I accepted all this because Oliver made it real and made it fit the times.You shouldn't read a book set in the 1800s with 21st-century expectations about relationships.  I do, unfortunately, all too often, especially with the more-recently written HRs where I don't have much patience with the jerk heroes.  But Oliver's flawed heroes don't exasperate me, for some reason.  The one here I was quite sympathetic to and this spunky heroine is one of the best in Regency romanceland.  She's not beautiful but she has character and spirit and doesn't put up with any nonsense from the hero or from any of his rakish friends.The plot here has been done over and over again in HRs.  This book is from 1997 so it's not a copycat of anything written since then but I'm sure there were similar plots in books before 1997.  However, Oliver's writing is so good, her characters so well drawn and the dialogue so sparkling that I didn't at all mind the overused plot.The plot is, if you're wondering, the meeting of a hard-drinking, hard-wenching young duke who really has no self-respect because of some things in his past and the innocent young daughter of an impoverished country baronet.  Sparks fly.  She's poor, dresses dowdily, and is not all that pretty, but that personality and those violet eyes intrigue him.  She is drawn to and disgusted by him in equal parts and somehow senses there's more to him than the self-indulgent persona he presents to the world.  But can there really be a romance between two people so far separated on the social strata? Well, it is a romance and must have that HEA but it's not all that easy to get there.One more thing:  This story is typical of those older Signet Regencies in that the intimacy between H and h does not go beyond kisses.
