In this novel set on the hundredth birthday of America, Gore Vidal takes as his mouthpiece Charles Schermerhorn Schuyler, a 62 year old diplomat who left New York in 1837 for Europe and now returns with his daughter, Emma, seeking, somewhat desperately, paid journalism assignments for himself and a husband for his daughter. Schuyler dines with newspaper editors and politicos from whom he hopes for jobs in New York and Washington, attends the Centennial Exposition in Philadelphia, and crosses paths with President Grant in the White House. Meanwhile Emma flirts and socializes with aristocratic families in New York and Newport.1876 was in many ways a low point in American democracy with a corrupt Grant administration giving way to a Republican successor who plainly lost the election to the Democratic governor of New York only to have the presidency stolen like so many other things. The novel doesn’t get too deeply into the politics to be boring but there are plenty of deliciously bitchy encounters with historical characters and Schuyler nicely describes the atmosphere of New York city at the time, having last seen it in 1837 an old Dutch-English village now the seat of the Gilded Age. Both the Schuyler career plot and the Emma marriage plot are kind of ho hum but the novel does sparkle in its many witty, epigrammatic observations.
