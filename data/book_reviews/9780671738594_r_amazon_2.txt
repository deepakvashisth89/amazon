Straub is clearly a fan and an expert on Lovecraft’s writing (him having edited the Library of America edition of Lovecraft’s tales) so this novel is full of references and uses Lovecraft’s texts as a plot device.However, its attempts to be different than its inspiring material and be more contemporary reminds me more of other authors that did similar homages more successfully. Straub does a good job making an intriguing plot, having crystal clear prose, and I really enjoy his characters here. However, it does lack the bleakness of its inspiration. What happens here is a good novel that feels like it uses the other writings as convenient worldbuilding material rather than emulating its style or its aesthetics.Oddly enough, it reminds me of 90s Stephen King, a frequent collaborator and respected contemporary of Straub, in that it feels like it wants to be more contemporary literature than cosmic horror. Think of Rose Madder and Dolores Clairborne or Insomnia where it becomes more of an exercise than something that really coheres together.However, I did have a good time. I am a sucker for 90s era horror paperbacks with their need to make everything an exploration of family ties and small town drama along side its more horrific conceit.
