I don’t use too many superlatives when reviewing, but this is, if not the best, certainly one of the best novels I have ever read. William Gay was a brilliant master of language, characters, place, dialect and dialogue, and story. I was impressed enough to travel over six hundred miles on the slim hope that I might get to meet this painter, drywall man, and carpenter on his home turf and see some of the places he describes so brilliantly in this novel.And I got lucky. Through the friendly intervention of a local librarian in Hohenwald, Tennessee, I spent an entire afternoon in William Gay’s home, listening to his stories of being on the set when this book was made into a not-nearly-as-good movie Bloodworth with Kris Kristofferson.  I heard his stories about the selection of Hal Holbrook to play a character Gay created in Hate to See That Evening Sun Go Down. Holbrook does an admirable job.On the day of our visit, this modest, almost reclusive author was writing an article for Oxford American magazine explaining what it was like to be honored as a Tennessee Williams Scholar (and later Fellow) and Walter A. Dakin Fellow and visiting literary scholar by Sewanee: The University of the South  without having graduated high school. He said he had labored over it for days. I suspected he labored over every sentence in all his writings.In his modest but nice Tennessee foothills home, I got a close look at his competed and in-process works of art, his books on music and music history, and a house literally full of books. Gay was truly a self-taught man. Many say his style copied Cormac McCarthy, and he told me he was an unabashed fan of McCarthy. But Gay had a distinct and wonderful literary voice all his own. I even got the inside scoop on his completed manuscript, The Lost Country when his agent called to discuss problems related to its release. Four years later, we are still waiting for it.I went home inspired to continue my own writing and to read his works many more times. Provinces of Night is the ultimate source, the definitive work on how to write southern dialogue and dialect, character and place descriptions. Each time I read them, his books make me laugh, cry, tingle with emotion, and gush with admiration.I will never forget the visit, this book, or this author.Read moreRead less
