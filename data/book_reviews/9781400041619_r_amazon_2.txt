Try not to get caught up in the religious aspect of this book, but rather the development of a new relationship between two young people.The premise of the book is built on Orthodox Judaism and how it appears when magnified within two different families. However, I found the story of the young couple and their growing relationship to be more interesting than their religious associations. Being at the beginning of my own marriage, I found it easy to identify with the young couple who is trying to establish comfort, identity and a sense of home as a newly married couple.The themes that hit closest to home revolved around setting limits with family, developing priorities within the marriage, learning how to be supportive within a relationship and maintaining your own sense of identity as a married person.It is a story that could be told with any number of themes, not necessarily just Judaism. This could have easily been told as a story about two first-generation Americans or a young Hispanic couple, etc. It seems to me that Mirvis uses Judaism as an overarching theme in her writing while examining the innerworkings of relationships between people of different associations. This is also true of her first novel, "The Ladies Auxiliary", where she examines the relationships between women in a small community, which happens to be Jewish.Overall, a good story and a fast read.Read moreRead less
