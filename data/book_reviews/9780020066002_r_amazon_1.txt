Speer was not in any way the "good" Nazi, if any such person existed. As this book makes clear, he was a full-fledged Nazi early on who was close to and never lost his love -- yes, love! -- for Hitler. Speer was fully committed to Nazi victory and worked to the very end trying to achieve it, even to the point of joining Doenitz's Flensburg government after Hitler's suicide, trying to keep the whole criminal enterprise going. Throughout, he made full use of slave labor -- those the Nazis captured. Speer didn't look like a thug, was quite good looking, tall, and glib -- you know, movie-star qualities that caused people to give him the benefit of the doubt. So he got 20 years in prison -- instead of the ultimate penalty that he had earned as much as any of those who got death sentences. This is a well-written book that explains the Hitler/Speer relationship without leading readers through every detail of the war, but newbies to the subject will get enough to understand the flow of that war and how evil even this so-call "good Nazi" was.Read moreRead less
