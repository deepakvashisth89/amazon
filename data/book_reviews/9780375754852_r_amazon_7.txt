William E. Burrows' "This New Ocean: The Story of the First Space Age" is an excellent account of space exploration through the late 1990s.  This is an excellent and very thorough work on this interesting subject, and it tells the whole story of the "first space age."Burrows' argues that the competition between the US and USSR drove much of the space race through the end of the Cold War.  As part of this story, he focuses much of the narrative on the governments (and the government bureaucracies), internecine bureaucratic battles, and how the development of satellites and manned spaceflight fulfilled government policies and fueled rivalries.Burrows does not ignore the human element, though.  He focuses on the early rocket pioneers (not only from the 20th century, but the dreamers from ancient times until the 19th century who wrote about and planned for space exploration).  Burrows also discusses culture's impact on space as the American (and less so the rest of the world) population was influenced and impacted by Sputnik, Apollo, and Challenger, as well as culture's impact on space program development (many of the early designers were inspired by science fiction writers).This is a long (600+ pages), dense, and detailed history of the space program, but it would not make a good reference because of its narrative quality.  Burrows also completely ignores the development of the space programs in Europe, China, India, and Japan as he focuses on the space race between the US and USSR.  However, this is a fascinating, comprehensive, and interesting book, flecked with Burrows' dry sense of humor and skepticism.
