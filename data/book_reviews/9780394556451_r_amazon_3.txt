Truman Capote is one of the all time great American novelists.  His work has enthralled me since I discovered him a few short years ago. I've read most of his work; his novels, essays, and reportage are truly masterpieces.  His prose is never stiff and unwieldy, his wit is sharp and tangy, and many of his works are difficult to put down.I saved this work for last, because, simply, it was his last work. After reading all the hype, knowing the effect the publication of these words had on his friendships, career, and life, even reading Dominick Dunne's "The Two Mrs. Grenvilles" (written from the viewpoint of Basil Plant, a fictional version of Truman); I felt I was ready to read "Answered Prayers."This book is well written, as always, but left me with a sad feeling.  I know Capote was the original "Mean Girl", nevertheless, I was saddened by his blatant betrayal of confidences.  Reading this so many years after it was written, none of the characters resonated with me (with the exception of the Mrs. Woodward character, whose story I researched after reading the Dunne book).  I laughed at the snarky characterizations of Capote's high society peers, but was a little saddened by the effect I know these stories had on his life.If you love Capote as I do; enjoy his snarky, mean girl wit; and want to read all of his works, I highly recommend this one.
