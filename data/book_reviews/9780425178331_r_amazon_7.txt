The narrow niche of writing fiction about the grandson and great grandson of U.S. Presidents attracted me to this book, as well as the fact that I needed a cassette version of something while negociating dead radio reception areas of Fairfax County, Virginia.  I borrowed the audio copy from a Loudoun County Library and delighted myself to the some of the best descriptive writing I have heard blending what I thought was a historical narrative with a murder mystery.  All was well through about nine of the eleven tapes while I found myself amused with the technology of the late nineteenth century and their fumblings with biometrics.  A few too murderers and a shocking passage where I almost found myself as the model for one of the characters.  Everything changed with a few lines of dialog from one of the victims.  The uninvestigated crimes of one minor character and elimination of characters who openly worshipped the Virgin Mary made the Panama scandal seem small and petty.  When the line was uttered dividing the world into one category and thrusting others into "Virginians" struck me so odd as drove though the Washington D.C. suburbs.  The lively explanations of the art of the cathedrals was simply a setup to decipher part of the mystery.
