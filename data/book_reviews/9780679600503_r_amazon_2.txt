This is a novel of a type that isn't written much anymore. It's a picture of the lives of two men, who happen to be Catholic priests, over the long period of their ministry in What is now Colorado, New Mexico, and Arizona. It's set in the period right after the US gained possession of these territories. The work of the men parallels the growth of the area. It's an interesting picture.Rather than have an over-arching plot that could only draw one set of conclusions, the book is a series of stories about one or both of the men separated in time, consecutively, over the period of time they spent in the Southwest. It's a beautifully written book full of descriptions of the stark beauty of the undeveloped territory and also its people. Full attention is given to the Navajo, the primary Native American tribe of the area the priests were in, the Mexicans who had moved in during their possession of the territory, and the Americans who began to arrive at the end of the story. Each adds something to the story of these men, and thus adds to the overall book.I've given this four stars for the writing, but the story did not, at times, engage me fully so no fifth star. Your reaction might be completely different. It was definitely worth the time that I took to read this, though. Hope you enjoy it if you do.Read moreRead less
