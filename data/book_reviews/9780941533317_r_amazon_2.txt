Fascinating presentation of the gospels. The author merges Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John into a single story. Think of a trial lawyer giving his closing argument. The lawyers takes distinct, different, incomplete, and seemingly inconsistent witness testimonies and shows how they are actually unified, complementary testimonies. The lawyer also combines the separate testimonies into a single complete story. That is what the author did here: the four gospels are really one single, but long, gospel. Differences are noted in parentheses and footnotes, but the differences are never substantial. The unity of the story shows through them. It's a fascinating and enlightening read.
