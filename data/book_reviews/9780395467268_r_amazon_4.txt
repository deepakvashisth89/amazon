Well-written & well-organized.  This is the U.S. version (Houghton Mifflin) of the Helm Identification Guide to a family for which taxonomy has been relatively static since the 1988 publication date.  The format will be familiar to anyone who has any of the other such guides, and the plates and text are of comparable quality.  Birders don't typically use such works in the field, but instead study them when not birding and when agonizing over a difficult identification problem.  The species accounts include paragraphs devoted to field ID, voice, detailed descriptions, measurements, geographic variation, habits, habitat & distribution.  Any birder or natural history enthusiast interested in ducks, geese and swans will appreciate the comprehensive scope of this guide.One minor quibble: as is customary, the page opposite each plate identifies the species portrayed, along with range maps & a listing of the plumages depicted.
