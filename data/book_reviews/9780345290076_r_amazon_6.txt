A book does not have to be likeable to be worth reading. In fact, Return from the River Kwai bothered me a lot. But it made me think, and think, and think. It’s the story of two shiploads of POWs being sent to Japan for work detail. The Allies bomb the ships, not knowing they are carrying prisoners.One of the two ships sank slowly enough for every man to escape. The other sank so quickly that many POW lives were lost. Drowning, dehydration, fear of sharks, madness, loss of hope and partial cannibalism were the prevailing (and horrific) themes of the second half of the book. Initially I was quick to judge the actions of the men. But then I realized that it would be normal after two and a half years of cruelty and deprivation to have a sharply-honed instinct for self-preservation. While this does not justify their actions, it does make them more understandable.All the survivors were hailed as heroes, which made me ponder the subject. On the one hand, surviving the horrors of a POW camp takes inordinate amounts of stamina and perseverance.  But on the other, true heroism looks out for the needs of others and years of deprivation had made these men practically incapable of that.It is a tribute to the resilience of human nature that most of the survivors went on to live normal lives as lawyers, farmers, firemen, postmen, meat inspectors, or artists.
