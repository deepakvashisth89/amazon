Hansen's first novel, The Chess Garden, deserves to be ranked with the best books of the 1990's.  All of the dozen or so Amazon reviewers gave it five stars and I would happily write a review adding five more.  I keep it  conspicuously placed  on my bookshelf so I am reminded of the fun of  reading it.  Perlman's Ordeal is another matter.  Although the book is  every bit the intellectual feast that the Chess Garden was, it lacks a  certain spark that would have kept me interested in the story and in its  protagonist, Dr. Perlman.  Whereas Dr. Uyterhoeven in The Chess Garden was  generously open-minded, Dr. Perlman is every bit the man who can't see  beyond his rather educated nose.  His ordeal is getting past that  limitation - something he never completely seems to do.  Hansen seems to  have let his own main character attentuate his vision.  Also, if there was  a flaw in The Chess Garden, it was that its characters were not completely  rounded - but somewhow it didn't really matter.  It does matter in this  book since we see everything through (via a third person narrator),  Perlman's rather narrow, fretful, selfish eyes.  This left me feeling cold.  Still, Hansen's a major writer and I look forward to his next book.Read moreRead less
