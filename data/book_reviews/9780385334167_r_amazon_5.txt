Generally I do not like novels. Most novels that are recommended to me turn out to be choked with filler, and often the same story could have been told using 25% of the text. I really like the protagonist in this novel. Maybe part of me identifies with her or her quest, maybe other people will hate her for that. While I do feel the last quarter of the book could probably have been edited down a little, I was very eager to see what would happen to Sharon Spiegelman next. I enjoyed her homage to Madeline in Chapter 17 (pg 238 hardback) "In an old house in Bellevue all covered in vines, lived sixteen big girls in two straight lines. We woke each day at seven A.M. We said hi to the rebbe in his frame. At seven-twenty we broke our bread. At seven-forty grace was said. We prayed in small groups from eight to nine. The beginner's group was, you guessed it, mine." Sharon is searching for meaning in life, and trying to find a faith that makes sense to her, and willing to try almost anything.
