The Osprey series of books on military topics are regarded as wonderful sources on the equipment, weapons, tactics and events of history's many wars. Many with an interest in Irish military history awaited the publication of this book with eager anticipation. Unfortunately, the new Osprey book on the Irish Volunteers is surprisingly flawed. A number of very careless historical errors have marred the book, e.g.: the author repeatedly calls the "Howth rifles" (arms landed at Howth Dublin in 1914 by the Irish Volunteers) the M1898 Mauser, when they were in fact M1871 Mausers. He designates them as 7.96 mm when the Howth guns were 11mm, a not unimportant point as the 11mm round had been outlawed at the Hague conference as a dum-dum bullet. In another place the author speaks of "Italian Martini" rifles, when, I think, he can only mean Martini Enfield or Martini Henry rifles (not Italian) and Italian Carcano rifles (not Martinis). He writes of "leftover pikes from 1798" when pikes were being regularly produced by the Volunteers on the Plunkett estate at Larkfield, Kimmage. Beyond these easily checked technical mistakes, the authors use the book to push a very pro-Irish Free State view that is arguable to say the least. The illustrations, a high point in the book series, in this tome are not great (O for Angus McBride!) and in one picture contain a glaring historical error. While this new title provides some new photos and information, overall the book is a let-down.
