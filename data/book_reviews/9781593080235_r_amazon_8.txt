I admit Victorian literature generally isn’t my cup of tea…So much of it feels like pretentious upper middle class drama about who is going to marry whom in order to raise/lower their status. What a privileged lifestyle to have that be one’s biggest concern in life.Middlemarch starts out feeling like more of the same. In the beginning, it reminded me a lot of Pride and Prejudice — an intelligent, prideful woman with standards being pursued by multiple men, preferring the ones who intellectually engage her while feeling repulsed by the ones who don’t.As the LONG story unfolds though, it ends up being so much more than that. No offense intended to Pride and Prejudice, but Middlemarch ends up being a deeper, more complex story with a more intricate cast of characters (and not all marriage/status drama).The length enables that to happen, but length is also the biggest thing that holding back the book’s enjoyability. There’s too many stretches that feel unnecessary and irrelevant. In many cases, you’re pulled entirely away from characters you’ve been following for hundreds of pages and thrust into a cast of strangers wondering who they are and why you should care.Some of those temporary detours pay off. Lydgate’s story was one for me. I couldn’t get myself to care about any of Bulstrode’s shenanigans though until it affected Lydgate, and there were at least a few others the story could have done entirely without.While the book is a bit of a slog, it is a worthwhile read.
