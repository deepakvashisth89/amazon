Simon Brett's Mrs. Pargeter series is, in my view, the very best of his writing.  His protagonist, Melita Pargeter, is a fascinating woman, a woman who lives by her own standards and rules, and who enjoys life very much.  Her first foray into detecting, related in "A Nice Class of Corpse" (also a delightful book), came about by accident.  In this book, however, Mrs. Pargeter runs across a few puzzling facts and decides there is a mystery to be solved - and why not solve it, thus adding some spice to life?  Why be bored, just because you're a widow in your sixties?  The supporting cast includes, first of all, her omnipresent and ever-caring deceased husband, a true gentleman among crime ring leaders.  Then there are all the somewhat shady helpers Mr. Pargeter bequeathed to Melita: the chauffeur, who used to drive for Mr. Pargeter, but now likes to drive rather more slowly; the lock man, who quietly slips out of prison to visit his wife and children or to do favours for the great Mr. Pargeter's widow; the man who could find anyone Mr. Pargeter needed, and who retains his skills; the man who used to be so good at making stolen cars saleable, who still has a wide circle of contacts and can find any car in England, and who is glad to make his current clients (a couple of Arabian sheiks) wait so he can help out Mr. Pargeter's widow.  There are, of course, the suspects, as well, seven of them.  As the clever, original, and well-thought-out plot unwinds, Mrs. Pargeter treats the reader to her inimitable view of the world.  Set in the 1990s England, in a world where people try to be what they're not and create false communities because they have lost the sense of real community, the story leaves the reader with the comforting feeling that community can still exist - even if only in the circles of once-criminals.  Mrs. Pargeter's original way of looking at things gives the reader flashes of insight into society, life, the world, and does it in a way that allows the reader to let it pass as a modestly stimulating moment of entertainment or to stop and think about it, reframing one's own existence.
