One of my favorite kanji books. Detailed, thorough, easy-to-understand. You can't go wrong with this study aid. It's organized using the same system developed by Japan's Ministry of Education so the first kanji you learn is One. Twenty-three kanji later you are introduced to Three, and the sixty-first kanji presented is Two. It's an odd system. Sometimes I feel I'm on a goose chase flipping back and forth between entries to track down elements that are in a kanji but introduced in other parts of the book. Another quibble is that the hand-drawn pictographs are a wee bit small. But these are small potatoes in an excellent reference guide and study aid.
