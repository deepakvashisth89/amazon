Pullman's novel tells the story of Lyra who lives in a world that is not as technologicall advanced as ours where witches, armoured bears and Daemons live. The Daemons are connected with every human in this world and they reflect their human's feelings and dissappear when their human dies. Pullman begins with Lyra spying on the Master of Jordan College, where she lives, and seeing how he plans to poison her uncle, Lord Asriel. She saves her uncle's life and then gets caught up in a large, complex plot against the Church and in turn against God. Pullman does not directly imply if he is an atheist, but challenges the Church in this alternate world, which is much more powerful than in ours. Pullman breaks away from the stereotype quest with a clear goal to accomplish. He continually has characers discussing Lyra and how she is the prophesised one, but sheds no real light as to what she must do, only that she must not know that she is doing it and in the end save the world. There is no clear 'Bad Guy' either merely an interesting character with very ambitious goals to get to the core of original sin. Pullman uses religion merely as a part of his plot, not as some blasphemous attack on Christianity so this book should be read with an open mind.
