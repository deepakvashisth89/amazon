Review: The Diamond MaskFew writers combine grand science-fiction ideas with intimate human drama as effectively as Julian May. The Diamond Mask, part of her Galactic Milieu series, demonstrates that talent beautifully.The novel centers on Rogatien Remillard, one of the most powerful metapsychic minds in human history. Gifted with extraordinary abilities yet burdened by emotional wounds, Rogi struggles to reconcile immense power with personal responsibility. Around him swirl family conflicts, political intrigue, psychic mysteries, and the continuing evolution of humanity itself.One of May’s greatest strengths is her ability to make cosmic events feel personal. The stakes are enormous—touching the future of human civilization—yet the story never loses sight of the emotional lives of its characters.The title’s “diamond mask” serves as both a literal and symbolic image. Throughout the novel, characters conceal their true feelings, motives, and vulnerabilities behind carefully crafted facades. May is deeply interested in the tension between public identity and private truth.As a retired library assistant, I always admired May’s willingness to trust her readers. The Galactic Milieu novels contain complex histories, large casts, and sophisticated ideas, but patient readers are richly rewarded.The book also explores themes of forgiveness, redemption, family loyalty, and the consequences of power. These human concerns keep the story grounded even when psychic powers and galactic politics dominate the plot.Edgar the raven considered the matter carefully.“CAW.”I translated this as:“A diamond mask may hide your face, but it rarely improves your decisions.”Random Drink PairingWhite Peach Sparkling TeaElegant, refined, and subtly complex.
