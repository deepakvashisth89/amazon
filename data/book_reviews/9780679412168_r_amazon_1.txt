This book is simply page after page of revelations about why JFK was the amazing man most Americans came to know as their President. This is a very detailed and complete history of his youth and life up through his first congressional campaign. It does not shy away from providing nearly endless detail on what some might consider minutiae, so if you're looking for an easy, fast read, you're out of luck. You will learn amazing never-before published details about JFK's relationship with his older brother Joe, his effervescent sister Kathleen (Kick) who died tragically in a plane accident before his Presidency began, and his life long gay friend & confidant Lem Billings as revealed through their frequent highly uninhibited personal correspondence; gain deep insight into JFK's experiences as the son of America's Ambassador to The Court of St. James's (England) in Europe during the build-up to WWII; read incredibly detailed accounts of his romantic relationship with Inga Arvad including numerous in-bedroom and telephone conversations obtained by FBI surveillance (not kidding), and experience the harrowing back-story of JFK's slightly mentally deficient sister Rosemary (a girl who was intelligent enough to keep diaries, do basic math and be presented to the Queen of England in London with the rest of her family) but who became a victim of one of the first pre-frontal lobotomies - a brutal procedure performed by criminally incompetent doctors that literally amputated her "mind" (personality and higher human functions) while she was awake leaving her permanently vegetal and incontinent. You will see JFK's absolutely authentic personality and individualism as well as his deep innate fascination with politics and human affairs emerge from an irresolute childhood so that by the end of the book there will be little doubt why this amazing person became such a beacon of hope in the triumphant post-WWII cum sinister cold-war world.  By the time JFK became President, he was a truly singular man, the product of extraordinarily varied, rich, familial, educational and cultural experience...all the more reason to regard his assassination as an epochal tragedy. Bottom-line, although it has minor stylistic and pacing faults, I simply can't praise this book and author/historian Nigel Hamilton enough. Thanks Nigel.Read moreRead less
