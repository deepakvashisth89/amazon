I downloaded this but did NOT receive the Allen Mandelbaum verse translation as one might expect from the Editorial Review section, which states: "Allen Mandelbaum has produced a living Aeneid, a version that is unmistakably poetry." -- Erich Segal, The New York Times Book ReviewWhat was sent to me was the 1885 ostensively prose translation by J. W. Mackail, M.A., Fellow of Balliol College, Oxford.Let me be frank: I don't care for this translation. Here are some difficulties that I'm having with it:o vocabulary--archaic/poetic-of old (a strange translation for "primus" (first))-bestead (not in my kindle dictionary)-ere (archaic)-attaint [of her deity](affront, wound, hurt?--give me the Latin: numine laeso. I know what it means.)-Aeneadae (not in my kindle dictionary, although I know it refers to Aeneas' friends/companions)And so it goes.o word order--if it's prose, adopt a SVO word order; if it's poetry, one expects some liberties taken to retain correspondence with the original or to fit an adopted meter. But here subjects often follow their verb unnecessarily.There are several directions a translator can take and I have examples of most for the Aeneid: literal, poetic, word equivalent, quite dynamic, strong correspondence, readable. I don't have any contempory versions, though such an approach might be very entertaining. Of all of these, I prefer Mandelbaum or Fagles. The former is a decent balance between correspondence and readability. The latter simply sounds good--I love reading it aloud.
