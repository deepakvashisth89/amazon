This book is a nice architectural catalog of structures (and districts) which have official landmark status on either local or national registers.  The buildings are lovely, and the book does a nice job of presenting the rich architectural ensemble of St. Louis.  Since the publication date is 2002, almost all of the structures included in the book are still standing today.  There is a short chapter at the end of the book dedicated to lost landmarks, but it is surprisingly short.  Enormously destructive "renewal" projects of the 1950s and 1960s galvanized the preservation community in St. Louis into one of the country's strongest.The major problem is the photography.  This book contains no native photography:  All of the photographs are from archival sources and are monochrome.  Many are of questionable quality, even for pragmatic illustrative purposes.  Some readers may like the historic images, but I prefer modern images of artistic quality.Coverage is strictly limited to the city limits of St. Louis, but (on the plus side) this book gives a much more extensive survey of the important (and often forgotten) North Side than does the AIA Guide by McCue and Peters.Recommended for architectural historians and lovers of old St. Louis.Read moreRead less
