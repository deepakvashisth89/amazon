River of Earth follows a family in the Appalachian mining culture in the early 20th century. The language is archaic and picturesque. A reader familiar with only present day discourse would be lost without an appended glossary, which unfortunately is not provided. The story is narrated by a young boy moving with his family from one dirt-poor camp to another due to the chaotic openings and closings of the mines. The father insists his best future is as a miner, despite the frequent closings of the local mines. The mother has no hope for the mines, and desperately wants a home with a plot of ground on which to grow fruit and vegetables. Despite the poverty and hard scrabble living conditions, River of Earth is peopled with warm-blooded characters dealing with he circumstances of their lives inventively with as much hope as they can muster.
