11 16 2015. I feel lucky that I found the psychologically complex, beautifully written mystery novels by Margaret Yorke in the library, a place I'm ambivalent toward. Although, apparently, she was or is often ranked first-rate alongside such masters of the genre as the incomparable Ruth Rendell and, more recently, Sophie Hannah, I had never heard of her.Margaret Yorke is perhaps a bit more pithy, less extravagant.This small book of short stories by her, which span the years, has stories with extremely repetitive plots, characters and settings.But her themes become both fun to see in their iterations, and given how lightly the main characters take the decision to and then DO murder their " nearest and dearest," they exert a strange fascination, like knowing someone who takes the wings off living flies and not being able to look away.I found it interesting to think that I could feel Margaret Yorke's own personal rage here.  I have not read a biography, if one exists, and only know that she was " divorced," but here she creates, again and again, put-upon spouses who kill to save themselves.There is also a theme of unwanted, yet also wanted, isolation from others, a sere state of heart but one which I personally, though I live with my husband and am surrounded by five adolescents and twenty-somethings, our children who live at home, understand more and more as I grow older.Read moreRead less
