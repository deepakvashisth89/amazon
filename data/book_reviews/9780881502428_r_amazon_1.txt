Dead Ernest — ReviewThere are detective novels that begin with a corpse.And then there are detective novels that begin with a corpse and somehow become one of the funniest mysteries in the Golden Age.Dead Ernest is one of the most entertaining entries in the Leonidas Witherall series, combining murder, misdirection, eccentric characters, and a steady stream of wit.I spent a fog-softened Eureka afternoon reading it at the Eye of Bodhidharma while Edgar the raven occupied his customary perch on the fence outside. The fog drifted through Old Town like an alibi that wouldn’t quite hold up under questioning.Edgar studied the title thoughtfully.His position was simple:“A dead Ernest is unfortunate. A living Ernest should probably provide an explanation.”Leonidas Witherall remains one of mystery fiction’s most delightful detectives.A retired schoolmaster with an uncanny resemblance to Shakespeare, he possesses an extraordinary ability to stumble into complicated crimes while maintaining the air of a mildly inconvenienced academic.What especially impressed me was the humor.Many mystery writers can construct puzzles.Few can make readers laugh while doing so.Alice Tilton (Phoebe Atwood Taylor) had a gift for comic timing.The dialogue sparkles.The situations escalate beautifully.The supporting characters are wonderfully eccentric.The mystery itself remains satisfying.Clues appear fairly.Suspects accumulate.Motives emerge.Readers are invited to solve the puzzle alongside Witherall.Yet the real pleasure comes from spending time with the characters.The book moves briskly from one complication to another.Every chapter introduces fresh surprises.The tone remains light without sacrificing suspense.That balance is difficult to achieve and one of the reasons the series remains beloved.⸻What Works Well✓ Outstanding humorAmong the funniest detective novels of its
