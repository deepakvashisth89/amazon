Although the idea that the universe is a living, evolving organism – which, incidentally, did not originate with Gribbin – is compelling, the author’s appeal to the theory of natural selection as a means of explaining its evolution is puzzling.  Natural selection, after all, is not a credible hypothesis, but rather an obsolete, 19th Century “guess” of sorts, with no scientific applications whatsoever.As a theory, “survival of the fittest” is appealed to primarily in the popular literature, as a tool for defeating religion; it has no scientific import.  Declaring that “the fittest specimen survives” is only true because it is a tautology.  It doesn’t explain anything about what fuels the evolution of the organisms in the first place.  How, then, can declaring that “the fittest universe survives” add anything to our understanding of the cosmos, even if one is to accept the conjecture that there are alternative universes out there?  Why are there alternative universes out there?  Where did they come from and in what are they contained?  Note that when the author states that the universe is an organic entity, he is making an ontological, or metaphysical claim.  In other words, he is making cosmic evolution not just a scientific problem, but a philosophical one as well.From a philosophical point of view, If one is to describe the universe as an organic entity, it is necessary to articulate this description in terms of the same sorts of laws which describe organic systems in a general sort of way.  To do this would begin with an understanding of the logical differences between physical and biological laws.  It would also involve a critical examination of the principle of natural selection, to determine whether it is in fact universalizable in the sense that it can be meaningfully applied in other sciences – or indeed logically acceptable at all.It isn't.  But as the latter project is long past overdue – as is the general acceptance of the idea that the cosmos is an evolving organic entity – the universe owes the author an immense debt of gratitude for bringing the subject to general attention.
