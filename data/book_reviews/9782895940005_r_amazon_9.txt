I have spent 18 months waiting for this book to come out.  Amazon.com e-mailed me about it, and I ordered it. The publication date kept being changed and finally Amazon.com said it wasn't available.  There are three books on the market (excluding this one) involving the Bernardo-Homolka murders in Canada. I contacted Amazon's Canadian branch, and found out it had been published there, and here.  The book is so critical of the Canadian judiciary's deal with Karla Homolka so that they could convict Paul Bernardo, that no-one dared publish it, only a small French Canadian publisher called Cantos, and it grew from there. Karla and her husband Paul happily violated three young women (one Karla's own 15 year old sister!) and killed them. Paul was also the notorious 'Scarsborough Rapist'.  What I find so interesting is that he never killed anyone, until Karla became involved with him.  There is ambiguous evidence that may show he was absent when the girls died. However, without Karla's evidence, there was no case.She did not come forward until he had thoroughly beaten her up, and the police were already on their trail. The hospital and judiciary found it convenient to label Karla 'an abused woman' suffering from 'traumatic loss of memory'.  Everything she said inculpated Bernardo and exculpated Karla. For testifying 'truthfully' she received an eight year sentence. Then the judiciary went back on its word and had Karla diagnosed as a psychopath. I think she is, as shown by the evidence in the other books.  Having spent the whole book tearing into Karla, Williams then expects you to feel sorry for her!!!  Why?  She served up her own sister as a virgin sacrifice and kidnapped two other young women knowing what would happen to them. She lied repeatedly to the police and at Bernardo's trial. She is lucky that the police and lawyers couldn't add two and two, or she'd be looking at 30 years, not 12!  God help the Canadians when she gets out in 2005 - she's pure evil, and she's going to be helped to change her name and disappear!  No wonder no-one wanted to publish it!  No-one comes out well from this modern day horror story, and I was not impressed by the author pushing himself frequently into the case - it only detracts from what he has to say, and also shows him in an unfavourable light.
