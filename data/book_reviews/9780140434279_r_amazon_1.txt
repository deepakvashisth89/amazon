This book was intriguing and fascinating for story, language, religion, history, character and etc.  I know that Middlemarch is the "big thing" to be concerned about in proving your literacy, and I know Silas Marner is an easy mark, but Daniel Deronda has more interest and depth than those books.  The dual stories were both interesting, and the paths of the two main characters were fascinating to watch.  I was afraid that Eliot was going to show antisemitism in the portrayal of the Jewish characters, but I thought she was fair and even openminded in the presentation of various aspects of Jewish life and outlook.  As a Jew, I was not offended, but I was rather intrigued to see the demonstration of how "proper English people" in 1870 perceived the Jews in their country.  I think that Daniel Deronda should be given much more serious attention than it has been--as in, I was not even aware of this book until quite recently, and I am very glad that I have read it now.I do want to add that the Wordsworth paperback is excellent for the footnotes and explanations that the commentator has added.
