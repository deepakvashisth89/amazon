Read this after reading the original Rama by Arthur C. Clarke, one of the top 3 all-time sci-fi writers.  Supposedly this was co-authored by Clarke and Gentry Lee, but notice Clarke's name is prominently on the cover.  Lee is much more expository and, IMO, far less capable of painting a vivid, evocative and poetic picture than Clarke can with just a few words and sentences.  This is but one notable difference between an acknowledged master of the craft and those who can merely plug away at it. So I was pretty disappointed at first, expecting more Clarke and less Lee.  In fact, Clarke may have merely passed editorial review and plot ideas on to Lee, sort of like an "executive director" for a movie, rather than involved with the nuts and bolts.However once I got past all the exposition and into the sci-fi, it sort of grew on me.  And of course the cliff-hanger at the end which made me order the sequel, Garden of Rama.In short, it's an OK to good read but probably not something memorable enough to keep in your library and read again, after fondly recalling the pleasure you got out of it the first timeRead moreRead less
