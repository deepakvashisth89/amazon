Okay -- I had a lot of trouble getting into this book by Jeter, the originator of the term "Steampunk."The book was written in Victorian English and the story told as narrative, with most chapters starting with the main character and narrator, talking about how he is writing down his experiences.  So, from the get go, you know he survives it all.  Does take a bit of the drama out of it, doesn't it?  :-)However, this book is full of well described characters, fantastical human-like creatures, lots of action and intrigue, and the much needed occasional comic relief.  So what was my problem?  I'm not sure.  I was 80% through the book (how do I know that -- I read it on Kindle, so no page numbers, just percent of completion) before I began to care at all about the main character.  I disliked many of the "baddies," but really did like the rest -- even more than the main character.  Shoot, I liked the main characters live-in servant better than I liked the main character.  In fact, I liked the dog he adopts about mid way through the book better than I liked him! Actually, his befriending the dog was the first time I felt any compassion for him.I'm not a stranger to steampunk novels and I can't say that I didn't, at least in the end, enjoy this one.  Just was too hard to become invested in the trials and tribulations of the main character.  You'll notice I gave it 4 stars.  It was well written, well researched, the language was spot-on for the time period, lots of action, lots of colorful characters.  Everything a good novel should have.  I'm certain others would love it; it just wasn't my cup of tea.Read moreRead less
