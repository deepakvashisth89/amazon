Being a "Downton Abbey" fanatic, when I discovered "The Passing Bells" on Amazon, I was intrigued.  I love historical fiction set during the Great War.  Since this book is the first in a trilogy, I wanted to make certain this novel was a worthy read before I ordered the other two books.  It didn't take but a few pages before I was totally hooked.  I bought the other two books in the series within days.  To my utter surprise, "The Passing Bells" was even better than Downton Abbey in my opinion.  A television show (mini-series, movie, etc.) is primarily a visual experience of what people are saying or doing.  It is difficult to portray the reasoning behind an action or what makes a character "tick."  Since I have an aversion to reviews that tell the narrative of a book (since I hate spoilers), let me say that "The Passing Bells" is not to be missed.  The characters are so engaging, the times so fraught, and the descriptions so spot-on for the time period -- I couldn't put this book down.  I ended it happily knowing that there are two other books in the series waiting.  I hated to read that Philip Rock died in 2004.
