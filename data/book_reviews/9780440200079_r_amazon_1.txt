I always enjoy Robert Barnard’s very British prose style —  urbane, restrained, but enlivened with flashes of wry humor and the occasional  colorful metaphor.The story  starts out in the grubby office of a magazine called Bodies.  It’s all about having a healthy body, and it’s chock full of photos of bodies, mostly undressed — but still decent.When four people are murdered in the magazine’s photo studio, police superintendent Percy Trethowan investigates.  His inquiries take him deeply into the world of competitive bodybuilding.  Many of the men who model for Bodies are dedicated bodybuilders.  I used to date a compulsive bodybuilder, and Barnard’s description of this world is spot on.The superintendent enlists the services of Charlie Peace, a very well-built young black man, to infiltrate the activities of a photographer whose photos of bodies may not be so wholesome.  Charlie is a very likable character who will appear in later books.Bodies is a delightful mystery, one of Bernard’s best.Read moreRead less
