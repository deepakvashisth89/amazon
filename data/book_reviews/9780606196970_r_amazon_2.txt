I was thinking the other day about which books from my primary school education were most memorable, and this one came to mind. On reflection, it's difficult for me to think of a book from grade school other than Foster's War that had as significant an effect on my thoughts about a time period while also imparting life lessons.The book is fundamentally about a kid growing up, but much of the emphasis is obviously on what happens around him during a unique time period in American history. Although I read this book more than 15 years ago, I still remember passages about Foster's father buying sugar (sugar!) for the family on the black market because of war rationing, the star hanging in the household's window because a family member was serving in the military, and the family putting its spare money into "war bonds." I remember the father discussing how neighborhood children addressed their elders, and also the distant relationship he sometimes had with the rest of the family compared to today's standards.These are observations that people who lived through the mid-century era have confirmed as accurate. I feel like I learned about the culture in a reasonably holistic way by reading this book. At the same time, I gained insights about the history of the period, and about the human costs of so-called "total war" as practiced in the twentieth century.I would recommend this book to anyone, but particularly to young people.
