This is Philip Dick's masterpiece.  It's not a science fiction novel.  It is either a hallucination or a divine revelation.  Or both.  Either way, in 200 years, VALIS may very well be holy canon of an established religious movement.  This is seriously deep and fertile material, which will inspire a great deal of creative head-scratching.Other readers may be correct that a bibliography is necessary to understand this book.  I don't know - I don't presume to have understood it.  Moreover, the whole experience seems to have baffled Dick as much as it does me.  With an unreliable narrator, and characters who are often themselves crazy, this book remains wildly open for interpretation.  But that doesn't make it difficult to enjoy.  New insights remain to be discovered within it both upon each reading and by each reader.Maybe this is not summer beach reading, but anyone with a taste for the weird will have a difficult time putting it down.  The story is deeply emotionally-affecting.  As other readers have correctly noted, this is really just the story of one man's inability to cope with the death.  But I disagree that that's ALL it is.  This is a religious text; religion itself is about the inability to cope with death.As I recall, I was first put on to this book by a reference in Robert Anton Wilson's bookCosmic Trigger I : Final Secret of the Illuminati.  When I noticed one of the characters in THIS book ALSO refers to THAT one, it inspired an odd sense of Borges/Escher-style vertigo.
