Agatha Christie wrote her 1st Hercule Poirot detective mystery in 1916 during World War I and the book was published in 1920. The background is important because of numerous references to the war. Poirot has left Belgium where he was a very famous police detective. Belgium had been invaded by Germany (a point not made clear in the story, at least not to this reader) and Poirot was forced to leave to go to England where he now lives. We are used to an obsessive, fussy, and deliberate Poirot with the famous mustache, a character which we receive in wonderful form from the famous author, but there are notable differences (none of them negative for the reader), including a noticeable limp (reason is never made clear).Styles is an estate in England where Mrs. Cavendish, her new husband and a number of family members and friends reside. In total, there are about 20 characters who come and go, any number of whom could be the villain in the mystery. I decided to keep a notepad where I listed each character by name with a brief description. I found the reading somewhat akin to playing the game of "Clue", with the possible suspects, weapons and rooms.As you work through the detective story, you will most certainly see some resemblance to the way in which Arthur Conan Doyle pictured Holmes and Watson (now Poirot and Hastings).You may guess right and figure out the plot, but I certainly did not. Agatha Christie did a wonderful job laying out the groundwork for any number of possible endings.The only reason I did not give this book a 5 was that this is a Kindle version and does not include attachments in an appendix. The attachments would have been very interesting to note and review. On the positive side, however, there are small pictures which I found very nice (and unusual for a Kindle version).The price at $0.99 was very reasonable! I enjoyed the author's 1st Poirot book and look forward to reading more.Read moreRead less
