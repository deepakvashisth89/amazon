To start, this is a long, long, long book, but it is worth the effort. There are some slightly strange chapters – one on Captain Ahab’s mental state, and another exposition about white. But much of the storytelling is excellent, even if it is a tad wordy – hence the length – Mr. Melville certainly likes to offer lots of detailBut this is not just a book about the White Whale, it is a peak into life in the 19th century aboard a ship, a commercial whaler, that I expected to circumnavigate the world at a time when such an undertaking was fraught with danger, and to be away for several years. The first sighting of a whale is about three hundred pages in, but it is breathless in its brief excitement. Later attacks lead to detailed explanations about whales in general, and sperm whales in particular. I emphasis again, some of the details are a bit gruesome.The mix of narrative and Crichton-esque explanation take us through the oceans, explains the details of whaling (sometimes rather graphically), the harsh conditions and much more.Long but an enjoyable read.Read moreRead less
