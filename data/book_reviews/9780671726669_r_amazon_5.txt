I read this book with great interest.  I know there is a huge polarity of Betty support and Betty condemnation out there and it was hard to understand why.  This book makes things a bit clearer.  One thing that is abundantly, unquestionably clear is that Betty had a rather severe case of personality disorder -- several doctors diagnosed her with a narcissistic personality disorder and others mentioned signs of behavior personality disorder..  I myself can speak with some familiarity with the topic of personality disorders because for many, many years I had to deal with a difficult family member who had a pretty severe case of borderline personality disorder. I was amazed to see so, so many of the same patterns of behavior in Betty Broderick.  In particular, the one that nearly bowled me over with painful and sorrowful deja vu, was the desperate, hysterical, histrionic, self-destructive, maniacal fear of abandonment that at times crosses into a kind of insanity.Yes, it is too easy to demonize someone like Betty.  This book helps to show that Betty was a human being who was more than her disorder, though it eventually and unfortunately grew so out of hand as to consume everything else in its wake. It also shows that her ex-husband and his new wife were not saints.  On the other hand, she is NOT a hero and does NOT represent the struggles that women suffer when they are truly battered and abused-- or left to raise children alone without support of any kind (often without having had a career or education of their own).  The author here bends over backward to make Betty more human, less demonic.  But the bottom line is that she was someone unable to control or understand herself in the least.
