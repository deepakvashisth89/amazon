When aged Eduard Schwermann arrives at the door of Larkwood Priory asking for sanctuary, the priory is thrown into the national spotlight. If sanctuary is granted the priory may appear to be giving haven to a Nazi; but if it is not, the priory appears to be sending an old man declaring his innocence to the police.On the same day that Schwermann's case hits the news, Agnes Aubret Embletom learns that she has motor neurone disease, a progressive degenerative nerve disease that will leave her helpless and eventually kill her. The only person who knew her past -- which included rescuing Jewish children in Paris during World War II, losing her own son and spending years in Auschwitz and Ravensbruck -- was her late husband. She decides that she needs to write everything down before she is unable to.Investigation into Schwermann's past show that he was responsible for sending hundreds of Jews to death camps but was given safety at a Catholic priory in France, helped with a disguise as a priest and papers to get to England. When a British intelligence officer encountered them and interrogated them, he released them and wrote a report bare of detail.Larkswood Abbot Father Andrew asks Father Anselm, a former attorney, to do some investigating to find out why the church gave Schwermann shelter.This is a long and complicated book. Nothing is what it seems. The story spans more than a half a century. Names change. Identities are exchanged. It's a fascinating story, but it does take patience to get through and understand.The name refers to the Biblical book of Lamentations, which is a series of poems mourning the destruction of Jerusalem by the Babylonians in 586 BCE.
