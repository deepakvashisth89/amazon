I don't know why every humorous English author is automatically said to be "like Terry Pratchett." I liked Terry Pratchett's novels a great deal, and mourned him like a friend when he died. I don't feel that Tom Holt is very much like him.  However, just as it is possible to have more than one friend, I enjoy reading more than one author. Tom Holt is endlessly entertaining, and of the several books I've read by him this year, this is my favorite. I'm a professional writer (uncredited, manuals) and I love to read good writing. His writing is read-out-loud perfect. In fact, my husband has heard a great deal of Tom Holt's writing because I can't resist but read them out loud sometimes! I wish more of his work was available in audio book format because I would love to hear someone read them to me. The humor is complex and wraps around itself. You'll need to read several paragraphs before you get to the pay off, on occasion. Holt presents the reader with an absurd premise--all of the characters of The Ring myths (yes, like the Wagner opera) are real. The ringbearer, in the form of a badger for thousands of years, is killed by a car. The driver of the car becomes the new ringbearer, with all the powers and responsibilities therein. And it proceeds from there! The story is completely fantastical, the humor is dry, the prose is witty. And the story is actually pretty touching. If that's your kind of book, you'll love it.
