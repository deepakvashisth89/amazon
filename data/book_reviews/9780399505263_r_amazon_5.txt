Yasunari Kawabata's novel, Thousand Cranes, opens with a brief but comprehensive history of Japanese tea ceremony. The prime locale and metier of the book is tea ceremony, but the canvas of Thousand Cranes embraces ritual and rigour of all manner of enterprise: courtship, betrothal, betrayal, guilt.The complexity of personal and generational historical resonance, relations and manipulations worthy of Dangerous Liaisons is further coloured by the complexity of amorous instincts and agendas. And at the base of it all is some of the most evocative appreciative prose of the female form and spirit, writ large against the subtle canvas of stars of blanketing darkness or near-morning, the centuries-old colour and crackling of ancient tea vessels, the flowers and symbols inherent in arrangement, wardrobe, the shape and shade of lip and limb.Read moreRead less
