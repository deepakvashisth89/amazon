The characters have very little consistent characterizations, lurching around from plot point to plot point in which how they behave seems only dictated by what story direction the author wanted vs any sort of overarching heroes journey or character development. Each character was clearly drawn from real life cultures and unfortunately misses the mark in so many ways that the arcs for these civilizations manages to not be vaguely racist and sexist so much as they are absolutely so. Females are treated as little more than love interests and almost all of them die in horrible ways for the sake of the main character’s angst or as a feeble resolution of problems he created. The main character even more or less rapes an interest but it’s treated as romantic and forgivable by the narrator.The opening chapters are fabulously done and I would definitely say as far as style, this author knows how to put words together in a really poetic and epic way, but even the beauty of the writing isn’t enough to surmount often massive leaps in time and point of view as well as the heavy handed political rants scattered throughout the book.It has the foundations for an awesomely dark and deep narrative but instead falls flat due to its characterization and pacing issues.
