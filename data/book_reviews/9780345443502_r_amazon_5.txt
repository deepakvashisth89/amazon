I had conflicting personal feelings about this book. It is well-written and Mills owns a GREAT deal of cultural and historical knowledge concerning the ancient world. He truly knew his stuff when he wrote this book--including scriptural passages from the New Testament and prophetic sources from the Old Testament.Despite all of that, the MANNER in which the whole package was presented was a challenging read for me, and (I'm not trying to flatter myself here) I have a fairly good understanding of the Roman world. Mills chose to present the entire book as though Pilate was telling his story in a most rhetorical way. For the Classicist, this method might work, but how many of us study rhetoric these days? True, it might be the way a Roman statesman would have told his tale, but it isn't how a 21st century audience is going to receive and applaud it.THAT'S the reason for my three stars. Not that it was poorly written. Not that it's not faith-based, though I will make no bones about my own faith. It's simply the MANNER in which it was presented, because fiction needs to tug at a person's heartstrings, paint the reader into a different world, or make them cheer for the protagonist. None of that is here. Frankly, it's hard to label this as a "novel". And I'm still trying to determine if it might be better classified as NON-fiction with the many references to scripture and ancient history as there are interlaced into it. However, the spine very clearly delineates it as "FICTION".If I could give this five stars for its research alone--I would. However, when one picks up a work of fiction, one wishes to be transported, and there was very little world-building, but instead an IMMENSE amount of cerebral work.So, read on and decide for yourself.
