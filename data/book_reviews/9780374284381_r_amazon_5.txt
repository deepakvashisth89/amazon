Reading “The Virgin Suicides” by Jamaica Kincaid was an interesting story to read. Published by Farrar, Straus and Giroux in 1993, the story is set in Grosse Point, Michigan in the 1970’s where the plot revolves around five sisters. It is written in first person plural from the perspective of a group of teenage boys in the neighborhood who aren’t ever specifically mentioned. Over the course of a single year, loads of drama unravel. Personally, this book had a very haunting and dark sensitivity to it. It kept me intrigued as to what was going to happen next. Kincaid uses a tone that keeps the reader intact rather than dosing off after a couple of pages. This is definitely a good read if your looking for shockers. Events in this book go for a roller coaster ride that doesn’t end; going up and down continuously.Suicide isn't the happiest of topics to talk about with anyone. The suicides of five sisters is even less pleasant. You may ask how to recommend a book to someone on such a dark topic? It’s easy: just read it! Throughout the story there are so many themes as well. Everything in this story is expressed so clearly that you can easily imagine whats going on or where the story is taking place at different points in the book. This is why I enjoyed reading this book more-so now compared to other books. I was able to connect to the book with the imagery and almost see everything unraveling as the book went on. This is one of the only few books I would read that deals with death. Overall, I really enjoyed reading this group and presenting it in front of my class. Making a presentation to talk about this book was stress free. Eugenides makes this an easy and interesting read. If you are looking for a book to read, definitely give this a shot.
