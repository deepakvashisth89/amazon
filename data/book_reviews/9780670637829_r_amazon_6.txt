One could find more detailed, analytical, academic studies on how Russia staggered towards revolution after 1825, but the always readable Crankshaw here produced a readable, even charming study that makes it clear: the fall of the Romanovs was a failure of personalities, not the inevitable result of socio-economic factors.His attention to the psychology of the rulers and to contemporary literature is excellent (one thing that really sticks in my mind is his account of Dostoevsky explaining to a friend how, despite his own wishes, he would fear to report an assassination attempt to the police - the kind of detail that speaks volumes).And Crankshaw's style makes the book a pleasure to read; he isn't above being catty, as in his endnote: "Those who think Lenin was a splendid person will know just where to read all about him."Read this, and then pick up his "The Fall of the House of Habsburg."Read moreRead less
