Escape and EvasionIan Dear served in the Royal Marines before working in the film and book publishing industries. He is a writer on military and maritime history. This 1997 book has a ‘Contents’, fourteen chapters, ‘Bibliography’, and ‘Index’. Escape and evasion have always been part of warfare. St. Joseph escaped with his wife and infant from Herod into Egypt, he could be the patron saint. The Napoleonic Wars were the first conflicts where prisoners were held in any number. This book is limited to the Second World War.1. “Crockatt’s Secret Empire” tells about the organization formed to rescue escaped British Prisoners of War. There are nine items of advice to appear like a French civilian. There was a code for use in letters, a BBC radio program sent coded messages.2. “Clutty and His Escape Devices” were aids to help servicemen escape or evade capture: wire-cutters, saws, maps, and compasses. Uniforms were designed to be similar to the enemy’s.3. “The PAT Line and Its Traitors” operated on the Franco-Spanish border to rescue escaped British soldiers. One operator worked for the Abwehr and betrayed the organization.4. “SOE’s DF Section” was formed to transmit agents and supplies. They used small boats to drop off and pick up agents. Extremely secure systems allowed the line to remain in operation.5. “The One That Got Away: tells about the one German POW that escaped to return to Germany. There were attempts that failed.6. “America’s Escape Factories” was named MIS-X; all of its records were destroyed in August 1945. But some were found in the National Archives and allowed writing a book. America had 155 POW camps.7. “The Indragiri River Escape Line” was in Sumatra so the British could escape from the Japanese. They sailed to Ceylon.8. “The Escape of the Cockleshell Heroes” is about the Royal Marines attack on enemy shipping at Bordeaux. Two escaped through France to Spain.9. “The British Army Aid Group” aided escaped British POWs from Hong Kong to travel to China. There were complications from the Chinese civil war.10. “The Scarlet Pimpernel and the Rome Organization” brought escaped prisoners to the Vatican, neutral territory. Others went to Switzerland. Money was given so prisoners could subsist in hiding.11. “The Little Cyclone” was a Belgian girl who saved hundreds, mostly air crew members. Narrow escapes were almost a daily occurrence. Some were betrayed.12. “MI9's Sea Escape Lines” operated from Greece and Crete to bases in neutral Turkey. There was a line from Brittany to England.13. “Die Like the Carp!” tells about the Cowra Prison Camp in New South Wales which held Italian and Japanese prisoners. The Japanese considered capture to be shameful. They revolted and tried to escape.14. “The Vatican’s Ratlines” were used to smuggle war criminals out of Europe. This was done by anti-communists who helped them to migrate to South America.This is a rare subject for a book.
