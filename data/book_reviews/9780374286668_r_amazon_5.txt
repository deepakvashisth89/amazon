I liked most everything about The Way I Found Her.  It attempts, with considerable success, to be both a coming of age story and a mystery story.  Combining these two well-trod genres makes a lot of sense.  Tremain's writing gives us multi-layered characters and there is even the occasional burst of 13 year old humor, e.g. noting that dogs aren't much help in a crisis.  I believed Lewis both as a budding adult and, in some rare but real moments, as a scared child.And yet, for all that enjoyment, I reached the end and thought, "well, OK."  The big problem is that the mystery part of the novel just isn't mysterious enough.  I, at least, enter a mystery expecting surprises and twists, but the narrative here -- powered by a 13 year old boy who out thinks and feels superior to most adults around him -- just plows pretty much straight ahead, ending exactly as this reader expected.  The opportunity Tremain misses in her writing is that 13 year olds can be unreliable in their stories and their understanding.
