John Sundman's novel has escaped every box I've attempted to confine it to. Science fiction is a misleading category, as is cyber-punk. There is science, a plenty, and it is fiction, certainly. Some characters might be described as "punk," in my limited understanding of that term. Unlike faster-than-light travel, the science is plausible, and the setting is now.Sundman makes the willing suspension of disbelief easy, and he makes page turning (screen swiping?) compelling. He puts his protagonists in trouble at once and keeps them there. A thriller, by definition.If that were all were to this book, it might be a candidate for anyone's beach reading list.It's not--there's more. It is a deep contemplation of social media, privacy, personal identity and where the combination can trend.What's astonishing is how a novel published in 1999 -- the year after a new search engine called Google appeared, eBay was thought of as a social media site, Mark Zuckerburg was 15, Twitter was seven years in the future. Internet connection speeds for consumers were up to 5,000 times slower than speeds widely available today -- so clearly paints a nightmare scenario of where universal connectivity, deep tracking of users, and concentration of information power leads and how easily we all find it to shrug off.Reading this book will make anyone paying attention to the ideas paranoid. The journey into paranoia that Sundman takes his readers on should arrive at profound concern about the world we now inhabit.Read moreRead less
