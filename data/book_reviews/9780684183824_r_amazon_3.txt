Well written pieces that resonate deeply. Many of them start with realist settings. The supernatural is slowly introduced, through hard-to-explain events but also through the growing psychological disquiet of the main characters. Often it’s hard to pick the point where we left the natural world behind. The classic setting of an old manor house appears frequently, but is varied with other, diverse settings. Along the way there is sharp social commentary, particularly in relation to the place of women, but these stories are open-ended in terms of their impact, and will stir up different things in each reader.The lady maid’s bell – 8/10. A lady’s maid is caught in the murky interplay between two men, and the women in their lives.The Eyes - 5/10. Well written like all of these pieces but I found this rather light weight and predictable, though other readers seem to have got more from it.Afterward - 8/10. A subtle tale that combines social comment with some themes from supernatural writing.Kerfol - 7.5/10. A melancholy story with sharp observations of how values have changed over the centuries.The triumph of night – 6/10. I was riveted at first but disappointed by the end.Miss Mary Pask – 7.5/10. The different ways that you can become a ghost.Bewitched - 8/10. Perhaps the most sinister and bitter piece in the collection.Mr Jones – 6/10. Didn’t grab me as much as most of the others. It felt to me like she was writing a pot boiler.The pomegranate seed - 10/10. A perfectly composed story. The setting is early last century, a wealthy household in the USA. The happiness of a marriage is eroded over the years by the intermittent appearance of letters left for the husband,. The writing on the envelope is in a strong hand but is so pale as to be almost illegible. After each one he becomes withdrawn and this gradually eats away at his wife, yet she is unwilling to open the letters herself, and he is evasive about them. The story takes up the themes of marital distance, male barriers, female rivalry. The title seems to refer to the myth of Persephone in which pomegranates are the fruit of the dead, and eating them condemns you to stay in the otherworld.The looking glass - 7/10.
