One of the best books I've ever read, and the only one I've read more than twice - in fact, five times, and each time it was a different reading, a different experience, a different interpretation. It's not only about noticing different things each time, that perhaps I hadn't taken notice of before; the book itself allows - or calls for - different interpretations. You may agree with the narrator's point of view or question it. You may view the two protagonists' road trip as a struggle for survival or as an effort to stop a war - and a world from changing for ever; as a political or philosophical quest, or as a romance. You may view the Gethenians as men, or as women, or as both at the same time, as transgender or as neutral, or - very simply - as human beings. You may see the book itself as a story about humankind or as a very personal one. Then there's the matter of what you'll make of the short side stories & how you'll connect them to the main story. It's a book of many levels, many facets, many different hues, a whole prism of a story, in a universe of its own, within LeGuin's Hainish universe. It may help if you've read other Hainish stories, but you won't miss any of its brilliance if you haven't. You'll only miss its brilliance if you don't give it a chance. In fact, its brilliance doesn't shine on you right away, it may take its time (well, a little bit) to discover what a work of pure genius it is. The first chapter feels like an intro to a surreal but kind of gritty fairy tale, told by a somewhat annoying narrator. I almost gave it up because of that. I'm happy I didn't, as it only took another couple of chapters to draw me in completely, no less because it's one of those stories where you get to find out that things aren't always what they seem (in fact, even when it doesn't tell you so.) It's now my personal all-time favourite, but that doesn't mean everyone will experience it as I did. You need to be ready for and open to new notions. A flat, surface reading may ruin it for you. Very specific expectations of it according to its genre may leave you disappointed. (The damage that genres have done! If only we could take each and every book as unique, how much better off we could all be! But the fact remains people tend to expect very specific things, and you may not find them here.) If all you're after is fast-paced action or steamy romance, it may not even be for you. If you're not open to something different from what you're used to, it may not be for you at all. If you're looking for something to support your political, human rights, feminist, LGBTQ or whatever crusade, you may or may not find (enough of) it here. If you've never cared for different cultures, if you're the type who travels about only to count the days to get back home, perhaps you should forget about it, for this is a book about the experience of going to or being at a new place. It isn't about space battles or about good and evil. It isn't a dystopia, it isn't extrapolation, it isn't a traditional love story, a traditional quest, a traditional anything. It isn't a book that cries out how brilliant it is, either; there's a lot of subtlety about it, and at times you may need to think sideways - which is probably the reason why it's so under-appreciated by the wider public. (Another reason may be that it's hard to make a good film or TV series of it, which would turn it into a big hit without losing its essence and atmosphere - though the beautiful radio adaptation by the BBC allows for a lot of hope in that direction! Do check it out!) If you need new horizons, new worlds to open up to you, you need first to be open, too. An open mind will draw you to its uniqueness and, most importantly, its humanity, the same kind of humanity you can find in all of LeGuin's books. Its greatest literary achievement, however, is that it remains fresh, relevant, original, innovative and unique - a novelty even now, half a century after it was first published. An absolute classic, even though it may not at first feel like it. This is a book for the eternal traveler, even if only through the pages and the long, winding paths of the mind. Just remember, when you travel to a new world, you need to at least try to understand its customs, its notions, its values, its culture.
