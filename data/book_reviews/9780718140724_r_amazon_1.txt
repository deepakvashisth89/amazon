I was considering doing the Pennine Way and read his book, but it didn't appeal to me as much as the Coast to Coast, so I switched focus to the latter walk.  It's such a delight to read his guidebooks, with his whimsical style; wonderful draughting of the diagrams, maps and drawings;  and quaint jokes scattered here and there.  It really brings home to you his passion for the Lake District -- I had toured through there in a car in 1996, but always wanted to go back and walk it.Naturally I got the full set of the LandRanger 1:50 000 topo maps as well, since it's never safe enough to rely on a strip map in a guidebook for finding your way in whiteout conditions or bad weather (common in this area)-- and we weren't planning to go lickety-split and do the Walk in the normal way -- galloping along the valleys and getting your backpack carried by a van to the night's accommodation in a Youth Hostel or B&B etc.  We took full packs with tents etc, so that we could camp out in the Lake District and do side trips up to the major peaks (e.g Scafell Pike) and save some money.  It was staged so that we stopped about every 7 days in towns to clean up, have a good feed and a grog, and get back out .  All up we took 28 days, about half the speed of those gallopers.Many a time the trail was confusing; and I had to get out Wainwright to find out the path through farmers' front yards; across the paddocks; over the stiles; and through towns.  A few times we got "confused" and went cross country , getting into trouble with farmers who weren't impressed with us going through their landOne time we were scolded by a sheep grazier for camping in his upper paddocks.
