If you like quirky, nihilistic characters and absurd plots, this book (and series) will be right up your alley.  The author's nihilism, amorality, and overt sexism infects all of this series but is really strong in this book.  Hence, I have read my last book in this series by van de Wetering.  Some of the earlier books were not so overt with regard to the author's philosophies and his 1970's-1980's sexism and so were more bearable.  They also focused more on realistic crimes instead of absurd plots that appear as the series progresses.I don't mind that van de Wetering has fun with his books--putting his characters in absurd situations that are totally unrealistic and often even cartoonish.  The dialogue between the two detectives, de Guier and Grijpstra, though a little hard to follow at times, is clever and amusing.  The Amsterdam setting of the 1970s and 1980s is kind of intriguing, though I don't enjoy the sections in the redlight quarter.  So, there were a lot of unique aspects to this series that initially drew me in until van de Wetering began to be too cute in his plots and dialogue as well as began focusing a lot on his philosophical beliefs."Hard Rain" was quite an engrossing read. The plot involved the Commissaris's old schoolmate and nemesis who heads an evil empire that is run through a charitable organization.  The commissaris is forced to deal with this friend/enemy against his will when several murders lead him to the man's bank and to his son. The dialogue between the two is not just back-and-forth threats but involves philosophical points of view.  The scheming games they play are intriguing.  In this book, however, I weary of more of the nihilistic talk about the world being meaningless, about morality being nonsense, etc.  But most of all, I am so sick of the way the author depicts females of his era who are striving to be feminists.  As far as he is concerned, they are only interested in sexual equality, which means the commissaris' secretary says women can now make disgusting sexual comments just like men and that she might like to be a whore sometimes because whores have a lot of fun. The secretary then prostitutes herself for a few days on behalf of the case and the Commissaris lets her.  Ugh!  Also, the discussions by the male officers about the women in the police department are always about their bodies, and van de Wetering can't describe a woman without mentioning her breasts.
