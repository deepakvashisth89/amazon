The protagonist of this book, Eddie Nye, framed for a crime he didn't commit, locked up for 15 years after killing two inmates inside and coming out completely alienated, with a case of what feels like shell shock to boot, is an extremely evocative, believable creation. Even his obsession with Coleridge's 'Rime of the Ancient Mariner", which should be cutesy and forced kinda sorta works -- I've known guys like this and a lot of them tend to be autodidacts, which almost by definition ends up meaning "intellectually obsessed with odd things".Unfortunately he doesn't get a story worthy of him. This here is an odd mish-mash, part of it is "Eddie searches for the answers on who set him up", answers which will not surprise you one bit if you've ever read a crime book ever, I assure you, and part of it is an extremely convoluted plot that, as far as I can grasp it, hinges on the improbable notion that Nye would unthinkingly do something predictable (or to be precise, that the plotter sees as predictable) on the outside.It reminds me a lot of lesser crime books that came out during their Golden Era in the Fifties and early Sixties. Many of those suffered from restrictive page counts, and you often got the idea that a much bigger, much better novel was somewhere inside there, fighting to get out. I'm not sure Abrahams had that excuse, though. Another idea is that this is a fixup novel from an aborted attempt at a series character -- the two plot strands are so different they almost read like they belong in  different books.In any event, this whole thing is very disappointing, made worse due to the nice touches here and there, making you wonder what could've been.Read moreRead less
