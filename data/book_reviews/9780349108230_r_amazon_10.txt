Jane Gardam's novel "Faith Fox" covers a wide range of topics and contrasts: the English north versus the South, the young and the old, the rich and the poor, the noble and the common people, the ex-hippie versus the traditional old Schoolmaster... And the author confronts us with a great variety of characters: There's Andrew, the young widower and medical doctor, left alone with a sweet baby daughter, Faith, who he does not seem to relate to at all. There's the baby's grandma Thomasina (What a name!) who used to be a perfect mother to her only daughter Holly. Shocked by her daughter's death and desperate by the great loss she flees from possible duties and rather escapes with a smart ex-general who however does not seem to live up to her expectations in the end. By the way, the general also gets entangled with Madeleine,  an old flame .. now a bit gaga - from decades ago...Everybody is on the move - mostly, from Yorkshire to Surrey and London, from London to Egypt and Scotland, down to Kent or for a short break in Oxford or Stamford.We accompany some of the characters to a big stately wedding, to a drab little teashop,  (almost) to a funeral, we experience Christmas and a christening.Altogether Jane Gardam developed a complex and intricate, if not somewhat bizarre, plot. Her characters fascinate and sometimes disappoint you (Why must Andrew have an affair with his brothers's wife?)However, those characters with some negative features are often really amusing: beastly grandfather Toots- an invalid suffering from MS -, the two teenage - half criminals with their brad Yorkshire acccent, and the loyal housekeeper Alice who is something like an angel of death to old Puffy.Well - did I whet your appetites? The novel is solid entertainment there is some suspense in it as well as moments of comic relief. Even though it may be deficient in consistency or credibility it is worth reading.Read moreRead less
