I had just finished reading Erasmus' famous tract `Concerning Free Will', and wanted to read Luther's even more famous response.  This is from the kindle edition and I thought it was a decent version.  There were not many extras such as a linked table of contents or many footnotes, but I found it sufficient for my needs.  This edition also included a couple of letters from Luther to some friends where he made additional negative comments about Erasmus.  It was very interesting reading.I must state up front that I am a firm believer in free will (I'm LDS), so this will color my reading of Luther.I found the first part of the book to be mainly a personal attack upon Erasmus.  The contrast between Luther and Erasmus' style was startling.  Erasmus is very tactful and careful not to offend and Luther is blunt and unpolished.Luther eventually settles into his arguments using logic and scripture and here he does a better job, though he makes personal attacks throughout.  His main argument is that since man is totally dependent upon God's help for his salvation, that he cannot possibly have free will.  He defines grace not as `divine assistance' as I do, but as completely `unmerited favor'.  This means that he believes that man can do absolutely nothing to obtain grace.This creates all kinds of problems that he tries to explain in very unsatisfying ways.  He believes that God is responsible for not only good, but also evil.  He defines two types of God's will, the first is `God Preached' and the second is `God Hidden'.  The latter is the stuff that we cannot comprehend and is his explanation of all of the evil and suffering in the world.  He basically says that it is not for us to question God on why so many people suffer and burn in hell forever and why he chooses at random to save some of these miserable creatures.  He has His own reasons that we will never understand.I personally agree with Erasmus and others that make clear that though we cannot be saved without God's help (grace); it is up to us to choose whether or not we will follow Christ.  I do not agree that having free will equals having the ability to save ourselves.Erasmus defined free will as follows:  `By freedom of the will we understand in this connection the power of the human will whereby man can apply to or turn away from that which leads unto eternal salvation.'  I think Luther got caught up with the idea that choosing to follow Christ meant that man could save himself without Christ's help.  That is not what Erasmus taught in his sermon.One last observation; I found copies of Luther's response to Erasmus almost everywhere on the kindle or any other format I wanted.  I had an extremely hard time finding a copy of Erasmus' original argument and could only find it as a used book out of print for 50 years.
