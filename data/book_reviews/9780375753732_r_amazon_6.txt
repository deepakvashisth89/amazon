I felt like this book had no specific purpose or identity. I also felt like it was written over a large expanse of time (as the author seemed to change his position on subjects) and possible even written by more than one author.It starts out as a woman who marries below her station in life, and the troubles of living with a collier who likes to drink away the family paycheck, and the struggles of starting a life together. For a bit the story turns away and focuses on the first born son. The moves to a different son. The finally settles in on the "second favorite" son of the mother.The story then moves through is coming of age, relationship to money (due to his father issues), relationships with women (due to his mother issues), and relationships with his peers (due to his brother issues). It finally resides with a young man, of no serious direction, having really made no decisions, having any resolve and no final answers.This book was long, thick, heavy and riddled with bad British references (to make it quaint)... and an occasional British word thrown out of position for "scholarly appeal".
