I was absolutely captivated by this book! Kingdon Ward was an interesting, observant, bold, resourceful, and somewhat eccentric man and it was a treat to read of his adventures in his own voice.I think great credit must go to the editor here, Tom Christopher, who provides short but critical context and savvy commentary, and who also chose these excerpts from what I imagine may have been a much larger body of work. He proves to be an outstanding guide, introducing us to what I certainly trust is the best and most salient parts of Kingdon Ward's writings. Bravo.Giving the book 4 stars instead of 5 because I agree with some of the other reviewers here that the book is too short. It would be a wonderful book if the maps were larger and easier to follow (with our hero's routes marked on them); it would be a wonderful book if there were accompanying photographs or illustrations.Nonetheless, an entertaining, nay, riveting peek into a lost world. (I have a son who loves to read fantasy books, from Tolkein to Patrick Rothfuss, and I remarked to him in the context of reading this book, "Why make up a world, when our own world is so very strange and wonderful?!")Read moreRead less
