The book was repetitive and narrator slow. I returned the Audible version but kept the Kindle.  The heroine is not particularly one of Stewart’s brightest, but the book is salvageable only because its characters are interesting. The story contains insight into the sometimes abusive interactions between men and women which mirror ones we may observe in our own lives or those of our loved ones.Following are my own thoughts on the book which may contain some slight spoilers.What makes women think they can change the minds of abusive men?  The heroine is real enough in that she displays a common fault of our sex in the way she seeks to manipulate the abusive men in her life into doing what was right, excusing their faults as simply manifestations of her own weakness. She seems to be alone and ill used by every man in the book, including her love interest.  I suppose, for me,  that is what saved the book from the rather ridiculous plot. She played the preposterous, manipulative game because she felt she had no other person on which to depend except herself.  This book was not my favorite of Stewart’s works, but the way the men used power and the way the women responded to it did seem true to life which can serve as a warning to avoid such people and such mistakes in real life.Read moreRead less
