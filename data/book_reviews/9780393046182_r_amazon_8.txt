I picked up Oyster at a thrift store, having never heard of Janette Turner Hospital. The book's packaging (the copy I found was from Australia) was attractive and the subject matter seemed interesting, so I forked over the dollar. Best buck I've ever spent.If I were teaching the Craft of Fiction in an undergraduate creative writing class, Oyster is the book I'd have them read toward the end of the semester, when they've learned the basic elements of the writing craft, e.g., character development, plotting and developing a story idea, narrative structure, use of diction and dialogue, use of foreshadowing, exploration and evidence of theme and, in particular, effective exposition, Hospital's well honed writing skill. In other words, Oyster is a good book to have in your writer's toolbox if you happen to have one of those.It's also a damned good novel, and Hospital is now one of my favorite authors, comparable to Margaret Atwood, Michael Cunningham, Ian McEwan and a few other contemporary writers destined to be remembered.Caveat emptor: Oyster is not an easy book. If you're looking for a nice, relaxing story, you won't find it in Oyster. Hospital makes her readers work, which not everyone enjoys having to do while reading a novel. If you fall into this group, don't feel bad -- just don't expect a lot of satisfaction if you choose to read Oyster. I think it's worth the effort if only for the experience of Outer Maroo, and I hope that even if a reader isn't interested in putting in the work, he or she still enjoys the story.Read moreRead less
