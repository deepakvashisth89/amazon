There are quite a few famous detectives in fiction. In my experience, Holmes, Poirot, Marple, Queen, and Brown crowded out the field for most casual mystery readers, so I don't think most people are as familiar with the team-up of Nero Wolfe and Archie Goodwin. Which, after reading this volume, I can certify as a shame.Fer-de-lance quickly subverts many of the traditions of mystery fiction. Our two heroes operate out of New York City, rather than England. Nero Wolfe is a man who has made quite a bit of money in his youth, and now wants nothing more than to indulge in his passions. While he still takes a few cases for supplemental income, he has committed himself to never leaving his home. To that end, he hires Archie Goodwin, himself a competent investigator, as a secretary and gofer, delegating all the work of investigating crime scenes to him. Together, the two take on a missing person case in their debut novel.The best thing about this novel is the prose. Archie Goodwin as a character hails from the film-noir genre, providing heaps of sarcastic insights into the people and world around him. I also appreciate how Stout illustrated his competence as an investigator, rather than just a blank slate to watch the main sleuth figure things out. And of course, Wolfe himself was a treat, being just as eccentric as Poirot, but with different tastes. He certainly had one of the best establishing character moments I've read in a long time.The one the flaw I found was in the central mystery. This story was a workmanlike 'investigation procedural', lacking in any red herrings or clever twists to keep the reader guessing.
