I cannot remember when I first read about the existence of this book (However, I can tell you it was way before the movie). This year – as it has been the most difficult in my life – I decided to finally read the book.If it’s your first time reading one of his books, you’ll take time to get used to his writing techniques as he doesn’t use punctuation the way it’s intended to be used. But after that initial shock I was fascinated.I was fascinated because he delivers good storytelling and writing is more about telling a story than using right techniques and all that bureaucracy. Here we have another fantasy where a man finds another man identical to him in every single aspect with bizarre coincidences. The story unfolds in a way you would never expect. Saramago takes risks, he’s not afraid to go “there”. As an amateur writer myself, I would have never gone there but there’s a reason why he’s a Nobel Literature Prize laureate. I am not going to give you all the details of the story but to analyze a little bit of it.First, and it’s been said before, the first theme of this book is identity: Who are we? This question is answered every day with your name, ID or whatever but TMA (the protagonist) had a perfect, but boring, life as a teacher thinking he knew who he was and it was suddenly contested by this fact.Another theme is the complexity of relationships. The protagonist enjoys loneliness even if the authentically loves his girlfriend and his mother. This hits me because I also enjoy loneliness and having a space and time for myself. Also, Saramago uses irony and sarcasm to add comedy to a dramatic story with an irregular ending and complex characters.There are several other themes addressed by the author on this book but to finish my review I want to address another one that hits me: change. At the end of the book the protagonist is forced, by circumstances, to assume a role he never thought he would assume. And he could escape of it but he doesn’t want to.
