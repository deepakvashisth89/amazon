After reading "The Winning of Barbara Worth," I found a copy of this author's 1907 novel, "The Shepherd of the Hills."  Daniel Howitt is a man with a past that he wants to elude.  He comes into the Ozarks and connects with Jim Lane.  He stays and tends to their sheep in return for living quarters in a small dwelling down by the pastures.  The hills seem to be home to mystical beings.  Many question whether ghosts exist as apparitions are seen and seem to fade into the woods.  Jim Lane's daughter Sammy is set to marry Ollie, whose rich uncle in the city will set Ollie up in town, a place that Sammy and none of the villagers have seen.  Sammy wants to be able to learn the sophisticated manners of a woman in the city to better fit in and convinces Daniel "Dad" Howitt to school her.  Her friend Matt Matthews is a big strong country boy with a heart of gold.  They've been friends so long that they try to explain away the strange attraction they have for one another.  Coupled with the fact that Sammy's fiancée Ollie is a man of slight build, we have a romantic triangle that needs to resolve.  Into the mix we have the brute character of Wash Gibbs, also a big man, who bullies many of the people.  There is a conflict with Matt that results in contests of strength, ambushes and assault.  As the novel weaves all of the threads together in the experience of Daniel Howitt, we find a man who comes to the hills and in the still of nature comes in touch with God.
