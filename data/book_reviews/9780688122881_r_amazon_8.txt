This is an interesting book with a somewhat misleading title.  I would not classify any of the material in this book is as being a "dirty little secret".  In other words, none of it is salacious and very little of it is something that someone or some government is trying to hide.  In fact none of the material is particularly secret, as it can be found in many other books.The book consists of facts, factoids and statistical information, presented in a very interesting and quite readable format.  It covers all of WWII, with perhaps an emphasis on the European theater of the war.  Unlike many books containing statistical information, I did not feel over burdened by it, as it was provided along with a lot of interesting text and facts that I was not aware of.  Let me cite one example.  Tracer rounds, about one in every five bullets, were used in WWII aircraft to allow the pilot to see where the rounds were landing.  Unfortunately, the ballistic characteristics of these rounds differed from that of the rest of the bullets, so hitting the target with them, meant that the other bullets would not be doing so,  and since only 1 in 5 of the bullets were tracers, most would be missing their target.  Furthermore, the tracers alerted the target that he we being fired upon, allowing him to take evasive action.  Thus, those outfits that stopped using tracers found their hits increase by 50-100%.While this book covers a lot of material and provides a lot of insight into WWII, I found that it did omit a lot.  For instance, there is no mention of the battle for Peleliu, which was one of the most horrific battles in the Pacific, and perhaps the most unnecessary.
