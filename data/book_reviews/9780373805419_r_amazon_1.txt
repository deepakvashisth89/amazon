*************FROM THE BACK OF THE BOOK******************Secrets and LiesWhen Lydia accepts a job in Bermuda as a nanny, she is stunned to discover her young blind charge's father is none other than Blaine Caldwell. She and Blaine shared a strong, sensuous bond thirteen years earlier, before their marriage plans had been cruelly shattered. Blaine's father had forced Lydia's departure by telling her a horrifying secret. Now the two experience that overpowering attraction again, but Lydia must tell Blaine the truth before the unthinkable happens...************************************************************Okay, I'm not even sure where to begin and I'm not sure how to post a review for this book without giving away what the secret is, but here goes...Blaine's father didn't want Lydia to date or marry his son, so he told her a lie. Think of the worst thing (the most disgusting thing) you could be told about someone you're dating and having sex with and that's the lie he told. Poor Blaine doesn't know anything, other than his model fiancee suddenly dumps him. Now forced together on a tropical island, with Lydia taking care of Blaine's son who has serious trust issues, she fights off her attraction as well as Blaine's advances.Sorry, I can't do this....spoiler alert.Blaine's father tells Lydia that he had an affair with her mother and so she and Blaine are actually brother and sister, only Blaine doesn't know about the lie, and doesn't understand why Lydia is even denying that they'd had sex 13 years before. Later we find out that because she believed she committed incest (and after her subsequent miscarriage) she had a nervous breakdown.I don't know what to say other than I found the entire story weird, odd, and troubling. And because she spends the entire book thinking Blaine is her brother, well, there is no romance, for obvious reasons. Which is why I don't understand why Harlequin would publish it as such. I mean, she doesn't find out that she isn't Blaine's sister until the end of the book, and so, neither does the reader, and by that time I'm too weirded out to care about the conclusion.I've given this 2 stars because the writing was decent, just that the subject matter is troubling.Read moreRead less
