In 1879, British High Commissioner Sir Henry Bartle Frere initiated a scheme to advance his political career by forming a confederation in South Africa, similar to that completed in Canada. With the Zulu nation standing between him and success, he put forth a series of inappropriate demands on the Zulu king and, when those demands were (of course) rejected, indicated the Zulus must be "put down" by the surely superior British military.This book is a novelization of the first battle of the Zulu-Anglo War. Local Boers and other residents were well aware of the threat the highly competent Zulus represented - armed with spears and a few outmoded guns but far more mobile and with enormously superior numbers - if angered, but the British command sadly counted titles and uniforms over contemporary experience.As a novelization, Endfield's book is accurate as to events and attitudes - as a quick look at Wikipedia and contemporary accounts will verify. It served as the basis of the similarly named movie, starring Peter O'Toole as commanding (and apparently only competent if facing Napoleon) Lord Chelmsford.Similar to other routs, such as Kabul and Cawnpore, the Battle of Isandlwana resulted in massive retaliatory aggression on the part of the British Army in subsequent campaigns, and changed the face of South Africa forever.
