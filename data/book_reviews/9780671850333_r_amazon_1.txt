This book was written in the early 1990s, so at this point it is quite dated - but I found that to be one of its redeeming qualities, as it is a fascinating snapshot of the rumors and hoaxes of a particular point in time.  It's from a few years before the internet existed for most people - from a time when rumors and hoaxes were mostly perpetuated by newspapers, television, radio, and actual word of mouth.It was interesting to read this and realize how many of the same rumors are still going around today, 20+ years later, in one form or another.  On the other hand a lot of the content that revolved around the political issues-of-the-moment now feels far in the distant past, if remembered at all.  Many of the stories are about issues and scandals of the George H.W. Bush administration, for example.The book has some nostalgia value at this point.  The majority of the content is pulled from the news of the 70s and 80s. It also contains a variety of hoaxes and scandals pulled from history, and from various parts of the world.The writing style is conversational, and it's an easy/light read, but sometimes it also reads like the rambling thoughts of somebody's unedited stream-of-consciousness recalling of stories, complete with the story-teller's opinions and off-topic tangents.  Sometimes the text is repetitive, and some sentences are so awkwardly constructed that it took a few re-readings to make sure I understood things like which character he was talking about, or where and when the story took place. I also encountered many misspellings and wrong homonyms that further confused things.  Frankly this is so amateurishly written that while I was reading it I initially thought it must be someone's self-published vanity project, and was surprised when I checked and found that it had a real publisher.Overall it was a good glimpse back at some classic urban legends, rumors and scandals.  I don't think there's any reliable information in here that would make this useful for any research purposes - but that's kind of the point.Read moreRead less
