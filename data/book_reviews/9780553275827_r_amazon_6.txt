James R. McDonough's "Platoon Leader" did for a platoon leader in Vietnam in his book what Charles MacDonald did for the company commander in WWII in his "Company Commander: The Classic Infantry Memoir of World War II."  "Platoon Leader" captures the sights, sounds, and smells of the battlefield including the unforgiving nature of combat and the seemingly randomness of combat.  As shown in "Platoon Leader," mistakes and errors of judgment in combat usually result in death or serious injury. During my 30-year Army career as a field artilleryman including a combat tour in Vietnam as a lieutenant, I often reflected on the randomness of combat and why one soldier would be hit and another unscathed.The book underscores the sacrificial love soldiers have for each other in combat even though they might not be friends in civilian society or even in their base camp.  "Platoon Leader" emphasizes the importance of training and discipline during combat operations.  The author does not shy away from the ethical and moral dilemmas leaders in combat face.  The forthright manner in which he presents such issues left me pondering how I would have reacted."Platoon Leader" is one of the best books on selfless leadership I have ever read and should be mandatory reader for all officers.  I wish that it had been available before I went to Vietnam.  Although I had a paper back copy for over 20 years, I truly regret that I just got around to reading on my iPad what should be regarded as a military classic on small unit leadership.
