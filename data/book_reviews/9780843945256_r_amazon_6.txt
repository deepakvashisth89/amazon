I'm embarrassed to write that I've read only two books by John Shirley, and I read them a decade apart.  It's a damn shame, because he's such a talented writer.  My first encounter was his short story collection, BLACK BUTTERFLIES.  I was in awe, especially with THAT WORLD.  I looked for his name in the bookstores, but I couldn't find much more.  That was 12 years ago.  My lack of success in finding his work stemmed from ignorance.  The man has a mountain of work.Just this year I read WETBONES.  I'm not going to offer a summary as so many of these reviews do.  I think you should ignore the negative reviews of this novel.  If you want to read a horror novel that doesn't flinch at the environment and consequences of addiction, a story that's not "kinda gross", but provokes a shuddering revulsion in the way that the best horror novels do.  It's a tale darkened by an otherworldly, supernatural presence that Lovecraft would have written about in a letter to August Derleth.  Most important, WETBONES rises above the mire because the story is rooted in strong characters that demand the reader's concern and attention.I loved it.  Now, armed with the internet, I can find and read Shirley's fiction to my greedy reading heart's content.So, read this book.  It's a discovery.Read moreRead less
