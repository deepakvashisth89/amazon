Growing up in the 50's and 60's and then having a career of 33-years as an environmental scientist, I experienced firsthand much of what makes up the body of this story.  The Cold War, the Civil Rights Movement, the Women’s Rights Movement, political assassinations, the Environmental Movement, and what might be considered a resurgence of civil disobedience as a tool in achieving social and political change tempered me on that road to adulthood, and surely influenced my political, professional and philosophical beliefs.  Alston Chase has reconstructed the cultural context for examining Environmentalism in America, focusing particularly on the Pacific Northwest and the logging industry.  It is a tale of extreme points of view. Of science ignored and poor science exploited.  Not to mention an unhealthy dose of politics and corporate greed.  You may find your allegiances changing over the course of this read, and that’s a good thing.  There has always been a degree of friction between those of the natural resource management persuasion and those of the preservationist persuasion.  My belief, for what it’s worth, is that in a free and open society it is good to have both.  This book has at times challenged that belief, and to that I can only say that those were very special times and I hope society learned a lesson from it all.
