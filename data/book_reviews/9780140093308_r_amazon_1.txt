There actually is a lot of plot in this book, a group of terrorists trying to set off a nuclear bomb in New York City, a child-torturing devil-worshipper turned skid-row serial killer, Vietnam horror stories, the Sorbonne riots of the 60's, a mafia mass-murder, an American drug courier's unpleasant stay in a Mexican prison, and a former junkie disfigured by his own mother turned arsonist. Not to mention  appearances by a ghost, a few cases of spontaneous human combustion and a very Dostoevsky-ish devil.Yet for some unknown reason, Bell decides to start off with a longish episode of the main protagonist watching the changes in the sky. (This follows a Roscoe and Enos prologue where if you blink you might not realize a murder just took place.)After about the first hundred pages, it's a tough book to put down, but those first hundred or so pages are a bit more of a struggle than they should have been.  Bell knows how to tell a story but for a while there he seems more intent on showing he can write pretty.Two stars off, one for the slow pacing at the start and one for a bit too much magical realism,  (the ghost added nothing and made me start to resent the devil worshipper and the spontaneous human combustion cases as well.)Read moreRead less
