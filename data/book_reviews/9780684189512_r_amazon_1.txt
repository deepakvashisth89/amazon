This is a piece of "token" history.  It is compiled from letters sent home by a missionary couple (most by the wife)  in China 1890-1900.  They relate a time  when the missionary appeal, especially to China,  was strong among Young American college graduates.  These letters describe  living in a village in interior China in a house lacking most expected comforts.  They did have several servants.  The letters are not complaints, in spite of difficult living conditions, and two of their children died, a third was born.  They steadfastly believed that  they were doing "the Lord's work" in trying to convert as many Chinese as  they could to Protestant Christianity.  There are reports of hearing anti-Western sentiments, some violence, which came closer to them in the last years as the movement known as the Boxer Rebellion raged into their neighborhood and murdered a number of missionaries, including the Prices and their young daughter.
