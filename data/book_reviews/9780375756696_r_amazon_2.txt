I was reading an essay on Keats recently, and realized that I only had two books of Keats's poetry, the attractive but brief volume in the Everyman's Library Pocket Poets series, and a 50-year-old Bantam paperback which is still in fairly good shape, but which doesn't have an index of titles or first lines. This book fills the gap very well. The Preface begins, "This book has been prepared on the principle that students of a poet have a right to all the available evidence on his artistic life. Books of selected poems, with minor pieces carefully excluded, fail to furnish such evidence." There are notes at the bottom of many pages. The ones I read are helpful, and they do not detract from reading the poems.The book is compact, with clear, readable type, and in very good shape considering that it was published in 1935.
