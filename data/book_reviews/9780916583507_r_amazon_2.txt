Many novels are well written, captivating and inventive but, ultimately, simple stories. They entertain readers for the period of time it takes to go from first to last page, impart some lasting images and invent, perhaps, a character or two to join the reader's own personal world of imaginary friends and acquaintances. Many enthrall with scintillating plotlines, exotic locations and fantastic participants. As such, writing is a noble craft, a welcome source of diversion and distraction from our day-to-day existence. And often that is all readers want or need.This book is more. This books reaffirms that writing fiction can be an art. And that a book well-written is beautiful in a truly singular way.Structured in ways different from most fiction it can be a challenge at first to allow the creation of the "suspension of disbelief" required of any fictional account. But once achieved the effect is mesmerizing, a story opening and growing and shifting and connecting in ways that not only linger long after placing the book back on the shelf but regenerating and reconfiguring each time the pages are reopened. Not just a "story" (is it even a story at all?) Not just a memorable character (is the character even "real"?) Not just a verbal creation (is the narrator omniscient or even reliable?): This is a book that bends and moves and goes its own way.And I use physical metaphors intentionally: I can only wonder what possible manifestations the tale might have been subsumed into if electronic/ digital media had been part of the publishing reality when this book was written (I welcome reading the perspective of someone who read this digitally instead of on paper: Any unique insights?) Perhaps William Gibson has an as yet unwritten masterpiece that could rise to that challenge...In short, I give my ultimate praise: After multiple readings over multiple years this is now firmly ensconced among my personal favorite books.
