I'm addicted to the cozy mysteries of the Golden Age, and Elizabeth Daly was one of the best. She was more than a maker of puzzles, she was an especially gifted novelist, with an incisive sense of brevity that assumes some intelligence on the part of the reader. Having read most of her books, I liked this one the best so far.Adding spice to the reading experience, I'm pretty sure this novella was inspired by a real-life unsolved mystery, the disappearance in 1910 of New York rich girl, Dorothy Arnold. While out shopping one morning, Miss Arnold bought herself a box of chocolates and promptly fell off the face of the earth. She was never seen or heard from again. No explanation, no body, no letter to Daddy saying she was running away, nothing, even to this day. I'm sure that at the time Elizabeth Daly wrote this novella, the mystery was still being discussed around New York, since Miss Arnold was from a prominent, wealthy family.
