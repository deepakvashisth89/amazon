I first read 'The Passing Bells' as an RD condensed book thirty or so years ago.  About once every five years I reread it.  Rock is not a famous name as a novelist, but in my private opinion this one outshines many offerings by famous names.  This novel has a clear objective which it achieves very well.The point of the book is to show the effects of a massive social upheaval, (World War One), on the lives of a number of various characters involved willy-nilly.  The characters are carefully presented and their various choices and reactions plausible.  The reader lives through some of their difficulties with them.Reading this novel is an enjoyable, if sobering, experience.  Moreover, when you turn back to read other books about that particular war, you find that this book makes its contribution.  Try reading it next to Babara Tuchman's well-known 'Guns of August' and you will know that well-written fiction has a serious role to play alongside narrative history.Ivan SayerRead moreRead less
