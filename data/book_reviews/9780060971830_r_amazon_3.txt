This book is illuminating. Yes It does require advanced reading skills and she does take a holistic approach, diverging to many different personalities but this is necessary to paint a picture of the time period and makes it easier to analyze and make conclusions.She gets criticized for her use or disregard  of references, but here is the thing.  If the references are defective then obviously they have to be disregarded. What impressed me is her use of character references to make conclusions that are starkly different from the references. One example. If the Lady Of The Lake was a master healer and had her hospitals to heal people, then for her to murder someone, let alone her mentor, master and friend, Merlin (Goodrich convinced me it was St. Dubrius in disguise) then this would be so contradictory and out of character for the Lady that the alternative that she stood by him and helped him till his dying day becomes much more acceptable. Goodrich uses good old common sense, logic and reasoning, and that is what so called historians who belittle her, fail to appreciate.She does not hide or conceal any negative references or slander about Merlin and the Lady of Lake, but brings it up in detail and then methodically demolishes it by showing the inconsistencies and the contradictions in the references slandering the Lady and Merlin.
