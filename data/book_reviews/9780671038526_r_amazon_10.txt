Quite a decent effort for a Trek book. It's interesting to explore the idea of a Federation "Dreanought", essentially a starship built as a heavyweight battleship rather than an explorer, and Ms Carey does a great job in presenting it - this is almost like a Star Trek technothriller in the Tom Clancy vein, actually.The idea of focusing on the minor characters is also interesting, but in this case it's an idea that does have its drawbacks. It's good to get a look from the lower decks, so to speak, seeing things from the point of view of those who aren't at the center of the action. But that said, the story turns out to pivot around those minor characters... and that feels kind of ill at ease. It's like, imagine you're watching a Trek film where Kirk is dealing with the situation and then somebody says "Oh, it's alright Captain, there's a Lieutenant on deck 12 who has sorted it all out for you." Okay, honestly Dreadnought! is not as extreme as that - Kirk and the senior staff *do* get to take part in the proceedings. But even so, it's weird to see so much of the story revolve around somebody who, in the Star Trek universe, would normally be a very minor part of things. There's definitely a touch of Mary Sue about the main character.Still, it's an engaging tale, well crafted, holds the attention, and is well worth a read.Read moreRead less
