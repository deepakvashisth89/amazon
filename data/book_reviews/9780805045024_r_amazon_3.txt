This is the third of Carkeet's Jeremy Cook tales I've read. He has created an unusual, off-beat, slightly ambiguous, but endearing protagonist who blunders along in his relations with others, somehow managing to come up with viable solutions to the situations in which he finds himself entangled. The speech scene at the end is particularly hilarious with Cook expounding on some unorthodox theories about the vocabulary of conservatives.  I do have to agree with the reviewer who was disappointed in the ending.  It seems too cynical, and negates Cook's epiphany, his altruistic act, and his plans for a new life--almost as if Carkeet had tired of him.  Still, I wondered if this isn't a clever smokescreen, leading to another Jeremy Cook book, and was half-expecting him to crop up in "From Away".  Anyone who has read H.G. Wells' "The History of Mr. Polly", will understand why.  From the blurb I just read, it doesn't seem so, but who knows--maybe a future project for Carkeet?Read moreRead less
