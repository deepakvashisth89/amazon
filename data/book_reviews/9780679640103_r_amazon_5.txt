Henry Adams, great grandson of John and Abigail Adams, grandson of John Quincy Adams, lived an amazing life.Given the time, place and position of his birth, he lived among the most important Americans from before the US Civil War through to the early 20th Century.  Living in Boston, Washington, London and endlessly traveling through Europe, he was friends and confidants of the greatest politicians, artists, engineers, explorers and artists of his time.  A list of his contemporaries placed at the front of the book reads like a Who’s Who of American and European society.He saw diplomacy firsthand as the personal secretary to his bis father, Charles Adams, who served as Lincoln’s ambassador to England during the Civil War, whose job was to keep England neutral.  He was a writer and historian of his day, with a keen eye on politics and diplomacy, appalled by the intrigues and scandals of the robber barons and corrupt government officials.Adams’ style is charming and self deprecating, often funny, and feels like you’re sitting in an 1800s salon over brandy and cigars as an erudite, witty, sensitive, deeply educated and thoughtful man takes you through his incredibly rich life.  In the last couple of chapters his attempts to weave together theories of history with endless references to “force” and “inertia” are a bit tedious and hard to follow, but his similar attempts to predict the enormous rate of speed and enormity of technological change wrought by the Industrial Revolution, was remarkably prescient.This is a tour de force by a brilliant historian who lived and had a front row seat to some of the most interesting and overlooked of all American history.Read moreRead less
