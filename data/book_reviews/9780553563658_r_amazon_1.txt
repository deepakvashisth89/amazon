Trust me, I wanted to like this but it has several flaws. It's main problem is that it feels like a mostly random mess of unrelated scenes until the final chapters where it gains focus and improves a great deal.PROS:-While based on scripts written for the Saturday morning Batman:TAS cartoon show, it still feels like the writer carefully wrote his text to appeal to an older audience as well as young fans.-As such, the general style of the writing is fine/good.-The characters/setting of the Batman universe are portrayed as appropriately cool/interesting and the plot starts to become engaging (perhaps even emotional?) in its final chapters.CONS:-The first 2/3rds of the book is not that much fun to read. Here's the reasons why...-The way the novel is written is that it depicts one short scene after another, again and again. Now maybe this would be okay. But very little happens in most of these scenes. And many of them feel pointless.^^^Also, very few of them feel like they are connected to each other. It just seems like a disjointed mess, as if someone were flipping channels on various Batman episodes and just writing down whatever they saw for a few minutes-Because of this as well, there are a ton of "named" characters included in the book (mostly civilians/thugs not super-villains or anything). And of course, many of them feel unimportant/unnecessary.^^^And the addition of all these "too many" characters just serves to add more confusion in a book that begs to be streamlined.-I'm someone who is good at suspension of disbelief but most of this book stretches it due mainly to the fact that a million coincidences are happening on one night.^^^It'd be like if the only full moon of the year came out, on your birthday, which was also the day of your high school reunion, and also ended up being the day a madman bombed half the city, while the mayor was kidnapped by someone else, and so on.^^^It's just kind of weird and it pulls you out of the story. Especially since none of the things feel connected or related to each other.-It is not until about 40 pages and 8 chapters in that anything interesting at all starts to happen, and even then...(it's not like you haven't read far better elsewhere).-This book is about half the size of a typical paperback - yet it does not feel like a fast read. You are rarely left curious to see what will happen next.And that's the biggest sin of this book - it never achieves any "flow", any momentum that would really get you to start to care.Well, except for in the final chapters, where the loose ends about a subplot starring Robin really start to payoff. But fans may already know that story and others may have lost interest beforehand.The conclusion of that subplot may make it worth a one-time read for collectors but others should think twice.Read moreRead less
