So perceptively and sharply realized, Sartre's soldiers and civilians are living in a dreamworld of defeat, 1940 France, and play out their emotions on a canvas of uncertainty and terror. Some try manically to escape their entrapment, some mope in depression, some rage, some laugh hysterically, but all are defeated. And they are abandoned by their officers, their army, their country. It's a sober portrait as they await the conquerors. In the text there are weird jumps in the narrative, from the countryside to Paris to New York, and from one character to another. And if you skip over a hundred pages, you won't have lost a thing. Existential entrapment and a need to take action are the motifs that constantly recur.
