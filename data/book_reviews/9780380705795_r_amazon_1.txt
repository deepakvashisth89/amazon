First rate mysteries without the murder--I found this refreshing.  While Amanda and Lugg are lacking in the stories, Superintendent Oates is the main supporting character.  The 13 stories are quite clever and IMHO satisfying, and many are humorous as well.  Campion is in fine form, as stated on page 237: 'You're too bright,' he said.  'You'll blind yourself one of these days.'  The most unusual feature, to my way of thinking, is the relationship between Campion and Oates--perhaps not as warm as some of the later Campion works.  BTW, this is not considered a numbered Campion book, but was published between #10, "The Fashion in Shrouds" and #11, "Traitor's Purse" -- two of her best IMHO.  Admittedly, if you prefer novels, you might not appreciate this book as much as I did, but I think things should be evaluated within their own context (in this case the short story).  Otherwise, very few books would rate 5 stars (or maybe even 4) vs. Shakespeare or Hemingway etc.For additional Campion short stories, Omnibus #1, The Mysterious Mr Campion, and Omnibus #2, Mr Campion's Lady each have 3 novels plus a Christmas story.
