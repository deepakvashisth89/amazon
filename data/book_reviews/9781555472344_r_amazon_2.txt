John Andrews and his wife, Hester, travel to Kenya where John is employed to help build the Uganda Railway. KENYA is set during colonial times and some of the problems John encounters seems much like those encountered in the westward expansion of North America: disease, predatory animals, difficult terrain, a sometimes hostile populace. But John must also contend with a headstrong wife who develops a romantic attachment to another man. The first half of the book is filled with a variety of action, including the the building of the railroad and kept this reader interested despite a few slow spots. The last half, however, chugged to a crawl like a locomotive climbing a steep grade. There is way too much domestic and nonessential detail in KENYA but, overall, the action and romantic scenes are quite well done by Mr Halkin and the insights into colonial Africa at the turn of the century are worth the time if you can speed read past the more meandering, plot-stagnating parts.
