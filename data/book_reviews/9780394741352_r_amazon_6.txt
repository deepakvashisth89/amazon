Some books make you relish the action, others cause you to marvel in curiosity, others make you laugh, and others bring you a plot line that is impressively deep. This one was none of the above.Nevertheless, when I finished it, I felt I had encountered a man who humbly and successfully encountered a profound downward change in his situation, who could habitually resist temptation, without whining, blaming, hedonic substitution, depression, or other standard literary responses to misfortune.The book's hero is a priest with plenty of worldly charm, access to influence, and an appreciation for fine cigars. For some reason, he is banished to an isolated prairie outpost. Instead of speaking at corporate dinner engagements, he is deciding whether to expand the parish hall or establish a small retreat center. During his exile, he manages to change his old, selfish habits and to maintain a stable dignity. This might not be terribly exciting, on the surface, but I was happy to have read it.I subtracted one star because I did not think the author developed much depth or explanation for how the main character developed his transformation.Read moreRead less
