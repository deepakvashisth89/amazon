Featuring an unusual plot arc, uneven pacing, and a somewhat unpredictable ending, "Call Me" feels at times a bit disjointed, never quite making up its mind whether to be a love story or a coming of age story and neither strictly failing, nor succeeding at both endeavors.Main character Michelle Karlsen is devastated when her long time boyfriend Adam is forced to move with his flaky widowed mother to California.  Convinced that Adam is the love of her life and determined to remain faithful to him even through the difficulties of a long distance relationship, Michelle sinks into depression, much to the alarm of her over-protective parents, who insist that she either see a shrink or start dating other boys.  The ultimatum feels weird, as does Michelle's tacit agreement to it and subsequent coupling off with clownish pal Charlie, who has long harbored unrequited feelings for Michelle.The plot meanders a bit, with Michelle and Charlie seemingly growing closer, even as Adam seems to be growing more distant, and one wonders why Michelle's parents, who ostensibly disapproved of Michelle dating Adam exclusively and clearly have no problem mandating their daughter's love life, don't force her to date boys in addition to Charlie.  Torn between her feelings for the two boys, Michelle sees it all come to a head when Adam secures a summer job in Wisconsin and tells Michelle he is coming home, and she is forced to choose between Adam and Charlie.The novel gets off to a slow start and never really picks up much momentum, leaving the reader feeling a bit manipulated and confused and never quite sure if we are supposed to be hoping for Michelle to end up with Adam, Charlie, or no one at all.(As an aside, anyone else think the cover model resembles an 80s Kristen Stewart lookalike??)Read moreRead less
