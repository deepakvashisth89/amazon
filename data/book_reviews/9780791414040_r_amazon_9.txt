Sorry Rita, RIP, but this reads like an apology to patriarchy.Painful to read, as most of the time is spent making excuses for gaslighting, narcissistic men and patriarchal or misogynist traditions, going through convoluted thought-experiments in attempt to interpret Buddhism as not-misogynistic, and generally showing how outdated and vile Buddhism is.  But, then again, maybe that was the point. The idea of reframing relationality as the centre "enlightenment" is powerful and when she talks about Buddhism in community later in the book, that's the good stuff.... However the thesis of convincing us or herself that Buddhism is still viable is depressingly weak.It is more a comment on the institution of Buddhism and scholarship in the West (and East) that "scholars" describe it as "balanced" because of the obscene apologies and submission Gross felt she had to offer to the pyre to be taken seriously.The centering of "enlightenment" discourse around women, relationality, and community is wonderful however and should make obvious sense even to conservative Buddhists, so hopefully she was indeed submissive enough for them not to feel threatened by the message.
