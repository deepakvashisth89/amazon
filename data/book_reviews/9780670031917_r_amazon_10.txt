There’s no doubt Brodrick is a superb writer, and as a former monk and barrister himself, Anselm is a protagonist he can write about with insight. This story deals with Paris during the German occupation, a resistance group set up by a priest to help Jews escape, the escape to Britain of a Nazi and a collaborator, an Old Bailey trial of said Nazi in the nineties, the complicity (or not) of the Catholic Church at the highest levels and layer upon layer of long-held secrets. So far, I’ve only read one other book by this author (writing as John Fairfax) and was similarly impressed. There’s such keen intelligence in these books I’ve often had to re-read in case I’ve missed nuances. He’s also a man of learning and keen insight. His turns of phrase are often deft, meaning that there’s much to savour in almost every sentence. Where he pushes it a bit is in keeping yet more revelations coming right up to the end. In the case of this book, the final revelation was a bit hard to credit and probably unnecessary to the plot, which goodness knows, was complex enough. However, he really knows whereof he writes, given the bibliography and the personal experience of his own mother, whose story shares elements with the Agnes of the book.
