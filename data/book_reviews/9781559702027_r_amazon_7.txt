It is hard to remain objective about a book like this. When dodging death is a daily affair, it is hard to read on without reflecting on the complex & overwhelming mix of morality, survival, sanity & fear at play, difficult to sum it up, elusive not to get caught up in it all.It is a story that spans 8 months, perhaps 10 - a story of liquidation of the Jews at Auschwitz & the horrifying machine like efficiency that the Nazis brought to it; a story also of the doctor's own role in this gruesome set up - hiding behind his tremendous medical expertise & being an indispensable accomplice to the utterly vile experiments of the so-called science at the camp. It is not without the occasional positivity, the twists of luck, the surprise friendly gestures & the camp's economics of bribing but by & large, it is a story of murder - at scale, continual & remorseless.No doubt it is a tale of the sordid inhumanity we're capable of. But it is also a story of survival. And survival, simply, is about the end, & not the means. It challenges our notions of morality, it puts us in positions where while we may be able to save our skins, we still die a little bit. And then, perhaps in delusion, or just instinctively, we repeat those very actions. The foreword of the book raises some very difficult questions about our larger values in the society we live in - the tale merits these questions, definitely - but it is just as true that fear & disease weaken mind, body & spirit. The doctor in his story does speculate about dying like a man instead of being murdered like an insect; though there isn't much rebellion that these hapless people, doomed to die anyway, ever put up.
