After making homemade wine for six years I recently decided to branch out into making beer. Because I was quite ignorant of the basics of beer making, I was looking for a short, simple guide. William Moore's "Home Beermaking" turned out to be just what I needed to read to get started. It begins with a brief description of the major types of beers (ales, lagers, etc.), which itself can be a mystery to the novice. It then offers a description of the equipment that every beer maker should have. This is followed by a chapter on the various ingredients (e.g., grains, malts, hops, etc.) and the way in which each one of them contributes to the finished product. The book also includes chapters on refinements and on problem shooting. There are several recipes for home-brewed beers as well. All in all, this is a great book for the novice. It can be read in an hour or so, although its greatest value is as a reference to have on hand when one is engaged in the beer-making process.
