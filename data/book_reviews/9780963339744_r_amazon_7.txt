I know I've read about the tragic story of Sylvia Likens before & was not only horrified by what was done to her, but, like most people I'm sure, I couldn't understand how so many people could be complicit in this crime without any of them feeling compelled to stop it somehow. I do think Jack Ketchum found the perfect way to tell this story by having a very conflicted preteen boy named David as the first-person narrator. I devoured this book in one day. Instead of just describing the gruesome details of the crime or trying to sensationalize the story, Jack very successfully humanizes David without whitewashing his part in the abuse, even if he's only a passive witness in his eyes. It helps the reader understand just a little, even if only from one of the abuser's perspectives, how easy it was to succumb to group think. They were groomed by an adult woman, one of the neighborhood's coolest moms that they've known their entire lives, who was able to convince them they weren't doing anything wrong as long as they followed her rules while playing "the game."  While she allowed them to string this poor girl up from the ceiling, humiliate her by stripping her naked & encouraged them to beat her, she initially drew the line at letting them molest or rape her & scared them all into keeping quiet. By the time David finally comes to his senses & realizes this can only end one way - bad - he struggles to do the right thing. At 12, he doesn't yet have the capacity to think things all the way through. It doesn't help that this occurred in the 1950s & he's constantly reminded that people should mind their own business & given conflicting answers from adults regarding right & wrong when it came to inflicting violence & witnessing it. We all know how Sylvia's story ended, but a small part of me still hoped for a last minute rewrite with a more hopeful ending. So while it was emotionally difficult to read, especially when some of it was left up to the reader's imagination, by the end, I definitely had a better understanding of how the kids were manipulated, how David found ways to justify his part initially & how something like this could actually happen. Jack was so successful at making you feel what David was feeling, there were times while reading that I could not only feel his amazement, confusion, disgust & utter feeling of helplessness at being a child faced with a situation that was completely out of your control, but I also felt his guilt & heartbreak. You know an author is talented when they can elicit such strong emotions from their readers.
