In its way, it's a wondrously melancholy account of the effects of the Depression and the Dust Bowl on a formerly middle-class, town-bred family forced to return to their ancestral farmstead to live off the land -- and what happens when that land proves unkind. Told through the viewpoint of the quietly insightful middle daughter, Marget, it relates how, according to their characters, some members of the family are redeemed and others destroyed, and how forces beyond them often don't care one whit about their inner strengths or weaknesses.I bought this book remembering its brutally tender imagery and wondering if I could translate that to the screen. It wouldn't be easy: Ms. Johnson's narration, through Marget's voice, is nonlinear  and inward. Yet the story is there, and it's one that can be visualized. I will try.Read moreRead less
