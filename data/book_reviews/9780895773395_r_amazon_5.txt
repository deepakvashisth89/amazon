I had not read this book since I was a young untraveled man.  I read it again while my wife and I were spending two months traveling as vagabonds in Turkey.  I chuckled and laughed far more this second time as I recognized that nothing, really, has changed in the last 150 years.We travel with one change of clothes, a laptop and two kindles.  We eat, sleep and travel as do the lower classes.  For us, this book is as relevant now as it was when written.  Hidden in the humor are timeless truths.As for those who criticize it for being politically incorrect, my comment is "Get a life!"  Aside from the fact that The Innocents Abroad was written in another time with different standards; if this books offends you, then you should stay home.We have traveled to 45 countries and have learned what true prejudice is.  Once a young American black couple had dinner with my wife and me, this on a ship with 700 Germans and 1600 Dutch.  Afterwards they told us that they had been traveling for some time in Europe and so enjoyed having an evening with someone "just like" them.  They recognized that they had more in common with us than with anyone else in Europe, whatever the race.
