Mr. Porter's translation of the 千家詩 is an impressive achievement, and I was looking forward to comparing his versions of these celebrated poems with the Chinese originals. However, the original Chinese texts have been omitted from the Kindle edition, greatly diminishing the volume's usefulness to students of Chinese and other serious readers. I wouldn't complain if Amazon had made it clear that the electronic edition was an abridged (i.e., mutilated) version of the printed book, though of course I might not have bought such a volume either.Who is responsible for this idiotic decision? And how long will it take Amazon (or the publisher) to adjust the promotional materials to reflect the Kindle edition's true contents? If the Kindle edition of a book differs significantly from the print version, prospective purchasers surely deserve to be put on notice of this fact.My irritation with Amazon and the publisher aside, I admire Mr. Porter's devotion to Chinese poetry and will certainly purchase a paperback copy of  the book the next time I visit a country with decent English bookstores.Read moreRead less
