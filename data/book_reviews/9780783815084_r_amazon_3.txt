Dick Francis’ writing is descriptive and emotional without being maudlin. The book begins with Sid Halley saying, “I had this friend, you see, that everyone loved”. The friend is Ellis Quint. Sid, a former jockey turned private investigator, discovers to his dismay that Ellis, against whom Sid competed in their jockey days, has done something awful, but no one believes Sid and he deals with a great deal of character assassination.There are some flashbacks, which alter the flow of the story. Also, this book was written when people still used fax machines and floppy discs, so it’s a bit dated. Sid’s character assassination happens in a newspaper, since the internet wasn’t as widespread when the book came out.Sid has become friends with a young girl named Rachel who has leukemia. This open up another side of Sid that would not have come through otherwise. I love the relationship Sid has with his former father-in-law, Charles.The story is somewhat dark, but I love Dick Francis’ writing style.
