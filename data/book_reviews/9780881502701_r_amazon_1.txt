The Cut Direct — ReviewThere are detective novels that begin with a murder.And then there are detective novels that begin with social embarrassment, misunderstandings, eccentric behavior, and somehow still end with a perfectly respectable corpse.The Cut Direct is the second Leonidas Witherall mystery and one of the most charming entries in the series. Written by Phoebe Atwood Taylor under her Alice Tilton pseudonym, it combines Golden Age detection with sparkling comedy and wonderfully eccentric characters.I spent a fog-softened Eureka afternoon reading it at the Eye of Bodhidharma while Edgar the raven occupied his customary fence post outside. The fog drifted through Old Town like a social slight that had somehow become a criminal investigation.Edgar considered the title carefully.His position was simple:“The direct cut is unpleasant. The direct route to pastries is highly recommended.”Leonidas Witherall remains one of detective fiction’s most delightful creations.A retired schoolmaster who looks uncannily like Shakespeare, he continually finds himself caught in mysteries despite having no particular desire to become a detective.What especially impressed me was the humor.The comedy grows naturally from personalities rather than gimmicks.People misunderstand one another.Situations escalate.Respectability collides with absurdity.And through it all, Witherall remains dryly observant and wonderfully sensible.The mystery itself is strong.The clues are fair.The suspects are memorable.The solution feels earned.Readers can enjoy the puzzle while laughing at the increasingly improbable complications.The supporting cast is a delight.Alice Tilton fills the story with energetic eccentrics who seem perfectly capable of turning a simple investigation into organized chaos.The dialogue sparkles throughout.The pacing is brisk.And the New England setting provides the perfect backdrop.⸻What Works Well✓ Excellent humorFunny without becoming silly.✓ Leonidas WitherallOne of the most memorable detectives of the Golden
