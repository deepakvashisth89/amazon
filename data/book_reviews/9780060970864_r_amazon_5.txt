Charming, as far as it goes. A strong, independent, educated woman shares wisdom gleaned from choosing to first leave mainstream modern America and and then to continue living in a cabin in the Ozarks of Missouri after her husband leaves her. She lives simply, runs a beekeeping business with hives scattered around on other people's fams, and tells the reader a lot of what she knows about insects and animals and birds and weather. Her interactions with locals who've lived their whole lives in the area are recounted with respect and humanity, and some of her stories are quite touching. She mixes in trips to Manhattan and Boston to market her honey, so city life is contrasted with her days of chopping wood. Overall, this is a good book for those interested in plain talk about nature and life off the beaten path. One does wonder if her area is still as untouched as it was when she wrote the book 30 years ago. My one quibble is that her wisdom and earthy intelligence seems a bit more calculating than free-flowingly authentic. She'd probably be a charming friend, but her writing is fairly undramatic and sometimes borderline trite. I guess that's better than being overly dramatic.
