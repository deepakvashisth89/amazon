This appears to have been the author’s first published novel, appearing amidst other post-Cold War Tom Clancy-style novels in 1992.  The setting is post-unification Germany which is suffering economically from the burdens of that process.  Of course, there is a Nazi-clone senior military officer (formerly East German military) who thinks he is the solution.  Echoing Germany’s dark past, he plans to use the reduced German military and security services to seize power for himself backed by tactical nuclear weapons to be taken by his forces from Germany’s American allies.I enjoyed his references to the National Training Center (NTC) at Fort Irwin, California, having worked there myself.  This was probably still new to many readers at the time of publication even as the NTC basked in the reflected glory of the 1991 Desert Storm war with Iraq.I picked this up in a used book store because of the title and because it called to mind Harold Coyle’s premier novel – Team Yankee—in both setting and plot.  Coyle, of course, went to write a series of novels based upon his Team Yankee characters, among other works.Todd Stone clearly draws on his own military experiences in his characters and his accounts of the tactical action.  I am actually inclined to rate Kriegspiel as slightly better than the original version of Team Yankee (though I recently learned that Coyle has published an updated expanded version that I have yet to read) which in its original form frankly struck me as an extended After Action Report from an armor miniatures table top wargame.  I should add that I nevertheless went on to read and very much enjoy Coyle’s later books, especially the feeling of watching an author grow and improve his storytelling skills.For reasons unknown to me, it appears that Todd A. Stone published only one more novel.  The reading public’s tastes not to mention those of publishers do change and evolve and I could only speculate as to why.  I did feel that the author made some heavy-handed choices with some of his German characters [SPOILER ALERT] using the names of Goebbels and Hemler has the feel of college sophomore or barrack’s humor.
