Great opening paragraph:"It's all true. When the squad car rolled up behind me and the loudspeaker blared, 'Hold it, right there!' I was, in fact, trying to break into George Dishey's house. But I had an excuse. Really!"The speaker is Emily "Em" Hansen, my favorite forensic geologist. Usually she works for oil prospectors of various sizes, but she gets caught up in murders and their investigations, where a knowledge about the oil industry, or geography and stratigraphy is important.In the case of "Bone Hunter", Em was invited by Dishey, a renowned vertebrate paleontologist, to speak on forensic geology at the annual meeting of the Society of Vertebrate Paleontology. George lives in Salt Lake City and the meeting is held at Snowbird (30 miles away). Em is his houseguest for the long weekend.Unfortunately for Em, the first morning, George storms out of the house after an angry phone call and doesn't return. She manages to lock herself out of the house without her rental car keys, hence the attempted forced entry.  Even worse for Emily, George has been found dead, gruesomely dead, and she's the chief suspect. Especially when it turns out that she's not on the agenda for the conference at all."Bone Hunter" is Sarah Andrews 5th Em Hansen mystery. I was particularly pleased with the first 3 books in the series. (I'm reading them in order: "Tensleep", "A Fall in Denver", "Mother Nature", "Only Flesh and Bones".), They have a spunky heroine, humorous interludes, interesting murders, and lots of new details of a field about which I know little.  However, the 4th book and "Bone Hunter" spend a lot of time on Em's personal search for meaning in her life. It just wasn't as interesting and gets in the way of the mystery story-telling, which merits one-star-off for me.Sarah Andrews' ability to turn a phrase, set a scene, make you see the characters in front of you, and let you see how and why people react as they do, remains excellent. Here's Emily when she finally gets to the conference, already infamous from the TV news as Dishey's houseguest, and with a cop to watch her: "When I had at last fully convinced myself that the talk I had taken great pains to prepare was not going to occur, I set to work pumping people for information about George Dishey. ... I knew that there would be no better time to pry into these other people's lives than when they were still interested in mine."Em is intelligent, average-looking, and a bit of a scamp. At one point the FBI takes her up in a helicopter, using her knowledge of map reading, geology and probable dinosaur bone fields. Em pulls out a geological map and says, "I BORROWED it from Vance. Took me a while to peel it off that board. He won't be missing it for a day or two."In "Bone Hunter" I learned how helicopters fly and, more importantly, how they fall out of the sky, as well as expanded my knowledge of how dinosaur bones are found and excavated. There is a lot of reflection on religion, how it shapes people and how people shape it - the exploration seemed open-minded to me. This book was originally published in 1990. Parts of it seems to predict prominent news of the past years. The Waco siege, for example, took place in 1993.Recommended intelligent and entertaining reading. Andrews' "Author's Note", explaining how she was steered to this plot and these characters is also entertaining. Now I'm on to the 6th in the series, "An Eye for Gold".Happy ReaderRead moreRead less
