Margaret Millar at her best out-writes her more famous husband Ross Macdonald. I only compare them because her neglect seems so unfair. Macdonald is a great descriptive writer but his psychological tropes are repetitive and cheesy and he's probably too heavily in debt to ChandlerMillar is an original, with an excellent unforced prose style. She too is interested in psychology, but to more subtle effect. Her novels are more mainstream than genre in that the characters are more interesting than the thriller plot. She does like a sudden twist in the last few pages, as here, and I feel the books would be just as good or better without it.This is called An Air that Kills in the USA, and, while the UK title is better, neither has much to do with the plot. It's a good read, though not one of her very best. Her 1950s novels are the ones to go for in general.She gets a mention in a Richard Thompson song, a guarantee of quality.Read moreRead less
