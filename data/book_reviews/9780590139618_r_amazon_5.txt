As an older reader (45) I may be outside the target audience but found this book outstanding reading nonetheless. Well written and highly inventive it sets a cracking pace, never flagging despite its length. While it can be read by younger kids it fascinated me by prompting some profound moral and theological questions, although young readers (like my pre-teens) may miss this altogether. I am now eager to read the remaining books in the trilogy not only to follow the adventures but to see which side of these questions Pullman comes down on - far from obvious at the end of this first book.If the quality is maintained in the sequels, comparisons between Pullman and CS Lewis will be well justified. This book certainly has the child friendly nature of the Narnia stories but without the ham-fisted parallels to biblical tales. The subtlety of his moral questions rank more alongside Lewis's adult fiction such as Out of the Silent Planet or Perelandra. This is a childrens book that can be read by a bright 10 to 12 year old, will challenge intelligent teens and still provide food for thought for parents.
