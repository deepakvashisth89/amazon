This is one of the best biographies I've ever read.A story of a man who was one of the greats - like all the great soldiers he took risks, was sometimes insufferable, could be vain, could be ruthless, could be kind.He transformed Japan, instead of heaping humiliation on them he did his best to heal the wounds and introduce democracy to an archaic dynasty - he succeeded. He insisted on women having the vote amongst others.He saw the pacific/Japan/Phillipines war as the main theatre when all along the main focus had to be Europe - after all did the Japanese really threaten world peace like the Nazis? - taking in the vastness of the pacific. That was because he knew the Phillipines and loved them - his father was a General there before him. His father was also an American civil war hero - McArthur never forgot that.For his eternal credit he saved countless lives by not confronting the Japanese but avoiding confrontation and occupying nearby islandsbuilding bases and airstrips. The Japanese were looking for all out war on the beaches but McArthur avoided that.The Japanese would after say this constantly frustrated them.Always at odds with the chiefs in Washington and after FDR - Trueman.He found the navy irksome to his efforts.One wonders what he might have been like given Supreme command of Europe ?And then Korea - the dividing line still stands.Read moreRead less
