I remember seeing - and liking- The Reader on one of the OTT channels some years ago. And recently I came across the book. Having a weak memory, I knew I would have forgotten most of the story so I bought it.I had indeed forgotten the story but it kept coming back like progressive deja vu as I turned the pages.It's a wonderful book. The story of Hanna and Michael, a love story that's more carnal than emotional. But it's also about guilt. It is set in post-world war 2 Germany. The Holocaust is history but the active and passive participants are still around.Michael belongs to the post war generation. As he grapples with the issue of guilt vis a vis his unusual relationship with Hanna, he also contends with  guilt in relationship to the holocaust. He ruminates, “our parents had failed to measure up during the Third Reich, or after it ended. How could those who had committed Nazi crimes or watched them happen or looked away while they were happening or tolerated the criminals among them after 1945 or even accepted them how could they have anything to say to their children? But on the other hand, the Nazi past was an issue even for children who couldn't accuse their parents of anything, or didn't want to."Because the protagonist grapples with such issues, the book is a lot slower than the movie, which I saw again on Amazon Prime after finishing the book. And it also goes a lot deeper.These are complex issues with unsatisfactory explanations and incomplete resolutions. The book makes you think but gives no answers.Isn't that true of so much in life?Read moreRead less
