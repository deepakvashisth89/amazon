"God's Bit of Wood" is Sembene Ousmane's third novel, that is based on the 1947-1948 railroad worker strike on the Dakar-Niger railway in French West Africa. The setting at first is in Sengal as the workers fight for better benefits and compensation equal to the "White" man. The chapters of this novel shifts between three other cities known as, Bamako, Theis and Dakar.Throughout the novel, portrays the struggle between the West African inhabitants (Railroad workers and their families) and the French colonial powers. Starvation and dehydration became an imminent effect from the strike.Ousmane did a wonderful job in exposing the struggles that men, women, and children all endured from strike. Generations and gender stances and roles shifted throughout the plot of the novel. In awe, it brought together a stronger community throughout West Africa. One thing I did not take a liking to, was the many characters. Ousmane introduced many characters into the plot and that made it easy to get the characters confused with each other.With that being said, I liked how Ousmane named each chapter by the name of the city and or by the main character that had a big impact on the chapter. By him organizing the chapters like that, it makes the novel's plot easier to follow.Overall "God's Bit of Wood" was a great informative read. I connected with book both physically and emotionally reading about the struggles endured.Ousmane would of never imagined when writing this book that this book would make such a impact on students and readers across the world today.*Read this book for Dr. Rhonda M.
