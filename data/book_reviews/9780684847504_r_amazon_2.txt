Shipping was quick. The book is in great condition. If you haven't, I recommend: a wonderful read. Who knew slave narratives could be so amusing? Reed addresses the malleability of history as well as exploring common caricatures who have emerged out of the rubble of slavery and Jim Crow. He turns history upside down and embraces anachronism. He reminds us of the absurdity of racial difference but also the frightening realization that in some aspects, little has changed. Come for the history, stay for the comparison of the slave master Swille to the Marquis de Sade. Reed jabs at the women's movement, politics, sexualization of the "Other," and miscegenation.
