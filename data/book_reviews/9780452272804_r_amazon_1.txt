Melanie Rae Thon is, in my opinion, one of the most interesting, original, and worthwhile writers in fiction today. It surprises me that many people are unfamiliar with her work, but at the same time I can see why that might be--she tends to write about subjects that a mainstream audience might find uninteresting or even distasteful, including addiction, poor rural life, prostitution, and the struggles of Native Americans. "Iona Moon" is not her best work (I think Thon shines most as a writer of short stories) but it is nevertheless a good read. My biggest complaint was that the central character, Iona Moon, doesn't really seem to change as the story progresses. The end was not satisfying to me because she seems to wind up in the same place as when she started off, only with a man who's able to love her because he's been lowered (by a car accident that crippled him) to her socioeconomic level.
