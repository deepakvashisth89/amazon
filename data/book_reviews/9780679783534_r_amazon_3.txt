A beautifully organized and written book that shows how unusual John Brown was, but brings him fully alive as a human being who simply refused to stand by in the face of the irredeemable evil of slavery -- or to accept any part of the white racism that sought to justify and nourish slavery among a people that believed in liberty and equality.As a nation we have replaced this deeply humane American, a dedicated, if idiosyncratic, follower of Christ and the Gospel, by a two-dimensional, cartoon fanatic. David S. Reynolds shows who Brown really was, where he came from and how his uncompromising, self-sacrificing commitment to ending slavery and white racism (which many anti-slavery whites embraced thoroughly), backed up by a willingness to use force and disregard unnatural laws to free captives, totally crystallized the endless debates over slavery.As Lincoln eventually had to admit -- because of the action of Brown's eloquent courage -- slavery had to be abolished 100 percent and immediately. This was not to attack the whites who supported slavery, it was to treat them as human beings, same as all other human beings, with the same moral needs. Brown never hated slavery supporters in their persons. He loved them as brothers and sisters. He opened the way for them to shed the inhumanity of slavery.Don't believe it?
