I was disappointed because the book arrived without a cover and was quite brown inside, but other than that, it was in good condition.The book itself is gripping and affords several readings. It's story is like a thriller. It's about an uncompromisingly individualistic young Chicago jazz pianist who is a genius without pride or ambition, other than to earn a living and retire. His extreme individualism evokes negative reactions and his seeming simplicity makes him a target for exploitation. Without intending to, he has huge effects on another virtuoso jazz pianist, and on the lives of a record shop proprietor, some concert promoters, a girl, and jazz club owners. The philosophy in this book is deep and as relevant and important today as it was when it was written. It is a deeply moving book, and for me, a very valuable one in terms of how it influenced my life as a late teenager in the 1960s.
