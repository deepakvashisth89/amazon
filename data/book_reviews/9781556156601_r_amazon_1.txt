This dates back to the 1990s - almost medieval, in the computing world. As such, it serves mostly as an historical document, illustrating a chapter in the history of file systems. That's just what I was looking for.Or so I thought. This brief book describes the NT file system at a conceptual level. That's a good start, but I was hoping for a bit more of the implementation detail. If there had been more than a sketch, I would have rated this book quite highly. As it is, lack of specifics about the lower layers left me unsatisfied.Perhaps my expectations were too demanding. I've worked in OS development, including a port of a logical volume manger, so my capacity for detail could well exceed that of the book's intended audience. Still, this presents neither an administrator's view of the file system nor a developer's, but does present internals that a typical user would never have had access to. That leaves me wondering who the target audience might have been.-- wiredweirdRead moreRead less
