I can't decide how many stars to give this one....I read it in one sitting (including while cooking dinner & walking the dog), so obviously it held my interest. But some of the holding came from me gaping, appalled, at the page & saying "They can't possibly have put up with that!"And I'm not talking about the dreadful rigors and horrific privations of polar exploration; I'm talking about the dreadful behavior and horrific attitudes of some of the people on that expedition. I don't feel like I can go into details, lest I be accused of spoilage, but for me the book would really have benefitted from some analysis --or even thoughtful speculation-- of why certain people put up so long & quietly with some of the deplorable aspects of some of the other people. I suppose when the medical autopsy of Hall was performed in 1968, it was before 'psychological autopsies' were much performed, but Henderson obviously did considerable research and I would've liked even just to see his own armchair opinions.  All I can say is, folks were a lot more forgiving back then, or something. I know military discipline was (& still is) taken very seriously, but good grief! The words "criminal order" came to mind more than once.since this is the first book I've read about Hall's expedition, I can't judge the accuracy of the writing by comparing his presentation of the facts against other sources; I can't help but wonder if some of the incredible behavior is portrayed in a slanted fashion. OTOH, I just finished "Frozen In Time" again, so maybe the Hall people did act that way because they were rendered wacko from lead poisoning from badly soldered tinned food, like the Franklin expedition people.FIT is an excellent book, btw, & I highly recommend the updated version, with intro by Margaret Atwood, to anyone with an interest in polar exploration. It really gives insight into how seemingly trivial things --how the food was canned-- can sabotage the most courageous and determined endeavor.
