My interest in DLR was sparked when I saw a rerun of an interview he did on David Letterman in the 80’s. It took me back to the night I stayed up late as a teenager to watch. I planned on turning the TV off before it was over and going to bed so I could get up early.  So much for that, I was glued to the set the second DLR came on. When Letterman said the interview was over, I was sad and moaned along with the studio audience. Re-watching it as an adult, I had the same kind of, ‘Aww, I don’t want him to go’ feeling at the end of the interview.  So, I got his book. Was tempted to pass on it because of the cover which suggested something more carnal. But, I got it anyway. He alludes to that part of rock stardom, but tastefully, and only tells a couple of those stories that were fairly benign.Overall, DLR did an amazing job of taking me through the journey of his life. He pulled me in, told me his story, and took me on an adventurous and emotional trip as good as any novel I have ever read.  I rooted for him, was amazed, laughed, and even cried. Yeah, I was surprised I cried too. But, his ability to make us emote is probably one of the reasons he hit the stratosphere as an entertainer. Anyway, this book tops my list of favorite books because it is a great American success story, at times I laughed so hard it brought tears to my eyes, and he surprised me with his depth of compassion. Bar none, the best autobiography that I have ever read.  Such an amazing story it almost reads like fiction, but we all saw it happen in front of our naked steaming eyes.p.s.
