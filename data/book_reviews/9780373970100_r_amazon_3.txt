SUMMER'S AWAKENING is showing its age.  What passed for romantic in 1984 doesn't work in 2012.  Summer Roberts, shy and overweight, takes a job as tutor/companion to James Gardiner's young niece.  After overhearing James refer to her as "fat as a pig" and "a hulking great girl", Summer resolves to lose weight and upon arriving in Florida with Emily to live in one of James' homes, she joins Weight Watchers.  Over the next months, Summer slims down and when James reappears, he's now quite attracted to her.  Summer, in true 1980s romance-heroine fashion, turns into a helpless, quivering mess whenever James kisses her.  She can't tell him to back off, can't speak up for herself, won't call him on being an absolute jerk, and over the next four years, James flits around the world being a hotshot computer genius with a bevy of girlfriends while Summer holds the fort with niece Emily.The book is written in a strange style.  Author Anne Weale is a Brit, and she has all of her American characters using "Brit-speak", which sounds silly and affected.  Americans just don't use terms like "in hospital" or travel "at the weekend".  On top of the ill-fitting dialogue, Ms. Weale has the characters spouting factoids out of nowhere. In the middle of a conversation, suddenly the readers is treated to a mini-biography of John Ringling or a set of hurricane statistics.  This is piled on top of her preoccupation with a perceived "rich American lifestyle".  James has homes in Florida, Manhattan and Nantucket; brand names are sprinkled across virtually every page; Summer and Emily move from Cape Cod to New York to Switzerland at the drop of a hat.Summer eventually finds she has some talent as a jewelry designer, and even ends up designing for a well-known jeweler, but as soon as that plot point is established, it disappears from the story and she heads to Switzerland for the summer.  So much for making a career for herself.James was a self-centered jerk who cruelly taunted Summer when she was overweight and only noticed her after she became model-slim, which peaks volumes about his character.  He comes very close to forcing Summer into sex in a fit of pique, saved only by the ringing of a telephone. I won't call it rape" because Summer is her usual quivering mess and can't seem to muster the strength to say "no" even though she doesn't want it.  All supposedly ends happily, but the romance is unconvincing at best and borderline distasteful at worst.SUMMER'S END might have been a good read in the 1980s, but I'm betting today's readers are going to find it uncomfortable, if not downright obnoxious.Read moreRead less
