Considering the title o the book I wonder if Eco had found ironically funny that "Misreadings" indeed was scanned in such way that it shows miswritten (I suspect not)About the content I didn't like most of the essays. The issue is that they are like very long jokes. I understand Umberto Eco is trying to mention familiar events or cultural aspects from a different perspective. You have future humans after a catastrophe analyzing why Italians didn't leave a library, some natives from Oceania applying anthropology to Europeans, and so on. The issue is that a comical pun once gets to long it turns into obnoxious insistence, "yes, signore Eco, I understood the reference, let's move please." But no... he goes on and on ad nauseam. The writing, admittedly educated clever and even poetic sometimes, is similar to the literary movement of realism magic, except that in those stories there is a progression, the magic is mentioned, and then the plot continues, in these essays I felt stuck in every essay. There is one exception though, a narration of the arrive of the Europeans to the New World told as if Humanity now would be arriving to Mars, with many famous names. I think that essay works because there is an event advancing the plot, and although still criticism of Western culture it is more subtle as anyway we have a flexible and idealized of the Europe of Columbus. In the other texts the satire is rather a rant, from people closer to us and as so it can feel more biased or unjustified.
