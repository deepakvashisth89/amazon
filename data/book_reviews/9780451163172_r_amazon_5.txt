This was, for the first 98% of the book, one of the most engaging, inspiring, and eye-opening things I ever read, but the final words from the author felt like a punch in the gut.For those who don't know, this book was released in several editions.  John Griffin, in his final moments (1977?), and only at this point, becomes a cartoon-caricature of the kind of pretzelized logic that makes "SJW" a pejorative term and got Trump elected.This is sad because I would say the insights provided up until this point made this book worth more than every book I was assigned to read on "the Black experience" in K-12 combined.  It explains where the "happy" stereotype comes from, which was always so bizarre to me given Blacks are now stereotyped as angry.  It showed me how desegregation was not (at least initially) about Black people who desperately wanted to eat dine in the same restaurants as bigots, which I always found so illogical, but rather that they didn't want to walk a mile to get a sandwich.They say that all the darkness in the world cannot extinguish the light of a single candle, so the truth and insight John Griffin offers prior to the final few pages matters far more than anything stupid or confusing he says.  Still, I cannot give this book the glowing recommendation I was certain I was going to.Read moreRead less
