This is a book which richly deserves to be reprinted in our own day.  Young gay people commonly have little idea, or none, of the senseless brutality of the laws referring to homosexuality until not so very long ago and, to some extent, even now.  Any retrograde government, as we see elsewhere, needs little to bear down on minorities for which it has little liking.  The infamous Labouchere Amendment took many years to repeal, resurfacing in different but scarcely more favourable forms in the Sexual Offences Act and, at least as much, in the now repealed Section 28.  Of the two main characters in this story, I believe at least partly on experience (see the Introduction), one was still under age for relationships, the other, his partner in secret of age, but not for a relationship of this kind.  Any legal or social disfavour on this ground may very well threaten his future, not least in higher education.  Particularly telling, and based on observation, is the grinding questioning of the private lives of both in the courtroom in which both find themselves.  Support here is vital, but limited.
