NOTE: My review follows this note, which I hope will avoid any misunderstandings you may get from the review. On 5-5-13 I discontinued my reading of THE WHEEL OF TIME series. Though I was pleased with the prequel and the first three books in the series proper, I gave up during the fourth book, THE SHADOW RISING. Why? I think it is sufficient to say that variety is the spice of life and that THE SHADOW RISING did not offer enough fresh material to sustain my attention.I looked at the fifteen volumes of The Wheel of Time--hefty volumes except for the prequel--and I asked myself, "Is it possible to read straight through this series without reading any other books?" I am sure it is possible, but lacking faith in masochism, I decided not to try. Still, to maintain a coherent degree of immersion, I thought I should read the books in close proximity. Therefore, as a compromise, over the past five weeks I have read the first four books, counting NEW SPRING as the first, and between each I have read a book of nonfiction. This procedure has worked well, and I intend to continue it.In THE DRAGON REBORN, the evil forces of the Dark Lord remain alive and well. Which means they are deadly as hell and not at all well. A stale theme, yes, but it did nothing to diminish the book's robust quality. With likeable characters and lively dialog, diverging and converging storylines, and dangerous circumstances, Robert Jordan retained me as a loyal page turner.Though not the only fresh item, an especially fresh one is Mat Cauthon. In preceding volumes, he was one of the main characters, though not much in the spotlight. Now he not only surprised me with his formidable fighting skills, he revealed a very powerful idiosyncrasy. I will not spoil the story with the details of that, but Mat, formerly just a designated horn blower, is now a true ta'veren--a person who is particularly important in the pattern of the age woven by the Wheel of Time.Read moreRead less
