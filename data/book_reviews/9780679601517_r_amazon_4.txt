Sometimes a book is so meaningful, the only real recommendation should be - READ IT. That is the case with The Fire Next Time; however, I have to say more.I've read this book several times throughout my life - first in the mid-1980s, then again in the early '90s, in 2008, in 2020, and just now. Each reading has struck me differently, depending on when I read it, but my most recent was the most painful. Baldwin’s clarity, rage, and hope remain unmatched, and his words feel more urgent than ever - a wake-up call we refuse to acknowledge.We have never truly reckoned with America’s history of racism. There were moments it felt like we were inching forward, but now, it feels like we’re sprinting backward, and it's heartbreaking.As Baldwin wrote, “If we do not dare everything, the fulfillment of that prophecy…will engulf the world.” Baldwin's voice is needed more than ever, not just to remember where we've been, but to ask ourselves where we are going.
