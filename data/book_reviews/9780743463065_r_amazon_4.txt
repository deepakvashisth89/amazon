Six books into the Ed McBain 87th Precinct series and I finally hit one that was not stellar. It's still worthwhile and worth reading, but so far the series has been superb and top of the line reading. This one not as much. For one thing, the crime and the victim are not that interesting, though the intricacies of blackmail and extortion, as outlined by McBain provide fascinating reading. But the character of the victim just doesn't hold a lot of depth to care one way or another about being murdered and by whom. The suspects as well are not that memorable, although the guesswork over them is not without appeal. McBain also indulges in some possibly editor/publisher dictated digressions involving the new detective at the precinct, who made his debut in the previous novel, Cotton Hawes. McBain explains in his introduction written decades later that he was being pressured to have a very compelling leading man in the series, someone good looking and virile and who would wow the potential lady readers, I guess. Unfortunately he comes off too often as being just a good looking manipulator who has little respect for women other than falling in love and bed with them in as fast a time as he can. Most of these digressions lead nowhere in the story and are just there to impress anyone reading looking for a dashing hero. The scenes with the new detective and series regular Steve Carella are very good, but there is a lot of time with the new cop on his own in this story and I just didn't find him original or interesting enough to want to follow him. The urban whodunit aspects of the plot were also not up to McBain's usual standards, not giving us enough information to invest anything in the victim or the suspects. The climax isn't very exciting or noteworthy, just a reveal and a motive that seems pretty contrived.
