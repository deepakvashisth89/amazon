If you're from Des Moines, you'll be excused if you just don't get it.Kem writes in a flow of consciousness that feels as if his books are written in a single sitting, in one long lucid draft that leaves you wanting to peel back the countless layers with which he constructs each and every story.  The fact that he focuses such talents on bonfire stories set around the surf scene will turn on two thirds of the population:  the one third that's ever lived within fifty miles of the left coast, and therefore can loosely comprehend the surfer jargon and mindset; and the one third that never did, but wishes they had.  The other third can never be happy, and need to find a good Emily Bronte novel to curl up with and not even bother with Nunn's prose.As another SoCal author, (gratuitous plug, see "Las Cruces") whose stories seep out of the same mystical terra firma that leads Nunn's characters ever back to the surf, I can only say that I grab his books as soon as they hit the electronic shelves, and pick them up as opportunistically as a surfer finds a good excuse to skip out whenever the sets begin to stand and curl.Dogs of Winter presents another spin on Nunn's life-as-a-surfer premise, and fails to fail.  Few writers compare, in terms of getting inside the heads and souls of their characters, and with Nunn's, you feel like you've bumped into each one them at some point, their memory evoked by the scent of sea salt in the air.Read moreRead less
