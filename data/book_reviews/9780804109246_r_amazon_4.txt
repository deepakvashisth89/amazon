This is the first of the Abe Lieberman novels and it is one of the best.  The only problem I had concerned some plausibility issues with the ending.  That would normally be a significant problem in the work of a mystery writer, but the Lieberman novels are 80% backstory and atmospherics.  I have yet to read a Lieberman novel that failed in those areas.Lieberman’s Folly differs from the later books in that it is a more linear narrative with few subplots.  I have described some of the other novels as being ‘visits to planet Abe’ or ‘visits to Abe’s world’.  In those novels there tend to be a number of cases on Abe and Bill’s plate but the narrative is dominated by visits to Abe’s brother Maish’s deli, to Wrigley field, to his home (with all of the entanglements brought on by his daughter’s marital problems and his wife Bess’s presidency of their temple), and so on.  Then we visit Bill’s world and his own issues with his alcohol addiction, his beloved Iris’ Chinese heritage, his difficulties with his ex-wife, Maureen, his son Michael, and so on.  I come away from the stories hungry for corned beef and fresh bagels and relishing the inflections of Jewish, Chinese and Irish dialogue.Lieberman’s Folly contains all of those elements, but it is also a straightforward mystery, a mystery centered on a prostitute who has volunteered to be a Lieberman informant, but then turns up sprawled on her apartment floor with multiple stab wounds.  A drunken homeless person has been seen in the vicinity along with a woman dressed like the prostitute who beat a quick retreat from her building.The prostitute’s story begins in Texas in a prologue set on the gulf coast and ends with an epilogue that ties the central plot line up in a nice bow.  The prostitute and her sister had hoped to leave Texas and build a bright future.  “But the Gods,” the narrator tells us, “had very different plans.”I recommend all of the Abe Lieberman novels; this is where they began.Read moreRead less
