I have now read four of the books about the Mackinnon brothers. This story is about Nick the oldest boy of the family but in reading the four books so far it seems to be more about the women in their lives. This story had many funny parts in and the authors descriptions of just ordinary things are terrific. But I can not get past Nick's behavior toward this young woman. Very early on in the story Chapter 3, Tibbie gets caught in a fence trying to unstick the family cow. Nick happens to come along but his behavior is not one of a gentleman.Yes he wants to get to know her better but he is always thinking of how to get her into bed. Because she will not make a deal with him, he starts to leave, leaving her still stuck in the fence.eventually he does help her but makes her promise she will meet him in the apple orchard. In chapter 4 she set upon by four wild young men in the town after the wagon she was in broke down.One of them uses a whip to take all her buttons off. They are holding her by her arms so she can not move. To me the scene is pretty close to a rape.Nick comes to the rescue but after he helps her with her clothes he kisses her.He later finds out about Beth, Tibbie's child. but does not do the right thing in regards to Tibbie. This is Texas, 1849, her father is a doctor, a prominent man in the community. Nick does not ask to court Tiddie or speak to her father. Instead he sidelines her as much as possible.
