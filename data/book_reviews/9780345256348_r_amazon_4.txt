Having read The Big Sleep years ago, I really don't like Raymond Chandler all that much. He has a distinctive style that turns me off and lulls me to sleep. Spotting Pickup on Noon Street in a book sale, I had hoped that it would a good old fashioned detective story although I later realized that it was composed of four forgettable short stories: Pickup on Noon Street, Smart-Aleck Kill, Guns at Cyrano's, and Nevada Gas. In each of them, I can inevitably sense the noir feel, but there is too much detachment about the characters that I had neither interest nor care for what happens during the story. Additionally, the characters are random and undeveloped. They just come out of nowhere, and it's too hard for me to have a liking for any of them. Yes, the stories were written in the 30's, making them primitive and unexciting. There are too many true crime novels nowadays that are way much more interesting and fun to read than any of the stories written by Raymond Chandler. Can it be probable that his works are outdated today? Why is that, you ask? It's because of the way that Chandler writes. His style is to keep the details taut, seemingly tough, and to the absolute bare minimum. Invariably, I feel like I am being kept out of learning what's going on during the acts, and it is annoying. The characters are too glib talkers and doers for me, making it all very unrealistic. It's just impossible. I find the stories extremely boring and nonsensical, sometimes to the point of comedy. The dialogues are rubbishy and cornily written, and I can't take anyone seriously.
