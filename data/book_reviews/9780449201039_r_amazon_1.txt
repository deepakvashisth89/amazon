Turning Points was Ellen Goodman's first book, published in 1979. From her interviews with 150 men and women from 1975 to 1978, Ellen documents changes taking place in society. The initial research for the book began when Ellen was a Nieman Fellow at Harvard in 1974. In the book, Ellen writes of “turning points” in the lives of those she interviewed. She writes of the causes, consequences, and lessons of change. Ellen offers some of her own interpretations but mostly allows the interviews to speak for themselves. She draws from her journalism background (Newsweek, Detroit Free Press, and the Boston Globe) to tell stories, true stories of people going through change. Or, as Ellen would say, experiencing turning points. Turning Points is not be read like a novel but a series of stories about people.
