Blindness is a fantastic book, the best I have read in a long time. It's difficult to explain without giving too much of the plot away, so I'll stick to generalisations.One of the most enjoyable features of Blindness is the writing style, which is at one both personal and almost documentary. The best way I can explain the style is inclusive. The main characters, who are thrown together in desperate circumstances, all give voice to their thoughts through the text - that is to say without traditional qoutemarks.Each character's thoughts are accounted for the same way, but the way the writer avoids confusion is by subtle narration. It's as if you are a bystander in the scenes where, every so often, the author pops up like a guide and whispers in your ear: this is why he thought this thing was important; aren't you wondering what she will say about that? I found myself totally absorbed in the action.The narrative of the book is harrowing but not to the point of hyperbole. There are descriptions of violence, destitution and sadness, but all are faced or dealt with in a way that most would find realistic or likely if indeed it were to happen to them. In are the selfish choices, uncertain actions and tearful failures; out are the Hollywood-style one liners or heroic speeches.Ultimately the book is almost a study of the modern human coping with as much dignity as possible in a world where contemporary comforts no longer apply.
