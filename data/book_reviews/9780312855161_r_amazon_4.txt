My favorite of the three books in this trilogy. It is the last but also a prequel of sorts to the first two and reminds me of some G Wolfe books  I’ve read, in terms of tone, themes and craftsmanship. This is the story of how Ser Olmy became who he is, within a larger play of human and plant ecologies sorting out who they will be and why, told from the first person perspective of Olmy, as a retrospective accounting.I have nothing bad to say about this book and only advise that it not be read out of order with the first two.
