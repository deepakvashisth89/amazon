I picked this autobiography up after being interested in reading about the infamous kidnapping related to J Paul Getty III. I felt that reading books on the kidnapping would be less insightful than the autobiography of the man that refused to pay the ransom.Unfortunately, this autobiography is rather hollow. Many sections of this book leaves the reader desperately wanting more information. Getty’s childhood has interesting tidbits but they’re never fully explored and he rushes through telling this stage of his life. This is also true when it came down to his 5 marriages. Pretty much Getty talked about who his wife was, how many kids they had, who his wife’s family was and then stated that they divorced him because he chose work over home life. He followed this pattern with each wife.Getty does mention that he does not entail to share the private lives of his friends and family members but it feels like he didn’t even share the private sections of his own life in his own autobiography. The kidnapping of J Paul Getty III is only 3 pages and not interesting.J Paul Getty mostly talks about what he did and the places he visited, the friends he had, but not too much about what he thought, or any obstacles he encountered. There is no climax in the autobiography. No satisfaction in obtaining personal thoughts or previously untold stories. It felt that everything he presented was public information.There is only one chapter, towards the end, that really seemed introspective. This chapter Getty admitted was just going to be an unfocused rant of thoughts he was having at the current moment. It wasn’t until this chapter and only in this chapter and nothing after that you felt that Getty was being truly genuine and speaking to you, not as a reader, but as a listener.His views on capital punishment, welfare, and feminism, were also illogical. It didn’t make sense. And then I realized that Getty was making statements based on things he was reading from the newspapers and television since he hadn’t visited the U.S in over 20 years as he was writing his autobiography.Overall I greatly wished that I had read a professional biography on J Paul Getty.
