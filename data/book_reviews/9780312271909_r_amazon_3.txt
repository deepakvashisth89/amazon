I found the characterization of Jean uneven.  She's introduced as an individual with some idiosyncrasies.  Being obsessed with fragrance is relatively benign but I think being unaware when you're articulating your thoughts would be a crippling social disorder.  She's lucky there, though, and never blurts out any irreparable faux pas.Soon we learn there were some irregularities with Jean during childhood and ultimately that there's still something extremely irregular going on with her.  But she's presented as intelligent, professionally successful and quite shrewd in her pursuit of the fountain pens she collects.  Insisting she wants to confront Stephen and finding herself repeatedly surprised to discover confrontation wasn't her motivation after all is an ingenuous and transparent conceit for someone with Jean's background.I loved the writing.  I was taken aback several times by a narrative with no use for exclamation points.  (Me:  "What?  Fan blades!")Now that I'm finished, I find Claudia the most interesting character and I admire Griner for keeping her sketch in the background.
