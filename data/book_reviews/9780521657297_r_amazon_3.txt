The great epistemologist, ....the 'central figure of modern philosophy, who set the terms by which all subsequent thinkers have had to grapple.'Kant's transcendental deduction, that a-priori intellectual faculties determine the form of experience, and so the conditions for the understanding, was developed as a solution to David Hume's refutation of the empirical deduction of certain concepts. In particular, that there is no analytic link between cause and effect, but rather, that causality is merely a matter of constant conjunction of events. Therefore implying that this concept is 'applied to', rather than 'deduced from' experience,... i.e. it is a-priori to experience. Likewise, Time is an internal intuition, ...a means of ordering experience given the nature of mind. Thus Kant adds to analytic propositions and synthetic  propositions, the notion of synthetic a-priori propositions.Given the nature of mind, we effectively supply the conceptual structure or form, in which experience is ordered and synthesized. This occurs prior to consciousness, again given the nature of mind, and thus is a necessity for experience to be possible at all. 'Reality per se', that is, Reality 'as it exists in itself', must then be conformed to our conceptual framework to be known at all,... rather than necessitating that our intellectual faculties conform to the Objects of 'Reality per se'. This was Kant's "Copernican Revolution" and retort to Hume.Reality as experienced, is termed 'phenomenal reality', or 'empirical reality'. It is implicit in Kant's transcendental deduction that empirical reality, must have a mind dependent component, i.e. the conceptual structure in which it is conformed. But what of unconceptualized Reality, that is, Reality as it is in itself, apart from mind? Kant called this Noumenal Reality, and is unknowable in principal.It is reasonable to subtract the form in which we know empirical reality, that is, the mind dependent components, and be left with an unknowable "something". This is a rational means of deriving Noumenal Reality as objective.Kant effectively demonstrated that metaphysics can not be a source of knowledge, as such questions are not amendable to the a-priori forms of the understanding, ...Kant's categories. As he stated, "I had to deny knowledge in order to make room for faith". Effectively, this necessitates an Agnosticism with respect to knowledge of God's existence, and places Atheism (if taken as being clearly delineated from Agnosticism) on the same rational level as Theism. Though Kant did not state this implicitly, to my knowledge.Viewed from a modern perspective and with respect to 'Noumenal Reality', it is reasonable to suppose that the a-priori intellectual faculties by which the mind evolved to synthesize experience at the macroscopic scale, are in effect artifacts, and therefore effectively artificial as applied to the microscopic realm as in quantum mechanics. Kant could not have foreseen this however.So in light of developments in modern physics, in particular quantum mechanics, Kant's transcendental deduction, should be restated as follows,... 'that a-priori intellectual faculties determine the form of experience, and so the conditions for the [intuitive] understanding'. But these are artificial conditions that are dependent upon the nature of mind. Indeed, no one has an intuitive understanding of quantum mechanics, despite it being the most accurate physical theory ever devised by man. Indeed, there remain immense conceptual problems, despite having gained predictive knowledge.Kant's epistemological analysis is relevant in the philosophy of modern physics, especially with respect to scientific (logical) positivism as opposed to 'objective realism'.The Copenhagen interpretation regarded the incompatibility between the unitary evolution of the wave-function and the non-deterministic state reduction as ultimately an epistemological issue, and not a fundamental issue with the completeness or otherwise of theory. The Schrödinger wave-function 'collapse' upon measurement, is a collapse into concepts, into conceptual values dependent upon mind, ....in it's experimental apparatus design, means of communication, and interpretation of results at the macroscopic scale. Mind necessarily presumes locality, counterfactuality, space and time, separability, and causality,.. for the intuitive understanding.Abraham Pais remarked that Bohr was the natural successor to Kant. W. Heisenberg developed a non-intuitive, operational approach based on observables, in matrix mechanics, while E. Schrödinger along with A. Einstein still expected Reality to conform to intuitive principals.
