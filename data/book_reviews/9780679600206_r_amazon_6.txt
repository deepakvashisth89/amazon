Constance Garnett's translations of Dostoevsky, which were the standard for so many years, have the virtue of being mostly literally accurate, but she clearly misses the nuances of Russian, not being a native speaker.This translation, by David Magarshack, who was born in Riga but educated in England, has the great advantage of being the work of someone fluent in both languages.  His version of NOTES FROM THE UNDERGROUND, which is included in this volume, is arguably the best in English, because it reads better than most other versions, with the possible exception of Jessie Coulson's.The Peavar version, the most recent, doesn't have the qualities of Magarshack, although it's touted as the best.Unfortunately, most of Magarshack's translations of Dostoevsky were done for Penguin, which apparently follows the practice of updating translations every decade or so, a dubious way of proceeding given that some translations don't need to be replaced.Signet Classics has wisely kept Magarshack's translation of ANNA KARENIN in print, and in spite of quite a few translations since his was published (including two superfluous versions recently), it's the KARENIN of choice.Read moreRead less
