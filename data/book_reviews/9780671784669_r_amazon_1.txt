This is one of the funniest books I’ve read, with numerous laugh-out-loud passages.Poor, put upon Wormold, British ex-pat scraping out a living in pre-revolutionary Havana, selling of all things, electric vacuum cleaners in a country with unreliable electricity.  He is devoted to the welfare of his teenage daughter, a devout Catholic with precocious and expensive tastes.  Drawn into spy service by a mysterious Englishman, his life is turned upside down with hilarious results.It is a farce, a satire of the secret service, which Greene was part of during the early part of WWII, and from whose experience some of the plot was derived.  As light as it is, Greene wants to make deeper critique of governments and nations and the inhumane things done to prop them up.  It is reminiscent of Evelyn Waugh, who was an inspiration to Greene.This reads very much like an old black-and-white English comedy, and it’s no surprise that it was turned into one, as were others, including his wonderful and far more serious “The Quiet American.”  The dialogue is witty and snappy, with characters talking over each other and misunderstandings galore, being pulled into a whirlwind of confusion.I love this book.Read moreRead less
