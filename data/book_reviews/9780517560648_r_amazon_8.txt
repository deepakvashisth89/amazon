This is a magnificent book.  I could hardly put it down it was so beautifully written and so powerful.  I knew a little about Elizabethan England, and some about Elizabeth I before, but now I feel truly enlightened.  While, obviously, we'll never really know what the motivations, thoughts and feelings were for the people of those times, or how Elizabeth was really impacted by the execution of her mother, the book presents a plausible portrait of what might have been; indeed what seemed to make the most sense for the culture in which they lived.One loves, fears, dislikes and admires Elizabeth  by turns, in this book.  Mostly, however, I found I could easily identify with her and her view of the world, power, loyalty and men.  Cecil and Leicester became real people to me...ones I felt I understood and knew.  In fact, many historical people, whom I had only a cursory interest in before, became fascinating players in a glittering, deadly, often bloody, political game played on Europe's stage in the 16th century. Without a doubt,  Elizabeth was the master of the game.  Yet, I got the sense of her vulnerability, too.  She was a multi-faceted personality with depth, passion, conflicting emotions, and a wry wit  and flash of mischief that beguiled.  Indeed, all the major characters in this story were multi-dimensional and very real, as were the settings.There is violence in this book.  It was a violent time, and some of the things that happened would be considered barbaric to us, nowadays.  There are also a few sexual scenes.  Nothing offensively graphic, but it's there.  This story of Elizabeth is not sugar coated.  It's realistic and  gritty, yet also lyrical and beautiful.  The historical events of Elizabeth's life are related accurately.I was sorry when it ended. But what an ending!  My heart still aches with the rightness of it.  I will read this one again.  There is so much to absorb that one reading alone cannot do it justice.Read moreRead less
