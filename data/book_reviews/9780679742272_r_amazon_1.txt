A S Byatt is an award winning author (also sister to equally celebrated Margaret Drabble.) Her stories and novels are often semi-autobiographical. In "Racine and the Tablecloth" the main character Emily is probably Byatt herself, being oppressed by a passively-aggressive school mistress. The story "July Ghost" is also semi-autobiographical--it deals with the ghost of a little boy living in a garden in someone's house and Byatt lost her 11 year old son to a car accident and has been haunted by the terrible loss ever since."Sugar" the title story, deals with lying and other sins of parents and adults--the mother is introduced as not being truthful and every remark she makes is followed by "or so she said." The family owns a boiled sweet factory (hard candy to us Yanks) and a sad little sentence about the aunts and uncles of the narrator being yanked from grammar school as soon as it was possible to put them to work for the family's sweet factory whether or not they had other inclinations, and how they escaped this was positively Victorian but told in spare words.Lots of pain in these stories and being haunted, not by vague spirits but by the ghosts of family past.
