I actually didn't think that this book was all that bad.  Yes, it was very formulaic, but if you follow certain HP authors, then you know that most of them always follow their own formula.  For instance, how many HP books have the mega-rich ultra billionaire, usually foreign?  Some authors pick English roses as heroines, other pick Australian beauties.  So the formula doesn't bother me so much.I titled my review "Oldie, but Goodie" because this book is an older edition.  You know that immediately because the book itself is dated in terms of technology and fashion.  And while one good discussion would have resolved everything, then we wouldn't have a book to read.I personally enjoy books that delve into the relationship after marriage.  I liked that this book was about a couple that wasn't fighting all the time.  Rather, they were in a marriage of convenience, but it was one that both parties actually worked at.  Sure, each one went into it secretly in love with the other, but they also didn't really try to hide that from the other side.  They were in a true marriage, working together (literally), sleeping together, going to functions together and just generally meshing their lives together.  The Evil Stepsister was a weak plot, but she served her purpose in the book.Bottom line: I think that sometimes you are in the mood for a simple romance.  And that's what this was.  It was a simple plot from beginning to end and the only real conflict existed in the wife's own mind and insecurities because her husband did constantly try to reassure her that he wasn't interested in the Evil Stepsister.  However, if that's what you're in the mood for, then this is perfect.I will also agree with another's review about how this author spends too much time on describing meals and outfits.  And in this book that tries to show the busy married life between H and W, we get a LOT of this.  I understand that the author is trying to describe how ultra-rich these people are, but: (1) it really does date the book, and (2) it fills up waaaaaay too much of the book's actual pages, and (3) the author needs to realize that some things, epsecially matters of personal taste, may backfire.
