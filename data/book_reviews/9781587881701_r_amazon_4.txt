A wild ride from beginning to end with author William Gay, a carpenter turned author somewhere around the age of 50. It is 1952, somewhere in the hills of Tennessee. Several generations of the Bloodworth family lead storied pieced-together existences they call life. Riddled with lots of moonshine, guns, women and some not-so-admirable behavior is a family that isn't completely bad beneath it all but certainly eccentric, often angry and very dysfunctional. The youngest, Fleming Bloodworth, a wannabe writer, is surrounded by a Dad and several uncles/cousins that aren't role models for much that is good yet for the most part Fleming remains steadfast, principled and sensible. I found him to be a great character, lovable and charismatic, offering some relief from the other eccentricities happening. Add a Grandfather that is quite a colorful character himself. He disappears, abandoning his family for a 20 year stint as a musician in Arkansas. (It seems like there was brief mention of him spending some time " in the Pen" as well.) E.F. (Grandpa) returns to Tennessee and his family in search of peace but as you can imagine, he generates a bit of controversy in the process. Then there is the uncle that sells hexes. Need I say more?If you like a bit of shock, twists and turns and just plain ghastly ugliness that is hard to fathom, you may enjoy this book immensely. It is quite well written. A few mentions though...keeping the characters straight is a chore at first. The writing style, like Cormac McCarthy, employs no quotation marks so that takes some getting used to. Keep a dictionary handy as well. He uses foot-long fancy words. And... be prepared for a few ghastly incidences. No spoilers from me. Read the book!After having read an article on the author, I wonder if some of the characters and situations were founded somewhere in his Tennessee upbringing. If any of these characters were modeled after real-life folks he knew...well that's a scary thought!Be sure to read the Prologue.
