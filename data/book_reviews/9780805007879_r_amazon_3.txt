Jonathan Parrish, a homosexual who has just celebrated his fortieth birthday with men of a similar life style,  is found the next morning stabbed to death.  His straight brother, Ralph,  who is visiting from Indiana  is charged with the murder.  Attorney Matthew Hope believes Ralph's story that he  found is brother already stabbed and simply removed the knife leaving his fingerprints as false evidence.  Matthew Hope  enlists private detective Warren Chambers to help find proof of his client's innocence.This is an interesting novel with a good plot line, dialogue and characters. There are several sub plots that are also interesting. I am only giving it a three star "It's okay" rating because I was  a bit disappointed.  I thought this series about a lawyer would have some courtroom scenes with legal schemes and action.  There was none of that. While reading, I kept forgetting who was the lawyer and who was the private investigator; they both concentrated on investigating the crime, not on any legal work.The one small bit of legality in the novel did no ring true.  At one time police Detective Morris Bloom and  Matthew Hope show up with a search warrant.  (Yes, police officers love to help a defense lawyer prove that they have arrested and falsely charged an innocent man.)  There is no scene in a judge's chamber showing the arguments put forth for issuing the warrant. Good thing, too.  As far as I could see there was no evidence whatsoever that would justify issuing a search warrant, especially for the premises for one of the wealthiest and most powerful families in the county if not in the country.The author's writing style was also somewhat disconcerting. He often times would shoot out sentences in a staccato fashion:"And silence.Parrish races downstairs.A door slams.Through the window, he sees someone running up the beach.Running through the rain.Wearing black.On the floor, his brother is wearing red."I suppose that is to give more of a feeling of action than would be the case of a simple paragraph describing the action.  To me it interrupts the flow of the story as I am brought to mind that this is the author writing, not the action occurring.My three star rating does not mean that people who enjoy fast moving crime stories will not enjoy this novel. I'm sure they will.  It was just not my cup of tea for the reasons that I have put forth.Read moreRead less
