Ben Hur is meticulously plotted but uneven in pace. The first 1/5 of the story moves very slowly. Some parts of the book are riveting, and other parts left me asking, why am I reading this? Late in the book the author addresses the reader directly, trying to explain why he belabored an earlier scene.Ben Hur sold very well in the nineteenth century, and again in the twentieth when there was a movie adaptation. I’m glad I read it recently, but I didn’t plan to write a review.Then I saw a tweet by the Babylonian Bee's Joel Berry characterizing the novel as “a work of Christian Zionism and love for Jewish identity before the modern Zionist movement even existed.”Excuse me, but what a crock. Using the framing of Berry's characterization, it is the opposite. Berry must have confused Ben Hur with Barabbas. The novel’s the story of a revengeful Jewish man who forms a militia to overturn Roman rule and establish the Christ's political and military reign on earth, only to abandon that view and accept that the Christ's reign is not of this world. He turns away from his countrymen who sought Jesus’ death, including his militia members, and becomes Christian after witnessing the Crucifixion.
