People have said it in many ways: this is an amazing book, a must read.Okay, for me, the book raised a question: can I, in good faith, align myself with the term 'Christian' given the incalculable harm that has been executed under this name - for centuries? If I encapsulate time and compare the fruits of Christianity to Nazism ... well a heck of a discussion may ensue. So indirectly this book can stimulate debate about core values versus actual outcomes and so on and on. What I mean is that the book reaches beyond its content to raise deeper and obviously challenging questions.To clarify the point: the book can make you weigh recent examples of evil, with which we have some familiarity and knowledge, and ask is this worse than Christianity's history of evil. Do I put Nazism on the other side of the scale or the shocking bloodbath that was the Islamic invasion of India some centuries ago or the egregious rape of Germany by Russia after WWII? Ultimately, does the 'Dark Side of Christian History' obliterate the value of Christ? Yes, for some readers, I believe it can get that weighty.So I'm saying, it's thought provoking and can raise disturbing questions. If you are a Christian, this is your history and part of your heritage. One's identity as a Christian is, in my opinion, obliged to own the history that goes with it. I'm not sure I can align myself with the scale of this dark side, this body of evil beggars belief.So beware if you read this book, at some fundamental level, you may feel gutted. One question about that is: is that a bad thing?Apologies if this review was unsettling or perceived as in some way reprehensible, The content of this book is much more disturbing; personally I am still reeling.
