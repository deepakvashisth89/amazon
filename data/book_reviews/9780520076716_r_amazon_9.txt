A medieval myth that addresses the naturalness of human behaviour even when it offends social mores that are considered to be sin. By taking the idea of a child conceived in sin - in fact in greater sin that the words imply according to modern euphemism -  and addressing his life and its eventual outcome, Mann examines his own ideas of good and evil. Read today, it can seem moralistic, and it is. It is also a lovely story.
