This novel was not at all like what I remembered from the movie.  But then I think I saw the movie in the late 1940’s so my memory is not that clear.  I am certain it was nothing like the book and I mean this a negative way.  I recall an entirely different story where a young woman meets Tarzan, probably because he saved her life from a tiger, and they talk.  Tarzan says, “Me Tarzan, you Jane” This was probably after she starts teaching him English.  This never happens in the original story.  In this great novel the story is much more complex and with many characters.  The ending is completely unexpected, at least to me.  This is a classic example where the movie does not do justice to the book.  I’m sure the movie version was done to simplify the rather intense plot so children could understand and enjoy the movie.  As I was preparing this review I learned that many movies were made about the character, Tarzan.  I think the one I saw was done in 1939 (viewed by me about eight or nine years later).Another thing I found interesting about the book is that the writing style is very similar to what we have today.  There is no Victorian or Edwardian vernacular in the book.  By this I mean the phrases like, “It was a singular moment in her life” or “They had a gay time watching the show.”  (Note:  After writing this I saw that this novel might have been edited with more modern language.)
