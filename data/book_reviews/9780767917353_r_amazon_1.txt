Engrossing read from start to finish by a former journalist for the Village Voice. Owen chronicles the NYC and Miami clubs scenes in the nineties, focusing on former Limelight/Tunnel/Palladium owner Peter Gatien and Risk/Liquid/Joia owner Chris Paciello. The book devotes a fair amount of time to Michael Alig and the Club Kids, major players in the success of Gatien's nightclubs and their eventual demise culminating with the death of small time drug dealer Angel Melendez and the arrests/prosecutions of Alig, Gatien, and Paciello. Fashionistas will also remember Ingrid Casares, Madonna's former BFF and former it girl in the Miami scene, who became Paciello's business partner.Having been to Limelight pre-Gatien ( and having read many of the Voice articles) I could not have guessed the level of corruption going on in these places...then again, when you go to a club or restaurant, do you stop to wonder whether the owner is involved in money laundering? Drug dealing? Murder? I remember seeing pretty boy Paciello in all the celebrity mags, never guessing that this guy was originally a minor player in the Brooklyn mafia. Fun fact: Mob wife Drita D'avanzo's husband Lee, who ran in similar circles as Paciello, is mentioned in the book. Another fun fact: Alig's room mate Brooke aka "Club Kid Brooke" sold Alig's gal pal Gitsie the drugs she OD' on.I don't, as others here have noted, think that Owen is being sanctimonious or prudish, nor do I think that he blames these clubs for all the evil in the world. His account is balanced and neutral. However, one would be stupid to think that Gatien knew nothing concerning the drug dealing that was going on in his club, even if he did not explicitly condone or directly profit from it. Paciello, on the other hand, is an outright murderer. It's amazing to me how someone can reinvent themselves by changing their zip code and knowing the right people.Read moreRead less
