If 9/11 Mysteries is the 'best' video for raising questions about 9/11 in a systematic and compelling way, then surely 'The New Pearl Habor' by David Ray Griffin is amongst the 'best' (if not 'the best') of the *books* available.In the same way as 9/11 Mysteries, the author carefully goes through accounts put forward by the Bush administration (or those close to it) and compares them to the accounts provided by other witnesses.  Gradually, and systematically, he examines all the contradictions between different accounts.  As a result, he persuasively argues that the Bush administration, and sympathetic news corporations, add to confusion rather than clarity.In a compelling penultimate chapter, he reviews what a person would need to believe for a theory of 'incompetence' by the Bush administration to be sustainable.  This is followed by a similar review of 'coincidence' as a plausible theory for the events of 9/11.  In both cases, he shows how these theoretical positions are much more problematic (and difficult to believe) than a theory of 'complicity' by the Bush administration.The language here is important.  For Griffin, any theory involving people working together to attack the US on 9/11 is a 'conspiracy theory'.  As he points out, the public are faced with choosing between believing a conspiracy theory put out by US government officials (involving 19 Islamic terrorists), and alternative conspiracy theories put out by its "critics" (theorising varying level of complicity by members of the Bush administration).  The issue, therefore, is not whether there was a conspiracy or not, but whose conspiracy theory is more believable.By following the evidence, Griffin makes the case for a full public inquiry.Dr Rory Ridley-DuffSheffield Hallam UniversityUnited KingdomRead moreRead less
