You ever get that feeling that someone was sneaking up behind you,  bitter, with a knife ready to splack it right in your neck?  The pain  being both alert, shaved, and terrifying like rusty nails? Well, you don't  get that feeling when reading these "poems."  On the  same level  of mediocrity as Hallmark cards, but not exactly, these poems  have a haze  of luke-warm, as if imaging someone repeating "blah, and  blah, and  blah" in the background, until an almost silence lulls you to  a  lobotomized almost sleep.  At least Hallmark cards know what they are.  "Florida" for example reminds one that TV isn't as bad as they  say it is  compared to bad pastoral poetry.  But it really isn't "bad  poetry."  Bad poetry  can be interesting at times.  Some bad poetry  can be spoken from the heart.  Some bad poetry still manages to prick the  spine.  No, these are poems to read when nature  calls to move things  along, or when nothing calls, or when death seems an option.
