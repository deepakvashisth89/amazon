As readers of my reviews know, I rarely review “classic” books.  Why throw in my two cents on top of a hundred years of critical study?  However, readers of my reviews might also be aware of the fact that I’ve never been much of a fan of Lawrence.  I find Lady Chatterley’s Lover to be very uneven and his “minor” work decidedly minor.  However, I ran into a number of instances where I was informed that Sons and Lovers is really Lawrence’s masterpiece, so I broke down and started reading this.  Finally, I’ve found a Lawrence novel that supports his fame.  This book is a masterpiece.Unlike other Lawrence novels, this book is very readable.  There are no dull passages or extended philosophizing here.  It is, first and foremost, a story about people—people you really believe live and breathe.  There are really two big divisions to this novel.  The first part focuses on the disintegrating relationship between Morel and Mrs. Morel.  Though told mainly from Mrs. Morel’s point of view, the sympathy towards her doesn’t change the fact that both clearly play significant roles in the failure of their marriage.  Perhaps it is that recognition that keeps them together in spite of their differences.The second part of the novel focuses on the children of the marriage.  There are four children who get real mention; however, after a brief interlude with the eldest son, William, the lens primarily shifts to Paul, who develops relationships with two women.  Miriam is a model of the ultra-religious woman, condemning herself and her relationships with the denial of this world.  Then there is Clara, a married woman who brings out Paul’s passion but cannot connect to him on any other level.  Of course, despite his introspection, many of Paul’s problems fall squarely on his own shoulders because of how he allows the shadow of his mother to fall over all his choices.Despite the fact that this was written 100 years ago, it still resonates.  There is so much more I could say—analyzing scenes, characters, and relationships—always the sign of a great book, but I will refrain.  Let me just say that, unlike his other novels with their clunky characters that seem to stand in for concepts rather than real people, this story is one that could play out easily today.  If you’d given up hope on finding Lawrence likeable, like I had, read Sons and Lovers before making your final judgment.  It might change your mind.  It did mine.Read moreRead less
