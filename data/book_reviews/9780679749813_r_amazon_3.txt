The author traveled through much of the Balkans, toting only a pack with a change of clothes, toiletries, and books.  He traveled by bus, train, boat, foot and thumb.  My wife and I also travel this way, except that in lieu of books we carry Kindles and a laptop.  In 2013 we intend to spend two to three months in much of the same areas.Kaplan does not claim an exhaustive historical knowledge of the region, nor to have a special understanding of the peoples and places.  Rather, he travels, mixing with the people and trying to understand why things turned out as they did, and what the future might bring.This is not a history book, nor is it a traveloque.  It is one man's insights into a confusing stew of peoples.  Does it represent a "western" perspective?  How could it not!  He is a western man, delving into a mixture of East and West, old and new, fact and myth.I found his comments and observations fascinating.  I have, myself, felt similar feelings and mental confusion when traveling in strange and (sometimes) unsettled places.
