This book does not speak to everyone. I have recommended this classic Hesse work to a number of friends and acquaintances to varying results. It seems to me, that much as Hesse discusses in the novel there seem to be a select group of individuals who can completely relate with this book's subject matter and others that are able to read this book, put it down, and leave it on the shelf with every other book they have come to read. Having said that, I firmly believe this book should be sold with a warning label. The life you were content living prior to picking up this book will seem empty and dishonest if you find yourself among those people who this book seems to speak directly to. I have known a number of people, including myself, who have went on to make dramatically life altering decisions following their embarking on the spiritual quest that is Demian. The book's passionate and painfully truthful confrontation of the self can be resisted at first, but the book will hang over you like a cloud for many months, mocking you as you try to limit its access into your psyche. Ultimately the ideas delivered through this classic work of fiction will radically alter the way you understand everything. The Demian disciple becomes the fish that has discovered water. Perhaps there has never been a time where the ideas of the young Hermann Hesse have been more relevant. This book liberates man from the shackles of a society that hangs those who resist its ruthless tides of conformity. I urge you only to take this book seriously, read it slowly and carefully, while recording your thoughts and reactions to the various episodes in this book.
