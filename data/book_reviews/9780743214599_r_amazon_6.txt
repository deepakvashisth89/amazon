I think I've read everything in this genre, after getting hooked by Forester as a boy.  Much of the genre is predictable, almost formulaic - Our Hero perseveres against political, romantic and nautical and meteorological challenges in order to fight tyrants on the seven seas.  Forester, Lambdin, O'Brian are the leaders in my opinion. This series (I've read up to 24) is consistent.  It's a fair read, with a difference in the story - this guy comes up from the 'pressed men' or involuntary enlistees.  Great angle - we usually see life only from the quarterdeck.  That advantage is soon lost, however, as Stockwin's Hero is perhaps the most self-centered captain of this genre. Very few names or interactions with others (other than his 'particular friend') and this really hurts the series later.  Unlike most of these books, Our Hero spends little if any time thinking of subordinates (leaser development, etc.) and has to be at the fore in all things.  Well, sure, the book is about him, right?  But no. He's a jerk.  His friend (Renzi) introduces his significant other to his family at the front door, having not told her he is quasi-royalty. Any self-respecting woman placed in that position would have broken a flowerpot over his head.  Prize ships left unmentioned (they win the battle, then they're off across the ocean - who is the prize crew?  You're now shorthanded - any thoughts? Logistics concerns come and go as the writer gets lazy.  After a while the story faults add up and get annoying.  With Kindle you'll always get typos and weird transcriptions - I don't mind that.  But Mr. Stockwin would have been well-served by having a focus-group of genre readers to edit and highlight the plotline omissions.  These books take an enormous time investment to do right - and he does his homework.  Overall, it's a B- for the series.
