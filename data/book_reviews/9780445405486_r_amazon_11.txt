A missing American tourist, an armed robber targeting cinemas, eight hundred lustful belligerent sailors on shore leave, a man chops his wife and her lover into pieces with an axe, the Chinese turn the water supply off and a turf war is brewing among gangs with the arrival of a brutal protection racketeer known only as the Mongolian with a penchant for chopping off fingers and ears. Set during the last days of the British occupation of Hong Kong, it's just another day for the cops of Yellowthread Street in the seedy district of Hong Bay.I was first introduced to the Yellowthread Street series of books by the sub-par TV adaptation of the same and spent the next couple of years hunting down every book in the series - no mean feat as they were all out of print and had been for several years. I have a battered second-hand copy of this book and although I dislike e-books I bought this one anyway. Because I was so excited to see it had been re-released. It's good to see them in print again if it is only is electronically. I'm old school. Paper copies please. With pretty covers.I'm of the opinion that William Marshall is a damn genius writer. He's spare, the plots - there is more than one - jump around all over the place and, yet, not one word is superfluous. There's no purple prose here and Marshall proves you don't need it. It puts modern waffly doorstoppers to shame. Dialogue is written with two or more conversations all going on at the same time - weird to read at first until I realised that this is how people actually speak and it adds an authenticity to it. Add to that some decidedly non-PC attitudes and some very black humour and Yellowthread Street manages the delicate balance act of being hideously gory and very funny. The characters are well-drawn in spite of the sparse writing - weary old hand Detective Chief Inspector Harry Feiffer, half-Chinese, quarter-American, quarter-Irish and with an identity crisis to boot, Detective Senior Inspector Christopher Kwan O'Yee, blunt instrument Detective Inspector Phillip Auden, newbie posh boy Detective Inspector William Spencer, the Chinese constables - Sun, Cho and Lee, WPC Minnie Oh and ancient cleaner Ah Pin make up the staff of Yellowthread Street along with ghoulish coroner MacArthur. Seedy and violent though Hong Bay is, Marshall makes you yearn to spend time in the company of these characters. I wish I could write as concisely as William Marshall, but as you can probably tell from this review, I'm a bit waffly too.There's been nothing much added to his blurb although I was bummed to see that Marshall died in 2003. A real shame as I would have liked more insight into his unique writing style and how he came up with the idea for this series.I can't recommend these books highly enough (I'm re-reading them on and am on no 9 now) - funny, brutal and downright poignant at times, it is categorically the best series I have ever read.Read moreRead less
