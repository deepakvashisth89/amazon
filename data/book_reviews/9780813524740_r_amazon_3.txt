I totally enjoyed the beginning of the book.  The author gave a vivid account of Stein's early home life She wrote with the clarity which gave me a precise picture of the dynamics which often explained the divisions in the family later in the book. As I continued to read, I especially enjoyed the parts which described Gertrude's active life. I loved the sections when she actually quoted Gertrude which let me see and hear and feel her as a person and as a writer who desperately tried to get her works published.. However, then Martin  introduced endless "pages" of names of people who surrounded Stein as she became more and more well known. I understand the biographer's motivation in listing reams and reams of famous and not so famous people who came to visit. But it was so boring. Just when I was ready to delete the book, Martin shifted gears and went into detail of Stein's involvement in the Great War. It was such a relief. I absolutely loved the scenes with "auntie". Much too soon Martin began name dropping again.She did the same with Leo and Hemingway. That's when I gave up.I will look for another book about Gertrude Stein's lifeRead moreRead less
