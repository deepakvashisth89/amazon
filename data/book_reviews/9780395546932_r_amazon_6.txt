I give this a 5 star rating because, in order to truly understand a historical event, you must (duh!) understand the zeitgeist where and when that event happened.  This book is crammed with details that bring the truths and the reality of 1989 Beijing to life. In almost a throwaway paragraph on page 7, Shen Tong's father's parents commit suicide rather than be paraded around town wearing dunce caps and humiliating placards due to persecution by the Red Guards and a corrupt mayor.  This detail, and countless other details like it, provide us with illuminating evidence of the frustration typical of people inside that culture and that time feel due to things such as absolute power, lack of representation, and lack of free speech and due process... abstract thoughts made real by the details and specificity of one student leader's life at an extraordinary moment in time... and in the 20 years that lead up to it.All of the stories and details of this book paint a picture of a complex and tormented society and the reality of a person struggling with conflicting information and advice, of veiled and direct threats, of power struggles and egos and the uncertainty of misinformation.  In that way, this is a history book that better captures the actual internal struggle that is true to the reality of most tumultuous events.  And yet, this book captures the reality of what feels to be there, in an incredibly honest way.The book is a fast and entertaining read, as viewed by a smart and capable 20-year old.  Poetic at parts, and diligent in its day by day recounting of the 40-some days that led up to the Tiananmen Square protests and with some horrific details as personally witnessed in the massacre in the area surrounding the Square.  Other books may capture the events as they will be viewed in the sweep of time.  This book captures the chaos at the heart of it.Read moreRead less
