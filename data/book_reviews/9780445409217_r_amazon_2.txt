Why Australian novelist William Marshall is not better known or more greatly appreciated as a writer of mysteries and thrillers is a great puzzle to me. Perhpas it is because he is so versatile and shifts locales so easily. His Yellowthread series is set in Hong Kong from the 1970s to the 1990s; his Manila Bay series used the Philippines; and his two New York novels utilized the late 19th century to great effect. He can create comic characters and construct blood-chilling climaxes; his plots are complex and rewarding; and his ability to evoke a sense of time and place is second to none."New York Detective" follows clever detective Virgil Tillman and muscle-bound Ned Muldoon of the Strong-Arm Squad as they attempt to solve a series of peculiar murders seemingly linked to a disgraced unit that fought in the Civil War. It is 1883, the year the Brooklyn Bridge opened, an era of corrupt politicians, environmental crime, immigration controversies and casual racism.
