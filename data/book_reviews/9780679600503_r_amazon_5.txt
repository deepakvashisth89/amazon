I learned about Willa Cather (1873-1947) reading “The Ideal of Culture”, a wonderful book of literary criticism by Joseph Epstein, who referred to Cather as perhaps the best novelist of the twentieth century, although she did not earn the critical acclaim of many of her peers.This book is not so much a story, or even a tale, as it is a mural of a time, a place, and an inspiring relationship between two very different characters—the missionary bishop and the vicar. The time is the mid-nineteenth century, the place is what would become New Mexico, and the relationship involves two French missionaries; one cerebral and one that might be referred to today as “salt of the earth.”The prose is beyond exceptional. The cadence is sure-footed and assured. And it flows evenly, not lazily, but not in a torrent, either.Cather’s descriptive ability is clearly without peer. “The whole western sky was the colour of golden ashes, with here and there a flush of red on the lip of a little cloud.” And, “The sun was sinking, a red ball which threw a copper glow over the pine-covered ridge of mountains, and edged that inky, ominous cloud with molten silver.”Her character development, however, is equally exceptional. We not only come to know the characters, we experience their lives along with them. We literally feel what they feel and share both their joy and their sorrow. They have tangible physical and emotional dimension.There is much to take from her mural that is applicable to today. No other writer, in my experience, has brought human diversity to life in the sensitive but realistic way that Cather does here. The Mexicans, Native Americans, Europeans, Americans (admittedly white) that populate the story are defined literally but with great respect and admiration. All told it is that diversity, which Cather somehow integrates with the landscape itself, that gives the mural such beautiful color and sensuality.Was Epstein right? I must say that of all of the classic and contemporary novels I have read, this may very well be my favorite of all time. I will most certainly read more of her work.Read moreRead less
