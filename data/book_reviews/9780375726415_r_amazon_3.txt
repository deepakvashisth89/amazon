I think Dee is an excellent writer; his sentences practically spring off the page with intelligence, energy and wit. But much as I enjoyed several of his other novels, I struggled with this one. It was certainly readable in the classic sense, but the satire began to grate and the ending fell flat on many levels. I won't describe the plot since others have done so in detail, but it's a takedown of our modern world and the pretensions that have come to inform so much of what we think, do, purchase and talk about. Much of the focus is advertising gone amuck and I kept thinking of Mad Men and Don Draper as I read about the mad ad genius who is at the center of the plot (and might be Draper's spiritual legacy 2 generations later). The problem is that satire on this level doesn't engage the emotions, only the intellect. The characters feel like caricature stand-ins for various issues or emotions and it's hard to care about them. Molly, one of the main characters, is an annoying cipher -- hard to understand what makes her tick and why she behaves as appallingly as she does. You just want to slap her.
