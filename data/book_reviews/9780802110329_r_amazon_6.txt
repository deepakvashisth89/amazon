Bharati Mukerjee's Jasmine tumbles through rural and urban worlds, through different cultures and continents, using different names, in an imperfectly linear narrative. By the time the story ends, the reader is familiar with several versions of the young Indian girl first met in the small village of Hasnapur. However, the ending exemplifies a kind of magical realism, where she is reunited with a man, and his daughter Duff, to whom she was previously attracted - although her circumstances have dramatically changed, leading to potentially tragic consequences - never resolved nor discussed.Read moreRead less
