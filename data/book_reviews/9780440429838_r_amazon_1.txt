So here we are, at (until recently) the denouement of the series.For me, having first read the series years ago back in middle school, this was a good way to end the series.  It seems to me to be written in a more melancholy tone than the previous books.  Tom is growing up, and the chapter simply titled Thirteen well captures the spirit of this change from John's point of view.  All things must end, life changes, we change, and the world spins on.  It's a hard thing for a kid to grasp the first time around, especially when it involves a relationship that's as close as the one between Tom and John.I think this book handled the transition from boyhood to young adulthood very well...and I say young adulthood because in the time period of the books, a thirteen year old male is pretty much a young adult.True to form, Tom still gets into a little more trouble (the Chute the Chute a notable example), but it's clear that his father is looking at these hijinks through an entirely different lens than he did in previous books.  What used to be written off as boyhood mischief is now taken more seriously, especially as this is in the wake of the rafting accident back in book 5.Overall, there's just a sense in the writing that the fun is at an end for John, who after trying his hand at similar hijinks in book 3 is undoubtedly aware that no one is going to fill Tom's shoes when he's gone.  So there's some savoring of the fun and games while they still last...after all, Tom is now involved with a girl and is getting too old for kid stuff, as he puts it.Not having read the recently published book 8, I just have to say that this seemed to me to be the perfect way to end the series.  It strikes just the right tone as we the reader have to say goodbye to this family that we've come to know over the series.Read moreRead less
