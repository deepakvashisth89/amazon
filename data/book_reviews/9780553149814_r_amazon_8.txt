Agatha Christie is a popular “based on” resource of screenwriters that sometimes one forgets the movie or television episode began as a book. It has been quite awhile since I watched the British production of “The Mysterious Affair at Styles,” so the story was relatively new. This “affair” is Poirot’s first case, and as my favorite of all Christie’s detectives, I had lovely images of David Suchet along with Hugh Fraser and Philip Jackson. I was surprised, yet still entertained, to discover the first-person retelling of “The Styles Case” is from Captain Hastings’s perspective, rather than Poirot himself. As Hastings recalls, a domineering grand dame, tight with the purse strings, is found murdered. He is on hand at the family home having been invited to stay by the victim’s step-son, his friend, while recuperating from injuries sustained at the Front. The victim’s recent marriage is a serious issue, and the dispensing of the Will throws suspicions on several of the family, friends, and staff. [Enter Monsieur Poirot] I knew Hastings and Poirot were “mon ami-s,” and Poirot himself had a life before Christie, so along with with an introduction to Inspector Japp, holes were filled in with their backstories. Christie’s descriptive language, narrative prose, and engaging banter was a pleasant surprise to how much I enjoyed her written word: concise, informative, and limited straying from the problem at hand. Ok, I know Agatha Christie gained her iconic stature from her words, but I was used to others’ interpretations. I totally recommend to all that reading the real murder mystery, the “Gospel According to Christie,” is an absolute must!Several seemed to have reviewed a paperback version with negative comments.
