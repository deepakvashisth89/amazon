Michael Winter aligns the reader with his cause. He tells his readers they are smart, as smart as his narrator. When Gabriel’s thought process becomes extremely difficult to follow, the reader is motivated to look carefully for the clues. The narrator says plot and action don't interest him. What does interest him are the inner workings of his characters. Anyone who has been in a relationship that they can't live with and can't live without, will relate to Gabriel and Lydia. Here are details that are so human and recognizable but not even memorable until this novel puts its finger on how small and painful it was. Beautiful words to describe St. John's and Newfoundland.
