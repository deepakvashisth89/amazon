I’m thinking this was a pretty much your average and nondescript run of the mill murder mystery here. It’s titled A Certain Justice, but I’m finding it ironic since there is little justice when it comes to the ending of the book, how it’s handled, etc.I think this book was fairly uneven in various ways. We are given this big set up and much exposition that leads to the eventual murder of a high profile lawyer (Venetia Aldridge) early in the novel and the subsequent motives for various people who would be considered suspects or at least persons of interest in this crime. However, then it all sort of is left by the wayside in the second half when things take a different turn.I had a hard time getting invested in this book, could only read a few pages a day, and something about either the author’s style or the way she presented characters or situations were underwhelming at points. There’s the fact that there seems to be so many diversions that stall the plot. Also, this book is dominated by characters who are unlikeable, mediocre or pedestrian at best (including her central characters), or at worst have too many skeletons in their closet or are weak, broken in some way. Anyhow, to me they all fell sort of flat.Then, we have all this build up to the ending and it’s sort of handled in a clunky, anticlimactic way. You figure after all the investigation and work, and everything, it would be better handled, or we could have been given a slightly more coherent conclusion to this murder mystery.Not to say there aren’t some moments of intrigue about certain characters and situations, and clearly the author knows her stuff when it comes to this genre, but there were just too many inconsistencies for my liking so I ended up feeling pretty “meh” about everything with reading this book.Anyhow, this is my first read from this author and many say it’s her best, so not sure I’ll continue on with the Dalgliesh series.Read moreRead less
