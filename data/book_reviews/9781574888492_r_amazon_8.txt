I knew the punch lines, based on other reading.  Much of this was intuitive.  Scheuer, however, backs this all up with facts and reveals much more besides.  He gets beyond my intuition - and makes me uncomfortable in several areas, because he effectively challenged many of my presumptions and beliefs.  However, I am glad I read this - and plan to re-read it when it comes back from the people I've loaned it to.My mother asked how well written it was.  Not only is the grammar impeccable (I found a max of about 2 typos - and two or three sentences that required thought to parse), but what blew me away was the that layout of thoughts was impeccable.  He had a point to make and he made it with precisely the right content.  If this reflects the quality of work done by our intelligence analysts, I'm much pleased.  Of course, we know this quality of thought doesn't make it through to people like the President - at least based on the administration's actions.The only place Scheuer shows emotion and drops the ultra-clear and parsimonious text layout is when he discussed the abuse of or corruption within the Intelligence Community.  It's clear that he loves the community and hates to see it bent to office-political lying (posturing; CYA behavior, ...).
