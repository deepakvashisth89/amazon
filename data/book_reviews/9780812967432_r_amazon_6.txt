Dombey & Son is a Dickens novel that I was never before aware of. It was made into a miniseries for BBC in 1983 (had no idea! must go watch it now!), and it certainly wasn't considered required reading in English classes. So I dove into it with no great expectations (yuk yuk).What was different in this novel, for me, was that it seemed so contemporary. Dombey is a character who could easily exist in a 21st-century novel. He's a character who could exist in our lives right now and not seem out of place, by attitude or habits. A businessman who cares only for his business, he's a villain unlike any other Dickens villain. Not like a Mr. Murdstone or Mr. Squeers, his villainy is not so much malicious as it is just cold, unfeeling and single-minded. Yet he's all the more chilling for it.The novel grabbed me from the beginning, unlike some Dickens novels that take a while to get going. (Bleak House's entire first half was such a tedious slog.) Dombey & Son is a real page-turner; it also has second wife Edith, who Dickens scholars like to say is the closest character to a feminist that he ever created. I thought so too, as I read it, at least until the end, because a real feminist would not have succumbed so utterly, constantly punishing herself for not conforming. Still, Edith puts in a valiant effort. And, as Dombey's sister is fond of saying, "one MUST make an effort."I LOVED this novel and highly recommend it.
