I have several books on cat behaviour and cat behavioural problems which I purchased whilst doing my training to become a cat behavioural therapist, but this book is by far one of the best. The book is well written, in a humourous style (it has you laughing out loud at times) but also in a way that clearly explaines cats motivations for undertaking certain problem behaviours, and the ways in which to combat these behaviours. It is not overly simplistic or scientific, so is suitable for both a lay person and someone working with cat behaviour in a professional capacity. The book contains some novel ideas which I haven't seen anywhere else, such as the use of Romaine lettice to keep hungry felines full! and is generally very well laid out, with enough substantial examples of a wide variety of problems without becoming to repetitive. I'm suprised its not more popular really, I have been working as a cat behaviourist at the vets I work at in a professional capacity for two years now, and I still find it an invaluable tool to dip into when a case is stumping me and I need a fresh angle to approach it from.My only criticism  is because the book is written by an American author his views on cat de-clawing and keeping cats indoors clash with the classic British view that de-clawing is cruel, unnecessary and inhumane and actually interferes with a cats ability to express its full range of natural behaviours (which is a wellfare problem in itself as far as I am concerned) and I believe the majority of cats are much happier having access to the outdoors, again for the reasons above. But in fairness, the previous is the done thing in America, so to a certain extent it is hard to be judgemental in that respect, as im sure us Brits do plenty of things the Americans wouldn't approve of either! but overall it is a brilliant book on cat behavioural therapy, and one which I would highly recommendRead moreRead less
