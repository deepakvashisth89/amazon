Taking the sparse known facts about the founder of Manichaeism the book tells a semi-fictional biography of Mani, from his birth to his death.The book left me with mixed feelings. It's a nice read that reminds of a biblical story, focused on the prophet and the kings, archetypal, clear-cut.  In addition it may serve as an intro into the history of the Sassanian Empire. But having such exciting subjects as Mani and Sassanids the book falls short in exploring them. It only scratches tenets of Manichaeism and lacks character study and historical complexity.Overall, if you are interested in Mani or the Sassanian Empire the book can be a good starting point, but otherwise it's a nice, but missable read.Read moreRead less
