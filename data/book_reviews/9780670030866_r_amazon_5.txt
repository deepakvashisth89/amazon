I had no idea that Elizabeth Gilbert had another book before Eat, Pray, Love -- and it's really great! After just a few pages, you can really see how Eat, Pray, Love took shape, I think, because this is also a book about someone who found himself, marched to the beat of a different drummer, and broke out of just about every mold out there.As an avid reader of all kinds of outdoor literature, with an emphasis on adventure literature, I really appreciated and enjoyed this book. It's a detailed account of a guy who did things differently and always was one with and comfortable in the natural world. He's politically engaged, too, which is interesting -- so not a total recluse.For me, this book is deeply personal and also somewhat spiritual, as it's an exploration of finding meaning in this world that can be so meaningless for so many of us.
