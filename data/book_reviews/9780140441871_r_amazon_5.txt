I thought this book was very interesting to read mainly because it is the words and writing of Julius caesar himself. The only problem I had with this book is the chapters/sections of the book. They were all very broad and I honestly think that if there were more subheadings or subparagraphs the book would have been a lot easier to understand and work with.Sometimes Caesar shifts between military engagements, political maneuverings, and personal reflections, clearer organizational differences would have enhanced comprehension and flow.Other than that, I think reading his words and the overall plot was cool to read and it got me hooked. It was fun reading Caesars thoughts almost and insight about the Civil War. The book starts with what is going on in Rome, with the Senate and Pompey. A lot of it is at first difficult to understand because it involves many many different names, towns, cities, etc. One aspect of the book which I liked a lot was Caesars time levying and gaining troops around Italy and Europe. I thought it was funny that many people/leaders just gave up and gave over their troops to Caesar. Another part that was cool was Caesar explains his relationship with the Senate and even his relationship with Pompey, throughout the book he almost never talks about willingly harming Pompey or and type of war, even with anyone. I learned a lot about Caesar and that he really was a sophisticated and leveled guy who yes was extremely ambitious but was not overly violent towards his enemies, he was fair. In Pharsalus I felt like I was there because of Caesars words. In terms of readability, it needs getting used to. By the second chapter you should be good.
