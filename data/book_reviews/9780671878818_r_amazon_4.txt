Any science fiction fan knows David Drake, who worked through his Vietnam experiences in the "Hammer's Slammers" series. Well, he's mellowed a bit. He hasn't forgotten what he learned about war, but instead of various iterations of tanks and artillery against underequipped natives, this time we have real space navies that Pellew and Nelson would have understood. Technology drives tactics, as it must, and both technology and tactics are laid out carefully without slowing down the action. But character should drive story, and here it does, with our lead characters being a young and ambitious naval officer (Daniel Leary, son of the politically important Corder Leary) and an exiled aristocrat turned librarian Adele Mundy, last survivor of a prominent house Corder Leary wiped out more than a decade ago. Daniel has hopes, but being estranged from his father, no realistic expectations. Adele has nothing--no hope, no home and no fortune. But when the Republic of Cinnabar is threatened by a more modern totalitarian state, Daniel can't give up--and Adele finds that proscriptions or no, exile or no, she's still a Mundy of Chatsworth, and not to be trifled with.As I said, one of the best space operas of recent years, based on Drake's understanding of war and knowledge of history without being cloned from the history books. The setting is reasonably believable and the characters plausible and interesting. If you like the sort of SF in which interstellar empires clash, you want to read the series, and you want to start with this one.RCN Forever!Read moreRead less
