And I don't mean that disparagingly, but in awe: "The Five Fingers" is a riveting, compelling, absorbing bit of cold war era Vietnam fiction that will consume any reader of pulp war novels, or simply has an interest in reading about Special Operations.It's interesting seeing some of the comments left here by people who question the veracity of the writing. Yes, there are moments that unwittingly reveal the make-believe going on here. Nobody wears a beret into combat unless it's in the movies, and bush hats were easy enough to come by in Vietnam. Nobody sets off on a month-long mission with a 35 pound pack. And dig how many guns these guys were carrying, sometimes three to a man. Did their ammunition belts not figure into that carry weight?Those concerns are irrelovant: The book is a big, ghoulish, hyper-violent Roadrunner cartoon. It's an entertainment, not a documentary or expose on what really happened. I'm sure that many of the exploits described were within the realm of the possible but faulting the book for being less than accurate is like faulting Wile E. Coyote for leaving the outline of his figure after crashing into the wall of a canyon.I first read this book when I was about fifteen and was just as impressed by it then as I am now. It's a fantasy of sorts, like the Rambo films, that allows it's readers to see the US allied forces in Vietnam kick some serious tail, and show a unique vision of the hell of war from a first hand perspective.
