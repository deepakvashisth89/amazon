Having read Maria Riva's autobiography about her life in the shadow of her mother, Marlene Dietrich, I became curious to see how Cheryl Crane documented a similar story. Although perhaps not written with the mastery of Riva's book (which, in my opinion, sets the gold standard in the My Mother Was A Hollywood Movie Star genre), I very much enjoyed Crane's clear-eyed account of her troubled childhood and youth and how she ultimately got her act together. It can't have been easy to relive some of those terribly moments (and there were many) but I felt Crane gave us an interesting insight into what life inside those golden mansions can be like, and the price that is all-too-often exacted.Read moreRead less
