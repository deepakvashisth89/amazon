This is a second hand paperback with a slight looseness in the spine, but it is well worth the little I paid for it.His poems are of his time - in other words they rhyme and scan (and I think some have been set to music)  and sometimes use words like 'Yonder' and 'Lo!' The sentiments he expresses are, however, universal, even if somewhat gloomy and cynical at times; many of his poems are about wars and young men dying in them, the same sentiments and anger that echo in all war poetry.  One of the lines in XLIII of a 'Shropshire Lad' is: 'Fear the heat o' the sun no more Nor the snowing winter wild', which is very like a later war poem.His first volume -A Shropshire Lad' was published in 1896 and he published until 1922, I think.One of my favourite poems of his includes the lines:'The sun is up and up must I,To wash and dress and eat and drinkAnd look at things and talk and thinkAnd work, and God knows why.'Read moreRead less
