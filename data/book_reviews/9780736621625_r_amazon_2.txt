TINKER, TAILOR, among others, was a better LeCarre novel (my opinion).  There is no Smiley in this one.  But this is different.  Although there is a spy plot -- Russian nuclear secrets being offered and the near paralysis of British and American spy services in trying to confirm, much less trust -- this is for me a love story.  The main character, Bartholomew (Barley) Scott Blair, loves Russia.  In its way, Russia loves him back.  And eventually, he comes to love, maturely and unselfishly (his words, more or less) the female lead, Katya.Sean Connery and Michelle Pfeiffer play the leads in the movie and they do it well.  But the book is significantly different and deeper, more complex, in part because it is narrated by a man never seen in the movie -- Palfrey, a British lawyer who works for their Russia group, i.e. Russia House.  And LeCarre buttons up the novel with a devastating truth on the very last page about choosing between love (Barley) and mistrust (Palfrey -- and by extension, all spies).Great read and, in the end, a great and warm love story.Final note -- this has some of LeCarre's best dialogue, much of which is transplanted whole into select movie scenes.Read moreRead less
