This is the 7th edition of the NOLO IEP Guide. It is of paramount importance to always check the newest edition before attempting to negotiate the best IEP for your child because there are sometimes (as now) recent court cases and decisions that will affect what you are able to ask for (or demand, rather). A new U.S. Supreme Court case, dealing with "appropriate" education measures, is explained in this edition as well.No matter what book or guide you reference to help you, it is a long, hard slog through the law to sift out the opportunities available for your special child. And then your school district may not, in this era of the pinched penny, be able to meet your requests. After four years of reading everything I could get my hands on to assist in the writing of my grandson's IEPs, I settled on picking up the Complete IEP Guide first, as the fastest and most reliable source of information. But if you are advocating for a child with autism and are, as I am, unable to afford an attorney to help you in your face-to-face with the "powers-that-be" who decide what requests they will and will not grant for your child, then contact your state Autism Society and ask for the name of a parent advocate. These are people who have been-there-done-that for years and will not only attend your IEP meeting with you, but will help you prepare thoroughly by making the most of the intimidating forms and information in the help guides - even this one, which is in my opinion the best.Good luck and God Bless!Read moreRead less
