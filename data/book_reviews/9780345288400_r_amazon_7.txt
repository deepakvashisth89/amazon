Brian Daley’s scripts for the NPR radio drama adaptations of the three original trilogy Star Wars films were excellent, and in this writer’s opinion, a large part of the success of those shows.  Daley demonstrated that he got what George Lucas was up to, and could skillfully expand on the stories from the films with the result that the joins between Daley’s original material and the source is nearly seamless.So, it’s little surprise that Daley’s Han Solo Adventures trilogy of 1979-80, among the very first of the Star Wars “EU” (expanded universe) books, have such a strong feel for the original trilogy.  Selecting Solo as his hero was smart move—in the period immediately post ANH, the character’s backstory perhaps most interesting to fans, was the roguish smuggler.  Wisely, Daley set his stories several years before Han and Chewbacca ran into the Rebellion and in the Corporate Sector, an area of the galaxy out of the direct control of the Empire.  This allowed him to give the story line of Lucas’s evolving film franchise a wide berth and avoid any conflicts.“Revenge” is the second book in the series, and while one does not have to have read the previous "Han Solo at Stars’ End",  the earlier book does introduce a couple of the characters in this book (and is a fine read on its own.)  Trying to make a go of it in the authoritarian Corporate Sector, Han and Chewbacca are desperate enough to accept an anonymous charter.  Their clients turn out be from slavers who try to commandeer the Falcon, neither of which sit well with Han or Chewie, nor does the fact that were not paid.  The motive for the titular revenge established, Han is off to get paid and adventures ensure. Along the way Han meets a wiley and beautiful agent of the Corporate Authority, gets involved in a rivalry between clans, leading to an exciting finish.  It’s a fun stuff, fast-moving and replete with the humor and action one would expect from a Han Solo tale.  Recommended for any Star Wars fan.Read moreRead less
