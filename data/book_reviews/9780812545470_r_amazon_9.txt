Interesting concept. Occasionally sweeps you along when the author stops being self-conscious and trying to be clever and gets on with the story. Characterisation not bad for a novel of this type. Unfortunately it's often overwritten and overwrought to the point of unreadability. The author appears to mistake opacity for depth. As it's a horror thriller, a large dollop of nastiness goes with the territory. But there's subtle and not-so-subtle. Marano's writing falls into the latter category. Apparently the author majored in mediaeval history, studying in the School of Theology, specialising in alchemy and kabbalah (!) (weird subjects they learn in Boston U). Some of his occult knowledge obtrudes into the text. Put another way, it's not really integrated; more like a mini-lecture. I liked his use of the "art of memory" theme through the medium of the academic Ed, one of the protagonists (cf Frances Yates' book on this). However I suspect any reader unfamiliar with the subject would be more than a little mystified by it. So in summary something of a curate's egg. Worth a read and persevering through the overblown, boring bits if you're into this genre. However it's too long, the sort of book many will give up on.
