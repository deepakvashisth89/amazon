I am glad this wasn’t the first book I ever read by Brad Meltzer because I would have missed  so many of his really good books - especially the Beecher White / Culper Ring trilogy and many of his stand alone novels.The First Counsel  was an exhausting read that left me frustrated. The premise was interesting, Michael Carrick, a rising 29 year old lawyer, a member of the White House pool of attorneys, dates the daughter of the President. From the moment he let her drive his car on their first date, the action never stopped. For an attorney, Carrick made some really stupid decisions as this unstable First Daughter smiled and batted her eyes at him. Before you know it, he is on the run for a murder he  says he did not commit. Yet, the evidence against him keeps piling up.Fast paced and a page turner, I was so frustrated at the attorney and his decisions. He certainly wasn’t thinking as he raced through this story. And, race he did. Just reading Carrick’s fight or flight ( mostly flight) responses exhausted me.I did enjoy the information about the power struggle for who’s who in the hierarchy of the In-crowd of The White House Employees. I was offended by the liberal use of the word “retarded.”  Meltzer’s book was published in 2001, surely we were moving away from the use of such an offensive word.I would not recommend this particular book though I would be remiss if I did not mention that though Meltzer  bombed on this book, he is the author of the wonderful children’s series Ordinary People Who Changed the World. A winning series for sure.My advice, skip this book but read others by this author. A gift of a three star rating for The First Counsel because, after all, he is Brad Meltzer!Read moreRead less
