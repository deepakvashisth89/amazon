This book is hard to define. It seems to be a little bit of everything. First of all, it isn't an easy read. The format is different, devoid of quotation marks. I like that part. The story seems to just spin and keeps going. Here's the thing. The author tends to wax eloquently about shadows in the night, smokey shapes in the trees, sunlight on a bare wall and on and on and on. It's a bit much but the author does know how to set a scene. The humid southern summers and icy winters are vivid and real.The characters are real as well. Their frank, common sense dialogue is spot on for a practical, backwoods people in a small poor town. There's a scene where a family friend gets paid with a pig. He tries to put it in his car. I can't imagine a funnier bit out there. Thankfully, there's a good bit of humor to balance the darkness. The Bloodworth family has it's demons and struggles. As a reader, I struggled a bit with the point of view. The story starts with Boyd, moves on to Fleming and then centers on E.F., the patriarch of the clan. While it worked for this story, it felt a little odd at first. As I say, the thing can be elusive. This one can be wonderful yet maddening, full and sparse, sad yet hopefull. In the end, it feels like real life with real people. What more could you hope for?Read moreRead less
