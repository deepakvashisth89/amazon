As a lifelong student of religion, philosophy, linguistics and history, (as well as a Catholic) I have struggled greatly with my understanding of Christ, his Incarnation and mission on Earth, as well as the meaning of His death and resurrection. In "Jesus and the Lost Goddess", Freke and Gandy dissect the history and influence on proto-Christian mythology as the culmination of post-Exilic Judaism, Neo-Platonic, Egyptian and Zoroastrian philosophical thought dating to the centuries prior to Christ's birth. The authors discuss the differences between "Literalist" and "Gnostic" interpretations of the Christian myths that emerged in the 1st through 5th centuries A.D. during the time that Christianity sought to establish its "orthodoxy", or right-thought, and the eventual victory of the Literalists, culminating in the exclusion of any and all other interpretations of the Christ . The authors explore the deeper meaning of the Christ myth as understood by the Gnostics as a deeply intellectual, spiritual and mystical process of transformation, transcendence and union with the Christ-consciousness in us all. This was understood by the earliest Christians as a path to deeper consciousness, and consequently, to a greater love, joy and union with the Godhead. Personally, I found this book extremely liberating from many of the distorted, ugly and warped theological principles of the Catholic, Evangelical and Fundamentalist Christianity that dominate the world today.
