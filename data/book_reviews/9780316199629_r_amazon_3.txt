Tony Earley’s here we are in paradise exemplifies what it means to write excellent short stories. Earley uses simple language to engage readers in his stories that are all too familiar. He captures everyday life in flawed characters that elicit introspection from their readers. Earley artfully uses mundane and seemingly unrelated topics to talk about difficult and painful subjects. For example, in “Gettysburg” we read about the scientific names of starfish and the historical happenings in Gettysburg, all the while we really learn how to ache for someone who has made a decision he now regrets. We learn about relationships and the human experience of what it means to love someone imperfectly. Earley accesses heavy topics through the means of boxing, basketball, science, dams, ducks, etc. Personally, I really enjoyed this approach because it was fascinating to see how all of these seemingly unrelated strands would weave together to form a beautiful reading experience. I was most impressed by “The Prophet from Jupiter”. Initially, I felt slightly overwhelmed at how much detail I was given in the story. I felt like we knew everything about everyone in the small town of Lake Glen, North Carolina. This so impressed me because it was so accurate. I felt like I had been truly transplanted into this small town, and if that were the case, I would know everything about everyone. Earley created a realistic world for me to invest in and be apart of, and amidst all the town drama we get to focus on what really matters: how much does it take to break the dam inside.
