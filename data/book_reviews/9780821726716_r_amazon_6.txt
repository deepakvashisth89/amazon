The first time I read Savage Ecstasy was about twenty years ago, translated from English to my language. It made quite an impression. The second time around was when I got my husband to download the English version on Kindle for me just a couple of days ago.There are things that I do like about the story. The author brought the natural environment and the daily living of the indigenous people to life. In terms of the inevitable conflicts where two people from different racial backgrounds meet through extremely hostile circumstances, the author nailed it. My husband and I came from different cultural backgrounds. It took us years to get around and understand each other. So, the struggles between Gray Eagle and Alisha feel logical and realistic enough to me. Would the story sound more convincing if Gray Eagle, the son of a Sioux chief, spoke fluent English, followed the customs and etiquette of the white settlers, and behaved like a polished gentleman (according to the white man’s standards) towards Alisha? I don’t think so.And then, there are things that I dislike. Was menstruation cycle a much more taboo subject than violence, rape, and abuse two decades ago? Was Ms. Taylor undecided about her plotlines? None of the females in the story, white or indigenous, seems to have menstruation cycle. Gray Eagle keeps Alisha in his teepee for months and takes her in the night multiple times on a daily basis. They even had sex out in the open in the middle of a thunderstorm. There has got to be some chance of Gray Eagle’s seeds of life meeting Alisha’s egg. But there is nothing. There is no trace of indigenous females taking care of their bodily function. There is no mention of Alisha being embarrassed about her female needs, having trouble asking Matu for help to deal with menstruation discomforts or fear of getting pregnant with the child of a savage. There is some description of how Alisha feels nauseous whenever handling bloody meat or game, giving me a fleeting sense of suspicion: is she showing signs of early pregnancy? But then it just cuts off. There is nothing more. Even when Alisha is at Fort Pierre, there is still absence of the age-old female nuisance.From where I am sitting, the worst part of the book is the editing. It is horrific. There are baffling punctuation marks popping up just about every other page. How could the editor allow a period to appear in random places of a sentence? Why are there Spanish inverted question marks in English sentences? Why are there asterisks, bullets, and carets where there is no need for any? This story has been around for more than twenty years. That is more than enough time for an editing-upgrade, isn’t it? Should I be content because I paid only five US dollars for a story? That’s one overpriced cup of coffee for me to sugar-coat.I do wonder if Gray Eagle and the braves he rallied together attacked Fort Pierre as planned after getting Alisha out of there and back into his grip, if White Arrow ever managed to reconcile with Alisha and be her koda once again, if the Oglala people accepted Alisha as one of them, and whatever happened to Chela, Matu, or Powchutu. There are so many questions I would have liked to find out in the following books in the series.
