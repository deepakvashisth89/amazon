This was the first Greg Iles book I ever read, and it wasn't the last.  When I started reading it, I was almost instantly caught up in the characters and the story.  His settings and themes are similar to John Grisham's books, but, although I like Grisham and have read many of his books, Iles is a much better writer.  In fact, I would go so far as to say that he is one of the best writers I've ever read.  His writing is poetry, his story is the work of an artist.  I cannot recommend this book enough.Not all of his books have taken me in this way.  Some of them are too uncomfortable, too graphic, and I steered clear of his books for a time because of that.  But I have recommended this book over and over without reservation.  I'm reading it now for the second time, and aloud for a friend.  It's a great read-aloud book.He has recently put out a trilogy (the third book is due out in March of this year), that features the same characters as The Quiet Game, and is just as good as this one, though there are some descriptions of things done to blacks in the south that are pretty horrific.  I still highly recommend this trilogy.I have read several stories that were written in the present tense, and there has always been a sense of disconnect that kept me from engaging in the story because of that.  Iles does this very effectively and makes it work.Who are the great writers in my opinion?  George R. R. Martin (The Game of Thrones), J. R. R. Tolkien (The Hobbit), Frank Herbert (Dune), among others.  And, of course, Greg Iles.Read it.  You won't be disappointed.Read moreRead less
