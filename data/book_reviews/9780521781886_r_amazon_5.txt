The author is very thorough in his descriptions of types of certainty and uncertainty. However, what attracted me to the book was a reference in another book to the "inverse" Bayesian approach Pollack describes. His description starts out interestingly enough with "=4?", and how one makes sense out of the it. He then moves on to "=25?" and expands the idea of "reversing" matters to find plausible solutions. All this is very clear, and then he gives no Bayesian example plainly laid out. No math. Just words.Well, this development is certainly in-line with the rest of the book, so it probably shouldn't be unexpected. However,  he has no references to anything that might help with the math, which is basically the problem with the whole book. I would think many readers might be curious about the methodology of probability and statistics.If one searches the web for his publications, I believe a few have Bayesian in their title. I suspect they are heavily weighted to geology. Of course, many uses of Bayesian techniques and their application can be found on the web. Too bad though he didn't at least point to some places of general interest who want to go further.A nice introduction to the topics of certainty and uncertainty for someone who wants to get across these ideas to the public.Read moreRead less
