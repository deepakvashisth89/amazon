This little book is beautiful, but some of the praise for it is misses the point by being too much.  River of Earth is not Faulkner; it has nothing surreal or modern or high-flown about it, and it and seems of no literary era or school. It speaks in lovely Appalachian English, which conveys a structure of thought and ages of wisdom, sort of like the language of Cold Mountain, but here plain and real instead of artsy-sounding.  Hard life is portrayed authentically but never nostalgically.  For long stretches of time, people barely stay alive.  The little glories of nature and the reality of death are always close by.  Seasons govern.  Some people are kind, others mean, and there's no understanding why.  Family is the cornerstone of everything. This book is not "chick lit," but the women in it are amazing.  The reader feels the worth of small things that nowadays we take for granted. There is a scene where the narrator's father is trying to save the life of a calf that is choking on a corn cob, and I have not felt so desperate about a scene's outcome in a long time: there is so much riding on it. Life humbles people.  River of Earth is meaningful.  It will switch on a little light inside of you.Read moreRead less
