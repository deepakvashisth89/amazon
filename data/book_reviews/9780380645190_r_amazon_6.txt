A long time ago I read this book for an English class.  I was to give a very short review of this book.  Calvin Cohn, a paleologist, is  Jesus Christ,, the group of chimps are the Jews, George, the big gorilla, is the Gentiles.The book begins with the ending of the known world.  God is angry about the way humans abused and misused his beautiful world, so he destroyed it in a flood.  Cohn, working at the bottom of the ocean, survives.  God forgot him.  When God finds out Cohn survives, he is angry.  No more humans.  Cohn meets a young chimp, Buz, who he treats like a son and Cohn becomes a father.  The two drift on their falling away boat to a beautiful island much like the Garden of Eden. The two drag what they could, that would be useful off the broken down boat.There is much of religion.  Cohn is trying to teach Buz about Judaism while Buz wants Dod to learn abut Christianity.  Cohn and Buz philosophize much.  Buz, a young chimp, is very intelligent and wants to learn and know.  Cohn, Buz and the big gorilla who drifts into their lives.  Buz hates the gorilla.Surprising, five more chimps drift into Cohn's and Buz's lives.  Where they comes from, nobody knows.  Or if they do don't tell.  Different chimps, different personalities, only one female which all the chimps want.  Cohn tries to teach all that they will be the ones to people the earth.  Humans will never be again.  God talks to Cohn, still angry that he has forgotten about this man.  Then along come three more chimps.  Two males and an elderly female, from who knows where.  Again don't know, or won't tell.  All chimps are learning to talk,Buz has become quite an intellectual.  Cohn has a tree school where he teaches at the bottom of the tree, his students study in the branches and listen to the lecture.Again to this beautiful island comes a group of baboons again from who knows where.  The chimps hate baboons.  They are different, inferior as is George, the gorilla.  Cohn teaches the chimps to love one another, to care about all.  The chimps are now in the majority.  Esau.a mean, male chimp places himself as the alpha male and wants to be who he is, a chimp as all of his group should want.I am impressed and shocked by the violent way the book ended.  This is a short book, but gives this reader much to think about.Read moreRead less
