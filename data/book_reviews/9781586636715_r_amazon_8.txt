Nathaniel Hawthorne is one of the foundational novelist of American History. Writing during the early 19th century as a son of the Puritans he was thoroughly American and his novels reflect Antebellum New England culture and sometimes the early Puritan world. He is an important figure; but I am not a scholar and I cannot review a book like "The House of Seven Gables" as a scholar. I will review it as fan of literature and from that perspective I can say that this is almost unreadable. the plot structure is sound enough and its way of giving voice to ordinary people bearing the weigh of family history is a compelling idea. But the language reflects the Victorian verbosity so common in these works and the omniscient narrator makes it even worse. I felt that the novel -- as a novel and not as historical document or even as a "romance" which Hawthorne called it -- is at its best when ordinary people are speaking. In a chapter where two people are arguing or figuring out a course of action (like the last chapter), there is a certain beauty to the dialogue. But far too often the voice is of the narrator telling us what the protagonist might be thinking, or what someone like the protagonist might think or what anyone might think if thinking is the kind of thing that people find themselves doing, etc etc etc. It's unreadable for modern ears, much worse than Shakespeare which of course is all dialogue but also worse than James Fenimore Cooper. At least something happens in his books. For instance at one point in "The House of the Seven Gables" someone dies; we know from the situation that he is probably dead, but Hawthorne dances around the scene for four chapters. Four chapters!
