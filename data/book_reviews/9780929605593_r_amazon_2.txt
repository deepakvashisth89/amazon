Prince Marvell, the fairy on safari, is unlike any of Baum's other protagonists: a romantic hero, wandering freely through the semi-civilized countryside, looking for good adventure, turning evildoers into good guys.  His sidekick, Nerle, is another unique character in Baum; the only one I can think of who's anything like him is Sacho in THE SEA FAIRIES, and they're not VERY similar.  Sacho forgives those who hurt him; Nerle actually thanks them.This book is not without flaws.  Like so much of Baum's lower-quality work it suffers from aimlessly-wandering-around syndrome.  Oz books are almost all essentially wandering around, but in the better ones like WIZARD it's not aimless and there's more going on as well.  This is like DOROTHY AND THE WIZARD and ROAD and most of EMERALD CITY--essentially plotless, a riding tour.  Also, the whole Twi adventure is way too long.Another reviewer criticised the illustrations and the manufacturing quality.  The original first edition of YEW had only a few (five?) color plates, plus some early editions had orange line-drawings stamped over the text.  I have seen several recent very-badly-illustrated republications of YEW and this may be one.
