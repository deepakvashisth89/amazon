Decent story.  It was way too sympathetic to a person who betrayed her husband and friends.  I found the Brit in the story to be too self righteous  considering it was the Brits her introduced Slavery into what became the U.S. and that the Union "army of the light" included slave holding states.  We tend to view history through the prism of today's morality.  The war took place less then 25 years after most northern states ended slavery ( it wasn't abolished in Maryland, Kentucky, and other Union states until after the war ended) and it was the Northern states that grew rich form the slave trade (shipping, Rum, etc.) and the cotton, for textiles, coming from the slave holding more southern states. Slavery could have been brought to an end in the south if both Great Britain and the Northern States had simply stopped buying cotton from the Southern States.At the time of the war a relative small abolitionist group in the North had come around to viewing slavery as a huge sin while forgetting the complicity of the North in the institution of slavery and at a time when slavery was still fairly widespread in the world.  The larger majority of Northern opinion didn't turn against Slavery into early to mid 1863 (or later) when the war had taken the lives of 10's or a 100 of thousand Union troops and "saving the Union" probably began to seems a little thread bear of a reason to continue the war.  To show how the abolitionist opinion was still not the totally accepted view of that time I can point out that Brazil was the last western hemisphere country to abolish slavery only after 1870.  Slavery was still justified in other places in the world for much longer.  Slavery wasn't officially abolished in some countries of the Middle East until the 1950's or 60's or later.  Even so, some forms of slavery still exists today.
