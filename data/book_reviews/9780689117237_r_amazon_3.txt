At the age of 51 and out of the thousands of books I’ve read  in my lifetime, Spangle remains my favorite book of all-time. From the time we meet Florian’s Flourishing Florilegium of Wonder’s pitiful mud show in the sticks of my native N.C., we can’t figure out how they will survive the journey to Lynchburg, much less the next 725 pages, and do it honestly. But they do and manage to make their way overseas, where they will perform for “the crowned heads of Europe”. But the goal, the ultimate destination is Paris and it’s to that end we aspire. Spangle is an 800 page ride that isn’t nearly long enough. The history-and period/circus vernacular-proves authentic and virtually flawless-as Jennings works his way through a very volatile America in the months  following the end of the Civil War to an even more volatile Europe. He moves his Florilegium with a masters skill, in and out of of interactions with historical figures and  events without compromising their-or his novel’s-integrity. Rife with the sex, violence and perversions only Jennings himself could ferret out (or invent-the Fondler from Journeyer comes to mind) Spangle is honest in the balance of gossamer and glitter to the fetid underbelly of the a business attractive to transients and those on the run.
