The tenth Quiller novel has the trademark first person narrative polished up to great quality. The thought processes undertaken in tight situations are convincing and lucid. Just for once, there is a sex scene which is integral to the plot, rather than redundant, although it occurs in an unprecedented and possibly distasteful, but credible, scenario. The opposition is possibly the most fearsome so far in the Quiller canon. All this is to the good. So why just three stars? (i) the plot is another "Quiller saves the world" story and we don't need such drama to keep Quiller thrilling, (ii) the crux of the plot turns on something of a cliche - I'll avoid plot spoiling, (iii) there's a strong role for a metaphysical force, Ki, in which I am not able to believe and (iv) the climax is far too melodramatic.Read moreRead less
