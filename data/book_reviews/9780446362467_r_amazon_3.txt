The tale of Doctor Marvin Brewster continues! Will the genius Doc realize that producing aluminum, also known as nickellarium, the most precious metal in the twenty-seven kingdoms, will raise a few eyebrows, not to mention interest rates? Will the lovely Pamela figure out how Doc's machine works in order to build a duplicate and go after her fiancée? Will the inhabitants of Brigand's Roost get their new wonder products to market? Will Bonnie King Billy realize that revolution is brewing? And will the evil Warrick the White continue to give the narrator grief?Maybe. Except that last one, of course, which is a definite yes!Now that everyone was introduced in the last book, Hawke starts actually moving the plot forward. It's a welcome development, and in fact things progress in positive leaps and bounds. The Inadequate Adept is a very fast-paced novel. Combined with its short length -- barely more than two hundred pages -- it makes the book go by very quickly indeed.The humor value of the book, though, is significantly less than that of The Reluctant Sorcerer. With the plot moving along, the author seemed to find less room for comedy. What remains is a bit forced. It never quite flowed as naturally as it ever did in the last book.Most of the comedy, in fact, is found in how the narrator is often breaking the fourth wall, often to complain about how his control over the villain of the piece seems to be strained badly. Warrick the White can sense the narration, and doesn't like it at all; it's immensely amusing when he argues with the narrator.And it's fun to see Doc building the beginnings of an industrial revolution in a sleepy medieval village. The reactions of the natives to his efforts are well worth reading.Still, the forced humor really makes this a little more of a strain to read than The Reluctant Sorcerer. Clichés abound throughout the book, but unlike last time they don't always work (though sometimes they do). There's also several scenes where I felt things went too fast; I wanted some detail, and all I got was a summary, or even a declaration that this or that happened.All in all, this was still a fun read.
