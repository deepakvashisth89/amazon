I've had the pleasure of having been born during one of the greatest eras of Rock music from Bill Haley all the way up to now. I've read Steven Tyler's autobio (quite good), Deep Purple (very good), Motley Crue's "Burn" (disgusting book about a not that great band), Nikki Sixx (see "Burn" about a bass player junkie who still can't play bass), the Allman Bros. (well done), Sebastian Bach (one of the best voices in Rock and quite an interesting guy) and now, the self-proclaimed "Diamond" Dave. I had previously read the other two books about Van Halen as a group, both of which were mildly interesting, but this was a book about one of the greatest front men in Rock history as told by the "Man" himself. I had seen a number of interviews with Dave on YouTube and was struck by how different he was from most of the rest of his ilk. Dave is one of the most intelligent and charismatic people in any genre of music. He's delightfully quotable, very knowledgeable about what makes a song commercially viable and, quite honestly,  was the real reason Van Halen was as huge as they were. Does he have as good a voice as Sammy Hagar? No! As a front man, other than possibly Steven Tyler, there is no better front man in Rock. This book won't really shed a lot of light on the inner workings or secrets of the band as it's a book strictly about Dave and, as such, couldn't be more interesting. It's the view of the world through his eyes and the world couldn't look better or be more interesting. He's the eternal optimist and you can't help but like the guy. He's one of very few people that I would love to sit down and spend a day with because his zest for life is infectious. This is not another book about a burnt out, not very bright, self-proclaimed Rock God,  ghost written to hide the fact that said Rock God probably couldn't stream enough words together to make a coherent sentence. The only short-coming of the entire book is that it isn't three times as long because you'll find yourself wanting to hear more stories and "Daveisms" from one of the most articulate and entertaining voices in show biz. He is truly a visionary in, not just music, but video production as evidenced by both Van Halen's videos and his subsequent work with his own solo projects (DLR band, etc.).
