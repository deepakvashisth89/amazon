“Death Claims” by Joseph Hansen is an early 1970’s murder mystery, starring Hansen’s main character, Dave Brandstetter, a gay insurance investigator who refuses to believe that a death claim is valid, insisting that it was murder. Dave and his boyfriend Doug are having relationship problems, as a side story.What’s really good about this story is the early 1970’s setting in Southern California, devoid of the celluloid flash and unsavory reputation it has in the early 2000’s. Instead the people and their lives are boring, ordinary and often pitiful. The SoCal picture that emerges makes a reader feel happy never to have lived there, then, or perhaps ever.The story is quite good, filled with the expected twists and turns.  As it turns out for me, I didn’t care enough about most of the characters to work very hard at solving the murder. But I did like the story. It picked up steam toward the end. There were a few too many characters, none likeable, except Dave Brandstetter himself and the young man in his early 20’s, who is a suspect and tries to confess.The detailed descriptions of furnishings, architecture, house and room ambience, geography and sounds and smells of the time are excellent.  For the year it was published (1973), it was a book well ahead of its time, given the gay themes. Even the dialogue is relatively believable.I give it a strong 3.49 for good writing and because the story stayed mostly on topic, rounded down to a 3.Read moreRead less
