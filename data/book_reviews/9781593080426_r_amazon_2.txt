O. Henry may seem like a strange author to choose to read in the twenty first century. Most of his stories follow the same formula—a pleasant recollection of pre-WWI Americana ending in an ironic twist. These stories appeared in the publications of his day, where the pleasure gained in reading one each month meant nobody much minded their formulaic nature.And yet, there is a deeper irony in O. Henry. The twists, though formulaic, all reveal something about the world that was previously undiscovered. There is a sense throughout his stories that the world is far more magical than we can know. The deeper irony is that O. Henry is using a formula to say that life is not formulaic.It’s not surprising that such a theme resonated in the early twentieth century. This was an era that proclaimed that all the laws of physics would soon be known; the enchanted world was in its death throes. When people read fiction they seem to have wanted a rehabilitation of the old Shakespearean saw, “There are more things in heaven and earth, Horacio, than are dreamt of in your philosophy.”And so O. Henry endures. Was he a great sketcher of character?  No. Did he tackle man’s most existential questions? Again, no. But he did write with a verve about modern life that resonates down the generations. Somewhat light but mostly well done short stories. Highly recommended.Read moreRead less
