My copy was printed in 1990 and has a different cover than the one pictured above.  This is a reread and sometimes rereads don't hold up as well.  I remember finding this very interesting and enjoyable read.  I was, at the time, a huge fan of Fred Saberhagen and his Berserker series and knew of Roger Zelazny.  In fact I have still only red a few books by the latter author. In the end I have to say, while the setting and the ideas are interesting there are a few flaws.  The book is very much like some of Poe's stories - dark, rich in images and themes, but short in plot.  We also barely see Poe, mostly seeing the events from his twin from another world, Perry.  Our world?  Was Poe from a different world?  How many worlds were there?Many of the characters are from stories from Edgar Allen Poe.  Of course the book suggests that his insanity and his stores were created by this twisted, broken series of events that happened in his original world which he saw, or sensed, through the eyes of others.  Basic plot, a scientist can turn lead into gold but doing so may also do more than make somebody rich.  One of the side effects may be the death of Poe.  There seems to be other things at risk but they are never made very clear and we end up feeling a tad empty.As a fan of Poe, and Lovecraft who was heavily influenced by him, I found myself disappointed.  When I first enjoyed the story I was not really a fan of Poe and only knew of his well known stories.  Now I am a fan of both, and their awesome and powerful works, the book seemed to fall short.  Maybe it is impossible to really make a work honoring Poe while at the same time trying to copy his style?  I have never thought of Fred Saberhagen as a fantasy author and have only been impressed by a few of Roger Zelazny's works.  In the end it just felt a tad too weak and empty.
