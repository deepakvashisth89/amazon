3.5 rounded up.I do enjoy murder mysteries with wit, verve and humor.  The Anty Boisjoly and Lady Hardcastle novels are great examples.This certainly has an intriguing premise.  A gay vampire from the American south relocates to a tiny English village with  a most improbable name (even by English village in cozy novels standards).  This is where I began to raise my eyebrows.  The names of some of the villagers were absurd.  The second eyebrow raise came with the narrator and protagonist - Simon.  He is an almost stereotypical gay southern character nearly crossing into camp.  There is a surprisingly large number of very attractive gay men in this tiny village occupied otherwise by vicars, spinsters, widows and aging colonels.  All of them according to Simon are dishy.  I had no idea of when the story was taking place so checked when the novel was published - answer 2002.  I guess that is over 20 years ago now but the notions still seemed overly outdated.  Finally the novel abruptly ends.  The murderer is uncovered without any real explanation - it just happens and then it’s over.  And while I’m not the grammar police in kindle - my goodness it was distracting to read with all of the missing paragraph breaks, periods and quote marks.And yet…. It was fun with an interesting cast of characters.  Light and not too taxing and written just well enough and propulsive enough to keep me turning pages.  I’ll try the next one.I’m also thrilled to read a cozy that isn’t a plucky heroine with the approved small town job and small town best friend and small town handsome and single policeman love interest.  Someone kill that template and I won’t ask who the murderer is!Read moreRead less
