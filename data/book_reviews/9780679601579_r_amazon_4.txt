At age 16 I struggled through the original book in French. I had arrived in the U.S.A. from Holland ten years earlier.  There was much I didn't understand, of course, but the power of those images, flowers, earth, animals, people, remained strong in me throughout the years. It has a sensual throbbing in the most ordinary of scenes and descriptions and I had immediately felt connected to Colette's relationship with her mother. I, too, had an eccentric but grounded mother, and although my youth was not spent in rural houses or gardens, it touched me deeply; the world of adult secrets trailing around and between words not completely veiled, nor completely clear, enchanted and kept me bound. No one needs to comment on Colette's amazing brilliance. Now at age 80, living for the last 18 years in elemental nature, not intimate nature as in the days of Sido, but in wilderness, I am again pulled into the rich webbing of Colette's writing.  I have re-read My Mother's House" and 'Sido' in the English translation. That seductive pulse of scents and deep aromas, literal and metaphoric, has again put its spell on me.
