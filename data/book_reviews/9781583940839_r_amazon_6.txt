I was skeptical when I first saw the title of the book. I thought to myself, "well, here we go, this guy is interested in selling his book so he'll plagiarize Cayce. Nothing new about prophets being plagiarized", my ego interjected. "After all, Saul of Tarsus became Paul the Apostle and plagiarized the teacher, Jesus". But because I feature myself to be open-minded and always up for a challenging read, I ordered the book. I must admit that the author, Wynn Free, did a comprehensive report on what was a considerably difficult assignment. His writing/reporting of David Wilcock's metamorphosis from his beginnings to Virginia Beach was free of bias . . . at least until the last few chapters. It was clear that through his exhaustive research (just the reading was sometimes exhausting) Free came to believe that Wilcock is indeed Cayce reincarnate. The big surprise is that the cynic in me wanted more and I found it in Wilcock's work and internet publications. Then I found myself mysteriously directed to other internet publications quoted in the book. It's been months and I'm not yet burnt out on the subjects raised, either metaphysical or scientific. Yep, Wilcock was not a believer himself at first and has written tomes on the scientific research that substantiates the messages he receives in meditation and dreams. Real or not, I'm grateful I tripped over this book and found myself on a journey into my own being. Related books that might be of interest are "Oneness" by Rasha, A Wanderer's Handbook, by Carla Rueckert (available for a hefty price or free in PDF on the internet).
