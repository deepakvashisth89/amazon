C.W. Sughrue a Montana investigator, who focuses mainly on missing persons and serving papers, is hired to find a woman's ex-husband, an author, who often disappears on binges. While working on the case, he reluctantly agrees to look for the daughter of another client.  The daughter, however, has been missing for 10 years.  The book is filled with eccentric characters, who live eccentric lives, and the underbelly of society is examined and revealed in all of its ugliness.  While there is much to be gleaned from the book and its observations of people, I had a difficult time getting through it.  I like noir, but to be honest, I'm tired of alcoholic detectives, drunken stupors, and far out angst.  I also have a hard time with the suspension of believe it takes for me to be convinced that a character who can barely go an hour without drinking a bottle of vodka or bourbon, can manage to function well enough to resolve any investigation.  Certainly the answers Sughrue is looking for are realistically messy and come a bit late in most instances and the conclusion, while it should be expected, is shocking, but while reading this book I felt as if I were wading through sludge.
