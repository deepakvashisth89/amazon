I first read this book as a teenager/young adult.  Recently I was doing some cleaning and came across my old Star Trek book stash.  Thinking it would be fun to re-read these books, I just had to revisit this one.I have to say, as a teen I really loved this book, but with more sober eyes and such, I have to wonder what the heck I was thinking.  One of the previous reviews got it exactly right – this is a Mary Sue story, plain and simple.  When reading this, it’s a wonder that the Enterprise crew ever got anything done without the expert and able assistance of Col. Elizabeth Schaeffer, a Special Security agent who, well, can do anything.  She dazzles everyone on board, from Kirk (who is overwritten as a horny teenager), Spock (who is prone to an emotional breakdown in Schaeffer’s very presence), and McCoy (who just seems befuddled).  Wish fulfillment on the part of the author?  Most likely.Which is sad, because the plot itself had serious potential.  The idea is that on a mission, several people were infected with spores that gave them astral projection powers while in a coma.  McCoy managed to remove the spores but somehow the ability remained, with subconscious desires being acted out.  It’s a combination of astral projection and sleepwalking.  This figures into the main plot as this sort of thing wreaks havoc with ambassadors on board the Enterprise for a diplomatic mission…though the ambassadors seem written to be aggressively stereotypical.  So what’s left but for Our Hero, Elizabeth Schaeffer, to save the day.Now a case can be made that the spores and such negatively impacted the main characters…but it would have been nice to show that rather than just assume it.  From the writing there’s nothing that shows such a progression.Anyway, as a Star Trek book it fails miserably.  It’s basically fan fiction.
