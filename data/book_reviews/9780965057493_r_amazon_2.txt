I'm trying to get my Dickens reading to a more complete place, so I'm going through some of his that don't have the famous titles."The Old Curiosity Shop" is a good book, but it has the distinct marks of a serial novel (which of course it was - one of his many), and feels as if it is tied up rather mechanically at the end with a laundry list of who's where and what's happened to them.  It also is a good example of a complaint you hear about Dickens, which is that he uses perfectly angelic characters to demonstrate the evils of early 19th Century life as they come up against perfectly evil characters, the motivations of either being unclear or very much stretched at the least... Hey, times were tough then, but there were reasons for it as society learned to cope with industrialization and the newly established capital markets (easy to see from our perspective).That said, I enjoy reading his prose.  Those ridiculously long and cumbersome sentences are really something special.  He can introduce a setting or character in a wonderful descriptive way that few others come near - a real master of letters, but this is not his most masterful work.
