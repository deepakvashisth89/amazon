Portent is set in an alternative 1990’s where natural disasters are growing so much that the Met office has become a branch of the military. A meteorologist called Rivers is injured in a airplane crash that was proceeded by a ball of light. He returns to work injured but is told to take time off where he meets a strange family where two children with bright blue eyes are having visions of the forthcoming major changes to the earth. They are part of a large group of children taught by the ‘Dream Man’ that they have rekindled an age-old link to mother earth and they will lead the way into a new world where humankind will work with the earth to fix the damage we have done. As always there is a malevolent force in the massive figure of Momma Petie who thinks that mother earth, rather than saving mankind, is trying to rid the earth of its parasites and she is determined to beat the children and make sure mankind is wiped from the face of the world. Will rivers and the family survive, or will Momma Petie help destroy mankind?James Herbert manages to pull you into, what is on the surface a pretty lame story, so much that my reading got faster and faster as I came towards the end. I could not put the book down for the last 100 pages as things start to come to a head and the children come face to face with Momma Petie. Rivers must fight the immensely strong Momma and her protectors before she manages to kill the gifted children so that her version of the future is played out.Although the book is well written by James Herbert, as always, the story in this book is just too lame. The world has been created just for humans to thrive and mother earth is trying its best to fix our damage whilst ensuring humans continue to live on and also change their ravaging of the earth for more sustainable ways! Let’s face it, humans will never change what we are doing until the earth is no longer fit to live on and something evolves to replace us.I have now read all of James Herbert’s books and it is a pity he is not around to write classics like ‘The Rats’, ‘Fluke’ and ‘The Spear’.Read moreRead less
