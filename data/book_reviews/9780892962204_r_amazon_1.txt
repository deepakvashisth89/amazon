I once wrote in a review that I thought that Loren D Estleman's Amos Walker was the best American series detective, but as I end my Lyons reviews, I think Jacob Asch may have equaled him. However Arthur Lyons' final Asch novel was a bit of a letdown, especially after its brilliant predecessor Other People's Money. This isn't to say it didn't have its strengths. Jake's sardonic narration was still intact and Lyons portrayed the Los Angeles of his time as vividly as Raymond Chandler or Ross Macdonald did theirs, maybe even more so. I think its major weakness was that the solution to the mystery was rather obvious to the reader. False Pretenses still had more twists than bestselling writers Robert B. Parker and Robert Crais were capable of, so I'll give it four stars.
