I am a scholar and teacher of literature and mythology at the university level. The premise behind this book is interesting, but Leeming has an overwhelming and distasteful eco-feminist agenda that gets in the way of the topic itself (and I am a woman). Feminism is important and should be discussed, but the author uses the text in order to go off on diatribes that are unnecessary and could be seen as sexist and hateful in their own right. A lot of this felt as though the book is a collection of seminar/conference papers stitched loosely together and centered more on the feminist agenda than the topic at hand. The Hero section is generally regurgitated Campbell. That is acknowledged, but there is nothing original in this book. The "Conference of Myth-Makers" is interesting as a way to reach undergraduate readers, but it is over-used. There are a lot of segments that could be elaborated upon more or required more historical and social context to support a true understanding of the assertions that were being made. I found myself a number of times going from "comment" mode into "edit" mode. Although the author has a number of published books and is a respected scholar, this particular book undermined that ethos. Perhaps this is another product of "publish or perish" that suffers from the need to put something together to save a job and pad a CV than to produce something worthwhile. Perhaps if this was presented as an eco-feminist text on myth (though there are more of those than is needed), expectations would be different and it would make more sense. As a text on mythology, however, it is disappointing and a little alarming that this might be students' first introduction to myth. They will come away with more misconceptions and unsubstantiated conclusions than before reading this.
