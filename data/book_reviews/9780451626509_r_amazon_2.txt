I have almost worn out my copy of this book -- in fact, the cover fell off as I put it next to my computer to write this review.In my opinion, this is the best treatment I've ever read on the subject of individualism. He published the book in 1957 when Communism in general and the Soviet Union seemed to be trampling all the rights of individual people. Jung does not advocate an individualism stamps on other people, but he shows how a community is only as healthy as its individuals, and that there are many things that can be cured only as these individuals acquire more self knowledge.He also gives a compelling argument for the importance of religion in the life of the individual -- in fact, Chapter 2 and 3 are devoted to this subject, I appreciate that he does not come from the standpoint of a "fundamentalist" in any particular religion.His main focus is on the individual responsibility for self knowledge, and then goes into a valuable explanation in chapters 6 and 7 as to what self knowledge actually is.It's fairly easy to read and understand, and time spent with this book is well worth it.Read moreRead less
