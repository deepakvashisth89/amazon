I am preparing to teach a French class for the first time, and a friend recommended this book.  Frankly, I am not very satisfied with it.  I don't like the exercises.  I prefer what I call substitution drills:  elle parle français (nous):  nous parlons français (russe):  nous parlons russe.  I don't think the exercises in this book give students sufficient practice with the language.  Also, the explanations are wordy.  I just obtained a certificate in Teaching English as a Second Language (TESL), and the "in" teaching method, community language approach, prefers demonstrating grammar rather than lengthy explanations.  I think it sticks better that way.  I redid the section on countries and how to say I am in or going to [specific country].  I put the masculine countries on one side and the feminine on the other and below wrote:  je suis au Canada, je vais au Japon.  Je suis en Angleterre, je vais en Espagne.  Less fuss and clearer.
