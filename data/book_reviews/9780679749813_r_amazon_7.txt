Having read "Balkan Ghosts" after "Imperial Grunts," the experience of reading this book was disconcerting.  "Imperial Grunts" tells of the present and is much faster-moving with only enough historical background beginning each chapter, to bring the reader up to pace with events in Columbia, the Philippines, Yemen, etc.In "Balkan Ghosts," the travelogue bogs down in a thick description, to use Clifford Gertz's phrase, of the Balkan landscape.  The impression left is that the Balkan economic, political, and historical landscapes are so immoveable -- because they are so bound to the past (past injustices, past failures and past wars) -- that no modern force of history, economy or war can move this nation forward.There is an apocryphal story that Clinton decided not to move a ground force into the Balkans after he read this book.  The story may be true, but it is certainly possible.  That is the effect of this book -- namely pessimism about change.  It is not the way histories are written today.Still, this book will conjure up memories of the Nineties, when we all helplessly watched the slaughter in Serbia, Croatia, Macedonia, Bosnia, etc.  Be prepared to revisit your own ghosts while reading this book, and ask yourself what you would have done different to help these people.Read moreRead less
