Not as good as the original. In the original the mystery of it all was the intrigue. As soon as you start unraveling that... I suppose it was considered essential... you destroy the mystery. The way the series went... to be honest... I was disappointed. All this character development... not Clarke's style at all. And we really wanted more Clarke. He was the master at making a story totally absorbing _without_ making us feel "emotionally attached" to the characters. That... actually is NOT a pre-requisite... maybe that fly's in the face of "current wisdom". But give us a great story, that we can't wait to find out what happens next, and _don't_ make us even really CARE about the characters? Spend virtually no time on that at all, and we _still_ care? THAT is an ART and what these books sadly lack. Unbelievably long passages of character development... occasionally, even, I'm sorry, boring ones. We don't necessarily want to know the characters, so much... just enough to get a feel for them... to think, at least, we "know" them... well enough... to let the actual STORY progress. Clarke was a master at this. Sadly, the follow ups to Rama were not written by Clarke. He was... an "executive producer". He had input, but...And, again... the first Rama book was all about the MYSTERY. I guess that naturally sets up the story of solving the mystery... but it sure does make for some very different books. And not "natural" follow-ups, but more... "intellectual" ones. Let's take an educated guess, and see where it leads us, as to the how and why.When it very fairly should have been, "Let's let it be a mystery forever". Clarke provided, in the first book, SUCH a rich tapestry that almost ANY story could be told there, without even delving into the "hows" or "whys".
