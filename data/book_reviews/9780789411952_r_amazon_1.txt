This large book is a compilation of all ten volumes in the DK "Look Inside Cross-Sections" series (despite the cover not mentioning it, it does include the "Bulldozers" book). I shy away from calling this work a "children's book", because while certainly kid-friendly (I adored this series as a kid and could never get enough), now that I return to it after having gotten my doctoral degree, I still enjoy it! Thus it is more aptly termed a "general audience" book. Advanced technical drawings these are not, but as an intelligent introduction to what's inside a swath of vehicles and as a work of hand-illustrated art in its own right, this volume is splendid.In the 1990s there was a trend of illustrated cross-section and cutaway books, the most famous being Stephen Biesty's landmark "Stephen Biesty's Incredible Cross-Sections", also by DK, with which comparisons cannot help but be made. Yet the DK series of cross-sections presented in this volume is not an "also-ran" to Biesty's body of work, but rather stands strongly on its own. This work is made up solely of vehicles, vessels, and spacecraft (no buildings). There are six individual vehicles that overlap with Biesty's work, but these are notable items (e.g. the Boeing 747) that it would not be comprehensive without; the two works complement rather than compete. This book covers both iconic and obscure vehicles and vessels: these range from the Ford Model-T to the Jeep, Cadillac, and Mini; locomotives from the Rocket to the Mallard and a diesel-electric; ships from a trireme to a tea clipper to the HMS Dreadnought and the USS Lexington; planes include the Fokker triplane, the Handley-Page biplane airliner, the Boeing 314 Clipper seaplane, the Concorde, and over a dozen fighters. Not to mention earthmovers, tanks, rescue vehicles, and spacecraft. Each section has about 10-12 vehicles, adding up to over 100 different vehicles!The only real flaw I can find is in the organization. The book is purely a collection of the ten-volume series, complete with individual glossaries as found in each. While this is fine, and overall the progression is from land to sea to air to space, in the middle is the section "Record Breakers", which focuses on all manner of exceptionally fast craft. If you are reading it from cover to cover, the speed-record-breaking boats and planes come before the normal ones! As an editor, I would have placed it at the very end instead. There may be complaints as to what was included and what wasn't, but the variety is impressive on the whole. Finally, some of the vehicles featured have gone out of service since the book was published, so some places that use the present tense would now use the past.In summary, finding a used copy of this volume is a economical way to acquire a wonderful series that explores the world of vehicles.Read moreRead less
