There are several highly important books for philosophers and each thinker has to study them. Undoubtedly, Immanuel Kant's Critique of Pure Reason is on of them.This Kant's Opus Magnum--in German as Kritik der reinen Vernunft--deals with some important areas of philosophy, particularly ontology, metaphysics, epistemology, God (three proofs of God's existence), partially physics, axiology, hermeneutics, history of philosophy, etc. The book was written in German and this edition was carefully translated and edited by Paul Guyer and Allen W. Wood.Mentioned edition contains several texts: this edition mainly follows the text of the 2nd edition of Critique of Pure Reason (1787, B), but--if there is difference between the 1st and the 2nd edition--this book either published all two versions as a main text, or mention divergences in 1st edition (1781, A) in footnotes. But book contains pagination of both editions, the 1st one (published in 1781; now mark as "A" edition) and the 2nd one (published in 1787, mark as "B" edition, it was "improved here and there", as we read on Title page). This edition contains also 'the third edition' of Critique, I mean Kant's Notes from his own copy of the first edition of Critique (published by B. Erdmann in 1881). What is more, this edition contains useful Footnotes written by Editors which kindly explain some problematical aspects and point at other works, either written by Kant, or written by other philosophers.To be honest with you, I usually study Czech edition/translation (transl.
