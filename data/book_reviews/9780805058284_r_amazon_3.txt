Good, objective analysis of the annulment process and how to manage it. I am on the receiving end of an annulment I oppose for the same reasons as Ms Kennedy: my two sons. But beyond that, the book helps to sort out the many conflicting and contradictory forces at work. For example, the Catholic Church is an ardent supporter of life-long marriage, yet feels empowered to tell a couple, or one of the spouses and their children, when a marriage - even one of 20 or 30 years - should never have happened and therefore be annulled. How insulting to the spouse who sacrificed and worked on the marriage, but more so to the children who wonder gee, if they weren't supposed to be married, then what am I doing here?And what does the process say about the millions of marriages the church could find nullable but the couple stays together? Does this mean in God's eyes those marriages are invalid but the church just hasn't been asked to weigh in yet? Preposterous. All this just so one of the divorced spouses can marry again in the Catholic Church? Fortunately, the Vatican is now trending more and more against annulments. The vast majority of annulments are granted in the United States where certainly there's more at stake monetarily for the Church to re-gain its divorced members who would otherwise be "ineligible" to be Catholic.
