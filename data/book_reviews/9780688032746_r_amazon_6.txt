This book was recommended to me in the early 80's and it had been on my to do list ever since. Now that I've read it, I really wish I had read it way back then. Written in the late 70's, extremely little of the content is now obsolete. Mander's observations about television are still as relevant today as they were all those decades ago.Mander successfully argued that television is subtly destructive to human beings and is not capable of reform any more than a gun could be modified to grow vegetables instead of shooting bullets. A gun does what a gun does, and television does what television does. At issue is the fact that most people only give lip service to the possible disadvantages of television technology. Mander lays it out and reveals the true nature of the beast, and how it is suited to controlling and manipulating people rather than actually educating them.On page 352, Mander wrote: "Without our gaining control over technology, all notions of democracy are a farce." And on page 349: "Because of television we believe we know more, but we know less." Out of context, these statements are less powerful than in context. Mander's arguments should be read in context and in full in order to see what he's saying.As a creationist, I rate this book is a "must-read" book despite the fact that Mander wrote as if millions of years of evolution is true.
