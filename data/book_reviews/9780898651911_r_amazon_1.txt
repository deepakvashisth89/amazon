I tend to reserve my five-star ratings for books that really 'blow my mind' which this one did not. But Zimmer Bradley gets my fifth star for a couple of reasons. First, I am a big stickler for writing style and mechanics. Too many times I have paid money for a book which has turned out to be lamentably edited and could have been composed by an eighth-grader... This is unspeakably annoying to me. Luckily, Zimmer Bradley's work is written so well, I literally did not think once about the mechanics of the work. It's well written, well transposed into Kindle format, and well edited. There are zero errors in the work that came up on my radar. Second, perhaps the most interesting aspect of this work is the premise. It's hard sci-fi (not the hardcore stuff with details of how star-drive works, etc, but heavy on the space and traditional sci-fi elements), but it's essentially a coming-of-age adventure story. Bart leaves home on this grand adventure, and the reader forgets that at the start of the novel, he was just graduating school (so he is presumably around eighteen years old). More on the premise: interstellar travel and the concept of a peaceful humanoid alien race (two central elements of the work) are handled so adeptly, they seems feasible and natural, especially by the end of the novel. The book is lacking the 'twists and turns' that many of us love in our fiction, but it's exciting enough to keep you engaged pretty much to the end. I could write so much more about this book, but suffice it to say, at more than fifty years since publication, it's still a relevant, interesting, and engaging piece that has stood the test of time. Finally, it's free in this format (Kindle) so I say buy it! I may use this one as a bridge into the sci-fi genre at story-time with my seven-year-old when she's a bit older...
