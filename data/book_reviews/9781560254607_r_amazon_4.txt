This is an affectionate if somewhat sugary memoir of this author's friendship and collaborations with Jack Kerouac, and rightly underlines the fact that Kerouac was not a "beatnik" but a serious writer.  Its description of the making of the film "Pull My Daisy" is fascinating and maybe unique.  It makes it seem a great shame that the film isn't available now except as part of an expensive DVD collection of the photographer, Robert Frank's work.  I wish I could see it.  The book distorts Kerouac's personality somewhat in saying that he never criticized other writer's work and was otherwise a saintly paragon.  Reading Kerouac's books like Desolation Angels shows that Kerouac could be quite critical of other writers-- usually with good reason, but sometimes cruelly.  The book gets less interesting toward the end, which becomes a kind of extended elegy to Kerouac, Ginsberg, and Corso as they die off--also a bit sugary and self-congratulatory.
