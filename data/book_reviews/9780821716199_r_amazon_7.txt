Having reached the halfway point in the Fu-Manchu series, I continue to enjoy them quite a lot.  This story immediately following upon the event of the previous book—The Bride of Fu-Manchu—with the Fleurette kidnapped and the evil doctor on the run, chased by Smith, Petrie, and Sterling.  So far, Bride has been the pinnacle of excellence but this one is not bad at all.For some reason, however, Mr. Rohmer has decided to write this novel without a specific point of view, which feels like a mistake.  The first three novels were told through Petrie’s eyes.  Then two novels were told by Shan Greville.  Last time, Sterling took the lead.  Since Sterling appears here, it seems odd that we don’t continue in his point-of-view.  I also have to point out that Mr. Rohmer has a tendency to overuse certain words and he’s discovered a new one for this novel:  crocked, as in “unable to play or perform due to injury”.  There are many crocked people here.That said, the story here is excellent with plenty of excitement, twists and turns.  As always, the atmosphere is wonderful.  Dr. Fu-Manchu puts in a few intense appearances.  Everything a reader wants from an old pulp novel like this is here to enjoy.Read moreRead less
