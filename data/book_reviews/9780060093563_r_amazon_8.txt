Mary Stewart was a gem. She can write about preadolescent boys and shaggy dogs, making them seem real rather than saccharine. Stewart's heroine, Charity, is ahead of her time. She is a young WWII widow whose husband taught her to drive like a race car driver in a time when driving was still considered an athletic skill suited to men. The chase scenes are gripping.Sometimes I could guess what was around the next bend in the plot. Sometimes not. I think some of the plot devices seemed familiar because Stewart created them and they have been used ever afterward.The settings in Southern France were captivating, although the air must smell since the characters are all heavy smokers.The abruptness of the main characters falling in love seemed unbelievable. And the man seems to exemplify what I think of as the Era of the Cave Man Hero. The man is typically a decade or so older than the woman and treats her as a girl. His method of announcing his feelings for the woman usually consists of grabbing her and kissing her so hard that her lips are in danger of bleeding.This is a terrific book if you squint while reading the romantic parts.Read moreRead less
