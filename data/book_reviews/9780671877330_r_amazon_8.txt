This book was issued in 1997 with a fourth edition issued in March 2006. Where have I been? This was my first exposure to David Drake. As other reviewers have stated, the story line has some gaps that needed to be filled. I did not connect the Category 4 Chief Administrator (John Smith) in the Prologue to the Category 4 (al-Ibrahimi) in the rest of the book until the very end. They were apparently the same person. The unit here (the Strikers) are designated as C41. The alpha designator indicates the size of the unit - C being about 100 personnel. Very few are actually mentioned by name. The unit is sent to a planet (Maxus 377) to fight the Kalendru with a population of over 500,000. The battle is fierce and C41 is extracted. End of discussion. The unit - C41 - is deemed unfit to be assimilated back into normal society and are redlined. Hence the name of the book. The Chief Administrator decides to use C41 to guard a colony of civilians being transported to a new planet (BZ 459) to be colonized. No one trusts anyone else as this evolves. On this new planet, everything is a hazard, even the vegetation which turns out to be electronic or smart. C41 finds a crashed Kalendru space ship which  is not supposed to be on the planet. The planet is populated by humanoids in addition to the smart vegetation. The soldiers and the civilians are attacked by 264 of the humanoids who are defeated by the few C41 personnel remaining alive. At the end of the book the Chief Administrator has knowledge not available to anyone else and instructs C41 personnel to look for a door/underground entrance to tunnels that house electronics that will turn the vegetation off. They do find the door, the vegetation is turned off and becomes normal trees and grass. The mission is successful and the story ends. While I agree that the story line had  gaps, to me it was written in such a manner that held my interest. The hazards that C41 had to face, while bizarre, seemed to be unique and well thought out.
