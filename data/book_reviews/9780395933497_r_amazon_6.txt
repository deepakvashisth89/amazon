Diana Preston is one of my favorite non-fiction writers.  Before the Fallout  - From Marie Curie to Hiroshima - was just fascinating.  I came across the audio Playaway version of A First Rate Tragedy, read by Philip Franks, at the library.  What a TERRIFIC read!  Franks is a fabulous narrator and he made this book come alive.  What makes Preston so good at writing books like this is the way she focuses on people as well as events.  She has a way of bringing to light what is unique and "hidden" about each individual, so that it becomes difficult to judge them - having experienced their human-ness.  I observed Scott's failures as a planner, but still admired him for his stellar qualities: the way he deeply cared for his family; struggled with the horrors of being and feeling solely responsible for the lives of his men in an unforgiving landscape; apologized to his men when he lost it; and continued to lead despite his own fears, doubts and sense of inadequacy.  This book was a perfect follow-up to our visit to the award-winning exhibition of Scott's Last Expedition at Canterbury Museum.  It is such a poignant and insightful look into the lives of men who valiantly battled nature to achieve their aims of gathering scientific information about the remote Antarctic region and reach the South Pole first for England.  I always used to think Antarctica was boring: it's white, cold, and dangerous, what's the big deal?  THIS was the book that changed that view for me.  Now, I want to read about Shackelton and Amundsen, and other polar explorers.  The tales about how human beings intersect this frigid, haunting land is what interests me. And this is what Diana Preston achieved so dexterously. READ IT!Read moreRead less
