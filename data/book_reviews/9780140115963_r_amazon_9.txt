OK! A great read and another series by a Canadian author that I am most definitely going to keep on reading. I stumbled upon this author in my mini-project to catch up on books that have won or been shortlisted for an Arthur Ellis award. For years prior to about 2010 it's difficult to find the shortlists so I have only looked at winners and Mr. Gough won for best first novel with this book in 1988 and again in 1990 with "Hot Shots" for best novel. First off the Kindle edition that I read is remarkably free of the usual editing and proofreading errors that quite often appear in eBooks. I surmise from this that this eBook must have been produced by scanning and digitizing a properly edited and proofread print book. I think most of the characters are fairly well developed and quite interesting whether likeable or not and Willows and Parker are very strong characters. The police unit really reminded me of Ed McBain's 87th Precinct in how they all seemed to work as a team even with their to be expected differences and I find it somewhat refreshing that the boss, Bradley, isn't portrayed as a bumbling idiot driven by politics or some personal thing as is the case in most police procedurals. There are a few things that have caused me to downrate this story slightly and I admit that some of these nit picky items might exist because I didn't quite get what was happening or maybe I read a bit too fast in some places. However the first little problem I have is I really can't understand how the title is derived from and is relevant to the story as the only time goldfish appear is in the investigation of the first murder when Atkinson breaks the aquarium and it would take quite a stretch for me to think of an aquarium as a Goldfish bowl. This leads to another small problem I have and that is the many completely irrelevant clues that are dropped in throughout the book and then never followed up on and completely forgotten and never mentioned again. For instance just what has the dead goldfish in the toilet bowl, that the detectives never saw and was never mentioned again, have to do with the story? I guess these extraneous clues are only red herrings (course it is Vancouver so something fishy should be expected) designed to try to keep the readers confused and mislead in the investigations. One has to dismiss the non political correctness, the smoking, drinking and attitude towards women as this book was written in 1985 and is set in the 1980s and it is quite remarkable that Parker is portrayed as strong as she is.I never saw the twist that lead to the ending coming and I thought the ending was fairly satisfactory even though I think the motive was a bit weak. I'm glad I found this author and I already have the next two in the series, although it was difficult to find book 2 because somebody pulled the old change the title's name game from "Death on a Number 8 Hook" to "Silent Knives", and I intend to read more of this series.Read moreRead less
