I find it odd that so many people jump on the "this will make the sabermetric computer geeks mad" bandwagon when it comes to evaluating this book. Give me a break, folks. This guy calculated every one of those numbers using a computer, through some arbitrary equation that he doesn't even attempt to prove. This is a showcase of bad mathematics, poor logic and essentially the WRONG way to approach baseball statistics. The irony in all this is that adjusted baseball statistics (like the ones that he's created) are all based on something that happened in reality.. so why throw the baby out with the bathwater? Why discard the reality in favor of some fantasy numbers just because it would be cool to see the Babe jack out 93 home runs or whatever?This book is the reason why baseball statistical research needs a peer-review system outside of SABR. As mentioned by other reviewers, there are things basically wrong with the equations that this moron comes up with, and the results show it. Baseball statistics have always been biased, and the correct way to get rid of this bias is NOT to adjust at bats and come up with absolutely arbitrary figures for the individial components of baseball play. What's even more laughable is his attempt at simulating post-season play for early seasons.. as has been demonstrated time and time again, there is NO way to accurately predict post-season performances: the sample size is far too small.
