I am big on balance, temperance (there's an old word!), prudence, composure, and the like. I have found various versions of this in different world views, including taoism (yin and yang), as well as various economics models and some natural phenomena. All these sources pass through a sort of "translation layer" as they meet my own sensibilities. But it is key to listen, which is a whole sort of approach, not merely with one's ears, as this book sagely indicates. This book does slather a lot of emotive (though gently so) / anthropomorphized content onto the universe for my usual taste, but the lessons are deep and very worthy.  Protestantism (in which I was raised) lacks an explicit monastic tradition, but being of that sort of bent naturally, I have looked beyond the sectarian boundaries of my youth. This book joins my favorite taoist texts as one I will revisit and draw from, as from a deep and wondrous well, multiple times, and get answers that enable me to live with calm and soundness of mind and decisions.Read moreRead less
