Kollontai, author of Love of the Worker Bees wrote her book during a time of the greatest upheaval ever seen in Russia.  The Revolution had turned Russia from a czarist regime to a communist state, and the ensuing changes  made the ousting of bourgeoise ideology difficult at best.  The economic  and personal change in thinking is highlighted by the characterization of  Vasilisa, her marriage to Vladimir used to illustrate the failure of  traditional marriage and the difficulty in turning ones political idealism  to reality within relationships.As well, the relationship between the  book's protagonists can be seen as as an illustration of  capitalism/communism with the latter triumphing over the capitalist  mentality and lifestyle.  Vasilisa can get a bit tedious with her refusal  to "see" Vladimir's infidelities no matter what the evidence she  is presented with, but again this might be seen as a referral to the  persuasive and deceptive appeal of capitalism, with its eventual  unmasking.An excellent text for women's studies or advanced english  literature where an emphasis on gender and women's writings from a global  and historical perspective is pursued.Read moreRead less
