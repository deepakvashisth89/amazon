I will start by saying that since it seems like Amazon funnels the reviews for various editions of Gaston Leroux's "The Phantom of the Opera" by different publishers under this listing, this is a review for the Chartwell Books edition of the book specifically. My copy is a 2023 printing of the book, so it is possible that my complaints will be addressed in future editions (which I certainly hope will be the case).Now for the review. I have never read the book in its original French version, and my only previous encounter with the story was through a highly abridged version I read in middle school. I therefore cannot comment on the translation quality or the faithfulness to the original novel, but overall found that the reading flows well and is quite vivid considering that it is a translation.That being said, I cannot in good conscience recommend buying this particular edition due to the comically poor quality control in regards to the editing and typography. The first 2/3rds or so of the book are fine, but at one point things abruptly go off the rails. There is at least one sentence that makes no grammatical sense somewhere in the book, and there are numerous occurrences of footnote numbers that either do not correspond to the footnotes or indicate footnotes that simply do not exist. For instance, on page 252 there is a mark for footnote #4 — if there were to be a footnote in that portion of the chapter, it would be footnote #7 (there is actually no footnote on that page). There is an identical error on page 263, and page 266 has a footnote mark for note #8 when it should actually be note #2... *sigh*. I'd also like to point out that the font is quite large for a book of this one's paper size, and the bottom margin is very narrow, forcing the text to almost the bottom of the pages.Finally, I want to point out that there is an excellent introduction to the novel by Susan Balée that provides some neat background and insight to the novel, its creation & cultural impact; it really is greatly appreciated. Something curious about the introduction is that Balée mentions that Leroux mentions the character of Sherlock Holmes in the novel, which is a detail wholly absent from this edition — which makes me suspect that the translation of the book is abridged... I will need to buy another edition to verify this, however.In short, unless the typographical & editorial issues I mention do not sound like a problem to you, do not buy this edition. I was very pleasantly impressed by two other books from Chartwell that I purchased ("Wuthering Heights" by Brontë and the "complete" H. P. Lovecraft collection), which contained maybe one or two typos each and none of the issues that plague this book, and this was a major disappointment to me.
