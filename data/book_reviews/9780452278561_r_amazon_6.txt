The blurb on the cover, saying: "Were Armistead Maupin to write Tales of the City and set it in Boston, he would have penned The Men From the Boys", caught my eye and motivated me to buy this book at a secondhand shop recently. It took me a while to get through it; one of those books that you start, put down, pick up again, repeat, until finally something clicks and you feel compelled to finish the damn thing! But in the end, I finished it and liked it quite a bit.Okay, perhaps this isn't a gay classic, and it's certainly not in the same league as the "Tales of the City" novels, but I found this to be a very well written and thought-provoking novel. As far as comparing it to Maupin's books, it wasn't nearly as funny or charming, and the characters not as remotely endearing. In fact, I found it hard to generate much love for any of the characters in this novel, although by the end of the final chapter I thing I finally came around to getting a better feel for them and at least empathizing with their tangled relationships and mindsets.The highest praise that I can give this novel is that the story felt very real. Yes, the plot --- if you want to call it that --- was thin, but in many ways it simply reflected the real way that gay men lived their lives. While I may not have liked all of the characters or the way they sometimes treated one another, much of it was painfully close to the truth in regards to gay life, especially as it was in the 1990s when this book was written and published. Because of that timeline, one might complain that the novel is "dated," but I actually enjoyed going back to that time, in all its glory and horror.Another way to gauge whether or not I liked this book was the final question: would I read another novel by this author? And the answer is a definitive yes. Mann impressed me as a very talented writer and I'd like to read other books, novels or not, that he has written.Read moreRead less
