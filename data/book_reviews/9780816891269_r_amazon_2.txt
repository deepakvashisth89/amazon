Although this book is rather dated (published in 1984), the author did an excellent job describing all aircraft designated under the pre-1962 bomber sequence, from the Keystone B-1 of 1928 to the B-70 Valkyrie Mach 3 bomber, but also the B-1 Lancer swing-wing bomber, providing descriptions and 3-dimensional drawings. There are a number of inaccuracies in this book, however:1. The Douglas XB-31 was actually allocated to the Model 332 and not the Model 423, since the Model 423 was conceived long after the B-29 and B-32 won the R-40B bomber contest, and the Model 332 that lost out to the B-29 and B-32 along with the Lockheed XB-30 had about the same wingspan as the B-29 Superfortress.2. The illustration and description of the Martin XB-68 as having a delta wing and three jet engines is a product of conjectural thinking by the author because the XB-68 had two jet engines and stubby diamond-shaped wings.It is quite interesting how Jones listed the XB-33 2-engine and 4-engine designs as separate entries because the Super Marauder was originally an improved version of the B-26 Marauder before being redesigned with 4 engines and a bigger wingspan. Yet it is quite ironic that he included the Matador, Snark, Atlas, Navaho, Rascal, Crossbow, and Titan missiles in the book because these were cruise and ballistic missiles, not bomber aircraft, although he lists the Tomahawk and AGM-86 cruise missiles at the end of the book. Descriptions of paint schemes for the aircraft in the book are provided, and an illustration of the WS-125 nuclear-powered aircraft is included.
