I bought this cookbook when it was brand new in the 1980s, and in some ways it shows that it's an "old style" health food cookbook. Back then, it was an effort to convince people to use unrefined sugar and whole wheat flour. We all used carob rather than chocolate. Health food didn't mean "...and cut down on butter and fat, while you're at it." The sweetener is usually honey, and the flour is mostly whole wheat pastry flour (which you can easily find today but was just slightly exotic back then).There was a time that I cooked a lot of the recipes in this cookbook, which is definitely more "desserts" than "snacks." We have some old favorites, such as the sour cream coffee cake that's in my oven at the moment. You'd think that with the hundreds of cookbooks I've acquired in the last 30 years another coffee cake recipe would have displaced this one, but I like this cake because it isn't too sweet. Plus because of the honey it has good shelf life; the coffee cake keeps for a few days left out on the counter.The cookbook also falls open to the recipe for meatless mincemeat, which we use every Thanksgiving (raisins, apples, citrus, cider, spices). It has a comfortable and easy angel food cake using whole wheat pastry flour (the answer to do with "what do I do with 10 egg whites after I used the yolks in something else?"). And a simple apricot upside-down skillet cake... geez why has it been so long since I made this?The snacks aren't objectionable; they just never thrilled me. Oat crackers? Bite-size quiches? Sunny buckwheat cornbread? Shrug. Maybe you'll feel differently.This isn't a fancy cookbook. I don't cook from it as regularly as I once did. But there's no way it's going to get cleared out of the cookbook shelves even when it is time for another Serious Culling.
