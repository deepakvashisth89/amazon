I too have waited a long time to be able to read this book again (about 47yrs) and what a disappointment it turned out to be.In my youth I recall it as being an hysterically funny book which carried humourous fiction to a new level - yes, I too laughed out loud on the train while reading it. But now, in my dotage, I find it is a juvenile, under-graduate style humour. Where once the characters were depicted as extrodinarily free-spirited odd-balls, not bound by convention, practicalities or good manners, I now find them just plain 'silly'. It is humour of the absurd, perhaps hugely popular in it's day (1933) and fashionable in the 50's & 60's but styles have moved on and I find it very dated. Rather than being engaged by the characters, I had no sympathy for them and really didn't care what befell them. For some reason I kept making comparisons with 'Who Framed Roger Rabbit?' - discuss.Sure, I'm a lot older now. I have changed. I don't like things I once did but in fiction I still enjoy the comic writing of PG Wodehouse. And in case I come across as some 'old fogey' I also get my rocks off on John O'Farrell, Ben Elton and John McCabeYes, I may have changed but 'Rain In The Doorway' hasn't - it remains 'of its day' and (my) taste has moved on.Read moreRead less
