I was so taken by "Presumed Innocent" that when I saw this book I immediately purchased the Kindle version. However, the book was nothing like I thought it would be. Rather than deliver another courtroom drama Mr. Turow wrote a lengthy psychoanalysis of the protagonist, Sandy Stern. The book opens with Stern discovering his deceased wife, who has taken her life. I'm not giving anything away here because this happens basically on Page One. The family gathers. Much time is spent with development of the relationship between the father and his three children. As they are only beginning to come to terms with the loss of wife/mother,  the Feds show up at the door with a subpoena delivered to Mr. Stern's brother-in-law. Horrible timing indeed! Thus begins the lengthy and gradually unwinding plot line. There are several surprising plot twists. Who is the "good guy?" Who is the "bad guy?" The answers aren't really fully revealed until the book's conclusion. All in all, I'm glad I read this book, but again, it was not what I expected. I think it's impossible not to love Alexandro Stern, an extremely complex man whose character and conviction are brought to the surface through some amazing prose. I recommend the book, but don't expect it to be courtroom drama. It ain't!Read moreRead less
