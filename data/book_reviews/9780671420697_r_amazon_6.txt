I found this book in a common room at a campground, and was entranced with the cheesy 70s cover. So I swiped it and started reading it. OK, for starters, Naura Hayden wrote the book with a very 1970s oh-so-casual-and-chummy tone. This isn't necessarily a bad thing, but she seems to think words like "vitamins" and "proteins" are too pretentious, so she calls them "vites" and "protes" instead. It took me a few pages to even figure out what "protes" were.Then there's the matter of her "energy shake", which apparently not only gives you more energy, it also clears up acne, makes you look younger, causes weight loss, and does everything else but cure cancer and make your hair grow back. She has zero medical or scientific evidence to indicate why this shake is any good, just a lot of anecdotes, which are worthless as evidence. And then she includes this cheesy "personal guarantee" that it will work, including her signature right there in the middle of the book. what is she going to do, send me money in the mail if it doesn't work? it is so obvious this book came out before the age of litigation. her publisher's lawyers would have FAINTED at such a claim if she tried to do it now.but anyway, as a good experimentalist, I thgought, what the heck, I'll try this shake. it's basically 2 cups of milk, 4 tablespoons of powdered lecithin, 4 tablespoons of brewer's yeast, some vanilla, some artificial sweetener, and a bit of vegetable oil. there you go, you don't need to buy the book now. It tastes absolutely foul, despite her chirpy claims that it's "yummy" (which, by the way, basically proves to me that she MUST have been on some sort of speed).to make a long story short, although I tried it for a month, the only thing I gained from the energy shake was foul-tasting burps all morning. yum.I give it two stars only for the entertainment factor.Read moreRead less
