Just finished my second reading of it -- 40 years after the first in CanLit class at Mount Allison. The Afterword by the late Timothy Findlay speaks of the denigration of this novel from 'the Pulpits and Schoolboards' when it came out in the 1970s. Hard to imagine that now. Yet, Morag's struggles as a single woman -- dealing  with sex (with a few men), marriage, divorce, childbirth, parenting, and making her way in the world (small towns and big cities) seem as real as ever. How wonderfully stories of all types layer across each other -- the stories we tell our children, the stories we tell ourselves, the stories in Morag's novels. I would give it any young woman, heading out into the world. And, as a young, now older man, well...it’s a window into a woman’s soul.Read moreRead less
