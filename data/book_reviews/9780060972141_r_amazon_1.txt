I recently finished Richard Elliott Friedman’s Who Wrote the Bible?, and I am still processing just how much it has shifted my perspective. As someone who has been a consistent reader of the Bible for a long time, I often found myself curious about the origins of the text, but intimidated by the dense academic language often associated with biblical scholarship.Friedman has achieved something remarkable here. He presents a deeply scholarly, well-researched, and meticulous investigation into the authorship of the Torah, yet he manages to write in a way that feels incredibly approachable. He lays out the historical and political contexts of the different authors, the J, E, P, and D sources, with such clarity that I felt like I was piecing together a historical mystery alongside him.What I appreciated most about this book is that it didn't strip the Bible of its significance for me; rather, it gave me a whole new layer of appreciation for it. By understanding the human hands, the specific historical crises, and the distinct motivations behind these texts, the scriptures feel more alive and "real" than ever before.Whether you are a scholar or just a curious reader like me, this book is an essential read. It respects the intelligence of the reader while opening the door to a much richer, deeper understanding of the texts we thought we knew so well. I can’t recommend it enough.Read moreRead less
