This book takes a different path than most books on concealed carry/self-defense in that it relies heavily on true-life stories about people who have used their handguns in defensive situations...or wished they'd had one. It is a good book for those who aren't quite sure if "packing heat" is for them. (Of course, only inexperienced media types and Rambo-wannabes use that phrase on a regular basis.)The first three chapters can be a strain for those who often read books and magazines about guns and want to get right down to the nuts-and-bolts, but they aren't wasted space and should not be ignored. Chris Bird is a newspaper reporter and the experience shows clearly. The stories he relates _throughout_ the book are thought-provoking and when I tried to second-guess those involved, I didn't have much success.In addition to data on handgun selection, holsters, non-holster methods, and basic techniques, the book also includes lists of schools that provide training in handgun self-defense and a relatively extensive section on state laws concerning handgun carry and use (5th Edition, 2008).Currently #3 on my list of concealed-carry books (behindThe Gun Digest Book Of Concealed CarryandIn the Gravest Extreme: The Role of the Firearm in Personal Protection), it is an enjoyable book that I will revisit regularly.Read moreRead less
