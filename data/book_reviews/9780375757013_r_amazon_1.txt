Pip, a lad of humble beginnings, yet intelligent and introspective, has a chance encounter with a rough character that haunts him throughout his life.  He is ever the keen observer of human nature and describes a full compliment of unique characters who come into his life.Pip finds himself the recipient of funding from a Patron, who wishes him to pursue the life of an English gentleman, rather than his expected role of apprentice to a blacksmith.  He meets Miss Havisham, who he believes to be his secret Patron.  Pip finds himself developing Expectations that increase as his new lifestyle unfolds, and he reflects about the simpler life he might have lived.Secrets not shared, the odd behaviors of some of the characters, and the possible motivation behind behaviors are constantly a puzzle that Pip studies and tries to construct.  As in modern day life, human behavior, while entertaining and curious, is often misunderstood.Readers who enjoy English ways and  who enjoy deciphering the old English way of speaking will enjoy "Great Expectations."  Occasionally one feels as if one is wading through wordy phrases like the dark and misty marshes from the story setting.  Patient readers will be rewarded with plot resolution, though perhaps not what was expected.  Dickens wanted readers to see and feel how our expectations may mislead us, or hurt us.
