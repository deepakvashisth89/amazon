A timeless classic for sure — this is probably my second favorite Thomas Hardy novel, behind only Jude the Obscure and probably just because I read Jude first.Hardy can be tough to read because he generally views life as grim, unfair and tragic. But like Dickens, they were awfully difficult and unjust times he lived in, and even today one can’t necessarily reject his worldview outright. Regardless, there’s no question he was an amazing writer and storyteller. Anyone who tries to tell you otherwise is but a fool.I got the cloth bound Penguin Classics version of the novel and I’m glad I did it. The binding is quality and the cloth certainly fits the novel. There are extensive notes which I generally eschew but which appear could be quite helpful and interesting to those with greater patience than I.
