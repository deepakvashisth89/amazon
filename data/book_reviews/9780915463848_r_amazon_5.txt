Horror and humor go together like peanut butter and chocolate, but so do the western and crime genres go together.  "Blood Trail" is one of Brian Garfield's (Brian Francis Wynne Garfield: 1939-2018) pre-"Death Sentence" hardboiled western noir novels under his pen name of Alex Hawk.In this short novel, Melinda Reeve and the rancher Charlie Harding are planning on getting married, but on the wedding day Charlie gets stood up.  When Charlie goes to find out what has happened to Melinda, he finds out that she is being kidnapped by gunpoint by the savage outlaw Milo Redgate, and despite going with Milo on the condition that Charlie is left alone, the end result is Charlie getting shot.It takes a while, but when Charlie recovers, he’s a very bitter and angry man, a man who blames Melinda for everything, along with Milo Redgate and his crew.So, he takes to the dusty trails to track down Redgate and Melinda and make them pay, and pay dearly.Left behind is Bisnell Keeler, who was Charlie's partner in their new ranch, and is love with Melinda, but who had lost out to Charlie for Melinda’s love.  So, while Charlie lights out with the intention of revenge on Milo and Melinda for his pain, real and imagined, he leaves Bisnell to eventually follow with the purpose of saving Melinda.  This is because Bisnell knows what Charlie refuses to acknowledge, that Melinda only went with Milo because Milo was going to kill Charlie if she didn’t go with him.The story quickly separates into three different viewpoints, each one showcasing Milo, Melinda, and Charlie.  Bisnell is a character who keeps wandering into all three’s storylines, and who keeps the novel from getting too episodic, and who keeps the novels storylines stitched together.For instance, Bisnell wanders into Melinda’s storyline on a frequent, and non-judgmental basis.  Through his interaction with Melinda we learn that after eventually escaping Milo, Melinda is now making herself scarce and making a living anyway she can, so that Milo can’t track her or Charlie down, even though Charlie has now become a nationally known bounty hunter and lawman.  Her worries are ironic as Charlie is as actively searching for Redgate, as Redgate is searching for Melinda, and both Charlie and Milo want revenge for real and imagined wrongs.Through Bisnell's interactions with Charlie, we learn of Charlie's growing anger and bitterness towards Milo, and also especially towards Melinda.  Through Charlie's storyline we see his devolving into a fanatically single-minded man whose bitterness has caused him to be able to control his mistaken obsession with Melinda.This causes Charlie to pick off Redgate's gang one at a time, while becoming as mean a human as Redgate himself.  While the novel will eventually have the requisite Charlie vs. Milo showdown, it will all be for naught as Charlie is already an extremely damaged man, even if much of that damage is self-inflicted.  This leaves us with an ending heavy with irony."Blood Trail" is a novel that manages to capture, in western form, the noir and hardboiled themes of love, obsession, and hate, as we watch two men destroy themselves over a woman.  Even though Melinda wants no part of it, and will eventually find redemption with the one man who truly loves her, and accepts her for what she is.It's a wonder that after Garfield became famous that none of his early westerns have ever gotten reprinted.  Too bad, if I ever come across more of his novels under the Alex Hawk name, I'll certainly check them out, but they're fairly scarce.
