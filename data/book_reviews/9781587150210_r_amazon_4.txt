This was a quick and easy read that felt more like a sequence of fractured fairy tales than a novel.  There are some interesting bits: gender reversal, overcoming the bad guys by helping them, a kingdom consisting of nothing but twins acting in unison, and a fake wizard.  But the whole is no greater than the sum of its parts, and the only thing connecting it all together is the hero who we realize early on is never really in any danger and indeed only embarks on the adventure out of boredom.  Oh, and there's a sidekick who I think is supposed to be funny (but isn't) who never really does anything important.  The most interesting part was the Kingdom of Twi, where everything is double including the ruler.  Like the rest of the book, that plot arc was hastily strung together and not fully developed.  If it had just a little bit more punch and pop, it would be for stars.Maybe it's meant to be a children's book.  In that case, it's not so bad.  I suppose you could credit the author for being ahead of his time (?) with the fractured fairy tale concept.
