Interesting book by the late Ben Bradlee, mainly for what he left out. As a close friend of JFK and a top journalist of his day, Ben Bradlee knew a lot about JFK's private life (and hidden public life). He chose not to expose what is common knowledge about JFK today: multiple if not constant adulterous affairs, nefarious dealings with the mafia, and other information that had it been publicly known when Bradlee knew it (1963; the book was published 1973 or so) would have seriously damaged JFK's political career. Therein lies the rub. When does a journalist stop being a journalist to protect his powerful friends? Apparently, after the first martini. That is the danger we see from a fourth estate that is in cahoots with politicians rather than striving to  expose what needs to be exposed.
