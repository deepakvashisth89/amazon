While Karen Armstrong's other works are entertainingly exhaustive in their scope, this book seems to drag under the weight of repeated contextualization between the time of the events covered and modernity.  Time and again, Armstrong feels the need to tie what she is covering in every chapter with what was happening in the Middle East at the time of writing leading to what feels like a distraction from the topic at hand.Altogether a good read though.  Her ability to synthesize a number of different events into a linear storyline is impressive and her commitment to the study of spirituality, deity and history has always been a favorite topic of mine.  I just wonder how differently this book would have been had there been less time spent on modern events rather than what is presented.Read moreRead less
