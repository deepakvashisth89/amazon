The author appears to have spent a lifetime researching and studying the life of Alexander the Great, but sadly that did not make him a good writer.Information is brought to you in a very long-winded way, occasionally jumping from subject to subject "without a warning", as if the author wanted to say multiple things at once, or came back and added info later. I'm not even sure what the chapters are for, as they don't seem to provide too much structure to the narration. Its been a while since I've read an interesting story written in such a boring and convoluted way.Usually, once you start reading a book you find a rhythm to sync with the authors style, but for the life of me, I could never get on the same wave with the book and reading it became an annoying chore.Besides that, the book is sprinkled with some glaring mistakes (such as corn being used by Alexander's army in ~330BC, while corn being introduced to Europe only in 15th century),  author's biases (such as adding "correctly" to description of marriage of an uncle to his niece "the Greeks, correctly, saw neither danger nor distaste in a liaison with a niece", or saying that Persians "were mostly unable to read or write _at all_" even though they just didn't use cuneiform for recording history, but rather wrote business and royal transactions).Author tends to take a simple concept, and convert it into a complicated web of explanations, periodically highlighting the present day human and science superiority, for example, making statements such as "the role of the female in conception was unknown, as it remained until the nineteenth century," in response to his own statements of Alexander's mother claiming her son was a son of Zeus  - I think there is a difference between understanding the existence and relationship between an egg and a sperm, and understanding of intercourse leading to pregnancy, so fairy tails aside, even in 300BC people knew how children were made and author's comment was absolutely unnecessary.I've stopped making notes on all the oddities after a while, so above are just some highlights.Granted, I'm not a scholar of Alexander, or an academic, but I think even students would not find this book very appealing, once they start reading it. That being said, the author did say "I have written self-indulgently" in the introduction, and it shows, as its written not for others to enjoy reading, but for the author to enjoy writing.Read moreRead less
