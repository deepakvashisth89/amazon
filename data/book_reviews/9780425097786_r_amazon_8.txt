This is literally the only one of Heyer's normally excellent mysteries I had to force myself to finish -- and definitely disliked. Always before skilled at handling a large cast of characters, she filled Penhallow with half too many, and almost all related to each other. Worse, the overwhelmingly male cast was made up of mostly brothers who fought and raged at each other and their father, the patriarch who egged them on and raged right back at them and everyone else. I can't recall ever reading a book without finding a single character for whom I felt any liking, far less any sympathy. As for the murder that finally happened, and it's aftershocks, all I can say that ending a book with the stage set for more troubles, even if the reader DID know whodunit as regards the murder, was so unsatisfying as to feel like a cheat.  I'm very glad this was the last of Heyer's mysteries I read; if it had been the first, I might well have missed the others, with no notion of how wonderfully different I would find them.
