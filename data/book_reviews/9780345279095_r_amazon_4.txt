The attractive Isabel Moore, now thirty, has suddenly been cast adrift after laying to rest her bedridden, Catholic-intellectual, and demanding father, who she has taken care of for eleven years while leading a very isolated existence. Fortunately, two Catholic school friends, Eleanor and Liz, are able to help her absorb some of the shock of reentry into the world.The book, at its best, is a look at the difficulties of reengaging with the world, but more so, explores the immense guilt that can accompany new found happiness and pleasures presumably at the expense of those left behind. Unfortunately, the characters, including Isabel - their dialog, interactions, behavior, thinking, etc - are awkward and strange, not easy to identify with. Her dealings with two newly met men are the epitome of awkward - not really believable. Isabel's willingness to assist the hateful former housekeeper for her father is even more bizarre.The book is not without interest and merit, but it is a bumpy ride.Read moreRead less
