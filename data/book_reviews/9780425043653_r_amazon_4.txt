This is an unusual book.  I had never heard of it before yet apparently it is highly regarded and influential.  In some respects it is ahead of its time; in other ways (including the narrative style) it seems a relic of the pulp era.  The main problem I had with it is that the protagonist - I won't say hero - Gully Foyle - is not very likeable.  For most of the book, there's not much motivating him besides revenge, and at least for the first half there's nothing much remarkable about his methods or thought process.  He's just a brute force surviving on will for revenge.  Not so interesting.  In the second half he has undergone a transformation and becomes a more interesting character.Mixed in with this is a heavy dose of surreal and trippy stuff (and this was a full decade before the psychedelic era).  Lots of strange and unexplained stuff.  I was intrigued by the "Scientific People", but they're just one of many elements that's one part funny, one part strange.  But I'm left with random questions like why is the telepath on Mars a 70 year old baby?  Why does it matter that Dagenham is radioactive?  What's up with Olivia's infrared vision?  What the heck is the deal with the Skotspy group on Mars?  I never quite understood the motivations of Jis or Robin for that matter.  Speaking of Robin - I think a "telesend" would actually have quite a lot of value, and I disagree with the author's choice to characterize her like she belonged on the Island of Misfit Toys.  And then the novel wraps up with a really quick Captain Kirk-style ending which ultimately grounds it very squarely in the 1950s-60s pulp genre.This book posits a future where megacorporations are extremeley influential and powerful.  What's funny is that in the 1950s he imagines that among the megacorporations that will be superpowers 5 centuries hence are names like Montgomery Ward, Sears-Roebuck, and Kodak.  Who even remembers Montgomery Ward anymore, and Sears and Kodak are both on hard times.  But to his credit he also mentions Westinghouse which is a confusing history but he may be on the right track with that one.Overall - I can't say I recommend this.
