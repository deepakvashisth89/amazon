As a victim of the BBC archives purge, ‘The Macra Terror’ no longer exists, much like the Macra themselves; at least according to the story. This seems like a shame because, although this novelisation feels a bit rushed at times and the latter stages are a little clunky, it appears to indicate that this might have been one of the better serials of Troughton’s first year. Unfortunately, the novelisation is relatively rare and as a result quite expensive to obtain.It involves the Tardis dematerialising in what appears to be an idyllic utopia, or, according to Ben, ‘a holiday camp’ (which might well be how it appeared in the televised version). Although Ben and Polly seem quite taken with it there is something odd going on and the Doctor is immediately suspicious.The plot could have been a bit more of a mystery, but this is lost firstly because of the title and because the images on the Tardis scanner at the outset give too much away. There might have been more intrigue without it, possibly resulting in greater tension.One of the novelisation’s strengths is in its depiction of the Second Doctor. It is easy enough to envision Troughton performance. It is also a story that balances having three companions quite well, although Polly doesn’t really have a role involved as much as the others. Setting Ben at odds with the rest of the Tardis crew for much of the story makes for an interesting dynamic.The characterisation of Medok is also quite strong and his role within the novelisation is positively enhanced from the televised version.The Macra can’t help but be a little bit disappointing though. There use is fairly limited in the story and they are utilised more as a threat in the background than anything. They are clearly different physically and mentally from the Macra seen in ‘Gridlock’ and their descriptions in the novelisation seem to vary between being insect or crablike. With the effects of the time it is hard to imagine how they were realised onscreen; not well going by the photographs that survive.Furthermore, the novelisation gives the impression that this is the Marca’s home world that human colonists have settled on. However, the Doctor destroys them in favour of the humans who although victims are also technically invaders without even attempting his usual efforts at trying to resolve things peacefully.
