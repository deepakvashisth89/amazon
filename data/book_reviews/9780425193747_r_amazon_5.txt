Save your money.  Harrar's attempt at a thriller in the life of a philosophy professor rates a 'D' grade.  He tries to float it on an endless parade of inane philosophic reflections and a course on 'The Necessity of Time' that's a waste of it and the tuition.  He is suspected of kidnapping a cheerleader on absurd grounds and insinuations by a bumbling detective in a plot that is disjointed and clearly amateur.  He and his unreal wife exchange excruciatingly insipid banter that I just kept turning pages to get past with no relief.  Imagine a wife that endlessly over-corrects everything you say and acts more like an investigator than the detective.  (The emotional peak is when she is outraged that he puts a teabag holder in the dishwasher, not worth waiting for.)  On top of that they have twin boys aged ten that plague Dad with put down treatment and confuse him with their identities that only Mom can tell.  The plot drags ridiculously with lame attempts by the suspect professor to search himself with introspection especially derived from a thinker named Wittgenstein - how profound.  The ending reminds me of making coffee and forgetting to put in the grounds - brackish water.  There's a coy hint at the old gimmick of a good twin - bad twin, but nothing conclusive.  Maybe Harrar's deep philosophical mind was above me but his attempt at mystery writing is beneath the book's cost.  Save yours.Read moreRead less
