This book contains 69 short and one very long story by Anton Chekhov-who is considered by many to be the best short story writer ever.  To be honest, some of these stories are great, others not.  One of these stories, Vanka, is one of the best stories I have read by any writer.  This volume is the first of three published by Modern Library and translated by Constance Garnett.  Much has been said about this translator and the quality of her translations(by the publishers of books by more modern translators), but I disagree.  I do not read or understand the Russian language, so I usually get at least two and sometimes three different translations of major Russian literature.  None of these translations of a given work are exactly the same-nor should they be.  These different translations give a slightly different viewpoint of a the work, giving me a better understanding of it.  I have also found that the Garnett translations stand up very well to the more modern translations-and are indeed better than some by the more famous modern translaters.
