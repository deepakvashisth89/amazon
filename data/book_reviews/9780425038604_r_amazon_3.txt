A very old-fashioned mystery novel, Died in the Wool combines beautiful descriptions of New Zealand with a keen eye.  The writing feels like you are reading a play more than a novel, but it's fun.  Despite the old-fashioned feel, the author's attitude towards the characters is quite modern. It also provides a view of WWII New Zealand that is a bit more complex than you might otherwise think:  even though the whites think of England as the motherland, they are also tied to New Zealand in a variety of ways, and do have some appreciation for the Maoris' knowledge.
