This book's a bit dated by now, but it's still perfectly charming, and it does its job. Esperanto's rules can be covered in a few pages with small enough print, so the distinction between textbooks is what else is there. The book spends a considerable amount of time in its intro - about 62 pages - discussing the complications of language, Zamenhof, and such. It then jumps right into the lessons. Each chapter covers a different part of the language with the rules, example words, some exercises, and a short script in Esperanto, detailing a particular part of the fictional Miller family saga. After the structured lessons, the rest of the book is a lot of reading material in Esperanto as well as various tidbits, useful idioms, and mini-lessons sprinkled throughout.The only thing I can really hold against this book is the dictionary at the end only goes Eo->En, not very helpful for a beginner. Still, though, I learned almost everything I know by checking this book out from the library, and I eventually bought a copy here on Amazon.
