Tourists — ReviewThere are fantasy novels about visiting strange places.And then there are fantasy novels that quietly suggest the strangest journey is learning that history, myth, and memory are still walking beside us every day.Tourists is one of Lisa Goldstein’s most original works. Blending contemporary fantasy, folklore, and gentle humor, it explores travel, family, identity, and the unexpected magic hidden in ordinary life.I spent a cool, fog-softened afternoon at the Eye of Bodhidharma while Edgar the raven watched visitors wander through Old Town Eureka with cameras around their necks. Every few minutes someone stopped to admire the murals, the Victorian storefronts, or the bay.Edgar watched them thoughtfully.“Tourists collect photographs. Ravens collect observations. I believe I have the better hobby.”The novel follows a family whose vacation gradually becomes something far stranger than sightseeing. Ancient myths, forgotten histories, and magical encounters slowly emerge from what first appears to be an ordinary journey.What especially impressed me was Goldstein’s quiet confidence.She never rushes the magic.Instead, she allows the fantastic to grow naturally from familiar places and everyday conversations.The result feels wonderfully believable.Goldstein has always excelled at making mythology feel contemporary.Rather than separating fantasy from reality, she lets them overlap until readers begin to wonder whether the extraordinary has been present all along.The characters are warm and believable.Their relationships matter as much as the magical events.The pacing is gentle but steadily rewarding.The novel invites readers to slow down and notice details—a quality shared by the best travel experiences.⸻What Works Well✓ Beautiful, understated proseElegant without calling attention to itself.✓ Inventive use of folkloreAncient myths blend naturally into the modern world.✓ Warm, believable charactersFamily relationships drive the emotional story.✓ Atmospheric settingTravel becomes an adventure of discovery rather than mere sightseeing.✓ Thought-provoking themesMemory, belonging, and curiosity remain central throughout.⸻What Some Readers May Find Difficult• Gentle pacingThe story unfolds gradually rather than through constant action.• Subtle fantasyMagic appears quietly instead of dramatically.• Reflective toneReaders expecting epic battles may find the novel more intimate.• AmbiguitySome mysteries remain open to interpretation.⸻Random Cold Drink PairingCucumber Mint Green Iced TeaCool, refreshing, and perfect after a long day of exploring unfamiliar streets.Random Snack PairingOrange Blossom Almond CookiesLight, fragrant, and easy to tuck into a travel bag for unexpected adventures.⸻Arvidis’ CommentArvidis packed a small paper bag with almond cookies and smiled. “The best travelers leave room in their luggage for surprises.
