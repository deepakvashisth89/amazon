This book makes the otherwise complex and dry subject of Game Theory enjoyable. By using examples we are familiar with, such as the British elections in the chapter: Anticipating Your Rival's Response, subchapter: Games the British Play - the 1987 elections pitting The Conservative's Margaret Thatcher against Labour's Neil Kinnock.In Resolving the Prisoner's Dilemma, I like the use of the US's Republican and Conservatives on how they had to co-operate due to an inevitable Policy Change to increase taxes for the best outcome just like it is in the best interest of the Prisoners to co-operate. What about the OPEC Member countries colluding to drive the price of Crude Oil to unprecedented levels until the Cartel collapsed - the Prisoner's Dilemma highlighted by OPEC Countries.It's the use of familiar events, people and situations throughout the book that makes it easy to read, and really helps in developing one's Strategic Thinking. The stories it tells covers Business, Politics, gambling and so on. This book really makes Game Theory accessible to non-Game Theory scholars, like me.Read moreRead less
