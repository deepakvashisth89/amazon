I consider myself as having an average intelligence (unless I'm among Clinton/Gore supporters, in which case I'm way above average). I feel that I can generally discover why authors introduce certain events or characters. However, in tha case of Perlman's Ordeal, I can't figure out:  1) Why Perlman's intense interest in classical music was important, 2) Why  his brother was a loser, 3) Why water played such a big role.Without a  doubt, Brooks Hansen is a far superior writer than me. However, this can't  be his best! Even my favorite authors (Updike, Kingsolver, Faulkner,  Irving, T.C. Boyle, etc.) write bad novels from time to time. I'm hoping  this was Hansen's bad one. In fact, I just bought The Chess Garden to give  him another chance.Upon reflection, I see some interesting parallels in  the book. It seems Dr. Perlman himself experienced a metamorphosis similar  to Oona. I feel there's some sort of connection between the music and the  water, too. I just can't put my finger on it.I thought the dialog was  hard to follow, too. Especially during the "skits" about  Atlantis. That's another confusing thing: Atlantis?Oh, one more thing.  Most books I have to struggle through the first 100 pages and race through  the last 100 in excitment. In Perlman's Ordeal I struggled on every page  wondering "what's this about?"
