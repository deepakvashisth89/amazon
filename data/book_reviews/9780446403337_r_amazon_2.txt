I just finished rereading this, as I'm going thru my Mongo library again, which I do every now 'n' then. I've thought Mongo is one of the great characters in fiction & have always been disappointed that Chesbro's books never got "bigger" than they did...I mean, he had to self-publish the last few, fer heaven's sake! The only one I haven't read all of is one that was published only in French, & that's only because trying to cudgel my aging brain into remember the french it knew so well when I was in my 20s is a slog. But Mongo's worth it.That said, this is not, IMO, the best of Chesbro's "Mongo Mysteries." During an earlier rereading I put a note on the title page, the gist of which was, the title ought to be "Long Chats in Whatever Key." There's one conversation alone which runs from page 82-122. Forty pages of a single conversation in a 217 page book is not a good thing. I realize it can be difficult to get a lot of backstory about a different character when you're writing a first person book, but just having 4 people sitting around a table & talking about it for nearly 20% of the book is not the best way to get it out, & certainly not up to Chesbro's best.But like always, Mongo is a fascinating & complex character & gets tied up (heh heh) with some scary & wild situations. Veil Kendry makes a reappearance, so there's some "ninja BS stories" (as Garth puts them) and situations. The bad guys are really guys in this one, not critters, tho how human they are is up for debate. This is possibly another good thing, since Chesbro generally falls flat on his face when biology creeps into his books ...like having snakes with eyelids & camels with sharp hooves (snakes have no eyelids, camels don't have hooves). This is very irritating to a bio-geek such as myself.I'm sorta semi-disappointed that the rumors of a Mongo movie starring Peter Dinklage have never panned out.
