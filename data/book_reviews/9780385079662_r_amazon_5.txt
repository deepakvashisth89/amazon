In “The Sufis” Idries Shah does not hold his punches.  Straight out of the gate he states, “Humanity is asleep, concerned only with what is useless, living in a wrong world.”  Shah strips from Sufism any of the woo woo spirituality often attached to mysticism from the east.  Shah grounds his projection of Sufism in the practical - knowledge we all could use.  Yet the reader will likely not see this practicality right away because Shah conveys this knowledge through teaching stories.  The reader will likely confuse  teaching stories with children tales.  They aren’t.  Teaching stories expose the muddled mind that stands between the normal human and their full potential.  Let me illustrate my point with my favorite story from the Sufis.On one occasion a neighbor found Nasrudin down on his knees looking for something. “What have you lost, Mulla?”“My key,” said Nasrudin.After a few minutes of searching, the other man said, “Where did you drop it?”“At home.”“Then why, for heaven’s sake, are you looking here?”“There is more light here.”Shah, Idries.
