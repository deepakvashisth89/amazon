Henry Shaw is one of those historical figures remembered vaguely for his association with a cultural institution but for little else.  “Henry Shaw: His Life and Legacies” puts flesh on the memory of this remarkable man who so enriched the life of his adopted home town, St. Louis.  Born in Sheffield, home of the English cutlery industry, to a tradesman in its hardware products, Shaw’s entry in the family business brought him to America.  His commercial success established Shaw as a much more successful businessman than his father.  When the father shipped merchandise to a poorly chosen business associate in New Orleans, it was Henry who found the shipment and arranged to sell it.  From this beginning Henry traveled around the country selling the imported goods until the rise of American manufactures in the years leading up to the Civil War made the trans-Atlantic trade less profitable.During his business career Shaw settled in St. Louis as his base of operations.  When he retired at a young age he devoted his energies to the development of a garden in the tradition of the great English gardens that he came to know in his native land.  Establishing relationships with accomplished botanists in America and England, Shaw turned his “country estate” into a garden that would bring delight to his later years and become a treasure of his city.  Having never succumbed to Cupid’s arrow, Shaw had only his city and garden on which to lavish his vast fortune.  Missouri Botanical (Shaw’s) Garden continues to honor his name and extend his love of botany into a third century.This is one of the many histories left us by the late William B. Faherty, S. J. that tell the history of St. Louis.  The tale is interesting, the writing superb.  Obviously extensively researched, the narrative never degenerates into a mere recitation of facts.
