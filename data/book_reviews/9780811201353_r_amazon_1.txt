Pasternak writes turbidly but poetically. One can see the bottom of the glass but the water is milky. One must read slowly or some event or point will slip by the reader. His visualizations of people, places and things are unique, not only in sentence structure (which could be due to translation) but in the metaphors he uses to make the reader see or experience something. The chronology is often interupted with a philosophical aside or mental journey. He does not divulge his deepest thoughts but one can guess. It helps to have read prior books on Pasternak in order to make educated guesses about hinted details. Most unique autobiography I have read.
