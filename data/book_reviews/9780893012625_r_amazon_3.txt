This is a fascinating documentary.  In the preface, we learn how the author became aware of Helga Estby.  The first few chapters give information about Helga's early life:  her emigration to the US at a young age, her marriage to a fellow Norwegian at age 15, their hard life in Minnesota, their move to Spokane, Washington, in 1887.  This helps explain how in 1896 Helga was able to defy social mores and, in a bid to save her farm, set out on a 3,500-mile walk to New York City, accompanied by her 18-year-old daughter.  Along the way, we see how Helga and Clara mature, meet different people (highwaymen, governors, presidential candidates, reporters, farmers, etc).  Will their gamble pay off?  How will they get back to their beloved family?This book was written in a style that is easy to read.  It includes pictures of Helga and her family at various stages of their lives.  A map details the actual route that the two women followed.  I particularly appreciate chapter 18, Lost and Found, and A Reflection on the Silencing of Family Stories, in which the author delves into the reasons that many bury important family information.
