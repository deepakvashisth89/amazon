This is the third volume in Caro's enormously ambitious series on LBJ, with an emphasis on his years as a US Senator from Texas. This is the longest book of the series thus far, and goes into more microscopic detail than any of the other books, which makes sense because it is here that you see the true brilliance and terror of the man, as well as the lengths (depths?) he went in order to obtain and augment his enormous amount of power. It starts with a masterful history of the Senate before coalescing around LBJ as he enters it and begins taking residence there. From there, Caro details the myriad ways LBJ used people and causes to further his own agenda, the book becoming more and more stunning in how carefully it explains to the reader one man's ability to rise above his elders in astronomical time and at great moral cost. However, for sizable stretches of time LBJ is not even in the book; Caro is primarily interested in showing the reader of the force of the Senate, but also its malleability, creating a context which gives LBJ's maneuvers greater substance. The parts detailing LBJ's Shakespearean personality and hungers were for me the most interesting parts of the book- love him or hate him, he is a great literary figure. I would be remiss, however, if I did not grudgingly admit that the large parts devoted to the bills and by-laws of the Senate were oftentimes dry and hard to be as engrossed in. Caro is a genius at his craft, and after reading all his books these were the only parts I was not entertained by (out of almost 4,000+ pages), but I was impressed throughout by his talents at continuing to break down complicated topics that were meticulously researched. All grumblings aside, however, I must recommend this as compulsory reading for anyone interested in modern American history or government.
