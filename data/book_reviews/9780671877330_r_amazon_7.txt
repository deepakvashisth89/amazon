David Drake's Redliners is a fascinating traditional science fiction. Initially, it seemed really brutal with the campaign against the "spooks," the Kalendru, an alien race very different from humans. But the effect of the brutal experiences Strike Force Company C41 had on Operation Active Cloak would have redlined them from further military duty.The Chief of Administration, the tyrant running all of Unity, decided they earned more than a being dumped into a world they couldn't relate to. So instead the C41 survivors of Operation Active Cloak got chance to integrate by providing security for a new colony, Bezant 459 colony. Unity is the communities of human settled planets.What was intended to be a moderately challenging environment for both colonists and C41's officers and strikers (soldiers) turned almost overwhelming. When they missed their planned landing zone, the colony ship crash landed in a bowl where the plants adapted to everything the humans did as though there was an intelligence controlling them.I loved the interaction between the civilian authority, Jafar al-Ibrahimi, the colony project manager, and Tamara Lundie, his assistant, and Major Farrell, the C41 company commander. Most of all, I loved watching the individuals thrust into this massively hazardous environment together grow as people.David Drake did such a great job of creating unique and interesting characters. It was fascinating watching plant life that adapted so quickly to the weapons and tactics of the humans. And finding some Kalendru troops in this jungle added further tension.And there were heroes. After all, what's a great story without heroes?The other thing that was intriguing element was how Drake addressed the question of what happens when you integrate computer capabilities into the human brain. Do the new hybrid beings lose their humanity and become impersonal computers?As a final note, Redliners was exceptionally well edited. I don't recall any errors grabbing my attention.
