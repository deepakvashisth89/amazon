This is one of the best books about the sea I have ever read. It is not presented in a narrative format per se, frequently departing from recounting or speculating about the events aboard the ill-fated Andrea Gail to impart scientific detail of the formation and actions of storms and waves, elaboration on fishing techniques, accounts of comparable perils on open waters, and yet not one word is wasted. It all serves to paint a most frightening picture of the ferocity of the sea, letting the reader have a fair idea of just what that brave crew was up against. The detail is such that the book would be entirely accessible to a reader with no prior knowledge of meteorology or fishing boats. But Junger's writing is not cold and technical by any stretch, indeed it is deeply emotional and empathetic, detail serving only to enable the reader to better appreciate the situation. Despite knowing from the outset the ultimate conclusion, one cannot help but feel a sense of thrill and of fear for the Andrea Gail's crew, hoping against all logic that somehow it will turn out differently, all while Junger enumerates at every step the ways in which they might have had some hope, until delivering a powerful emotional blow which got a few tears out of me, stating simply the inevitable, final conclusion.
