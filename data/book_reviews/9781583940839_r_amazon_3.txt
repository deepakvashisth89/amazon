I have just finished reading this. In an age where we have accumulated at least 150+ years of reliable empirical data, flowing in from our improved methods of fact-gathering and data verification, David Wilcock is one of those folks taking on the monumental and headache-generating effort to help us to connect the various dots.Much of this effort naturally carries the inclination to cross the line into speculation. A lot of the most prominent researchers ("dot connectors") in this effort tend to have poor control of their fact presentation (perhaps due to their passion), and bury "the meat" in between theories and extrapolations, making it harder to distinguish between what is fact and what is theory or interpretation. Wilock is not on the deep end of folks who do that. He is a lecturer and researcher who does a particularly good job of presenting facts, including their citations, in proper order and relevance before moving onto his extrapolations. The authors have done an excellent job at shaping this book, and you will find all kinds of citations and notes regarding their sources, further elaborated in the book's appendixes.As other reviewers have stated, the reincarnation of Cayce is really not the primary focus (or value) of this book. The wealth of facts presented (of measurable, verifiable evidence of energetic change in our solar system, including US!) is excellent, and the extrapolations are thoughtful, logical, inspiring and fascinating. The text is well crafted to be "digestible." The general hypothesis is that these energetic changes to ourselves and our environment, imparted through natural (and magnificent) processes of nature, are in fact preparing humanity for another evolutionary "jump" to the next level. The evidence of this having already begun is also compelling. It is not hard even for the lay person to suspect that the pace in which we learn and grow has increased exponentially in the last brief centuries, opening the potentials (and realities) for new conflict, new lessons, and new revelations. The presentation embodies the hope that it is all leading toward a great, ultimately positive future - Hopefully inspired and motivated by an appreciation for peace. The book makes the case that these qualities are built into the fabric of reality itself, and that the move toward these cultural states are not purely sociological in nature, but also edged on by the natural processes of physical evolution. You might say that humans have made a habit of perceiving evolution to be purely biological and gradual in nature, instead of noting the details of how evolution impacts (and improves) consciousness as well. They will introduce you to experiments and records that imply how evolution is often NOT gradual at all, and also elaborate on how these jumps have impacted consciousness in the past, and thus given rise to the surprising civilizations of our history.I found the large middle section of the book, having to do with Wilock's psychic channeling sessions (and containing large segments of raw transcriptions from those sessions) to seem a bit out of place in this book. Your reaction to that section will depend on how you feel about such things. If you are open to humans be able to access information from higher intelligence sources (by use of meditation, trance, prayer, dream, etc) then you will find the transcriptions both inspiriting and fascinating. There is no shortage of love-laced wisdom, and suggestions to improve both individuals and humanity as a whole, within those transcriptions. In that regard, the ultimate source is irrelevant. By themselves they are fascinating, and are surely worthy of being the focus of an entirely different text. However, I do assert that they are a bit out of place here. If you are not open to these kinds of things, you will find the middle to be somewhat irrelevant hard to push through, due to it being somewhat less credible, even if good-intentioned. This may kill your ability to reach the magnificent final section of the book, where "the dots are connected." The middle aside, the rest is well presented and verifiable, and is likely to leave a mark on your thinking.Read moreRead less
