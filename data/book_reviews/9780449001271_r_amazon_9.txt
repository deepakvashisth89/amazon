A prim and proper Englishwoman, Amanda, gets stranded in Adelaide, South Australia. Out of desperation she takes a position as governess to the three wild children of single father Patrick O'Reilly, descendant of convicts, on his outback station (farm) in the Flinders Ranges of South Australia.The trope is a familiar one - governess tames (sort of LOL) the wild children, and falls in love with the master who luckily falls in love with her too. But it felt fresh to me, partly because of the setting so beautifully depicted by Proctor. I'm Australian, and lucky enough to have visited the Flinders Ranges. The descriptions in the book rang true with me. It's a remote, wild and dusty place, but also full of rugged and magnificent beauty.However I think the book will appeal to any historical romance lovers, not just Australians. The story is told in a lively manner, with interesting characters who for me were more than just cardboard cutouts. Proctor is a quality writer. I recommend.Read moreRead less
