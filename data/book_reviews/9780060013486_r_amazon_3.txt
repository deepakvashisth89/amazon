Before Elmore Leonard began writing his string of glorious loosy goosy crime novels about Florida, Detroit and LA low lifes, he wrote westerns, and no surprise, they were good. Good, but not great, as this specimen, from way back in 1956, shows. Leonard always plotted well. His settings were 100% authentic. His characters were genuine, though often off center, personalities. That’s where this slim paperback novel differed from his mature works. The narrative is still good but the characters are less themselves, not as idiosyncratic as later citified Leonard characters were.The plot is simple: Corey Bowen, wrongly convicted, is serving his term in a work camp in Five Shadows. The head of the camp is a crook. He doesn’t care about justice or fair treatment, just money. His men kill any convict who gets out of line and they get away with it. A brave young woman thinks Bowen got a raw deal and sets out to bust him loose. Along the way, Bowen tries to escape, is brought back, and beaten to a bloody pulp. You know the good guys will win in the end in this kind of pulp novel. Leonard does it well but you know what’s going to happen and that reduces the tension in reading it.
