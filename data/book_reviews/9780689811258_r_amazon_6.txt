One thing I love about Margaret Peterson Haddix's books is that she never preaches or talks down to her audience, but still gets her worldview across in a straightforward and non-annoying way. Her characters are not perfect, they are all real human beings and therefore not all of the "good" characters are Christians. She knows how to write a good, honest story without preaching.Never in her work is this more evident than in Leaving Fishers, possibly my favorite of her books. Dorry, lonely and miserable in her new home, gets taken in by the Fishers, a religious cult that has taken basic Christian doctrines waaaay too far and purports to give eternal answers if only its members give up free will, free thought, their ability to question, and everything else that makes them human. It is honestly scary to read the parts about Dorry's "Fisher parties", because the casualness is so forced, everyone is so intent on "hooking" a new Fisher, and the whole feel is so demonically creepy that it seems every Fisher is nothing more than a robot who was tricked into working for Satan by systematically and coldly trying to work for God.As the title gives away, Dorry eventually leaves Fishers, and makes some decisions about religion for herself. But that's the beauty of this book: She never says "the sinner's prayer" and "gets right with God". Not yet, anyway. Haddix's point doesn't seem to be, "If you're involved in a cult, get out and then accept Jesus." Nothing's wrong with doing that -- unless you are told to do it and pressured into doing it. How is it any better to become a true Christian because of pressure than it is to become a member of a cult like Fishers because of that same pressure? Haddix seems to be saying that you should never let others think for you, which I agree with, and by making Dorry unsure about any religion after escaping Fishers everything is just more realistic. I did like, though, her response to Zachary in the end, that Fishers was evil but religion wasn't, necessarily.This book shows the danger of people beginning to place more importance on "chalking one up for God" and then quickly indoctrinating them without caring about them anymore, than on honestly just wanting to share the truth of what they have experienced with someone else, in the hope that they might want to try it too. When people begin to place the most importance on the first, what once seemed to be a simple Christian outreach becomes decidedly more sinister.Rating: Very GoodRead moreRead less
