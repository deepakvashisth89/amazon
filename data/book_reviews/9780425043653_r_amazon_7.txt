It’s honest. It unveils the Human for what it is, naked in its expression. What are we? We are sudden love. Spite. Growth. Hatred. Movement. Focus. Depression. Yearning. Vile. Honour. So much and often so little.The story forces one to reckon with the Human condition through a man who just wanted to be saved and instead was pushed into the fringes through a series of horridly unfortunate events. Life changing events that caused the entire meaning of his existence to be rage.There are multiple forces, both technological, circumstantial, and political, that vie for his attention as the Solar System at large descends into madness. Foyle undergoes a journey of both redemption and grotesque violence. He learns what it means to be a person, begins to see the world with fresh eyes, yearns to better himself and his fellow man.The story can sting as it blatantly brings to bear certain difficult facts about Humanity. We can truly be wretched. But only when we choose to be. And we can always choose to be more though most of the time we’re too preoccupied with day to day undertakings to even care. To even delve into a remote concern for those around us. Through reading you will develop a deep understanding that this cannot possibly be the right approach. If you want proof, simply hand everyone a nuke and we will see what happens.Ultimately we must join hands and unify, work toward something greater, redeem ourselves and cast away our violent and anger filled urges. Even things as common as having little concern for those in need, such as the homeless. The novel is thick with meaning. It will force these notions to the surface of your mind, driving you to reckon with your perceptions as to what the future of this world *must* look like if we are to last for much longer.There is intrigue. Betrayal. Love. Passion. Noir. Elements of Cyberpunk and so much more. It’s a literary expression of high art and I loved every second of reading it.For those curious, I would call it soft science fiction given it’s a product of the 1950s though this did nothing to stifle my enjoyment of the read. Back then we felt the mind was not unlocked so there is a lot of ESP and paranormal activity such as telekinesis from this era. The journey makes some of it integral to the plot but it only serves to drive wonder and a sense of adventure.
