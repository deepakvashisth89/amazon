One of Drake's darker novels, inspired probably by his own dark experiences and nightmares. Despite this, I found it to be a very positive work - the death and sacrifice tinted with sad honour and the whole story ultimately silhouetted against a great, dim hope. The book doesn't seem to be rooted in any universe of Drake's with which I am familiar, and the outlines of history and culture are new, but as indifferent and strange as any future or reality. While the nuts and bolts are interesting, and the tale is compelling, it is the characters, each with their burden of flaws and mortal failings, that really gripped me - struggling to understand and survive, and ultimately each learning truly to live. The theme of civilians and veterans learning each to understand one another, and appreciate one another, was thoughtful and carefully developed. This book stands alone really well, and is an exemplary piece of sci-fi, as well as mil-sci-fi. You don't have to be a fan of the latter to enjoy it.
