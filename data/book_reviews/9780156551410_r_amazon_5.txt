Georges Simenon was a prolific writer, completing on average four books a year.  Many of them, 78 to be exact, were detective novels featuring Inspector Maigret of the Paris police department.  After  reading "Maigret's Rival," I must assume he was under contract to submit so many a year, and this one must have been close to a publisher's imposed deadline.  It seems like a rush job from start to finish.  Maigret is sent by his supervisor to the small rural community of Saint-Aubin to unofficially look into the death of a young man, which may or may not have been murder.  Once there, Maigret discovers a former detective rival of his, one Inspector Cavre, nicknamed Cadaver, is also on the case.  Why and who hired him?  That's about as mysterious as this mystery gets.  There is none of the smart inquisitions or clever detective work so often found in other Maigret novels.  It's just so much page filler where nothing is resolved and the action goes nowhere.  It's pablum for the mind.  If I had read this Maigret book first, I never would have read another and would have missed out on some fine mysteries.  Skip it.Read moreRead less
