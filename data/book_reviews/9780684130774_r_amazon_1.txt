"The Killing Time: The German U-Boats 1914-1918", by Edwin A. Gray, as the title says, is a book about the German U-Boats during the first World War.  Mr Gray is a British author who has published numerous works, most of which deal with submarines.This is a fine book that covers the different types of U-Boats, how the mission of the U-Boat transformed from attacking only warships to attacking merchantmen,  and how the U-Boats evolved from basically unproven curiosities to effective war weapons.  Mr Gray also covers how the British defenses evolved over time to combat the U-Boats, so that by the end of the war, the Allies had managed to dramatically reduce the effectiveness of the German submarines, although only at great cost.Although submarine warfare in World War II is what sticks out in popular memory, the German U-Boats in World War I actually sank more overall tonnage and probably came closer to winning the war for the Germans than did their World War II counterparts.As an aside, many of the first person anecdotes included were taken (with permission) from "Raiders of the Deep: German U-Boats in World War I", a book by Lowell Thomas written over 40 years before "The Killing Time".  In 1928, Mr Thomas interviewed many of the surviving top-scoring submarine aces from World War I, and put together accounts from the German side.  Although it's a very uncritical book and some of Mr Thomas's facts could have used a more thorough checking, his book is a good companion to this book, offering exciting first person perspectives on many of the campaigns and events covered in Mr Gray's book.I highly recommend "The Killing Time".Read moreRead less
