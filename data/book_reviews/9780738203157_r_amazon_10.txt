I'm a fan of Ghiglieri, he has a PhD in Ecology, has spent his professional life running white water river trips and has travelled extensively in countries where clean water, schools and doctors are not de facto, so he has the scientific expertise and rigueur, combined with some down-to-earth outdoor advice and the hard experience of what non-first-worlder life looks like. He spent years observing chimps in remote areas of Africa. I have read "Death in Grand Canyon", "Death in Yosemite", "Canyon" "East of the mountains of the moon: chimps in Ngogo" and "Chimps in Kibale forest", now while the two NPS books are ostensibly about death, they are in fact very factual, handy books full of outdoor activity advice in areas which are dangerous if not treated with respect, and the Ngogo book is about killer apes, but in fact, it's a great narrative about his 2 years spent with the chimps in Uganda, so these are all great reads that I highly recommend, so technically this is Ghiglieri's 4th book on death and killing, but it's the first one which is really a dark and depressing read. I got to page 160 and decided to take a break. I'll finish it later. It's extremely interesting and far more academic in style than his other books, the facts and figures are fully referenced, it gets you thinking, in many ways which logically follow on from the chimp observations. Apparently there is a field called "epigenetics" which considers which aspects of human behaviour are genetic versus which aspects are due to the environment we grow up in and our personal experiences, nature versus nurture as some put it, genetics versus sociology/psychology. This book is essentially on epigenetics. There are more neutral (i.e. without the focus on men and rape/murder) books written on the subject and I'm tempted to read one. I am going to point out that I am female and that discovering that my lifelong fear that "all men are mean" actually has a scientific basis is something I am going to take a while to digest.  I remain a Ghiglieri fan and hope he brings out a "overview and thoughts of my life" book at some point, because I would love to hear what he now has to say looking back over his life from his later years.EDIT: I went off and read a couple of psychology books and psychologists are fully aware that we are a combination of biological, genetic, neurological, endocrine and environmental factors, we all have genes, not all of us become psychopaths and not all psychopaths actually commit rape, murder, etc. I kind of feel that this is not Ghiglieri's field and he maybe shouldn't be reaching psychological conclusions when this is perhaps not his background.
