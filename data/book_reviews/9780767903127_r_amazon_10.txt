Written by Stephen Davis, an experienced rock biographer (he's written books on Led Zep and Aerosmith), Old Gods Almost Dead, is a great  blast through the Stones' first forty years.As with any work of non-fiction, the author can be criticised for omitting facts that one reader might consider essential, or dwelling too much on something that another reader might think its trivial. For me, Davis got the balance more or less right: this is about the Stones and the Glimmer Twins in particular. Their various wives and girlfriends get a mention to a greater or lesser degree but in its essence, this is a Boy's Own rock n roll story.To give you a sense of the pacing, it's not until we get approximately half way through the book, until we hit the holy quartet of the Stones' canon, starting of course, with Beggars Banquet. The final quarter (or even less) covers the much less interesting late 80s onwards, which is pretty much all about bigger stadiums and more elaborate sets. Perhaps an updated edition will include the 50 and Counting, Hyde Park gigs..The author doesn't appear to have interviewed the Stones themselves, so this is really a compilation and distillation of other sources - a greatest hits, if you will but Davis covers everything you would expect and writes with enough style to have kept my interest throughout.Sometimes I did feel the author got carried away with his own sense of myth-making and sometimes it felt like Charlie Watts and Bill Wyman became minor characters in their own stories but as I said before, this is very much about the dynamic betwixt the Stones' brain and heart.After reading Old Gods Almost Dead, I did find myself having a renewed sense of appreciation of just how vital the Stones were to the turbulent 1960s and how damaged they became in the 1970s, so this book is excellent at giving some cultural context to the albums.There is a cliche that writing about music is like dancing about architecture but after reading each chapter, I immediately went back and replayed the relevant albums, despite having heard and loved them, over many decades already, which is the highest compliment I can pay this book.If you are already a Stones-ologist, you may not find much new here but if you love their albums but haven't actually read much about them, Old Gods Almost Dead by Stephen Davis, will make you thankful for being alive at the same times as these soul survivors - and that we are unlikely to see their kind again.Read moreRead less
