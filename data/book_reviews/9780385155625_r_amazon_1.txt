This is only the second in the delightful Peter Shandy series so the reader new to it has a good deal to look forward to. It is not at all necessary to have read the first to fully enjoy the second, although the systematic reader may prefer to maintain the order of writing. Those new to the author, who is quite contemporary, although she passed away a few years ago, might be interested to know that she wrote four series of books, probably the best of which is the extended story of the Bittersohn's with this one taking second place. All are available in Kindle editions, each has its own setting and its own set of idiosyncratic characters.The setting is an agricultural college in New England in which two stunning events have struck Professor Shandy within a period of days. While buying a set of fine dishes, he and his wife are present during a holdup  in which a fortune in gold has been stolen and his wife taken hostage (and released) to help effectuate the escape. Within days, a College employee is killed and a prized pig stolen. The former had no impact on the College but the latter threatened to destroy the work of many years of breeding as well as being, of course. a shock to the sense of security of those associated with the College. To help the College deal with the latter, Professor Shandy is called upon by the Stalwart  President to lend a helping hand to the police in solving the murder and finding the pig. There is a bit of the Wodehouse flavor to the book, of course, quite deliberately so, with the murder, however, creating a bit more seriousness than is found in the Old Master. The book is essentially light-hearted with a sprinkling of love affairs among students and faculty to add to the problems of Professor Shandy who has become, as a result of the events detailed in the first books, a sort of guru of problem solving for the Viking-Like President.The book is easy and pleasant reading, the characters pronouncedly characters and the outcome foreordained. There is nothing here of the cuter side of the ' cozy' as it is often written today, none of the need of the Little Woman to Show that she is Superior to the truly little Man, here Man and Woman cooperate (or conflict) as individuals, each contributing equally to the final outcome, an amusing book with interesting characters, achieving a satisfying outcome.Read moreRead less
