Brief summary and review, no spoilers.The story starts out in 1941. A young 5 year old boy calling himself Simon Thorn is one of a group of children that has been transported to the rural English countryside during the London Blitz. The problem is that no one seems to know who he is and how he got on the train. Luckily for him, a kind-hearted childless couple take him in and love him and raise him. He takes their last name and becomes Simon Cutheridge.Years later, in his twenties, Simon moves to London and happens upon a street that seems familiar to him. He follows it to a home, and has an awful sense of foreboding and deja vu. He knows that was his childhood home.This story is Simon's search for his identity, and it takes him places he might not want to go. Simon had nightmares of hearing terrible arguments and witnessing abuse as a child, and he is about to find out what had happened all those years ago.I have read quite a few Robert Barnard mystery books and they are always a treat. This one is no different, although I have to say it wasn't my favorite. It was a little slower-paced than others I have read and I may have even been tempted to skim at times. Still, I would recommend this book both because it's well-written and gives you a great sense of time and place, and because as with all Robert Barnard books - there is a great twist at the end. And this is a writer who plays fair and square with the clues.Recommended.Read moreRead less
