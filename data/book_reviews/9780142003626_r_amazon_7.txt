You know, when your loquacious great-aunt starts to tell a family story, that "color" likely will be more rewarding than the action in the story itself. This rambling novel is like that. It is replete with great nuggets and extended stories which feature colorful small-town stories. They are recollected by a boy looking back on local events and characters. Usually hilarious, these events and characters lack menace and cruelty in favor of charm and humor. Pearson's later novels, every one of which is worth reading, have evolved, but have not lost the essential charm of this first one.
