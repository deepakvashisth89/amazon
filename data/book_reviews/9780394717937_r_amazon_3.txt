Myrdal strives to capture the human drama of village life. And since he is writing in the wake of the Great Leap Forward, we might expect an emphasis on grassroots politics. But I suspect the realities Myrdal describes are mostly older than any modern revolution. The casual approach to parenting, the neighborly approach to law enforcement, the grinding work and poverty, the women's desires for freedom -- all probably reflect realities little changed by the government in Beijing.
