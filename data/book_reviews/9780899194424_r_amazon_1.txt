This biography of Janet Flanner's family beginnings in Indianapolis and her migrations to Chicago, New York, and throughout Europe is stolidly told.  Brenda Wineapple presents Flanner's life as fragmented, restless, peripatetic, and deceitful.  Wineapple thinks Janet Flanner failed to acknowledge herself, traceable to effects of Flanner's undertaker father who committed suicide and her theatrical mother whose own orientation toward grandeur was apparently frustrated by marriage.  Flanner's success as a writer of her Paris letters to The New Yorker magazine (1925-1975) was made possible, Wineapple emphasizes, by the help of friends and lovers and by first-rate editing. Appearances by Noel Murphy and Nancy Cunard enliven the book. Tales of Janet Flanner among the women's community in and around Paris are more vividly told elsewhere.
