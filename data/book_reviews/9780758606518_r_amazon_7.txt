Luther: Biography of a Reformer by Frederick NohlI found this book at a community book sale.  I didn't realize it at the time, but the book is a complement to the 2003 movie "Luther" and includes color photos from the movie.Martin Luther was raised by an upwardly mobile father in the copper mining and smelting trade.  His father wanted him to become a lawyer.  However, Martin Luther, convinced of his utter sinfulness and inability to please God, became a monk in a desperate attempt to cleanse himself.  While acting as a professor and priest, Luther came to the conclusion that man is not saved through priests, indulgences, and good deeds but through faith in the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus.  Although, Luther was not the first person to come to this conclusion (Wycliffe, Hus, and others before him have also taught this truth), he was a significant figure in the protestant reformation.The book offers a clear and summarized account of the life of Martin Luther.  The book presents a fair picture of Luther, discussing his frustrations and some of his faults as well as his victories.  The book is written at layman's level and should be an easy read for anyone.
