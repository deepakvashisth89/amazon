Much attention has been focused lately on an “epidemic of loneliness,” with speculation on its cause: did it arise from the isolation of Covid 19, or perhaps from the rise of social media?Sherwood Anderson’s book shows that alienation and the search for a meaningful life caused loneliness 100 years ago.Anderson’s collection of “grotesques” examines people who feel out of place in the small town of Winesburg. Some are very intelligent and some seem not as bright. Some are beautiful, some decidedly not. Many feel they have special enlightenment they wish to share with others but don’t know how. One waits his whole life for God to appear to him and validate the man’s special status as a favorite of God’s.The subjects of the stories have a great deal to share with others, but can’t express in words what they feel, even if anyone listens.Young George Willard, the town’s only newspaper reporter, holds the stories together. All those people struggling to find universal truths and meaning for their lives turn to George, the one person they each feel can understand.There is a great deal of content packed into these character studies and Anderson can sketch them and the Winesburg setting to make a reader see it all with a minimum of words.Sometimes I saw them too clearly and the empathy was too much. The characters were trapped within themselves and living in their heads with no way to communicate with others.
