In "The Culture of Disbelief: How American Law and Politics Trivialize Religious Devotion" by Stephen L. Carter, he explores how  religion has remained a divisive point in American politics and private  lives.  He explores how many people view the addition of religion or  religious topics into mainstream society as a tool of the far right. This  book is easy to read and thought provoking. Dr Carter explains how religion  is diminishing throughout many aspects of our culture and society.  The  examples he uses to illustrate the changes in politics, schools, and  society tend to support his opinion.  Although he is Catholic, he does not  show preference toward that form of Christianity.  The Supreme Court and  legal opinions showed a great deal of research in order to support his  opinion and led the book an air of credibility.  He is quite adept at using  the legal precedent to support his personal ethics, particularly his  reinterpretation of the first amendment.  The premise is flawed however.  Whereas he shows no particular bias towards Catholicism, he is definitely  biased towards Christianity.  And to this end, the book is disturbing.  He  completely negates the fact that not everyone chooses to be religious  within this society.  The first amendment was drafted to protect the  personal (including religious versus non-religious) freedom of ALL  citizens.
