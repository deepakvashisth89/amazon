Having stood as an independent parliamentary candidate myself I have a fellow feeling for Martin Bell. Though my circumstances were different (up against an 'establishment' Conservative with full fox hunting credentials: I lost big time) from Mr Bell's (up against a sleazy Conservative with little integrity left: Mr Bell won big time), I thought that it would be helpful to compare notes via Mr Bell's book. It was helpful and, once again, I learned a lot from reading. I shall know better next time.But Martin Bell's 'An Accidental MP' is much more than a guide for independent parliamentary candidates. It is most amusing in parts - his confrontation with Neil and Christine Hamilton on Knutsford Heath and his being 'lost' in the House of Commons ranking well with the best of Feydeau's farces - and deeply insightful in other parts. In a sense, it is the ultimate outsider's guide to British politics and the House of Commons as viewed from the inside.A particular episode in Mr Bell's brief tenure at Tatton (from 1997 to 2001) illustrates perfectly the man's 'accidental' power. He had promised that, if elected, he would serve but one term. He kept his promise, though public pressure to act otherwise was substantial. My assumption is that, if he had stood again in 2001, he would have been re-elected on his record. By not standing again, he ensured that the Conservative candidate was home and dry for life. The Conservative candidate was one George Osborne. Of such decisions is history made.This book is beautifully written, as one would expect from a world-class journalist, and it deserves high praise and five stars.Read moreRead less
