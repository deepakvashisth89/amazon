Historian Lillian Faderman has been a pathbreaker with her research and writing about women’s role in US history and the cultural and social context in which LGBT people have negotiated their place in American society. To Believe in Women is one of Faderman’s earlier works, and it has informed and inspired other historians and writers to research further into these realms. I have benefited greatly by her research and histories in my own writing.To Believe in Women focuses on four public realms in which lesbians have had greatest impact: voting rights for women, social justice, education, and the professions. Within each area Faderman profiles women who were some of the most influential public figures of their times. What’s interesting, of course, is how many of these women are not known to the public as being lesbians. Their full lives have often been hidden from history -- sometimes by the women themselves who felt they must do so to have any public influence.
