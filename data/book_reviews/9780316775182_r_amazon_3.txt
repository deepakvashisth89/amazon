I love the story of Queen Esther, her courage, and her faith. This book presents that and MORE.  So much more.  It's really fabulous as a multi-tasking conversation starter with children.Some of the things I loved about the book and discussions it could begin (warning, there are some spoilers here):1)  Strong female roles!!!  The protagonist is a female, the actress who takes on the villains role is a female, and of course Esther is the heroine of the Purim story as well.  Also, I loved that the Father in this book is depicted making cookies in the kitchen (complete with flour on his nose).  The male and female characters are well integrated and varied (both parents have lines, one boy is an excellent actor, another is not, etc).  Their is a subtle "girls can do what they choose to do" message in here---evidenced by the parents choosing a lady to do the villain's role and encouraging the daughter to open her mind to that---and that lady doing a wonderful job.  And then, of course, God uses Esther to save the day.  It's really a great story for being empowered about being a girl.2) Cross generational.  Love the "real" conversations between the adults and children here.  I love how the daughter is encouraged (but not forced) to see older generations as "friends."  And I love that that friendship blossoms.  Wonderful discussion opportunity for not just respecting but befriending and appreciating elders.3) Two tales in two very different times.  There are two stories going on here: The story of Esther, which leaks into the main plot bit by bit (in ways that will suit those who are and those who are not familiar with this tale) and the story of the children.  I love that this story stretches across allllllllllll that time.  I feel it makes the Esther story more real and relevant to see it in this way. It invites the children to process and enjoy the story along with the characters.  It tells and important story and supports tradition as well.4)  Awesome Extras!  There's a recipe for Hamestachen cookies (yummy, btw).  And there's also a one page Story of Esther for those unfamiliar with the story.  And there's another one-pager on the history surrounding Purim and Purim plays, etc.  Non-Jewish children (and perhaps adults) will be able to learn some new vocabulary.Read moreRead less
