Obviously of its time, but surprisingly fun and easy to read. Sort of hard to imagine that to most of the British colonialists in India or to the naval officers fighting Napoleon, this book would have already been considered quite old.The first half of the book is a classic (the classic) shipwreck survival tale and very clear in its parallel of the castaway's physical survival alone to everyone's spiritual survival in the privacy of their relationship to mortality. A worthy companion to Milton or to Pilgrim's Progress, both of which it echoes in a lot of ways.The second half is a straight up 18th century adventure novel, which is quite fun. The attitudes towards servitude cross over, even for the time (I think), into pure fantasy. Easy to see why it was so popular for so long.Read moreRead less
