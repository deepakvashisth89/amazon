I agree with the previous reviewers that this is a fast-paced, whiz-bang, and well plotted story that incorporates an impressive display of knowledge of war games gadgetry and the inner workings of the Soviet and the US national security appartus, as well as the sites of their deployment and details related to those sites. (I assume these kinds of details are authoritative - I would not know any different - but they are important to the story, and believable.) Most of the characters are somewhat wooden (e.g. as displayed in too often forced dialog especially in the Kremlin scenes) and either predictable, or unbelievable, especially the two principles, protagonist Nick Delan and paramour Clio Bragana, as it relates to the development of their relationship, which I found too coy, too tentative, too much guided by politesse instead of passion, and therefore unrealistic. Nevertheless, this is a minor flaw and a relative one, as Chubin does somehow nevertheless manage to create reader sympathy for Nick and Clio, and therefore willing suspension of this kind of critical viewpoint. If this kind of plot and atmospherics are up your alley, you can probably just ride along the story line and enjoy this book more than I did.
