Ngaio Marsh was considered one of the 20th century's Queens of Crime (with Agatha Christie, Dorothy Sayers, and Margery Allingham) and this book illustrates why.  Inspector Roderick Alleyn of Scotland Yard is vacationing in New Zealand and falls in with an acting troupe on a cruise and then on a train.Things to like: Marsh's description and love for the New Zealand setting; the portrayal of an acting troupe feels very authentic, the well-clued mystery.Things to dislike: the early 20th century depiction of  racism to the Maori people (the Maori doctor is an Oxford-educated physician but Marsh describes and shows him as being 10% "savage".)One big problem with the Kindle version is that it's missing the floor plan and a drawing that Alleyn makes as he investigates.  I own both the Kindle version and the paperback (but sadly can't read the paperback anymore because the print is too small for my aging eyes.) Major understanding of the mystery is conveyed in the pictures and they are not there.I strongly recommend the book.Read moreRead less
