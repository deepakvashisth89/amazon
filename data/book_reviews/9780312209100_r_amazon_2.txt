Gail is staying at a packer's lodge preparing to ride into the Sierra Nevada's with her 2 horses and dog. She goes for a walk and comes across a man who was trying to commit suicide by shooting himself but he's still alive. Gail tries to figure out what the dying man meant when he was talking about green fire in the horse's bellies. She and the dying man are both vets so she thought he had some colicked horses he couldn't save. She goes back to the lodge and an helicopter ambulance is called. He dies the next day.The book started out with this strong opening and then it kind of stalled mostly bogged down with too many details of what was going on in every minute of Gail's life. Gail and her animals set off on their trip and the description of the scenery and the trails is amazing. Very well written. But just too much details that didn't move the story along but were great for imagery. This is a trip I wouldn't have attempted on my own and Gail certainly got herself and her horses into territory that I hope I never have to ride in.Gail meets other campers on her journey, some are friendlier than others. Then she comes across parts of the trail that have been booby trapped. She thinks it might have been the work of another camper she met who hates horses. Then when she crosses paths with Blue Winter and somebody shoots at them she doesn't know which one of them is the intended victim.The last half of the book had lots of drama and became a real page turner. Nice job.I also want to mention that the author loves dashes. The ebook formatting program not so much. There are no spaces between words and dashes so the book is inundated with lots of oddly hypenated words. It was well written enough to get past this annoyance. I'd definitely read more of this author's books.Read moreRead less
