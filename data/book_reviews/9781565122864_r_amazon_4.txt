From moment to moment, I thoroughly enjoyed this book.  The story is told from the perspective of Louise, a highly observant (and snarky) young woman who lampoons her Southern Baptist family in all their garish, upper middle-class glory.  I loved the narrator's descriptions, which were both original and dead-on, and thought the author's style was, overall, top-notch.Some reviewers have commented that Louise's substance abuse and rebellion wasn't believable, but I disagree.  Throughout Louise's portrayal of her family, I FELT her rebellion coming on.  The substance abuse was foreshadowed very early in the story (with her penchant for drinking vanilla extract) and given the dark turn the story takes, it seemed entirely plausible to me.  To give more rational explanation for her behavior, I think, would undermine Sumner's expert characterization of a troubled, religiously-repressed teenage girl. It rang very true in my opinion.What might come as a surprise if you're not expecting it is the shift in tone.  The books starts out fairly light and funny in an offbeat sort of way, taking a sudden dark turn that seems to come out of nowhere, but is actually quite neatly foreshadowed.  I thought Sumner does an excellent job of presenting seemingly unimportant details, which later hit you over the head with their consequences.So, why not five stars?  If I have any criticism of this book, it's the ending.  Sumner seems to be in her element with her withering descriptions of this Southern Baptist family; when Louise joins the circus, the book starts to fall flat.  There are definitely some great moments in there, with amusing characters who are nearly as bizarre as her family members, but overall I thought the ending kind of fizzled.  I didn't get the sense that Louise learned anything from her experiences, or made any progress either forward or backward.  Everyone seemed to end in pretty much the same place they started, except perhaps a bit more frazzled and disillusioned.Overall, though, I really enjoyed this book.  It's the kind of book I will go back to and underline favorite passages.  Sumner definitely has my attention.Read moreRead less
