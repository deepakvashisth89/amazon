This pioneering book of cultural anthropology, written in the mid-World War years by one of the true pioneers of the field, provides insightful and detailed information about the life of a pre-modern society of Sudanese living along the Upper Nile, the Nuer. Evans-Pritchard spent considerable time in the field investigating the Nuer, learning their language, tweezing out their remarkably complex culture and society, and analyzing their relationship to an unforgiving ecology, their economy based on cattle raising, their structure of family and tribal descent. The chapter that explores the Nuer management of and dependence on cattle raising alone is worth the book's purchase price. Most of all, Evans-Pritchard makes the reader aware of the complexities and sophistication in a pre-modern; native culture and society, bereft of writing, most private possessions, law, and institutionalized political, economic and social leadership.Read moreRead less
