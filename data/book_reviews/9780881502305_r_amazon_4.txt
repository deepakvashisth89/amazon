[colloquial definition] A cast-iron frying pan, orig. one with legs for use on a hearth.It's wartime out on the Cape (1942), and the place has undergone some dramatic changes since Asey Mayo was last home, six months ago.  He's been working at the Porter plant, making tanks instead of cars.  When he finally gets two days of vacation, he makes his way back to Cape Cod, only to peek in the window and see his cousin-in-law Jennie conducting a first aid class in their living room.  Before Asey can walk in and have a true homecoming, the whole group leaves in a rush to address a practice "problem" at another location.  Now Asey can do a bit of relaxing in an empty house.  But soon enough his friend Doc Cummings arrives and greets him; and soon enough the two find a dead body in the buttery.  It's Philemon Mundy, dressed up to look exactly like the dummy the First Aiders were working on.  He's been hit over the head with the ubiquitous bunt instrument.  Jennie's iron spider hangs nearby and appears likely to have been the murder weapon.Asey sets out to find Jennie and to see what she might know about Phil Mundy.  Doc Cummings begins to do a bit of investigation on his own.  He discovers that due to certain circumstances of the seasoning process, it would have to have been a brand-new frying pan that made the mark on Phil's head.  That leaves Jennie and her spider in the clear.  As Asey and Doc go their separate ways around East Aspinnet and keep their ears open, they learn more about the victim.  Seems as though Phil has been telling everyone lately how much he has wanted a spider just like the one his mother used to make pancakes.  Coincidentally, six spiders recently appeared in the display window of Bob Corner's hardware store.  Thinking of Phil, some of his friends and relatives bought them up separately, each one intending to give the pan to him on his birthday.  But Phil was not always the nicest person.  And someone decided to bash him with one instead.So the question is:  Who bought the six spiders?  There was Kay Mundy, Phil's sister; Jonah Ives, a fellow First Aider and possible spy, according to local lore; displaced Boston Brahmins Sterling and Grace Hazard, who may have been trying to find Phil a job; their daughter Tiny Hazard; and local widow Mildred Tuesome.  The last two have crushes on Phil and might have been competing for his attention.  But that adds up to only five.  Who had the sixth one?  Did Phil break down and buy one for himself after all?As usual, Asey find himself in the thick of things.  And as usual, there's a lot of running around and having to explain the situation to the local, clueless authorities.  Asey's modus operandi is made more difficult now by the presence of the military in the area and by concerned civilian spotters on the lookout for suspicious behavior, even sabotage.  His name alone no longer works its former magic.  Evidently it's a "mum week" for the residents.  But Asey perseveres.  "There were motives all over the place, when you stopped and thought about them, and they were motives that ought to be good and sound, like money, and love, and jealousy, and revenge." (p. 238)  And of course it's inevitable that someone else will get smacked in the head with a spider and left in the Mayo buttery.  At least that second murder serves to narrow the field of suspects.So much for a vacation.  As soon as he solves the crimes, Asey's got to go back to work.  And quite frankly, it will be a pleasure and relief, after dealing with the war-time weirdness going on, on the Cape."The Six Iron Spiders" is an interesting installment in the Asey Mayo mystery series.
