I thought I would find this book dark and foreboding when I read about it, but au contraire, I found what it essentially a diary by the dwarf who is the court favourite and confident of The Prince, totally absorbing, as if you have been drawn into the spider's web of this man's (the dwarf's) psyche and modus operandi. The description of life in Renaissance Italy is captivating, detailed and convincing. The author's craft is such that you don't notice it. It just works seemlessly. The inner psychodrama in the mind of the dwarf is complex; Jungian, Faustian and Machiavellian, always cynical, opportunistic, but strangely optimistic as you see in the end. I cannot remember any book which is both delightful to read but also disturbing as you navigate the subterranean sewers of the human mind. As he does not regard himself as a human but of another ancient species (dwarf people), he views mankind and his behaviour like someone from another planet and it this fresh perspective which I found both revealing, disturbing and quite often, accurate and true. The Greek word for a dwarf is "nanos" which gives us nano- in computing and mathematics. This "nanos" paints man, not as he likes to see himself, but as he really is, at least most of the time.
