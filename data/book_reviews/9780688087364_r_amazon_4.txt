After thirty years I suppose there had to be a clinker, but damn! So, I’ve been reading Ed McBain/Evan Hunter since the 1980s, after discovering his 87th Precinct Police Procedurals on my parents’ book shelf, and with over 150 books I have yet to “catch ‘em all” as the kids say. This week I pulled down 1989’s DOWNTOWN, a stand-alone Ed McBain title out the realm of the 87th Precinct or the Matthew Hope books. And it stunk. It stunk real hard. I feel bad saying this since at a book convention I had the nicest chat with Mr. Hunter, but now that he is passed away I suppose I can be honest. DOWNTOWN was like a really bad Hard Case Crime novel. I can’t but think that Mr. Hunter must have seen Scorsese’s AFTER HOURS and Stone’s PLATOON and decided to put a “wrong man” twist on the story of a Florida orange grower in downtown NYC (shadow of the trade center) on Christmas Eve. Over the next two days he encounters Hollywood types, Mafia types and other NYC floaters, while flashbacks from Vietnam, for some reason, echo his decision making. The plot never makes since and for some reason is extra complicated, more TRUE DETECTIVE than CHEAP DETECTIVE. All of that nastiness being said (and I do feel bad) Hunter is still on our greatest writers, and if he wants to be influenced by Scorsese’s weirdest movie than, hey, have fun.
