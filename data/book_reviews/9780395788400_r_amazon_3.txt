Farr's epistolary novel about the life and passions of Emily Dickinson is a mesmerizing, inspiring, zesty read. Based on facts gleaned through letters and Dickinson's poetry, the story elaborates on Dickinson's 10-month school career, on her passions for women and for men, and on her final, ailing years. Like Cunningham's "The Hours" and Zackheim's "Violette's Embrace", it's a wonderful tribute to a woman whose true life is obscured by history.
