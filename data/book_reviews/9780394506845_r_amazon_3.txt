I originally read the series of articles that appeared in Audubon Magazine that eventually turned into "The Hunt". I recall the furious letters to the editors that accused Audubon of wanting to denude our forests of all critters and those that huffily cancelled their subscriptions because of their anti-hunting stance.  Goodness gracious, it seemed to me at the time that Mr. Mitchell must have gotten it exactly right.  I think that he did.  This book is not really about hunting, it is about our relationship with the outdoors and the creatures that live there.  When Mr. Mitchell observes that the wild is not a museum diorama, with animals living in harmony, with no blood and no death he raises the key point in wildlife management: nature ain't purdy.Give this book a read and, if you want to think about the question of hunting rather than take a stand and circle the wagons, look up Jose Ortega y Gasset and read some of Faulkner's short stories like The Old People and The Hunt.
