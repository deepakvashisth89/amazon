Catherine Drinker Bowen has written a wonderful book. Miracle at Philadelphia is the story of the writing of our US Constitution. As understood today, the term "Democracy" was in its infancy when the Constitution was written and just like today, back then it meant different things to different people. But one thing we know for sure, this form of government America enjoys today had no precedent in 1787. As far as applying democracy to nation building is concerned, 1787 was ground zero.Today, 220+ years later, there are over a hundred democracies worldwide, each sporting their own unique differences. Most importantly, however, all of the current democratic governments that subsequently followed America's lead have, when establishing their respective governments, based their foundations on the principles laid down by our Founding Fathers during that summer of 1787. America's Founders changed the way the world works for all time. Indeed, it was a most mesmerizing occurrence.As the prefix to this book states miracles do not occur at random, nor was it the author of this book who said a miracle occurred at Philadelphia. George Washington said it as did James Madison: Washington in a letter to Lafayette; Madison in a letter to Thomas Jefferson. The focus of this book is to deliver the discussions and deliberations of the participants, the framers of the document that still guides America today. Their vision was remarkable, although it is quite likely each really had no idea of the far reaching consequences or the global impact that their consensus would have.It is the political creativity of that summer that most excites our admiration today. How wondrous is it to stop and realize that every political institution we rely on, and so blithely take for granted today, was invented before 1800. Not another one has been added since! The Constitution's stability is unparalleled. While over 10,000 amendments have been recommended over the years, only 27 have been formally adopted and 10 of those 27, the original Bill of Rights, were adopted prior to the Constitution's ratification by the original 13 States. It is the shortest and oldest written constitution of any major sovereign state. Equally amazing is that two men in their thirties and one in his forties managed to write the Federalist Papers in six months, explaining the Constitution and it workings so completely and in such detail that hundreds of universities have not been able to duplicate their feat in the past two centuries.This then is the subject of this book and yes, Bowen's story includes some quite serious flag waving. But understand that the flag is not waved for us today, but rather, is waved for those who preceded us. We today are simply the beneficiaries of the enduring compromise the Founders engineered so many years ago. Today we we are proud to call that amazing compromise, one that has been handed down for over two centuries without serious change, the United States Constitution.This is a well written work that clearly and efficiently communicates with the reader.
