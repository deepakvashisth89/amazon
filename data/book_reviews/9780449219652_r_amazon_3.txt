Someone must have mentioned to Corinne Holt Sawyer that the title of her previous work, “The J. Alfred Prufrock Murders” had nothing whatsoever to do with the story, other than an epigram before and after. In this book J. Alfred Prufrock kicks off the action (although he has nothing whatsoever to do with the subsequent plot.)I plodded through the first 2 chapters of this sequel simply because it was given to me and I wanted something  frothy. After the first two chapters I put it aside for the local “Little Free Library” as being something someone else might like, but not me.  Then I picked it up again for lack of anything else and found the charm and quaintness that have endeared Sawyer’s characters to a certain subset of readers – old enough to understand age, and young enough to laugh at it.This plot is better than the earlier work – there are several plausible murderers, and the final choiceis satisfying.  The two septuagenarian amateur detectives are still a bit too cute for words, but once you get past the first two chapters and the murder is actually committed, this is a fun read.
