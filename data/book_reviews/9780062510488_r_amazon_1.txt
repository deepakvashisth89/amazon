Thurman's "Introduction" to the book is worth the price of the book itself, although both of course are mostly of interest to those with a deep curiosity about and profound reverence for the almost unimaginable brilliance of Tibetan Buddhism: its portrait of the human condition, its mapping of the human psyche, its articulation of the path and stages of enlightenment, and its unveiling of the vast dimensions of the quantum multiverse in which we live and move and have our being.  The book need not be read from cover to cover to appreciate its richness.  After a close reading of the "Introduction" (which starts out rather technical but quickly picks up speed), the book may be perused at one's leisure, and returned to again and again.  It is full of illuminating insights and memorable quotes (despite the sometimes tough going and inevitable repetitions and challenging details).
