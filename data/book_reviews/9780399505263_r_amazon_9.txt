Marvellous story. It is like  a play of several acts in which the interlinked characters live and move in such limited or circumscribed and oddly incestuous circumstances that even suicide is an option.Love is never named . It something that the characters feel, but put aside in favour of brief erotic encounters. Beauty is recognised but the mindset can violate it as if it is wrapped in a dirty cloth by one's own memory and the actions of one's parents.Of course the West would call this trauma, but not in this novel.You have to put up with the 'slight' cultural misogyny of women with perceived masculine traits, having no other positive redeeming qualities. If it were a Western novelist the perfect woman  would be marred by a vicious mole of nature but you won't know what I mean unless you read itI absolutely love all the detail of the book. The teahouses, the idea of the traditional values, the strange arcane nature of the tea ceremony and the actual tea vessels. The indelible stain of the geisha on the lip of one .
