Positive:Masters of Doom is an absolute thrill for anyone curious about the wild, untamed days of early video game culture. David Kushner captures the story of John Carmack and John Romero, the legendary duo behind Doom and Quake, with so much energy it feels like a ride through gaming history. The book dives into how these two visionaries, with their brilliant but clashing personalities, built id Software and revolutionized gaming, not just as a tech feat but as a cultural movement. It’s inspiring, chaotic, and gives a real sense of how these games defined an era.Negative:One thing to note: the book can feel a bit focused on the tech side at times. For those not into the nitty-gritty details of game development, it might slow the pace a bit. Also, while Kushner covers the tension between Carmack and Romero well, he sometimes glosses over other team members who played key roles. Still, for anyone interested in the intersection of tech, creativity, and pop culture, these are minor issues in a book that’s largely a blast to read.Read moreRead less
