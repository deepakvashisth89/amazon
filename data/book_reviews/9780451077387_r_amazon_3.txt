From the back cover:  "A warrior king vowed to celibacy - and his beautiful young bride aflame with desire... They had come together in an arranged marriage. Edmund, the Anglo-Saxon ruler of an embattled kingdom, vowing himself to celibacy to purge a haunting guilt. And the lovely Aleen - his untouched bride tormented by her awakening womanhood - suddenly fleeing a nunnery to shelter herself from the mounting tide of burning physical need rising within her. Here, in a rapturous romantic epic, is a man of awesome power and passionate emotion, and the young woman who tantalized him with desire until, in trembling, triumphant consummation of their undeniable love, she stirred the fires of his heart anew."Golly!  I only wish this book were half as racy and exciting as that description makes it sound.  I will say there are several beautifully written passages, which make my rating a bit higher than I would ordinarily give a book whose plot I can only describe as "stagnant."  The bits of lovely stuff are bookended by the lengthiest, most distant love story one could ever hope to read.  Edmund and Aleen have very little connection while the author tells the story of their white marriage.  Aleen is given no quarter by her husband's people: she's mocked, despised; even the little stepdaughter she loves is taken from her, yet somehow Aleen falls in love with Edmund, who's totally unaware of Aleen's difficulties and a gentle-wanna-be priest.  In fact, she loves him enough to enter a convent during the last third of the book - not, in fact, due to "burning physical need" as the book cover would have it, but rather to release Edmund (who's sworn a vow of celibacy after the death of his first wife) from their arranged marriage.  The reader is allowed into Aleen's head, but not so much Edmund's; this unevenness flaws the love-story aspect but does provide some marvelous character development for Aleen.I struggled to stay with this story and became impatient with its emphasis on keeping poor Aleen (who deserved better) and clueless Edmund apart - until the final five pages, which contain a beautifully written reunion scene and Edmund's moving written confession of love - but what a long, long, wait!Read moreRead less
