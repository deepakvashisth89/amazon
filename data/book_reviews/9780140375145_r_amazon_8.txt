Another short middle-school thriller from Sleator, who explores what’s beneath the façade of an extended family, and, by extension, the secrets we're all motivated to hide from one another. The pace of the novel is breakneck, while also guiding the reader through the processes of analyzing dangerous situations and engaging in mental combat.As usual, Sleator sets the scene within a family. Teenaged protagonist Jared can't lie successfully, so he has grown up to be a fairly benign and transparent person, but he discovers that the family he thought he knew is more conflicted than he knew. (Yes, the teenaged boys in the story have fantasized about their girl cousin, but this novel is not explicit, and there are no sexual encounters whatsoever.)Sleator’s speculative young adult novels are nuanced, with family conflict, suspicion about authority, and the eventual revelations of hidden motives. (R.L. Stine, the Goosebumps author, was a big fan of William Sleator.) Sleator builds fantastical worlds for his readers but points out, as most spiritual adepts know, that the fantastical also comes at a price. Don’t believe blindly, and understand that some people are dangerous, selfish, and controlling.Sleator also brings realistic qualities of ambiguity and uncertainty into his novels, refusing to neatly tie up loose ends, and this novel is no exception.
