This book was eye-opening. As a water resources engineer, I found Cadillac Desert both fascinating and deeply unsettling. Reisner exposes the political ambition and engineering bravado that fueled the transformation of the American West — often at staggering environmental and financial cost.What struck me most was how agencies like the Bureau of Reclamation and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers operated with near-limitless authority, building massive water infrastructure projects with little regard for long-term consequences like siltation, habitat destruction, or the inherent aridity of the region. Reading this, I couldn’t help but reflect on how much of that legacy still shapes how we manage water today.The revised edition’s postscript added valuable context, showing how some of Reisner’s predictions have unfolded in the era of climate change and prolonged drought. I only wish this had been required reading in undergrad — it’s essential for anyone in water, policy, or environmental fields.Highly recommended.Read moreRead less
