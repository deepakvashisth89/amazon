Apart from mentioning the Burma railway, this book is not a sequel to the film, Bridge on the River Kwai. That classic film was a fact-based work of fiction but this book is a true account, based on the survivors' testimony, of what happened to two thousand allied POWs who were shipped to Japan to work as slave labour. The horrors of the Japanese POW camps are well documented in many other works and are reviewed here as well but there is humour to be found here also, although not much and hard to find. This book is as much about the human spirit and the will to survive as it is about man's inhumanity to man.Ex-US submariner Cay Blair Jr and his wife Joan worked together on this book and travelled to the UK and Australia to interview survivors. The accounts tell not only of their lives under the Japanese but also of their rescue and reception on repatriation. As a footnote, this book was made into a film directed by Andrew V.
