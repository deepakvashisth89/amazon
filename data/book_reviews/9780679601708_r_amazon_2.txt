I bought this book as a gift for my step-father. Admittedly it isn't the most compelling Civil War book. It doesn't weigh the reader down with undue troop dispositions and endless dispatches. And for that reason, it isn't as trustworthy as drier Civil War fare.That said, it is remarkably fun to read. The narrative makes you turn pages to the end and tells the fascinating story of the siege of a hilltop citadel on a Mississippi bend. Some of the most riveting parts of the book take the form of intriguing skirmishes on the river itself. Coupling that with an apt portrait of Grant as a relentless commander fighting his own high-command with the same intensity he gave the rebels made for a great read. I'd recommend this book to aficionados but it's especially for those readers not as familiar with the American Civil War.This is the funnest, briefest Foote narrative I've read.
