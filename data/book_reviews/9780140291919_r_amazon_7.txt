Having read a lot of historical fiction of the tall ships of the 1800s, I happened on this memoir of a merchant seaman, who happened to live in Newburyport, not far from where I live, and I'm planning on seeing his home next time I'm there.  This is an understated, nearly matter-of-fact account of amazing voyages.  You almost figure Tyng doesn't realize how incredible his life was.  He doesn't brag or use inflated or emotional language but merely presents the facts of his travels, telling the good and the bad.  I don't mean to say this is a flat or boring account.  Despite the tone, it is an engaging and even exciting narrative, in spite of the modest author.  Each chapter is a separate voyage, and each one is memorable.  This is a book to savor and read slowly to appreciate.  It will transport you to the age of sail.Read moreRead less
