I really love this book, which is a relief to be able to say as there seem to be many books about drawing that I don't like very much.  This book is about creativity and art. It's written mostly for older children and adults but also adults.  I used it to help me with my art work.  It doesn't concentrate only on one kind of drawing and say that is the best or only way to draw.  In fact it encourages you to draw realistically and 3 dimensionally if you would like but also imaginatively.  It is also okay to use flat styles such as those used for design and illustration, or draw symbolically and so on.  It takes you through the processes of making marks, isolating what those marks might be, by putting them into 5 elements of shape families (something I found helpful in simplifying the process of putting down on paper what you see). You are guided through doing line drawings, shaded drawings, contour work and so on. You even get to draw upside down pictures like in Betty Edward's book, (credit is given) but is a much better book in my view. This book talks about creative methods in a creative way rather than in a rigid way, while encouraging you to make the art you enjoy and that makes you happy. Other areas looked at are drawing negative spaces, how to use tone and shading and so on.I concur with the author in that there is no such thing as a perfect way to draw; art is expressive and that is encouraged in this book - make it your own.  The book guides the learner through the first tentative drawings, to planning out your own compositions and giving you solutions to the problem of what to draw (very helpful).  In the examples shown, both children and adults make great progress in their work. It covers perspective, shading, proportion and figure drawing in ways that get you to explore for yourself how these work.I love the tone of the book.  It's not a book that says oh don't worry about it looking right, it's all art anyway, no the author does want us to learn to draw and do it well. It is not pedantic though, as the important thing is not to be over critical and that there are just  different ways to do things, some which we like and find more pleasing than others.  The person to please is yourself.  If you like your picture at the end and you enjoyed making it, then it is good art. What other people think of your art is not your business (that is not an original thought, I read it somewhere).I have come back to this book after a while, and am finding it so useful at the moment in order to polish some rough edges that I have just got round to. Particularly helpful on ways to find a subject to draw and then planning how we are going to set about it.  This involves choosing the media as well as the subject, the composition type, where you might find references to use, what kind of design and so on, there are more choices than I realised to be honest, so no wonder I got confused. This part really helps to decide on what to do for your next piece of work and stop procrastinating. It's very interesting and a new way for me to work.  The figure drawing is something I am going to be working on too.  A simple seeming method, using crude shapes to build up the body is used to construct the overall figure, which to me at least makes some sense, unlike so many other systems I have seen which just left me confused.I have found everything in this book well explained and engaging.  It is an interesting read and it is also a very practical hands on book.  There are many suggestions made.  I just find it a bit different from other more step by step books out there and I like that. There is a lot of scope contained within the pages.I guess some people might not like it, as it is written primarily for children - either for adults to work through it with them or for older children to do entirely by themselves.  Perhaps that is why I enjoy it particularly. The author holds classes and adults do attend.  There is work by both adults and children in the book which is not at all patronising.  I love this book mostly because it is about the processes and the love of making art in all of its guises.  Not a book that has a pedantic approach where only perfection is good enough.  Your art making is a journey and you can make good art at all stages, it doesn't have to be technically perfect to be considered so. It's a well rounded holistic approach.
