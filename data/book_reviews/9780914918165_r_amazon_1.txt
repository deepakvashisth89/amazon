Extremely informative and detailed.  Hand is a more 'logical' astrologer and goes through some logical fallacies associated with particular astrological concepts like dignities.  But there is definitely a great deal of philosophical focus, just without what some might call 'mystical fluff.'  While he has a wealth more knowledge than I do, I tend to disagree with him in particular areas.  Regardless, he is like an encyclopedia of astrology, is a very good writer and makes good analogies to convey different astrological ideas.This book goes through a LOT: Sidereal and Western systems, the way the Zodiac works, signs, planets, houses, aspects, the 4 angles axis, nodes, rulerships, midpoints, some info on asteroids etc. etc.  The only 'cookbook' element of this book is describing the relationship of planets to each other, but its a summary for all aspects (not giving individual ones).  For example, it will give a basic relationship of Saturn/Neptune but won't specify a square vs. a trine etc.  I suppose he really is just giving a conjunction aspect because the descriptions are really just fusing any two planets together.If you aren't familiar with any of Hand's books and are more of a novice, I'd recommend getting this one first.  It's a decent introduction book, but will definitely be overwhelming at times to a beginner fresh out of the gate.  I'd say it's suited more for those who have more of an 'advanced beginner' (I know sounds like an oxymoron––meaning you're familiar with basic signs and planets) to 'moderate' astrological knowledge who wants to take it to another level and reinforce their foundation to build it up right.  I sort of put myself into the 'moderate' category, and even I still struggled to grasp particular elements of this book.  I'd say 80% of what was written I was able to grasp in a solid understanding.  I'm sure professional astrologers would find some insight as well, but this book has been extremely helpful for someone such as myself.Read moreRead less
