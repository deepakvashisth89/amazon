The Outside World is an engaging character study focusing on two Orthodox Jewish families living in Brooklyn.  Unfortunately, well-drawn though the characters are, there is not an interesting enough plot line to make this a truly compelling novel.The book starts promisingly enough, with a crisis in the life of each of the two central characters.  There is the "conversion" of Bryan/Baruch from a Modern Orthodox to an ultra-Orthodox Jew who chooses to study at an Israeli yeshiva rather than go to Columbia University (much to the horror of his father).  And there is the crisis of Tzippy, who resists almost unbearable pressure from her mother Shayna to hurry up and get married. But the story soon bogs down in an abundance of small details.  Not that much seems to happen after this beginning, and what does happen is predictable.  Probably the novel could have benefited from tighter editing.Mirvis's strength is in her characterizations.  All of her characters are memorable and drawn with some sympathy and understanding, even those who have been targeted by Mirvis's critics as less-than-ideal embodiments of Orthodox Jewish life.  Shayna, who goes off the deep end in allowing weddings to become the central focus of her life, is herself a convert to ultra-Orthodoxy, and seems driven in her manic zeal to achieve the "best" wedding ever for her daughter.  Meanwhile, her husband, Herschel, lurches from one business fiasco to another in a misplaced quest to bring to life visions dancing in his head of "vast opportunities".  But he is more than just a schlemazel, and she is more than just a monomaniac; there is a very human sadness in their desperation to fill their lives with meaning. Both Shayna and Herschel aspire to great things; in some sense they are spiritual seekers, however misplaced their quest.This novel is a good "book club" read because it focuses on the spiritual yearnings of its characters as they exist not in the world of shul and prayer, but in "the outside world".  Although it doesn't develop the issues it raises (for example, was Bryan's "conversion" a triumph or a disaster?), it does bring them to life in a meticulously realized fictional world.Read moreRead less
