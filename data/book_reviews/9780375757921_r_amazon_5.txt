One of London's masterworks, John Barleycorn is often confused with exact biography.  London was a binge drinker, not a day-in, day-out imbiber.  But in this book, written during the push for Prohibition, he offers a poetic and shocking tale of how one can become an alcoholic and the tricks it plays on one's mind.  His vision of "The Noseless One" who tricks the drinker is brilliant.  His account of growing up in a port city where men spent free time at bars matches sociological discussions of becoming socialized to a drug or alcohol.  He appreciates the special confluence of drinking with images of masculinity.  Members of AA recommend this book, and for good reason.
