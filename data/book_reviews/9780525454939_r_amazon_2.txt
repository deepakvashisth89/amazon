Readers will find themselves getting lost in the delightful, timeless drawings in Diane Goode’s Mama’s Perfect Present.  Goode has illustrated over 57 books, furthermore authoring several of those.  She has won the Caldecott Honor as well as had many of her books become New York Times Best Seller(s).  Her 1996 original, Mama’s Perfect Present, carries on the story from her 1991 original Where’s Our Mama?, and demonstrates both her undeniable skill as a multi-award-winning illustrator, as well as her creativity as a children’s book author.Mama’s Perfect Present narrates the story of two Parisian children on the hunt for the ideal birthday gift for their mother.  They spot many suitable gifts along the way, each of which are spoilt by their dog, Zaza, but decide every time that it is not the present they’re looking for.  In the climax, they come across an art supply store and decide to create a painting of their mother with all of the nice things they saw on their journey.  They give the painting to their mother, and she declares it perfect—the siblings’ hunt has come to a happy end!The tale is told in a simple, 2-3 sentence per page structure.  Each section of serif-soaked text provides clear, concise description of the story in the voice of the sister’s dialogue.  Using her initial reactions to the gift ideas—“’How about a dress? This one is sensational, and Mama loves red!’”—Goode establishes new scenes, and with the sister’s reaction to Zaza’s mischief—“’That dress was too sensational. Mama doesn’t like to show off.’”—she concludes them (6, 9).  This formula is followed up until Zaza inadvertently leads the sibling duo to their perfect gift.The writing unmistakably works in tandem with the illustrations, often revealing more than the text does.  In fact, her story begins even before the first page of text—Goode’s title page and dedication page feature drawings of the two siblings preparing to leave their house for the day.  This charming revelation is only reinforced by a depiction of Zaza the dachshund and new dog friend touching noses in a sweet epilogue on the back cover.  During the story itself, after each proposed gift idea, Zaza finds a way to unintentionally stir trouble, causing the children to move on to another gift idea.  Each of Zaza’s antics are portrayed in gorgeous 2-page spreads that give the reader full disclosure of the havoc wreaked on the streets of Paris.  Goode fills the book with soft, charming, and skillfully crafted visuals to support the characters’ whimsical world.  She even alludes to French artist Georges Seurat’s A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte, recreating the famous classic painting and including the book’s two young protagonists in the festivities.Mama’s Perfect Present, in its elegance and enchantment, provides young children and their parents the opportunity to bond over silliness and love for their families.  The sweet tale, while short, is made classic by its abundant and stunning art.  Additionally, its nod to Seurat’s famous painting cultivates the Parisian setting, allowing kids to travel to France through the adventures of the two siblings.
