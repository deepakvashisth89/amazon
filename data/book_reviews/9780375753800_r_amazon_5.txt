Humor and satire in a seriously cautionary and subversive way.  "Black No More" explores the affects of eliminating race – color, actually – from the American landscape by a medical treatment that removes pigmentation and straightens hair. Written in 1930 by a leading black conservative, the story interrogates the social, economic and cultural ramifications of the removal of visible blackness while lampooning black institutions charged with the protection and advancement of same.  Dr. Crookman is a renowned black scientist who has discover the treatment and believes that by turning black people into white people would rid America of its most controversial and all consuming problem – racism. What the doctor doesn’t foreshadow is the trickle down affect of his experiment on black business, culture and white’s perception of who they are.  Although poignant in some ways, the story is predictable in others; even still it’s an excellent tool that could help America broach the race discussion with humor and insight.
