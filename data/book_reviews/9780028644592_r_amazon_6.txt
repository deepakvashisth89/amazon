A good overview of Buddhist beliefs, history, and its different major schools. However, the book contains many minor errors that may not be important for someone looking at Buddhism for the first time. However, I felt the need to write this review so readers understand the book contains some errors.Soto Zen does not focus on the breath as the author claims (a common mistaken belief). Nor do they seek to empty the mind during meditation (they observe the thoughts in the mind). In addition to Soto Zen, the author claims "the practitioner can often 'just sit.'" That is downplaying the practice quite a bit since "just sitting" is the sole point in Soto Zen. Also, the author then makes the common mistaken view that Koans in Rinzai Zen are unanswerable. Koans do in fact have an answer.Additionally, the author writes "Now that we've looked at two schools of Buddhism, Theravada and Zen, let's look at the third, Vajrayana." Theravada is a branch of Buddhism. Mahayana is another branch of Buddhism. Zen happens to be a school of the Mahayana branch (along with many others such as Pureland). This was a very confusing statement as it makes it seem like Zen is a branch of Buddhism on the same level as Theravada and Vajrayana (Further, the whole chapter scheme is slightly confusing because instead of having a chapter on Theravada and then a longer chapter on Mahayana that includes Zen and Pureland, the author leaves these as stand alone chapters).I realize this is nitpicking. However, I found these errors solely in the small Zen chapter (Zen Buddhism is what I am most familiar with) so it is possible that similar types of errors and common misconceptions exist in the other chapters as well. This is a good intro book into Buddhism's teachings and history.
