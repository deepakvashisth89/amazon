As a practicing therapist since 1981, I've studied what Weiner calls the "Inner Dummy" since 1988. His book is sincere and interesting - and (in my opinion) misses the real answer to our "irrational" behaviors and how  to curb them.The answer seems to lie in something recently called "Inner-family Systems Theory," proposed by a variety of mental  health researchers pre-dating Carl Jung. Three readable books explain the answer to Weiner's quest:1) "Embracing Our  Selves," by psychologists Hal Stone and Sidra Winkelman; (for lay  readers);2) "Internal Family Systems Therapy," by psychologist  Dr. Richard Schwartz (Guilford, 1995; clinical - and very readable); and3) "The Plural Self," (Sage, 1999) edited by John Rowan and Mick  Cooper. There are many similar titles by serious researchers.I believe in the ideas these authors propose, for I've seen them work with a range of my therapy clients over 14 years. From my research and professional practice of inner-family therapy, my own conclusions and recommendations are now available:4) "Who's *Really* Running Your Life? - free your Self from custody and guard your kids" (Xlibris.com, second ed.. 2002)I agree with Weiner's ultimate conclusion that some form of "therapy" can often reduce our compulsions and obsessions  (including addictions). He unfortunately omits in his exhaustive summary of  therapies the one that really works directly to harmonize our "limbic  programs" (personality "subselves") under the innately wise leadership of our true Self.I applaud Weiner for raising the question, and refer you to the other books above for the *real* answer.Read moreRead less
