Tawni O'Dell's debut novel, "Back Roads", is possibly one of the greatest books I have ever read. No matter how often I return to the pages, I always find myself lost in them, drawn into a world that, despite having no real comprehension of it, I become completely involved with and caught up in.Unfortunately, despite this novel being championed by no lesser name than Oprah Winfrey, the author seemingly vanished afterwards and nothing happened. Admittedly she wasn't as well received in the UK, possibly due to the small town American way of life as depicted in "Back Road" not translating too well over here. But even I was surprised when I discovered there was a second novel.I didn't hesitate to make the purchase, but I approached the book with a combination of enthusiasm and fear. The former in the hope that this book would prove to be as good as her debut and I would be in for a great reading experience; the latter in my concern that the opposite would be true. After all, when you have something this good, the only way is down.Coal Run is the name of a small Pennsylvania town devastated by an explosion in the coal mine that employs many of the town's men. Later, it is rendered inhabitable by the fires raging in the mines, making the area unsafe for habitation. Even later, an accident at the old mine site takes away Coal Run's most famous export - the great Ivan Zoschenko, son of one of the men to die that day - a man who is about to start a potentially long and glittering football career with the Chicago Bears.As the inhabitants of Coal Run moved down the road into Centresburg to start a new life, so does Ivan. He moves a lot further, down to Florida in an attempt to run away and to forget his past as Coal Run's great hope. There he stays until a mysterious note calls him home to deal with the release from prison of a former college team mate of his, a man sentenced for beating his wife nearly to death."Coal Run" is a week in the life of the former Coal Run residents, leading up to the release from prison of Reese Raynor. It shows how the whole community can be affected by such a thing and how, many years on, they are still haunted by the loss of their former town, no matter how hard they have tried to put it behind them and get on. As with any small town, there is love and heartbreak, life and death and even a local hero can have a skeleton or two in his closet.Whilst not as immediately compelling as "Back Roads", O'Dell has again given us a slice of small town America that you can find yourself lost in. Whilst I didn't get nearly as involved in the life of Ivan Zoschenko as I did with Harvey in "Back Roads" and didn't find him as sympathetic a character, I did find myself wondering what he was about to do and, like much of the town, wondering what he would do afterwards.Once again, Tawni O'Dell has made me find familiarity in a situation and a life that has nothing to do with me. Whilst I have visited Pennsylvania, that is where any similarity between my lives and the lives of the characters end, but I still find myself walking amongst them and understanding their motivations. I can't vouch for the accuracy of O'Dell's depiction of small town America, but I can certainly confirm that it has a way of captivating you and making you feel and fear and laugh along with the people who live there.What makes this story so readable is that, even when she is writing about the darker sides of human existence, such as death and a desire for revenge, O'Dell breathes beauty into it. Some of the phrases she uses can take your breath away. There is poetry in her words and goodness in every heart, even though it can seem buried pretty deeply in some of them.In the end, my fears were groundless. Yes, "Coal Run" will always be second best in my mind to "Back Roads", but it's a fairly close thing, much closer than I feared. If you're a fan of heart warming stories about lives that are well written enough feel true, you're going to love "Coal Run". If you ever read "Back Roads" and were as enthralled as I was, you need to be enthralled again and if you like good writing, you need to read "Coal Run".This will be a book you will be able to read over and over again, possibly finding a different nuance every single time, but being equally as involved in the story on each reading.
