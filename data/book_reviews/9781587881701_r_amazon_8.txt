Well, I just finished slogging through Provinces of Night.  I think I can say that I "appreciated" the novel although I didn't really "like" it.  The characters are interesting and well developed -- although I'm not quite sure what purpose some of them served (Junior Albright, for instance).  Several things troubled me: the lack of punctuation, particularly quotation marks.  I'm a former high school English teacher, and I always told my students that they should not do things that were distracting to their readers.  The lack of punctuation was REALLY a distraction to me.  I don't want to have to re-read a passage I've already read because I discover it was dialogue and not narrative.  I was also distracted by the overuse of similes.  Often one sentence would consist of three similes; one can only take so many "likes" in one sentence.Anachronisms bother me, too.  The novel was set in 1952.  At one point, a character says he was "going where the weather suits  my clothes, as the song says."  Well, that song is "Everybody's Talkin'," and it was released in the 1960s, about 1966, and was used as the theme song for Midnight Cowboy, which came out in about 1969.  Oops!I struggled a little with plausibility, too.  Was there no adult in the life of Fleming Bloodworth, a 17-year-old boy, who could step in and give some life direction?  Seriously?  No one?Personally, I say if you want some good Southern fiction, give William Humphrey a try.  Read Home from the Hill or The Ordways and skip William Gay.
