I truly enjoyed this mystery, which is set in Scotland. This book is the fourth in a series, though it can be read as a stand-alone.Inspector Rutledge is a very interesting detective with a rather startling form of PTSD from WW1; he hears the voice of another soldier who was under his command. In an almost literal way he is haunted. Rutledge fears being labeled a madman, as many PTSD survivors were, and put away in a sanitarium. He fears that he will lose his position at Scotland Yard and be unable to do the work that is giving him a reason to live.In this story, he is investigating the disappearance of one woman when he discovers that an overlapping case involves the sweetheart of the man whose voice he hears; she is, in fact, the prime suspect. He feels honor-bound to try to ferret out the truth of her case while working on his own, but he must walk a fine line, as the local police are satisfied that she is their murderer. It's a mystery with many twists and turns and a rich texture provided by the complex character of this troubled detective.One drawback to reading the whole series is that the grim realities of WWI and the immediate postwar years are an inescapable background; the reader has to come to grips with that war in the course of reading each story, and some may find it too dark. If you can take that history and enjoy psychological complexity in your detective, these will be rewarding books.Read moreRead less
