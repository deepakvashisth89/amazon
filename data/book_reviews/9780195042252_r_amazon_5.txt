Got interested in this book after seeing the trailer of the movie Benedetta on which it is based, that will be screened in the 2021 Cannes Festival.  Seems a very well-produced movie, with the ambience of the seventeenth century.The book is written by a historian and hence it takes very good care in distinguishing what is a known or corroborated fact and what is based on the mores, customs, procedures, and other cases of the time.  It is in this last facet that the book excels, in setting its historical context for 21st century readers.  As the author states “It could be argued that imposing twentieth century notions of fiction on seventeenth century notions of reality is in itself to create a historical fiction”.The description of the trances, deceits, etc. of Benedetta are wonderfully expressed as are the investigations on the alleged visions, stigmata, marriage to Christ, etc. and put in the proper context of the beliefs of the Catholic Church at the time. Fortunately, the records of some of the investigation survive.What disappointed me was the title.  By it it appears as if the problems of Benedetta were mostly of a sexual nature, and they were not.  Yes, she engaged in a lesbian relationship with another nun, but these seems to be relatively a minor concern of the authorities, as compared to the theological problems.  The description of the sexual issues only starts in page 117 of 140 effective pages of the book (excluding transcripts and footnotes).It seems that the tile was chosen by the commercial department of the publisher to entice sales.
