So there is this genre of story, of which Egan has written a significant number of the most prominent examples, which takes an interesting and almost fringe and deeply theoretical scientific idea, and makes an engrossing human story out of it.In every case that I can think of, in order to form a novel-worthy link between the theoretical idea and the actions and feelings of the human characters, you have to significantly distort or misinterpret or oversimplify the theoretical idea, especially in math and physics.And Egan has certainly done that here; the idea that a single human thought or action or realization or whatever it is at a critical moment could have such a major impact on all of objective reality, is trippy stuff that is certainly not consistent with any plausible scientific theory I can imagine.(It is on the other hand quite consistent with more solipsistic and subjective theories, but Egan is explicitly not going there in this book.)But that by no means ruins the story for me. The human part of the story is fun and interesting and endearing, and the scientific part (however messed with it has to be to link to the human part) is still thought-provoking and interesting and well told.Also, as in most Egan stories, there are side notes about the future and technology and humanity that are noteworthy and worth thinking about all by themselves, however much or little they have to do with the overarching story.So as well as the ultimate question of the origin of the universe, we have things about biotechnology and anarchy and statelessness and media and economics and power and gender and sexuality and a bunch of stuff I'm sure I've forgotten to mention, all of them presented in an interesting and extremely competent way.(I personally think that he stumbles a bit on the love-without-sex thought; his protagonist gets it SO CLOSE, but then seems to lapse back into our current overemphasis on sex per se. That may be just my personal quirks talking there, though.)So anyway, yeah, you should almost certainly read this, if the description sounds at all like something you might like!Read moreRead less
