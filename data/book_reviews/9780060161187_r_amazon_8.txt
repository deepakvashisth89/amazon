And this time in an unlikely place—Washington DC—and while both were on vacation.When an unidentified murder victim is found near railroad tracks, an FBI friend of Joe Leaphorn calls him to ask for assistance in looking for tracks. Finding none, Leaphorn returns home, but he can’t get the “man with pointed shoes” out of his mind. His pursuit in trying to identify the killer leads him to Amtrak offices in Washington.In the meantime, Jim Chee arrests Henry Highhawk—a “want to be Navajo”—on a fugitive warrant for digging up two graves. The man works for the Smithsonian but is a bit of a militant. When he asks for a Navajo lawyer, Janet Pete steps in.Chee, who had planned to visit Mary Landon in Wisconsin ends up going to DC and meets the man he arrested.Is Highhawk connected with the murdered man, and if so, how? Eventually, Leaphorn and Chee meet up and solve a mystery that reaches farther than the Navajo Nation or the United States.This book didn’t grab me like the rest of the series. The ending seemed a little rushed, and although part of the mystery was solved, there were a few things left open. Nonetheless, I plan to continue reading the remainder of the books.Read moreRead less
