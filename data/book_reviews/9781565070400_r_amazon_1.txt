When I was 10 I found my moms book of The 15 Minute organizer by Emilie Barnes and absolutely loved Emilie's perspective of how we view projects and making them easier to handle and how God views our efforts and accomplishments. I'm 31 years old now and every now and then I still read her book. And I have noticed how much I have used many of the ideas she presented in my daily life. Bob and Emilie Barns are wonderful Authors that base their teachings on the Word of God. So YES, there are several "Bible quotes" in their books and God bless them for it! I know as a Christian I do my best to live every aspect of my life in the way God would have me to do. In their books they provide you with the appropriate scriptures to inform you (like in this particular book The 15 Minute Money Manager) how God views money, our spending, debt, a hard worker vs a lazy person and carelessness plus more. The comment of  "I doubt God put us on this earth to take care of acquired material things for Him. There must be a much higher purpose. I sure hope so" is incorrect when referring to this book. It's clear that the Author of "Is This A Financial Bible?" review completely misunderstands the point being made by Bob and Emilie Barnes. To try and put it as simply as possible; they are trying to help you see and understand that God doesn't want you to to be prideful and greedy. Two things that can very easily consume everyone and even the strongest of Christians. The book is meant to help you put your priorities in order and your values in the right place. The Barnes are letting you know that God has told us in His Word that EVERYTHING belongs to Him and warning us not to get caught up in the ways of this world. Even the money you work for or something you've made with your own two hands don't belong to you because God gave you the energy, strength, and time to do it, so it is all in His name and glory! So NO, God did not put us on earth to just "take care of acquired material things" He wants us ALL to be saved and to ultimately go to Heaven to be with Him. But in the meantime He does want us to show Him our humbleness, good stewardship, patience, wisdom, and understanding of His Word. He wants us to show Him through our actions that He is more important and appreciated. And one of the ways we can do that is in how we manage our money and possessions. For example are you putting Him first by being eager to go to church Sunday morning and joyfully giving when the offering plate comes around OR are you putting yourself and your worldly possessions first by making the choice to do something like staying at home to fix up your hot rod instead? It's not that God doesn't want you to have nice things but if you put worldly things before Him and take more pride and joy in them then you're going to have a hard time entering the Kingdom of Heaven. THAT is what they are trying to teach you in this book. As Bob Barnes explains in the introduction of The 15 Minute Money Manager "The first half of the book gives a basis for looking at money properly, and the second half offers insight into how to specifically manage your money".
