I live only three miles from the House of the Seven Gables and unlike the tourists who ask "which room did Clark sleep in?" I always realized that this dark, old dwelling by the side of the harbor down a very narrow street was connected to a novel by Nathaniel Hawthorne.  Indeed, we heard about this in grade school though we didn't read the book.  In fact, I only read the book recently, though I read "The Scarlet Letter" some years back.  I would say that the latter is a much better novel.  Growing up so close to Salem, perhaps we received the idea that Hawthorne,  our local guy, was one of the giants of American literature.  Now I'm not so sure.Hawthorne's sister lived in this house that did have a giant tree standing outside which was cut down only in the last ten years.  Hawthorne sat within on dark nights and imagined a stern, wealthy burger of the 17th century getting hold of the land by accusing the actual owner of witchcraft.  When the hapless victim went to the scaffold he cursed the usurper and his following generations.  Thus, the house proved unlucky and ghost-ridden.  The curse worked.  A priceless deed to a great tract of land in Maine disappeared.  If you read this novel you will find out what ensued.  But stay impetuous reader!  If you launch your ship on these waters, you are going to find a huge amount of excess verbiage.  The story itself is quite thin, made up for by Hawthorne's soliloquies on everything and by extremely overwritten passages supposedly declaimed by various characters.  The ending could have been penned by a Hollywood scriptwriter.
