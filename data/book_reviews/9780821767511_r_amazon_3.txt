This book sucked me in almost immediately. I'm a sucker for the "brooding but deeply honorable" Regency lord, especially when he's partnered with a lady who's strong, but struggling against difficult circumstances. Andrew and Maeve fit the bill perfectly. There is an immediate, powerful connection between them, which I loved. I instantly felt how right they were for each other. They are both very complex, rounded characters and the romance between them never feels forced or rushed. Even the side characters feel like real people, not cliched stereotypes.To be sure, there were things I didn't love. I felt the couple was too cavalier with protocol--I would have been okay with them accidentally-on-purpose meeting often in the ruined wing at night, but to actually schedule such illicit visits...how could any Regency gentleman, let alone any governess be okay with this? But overall, Act One and Act Two had me utterly enthralled.Then, unfortunately, the story had to end. And this is where things started to fall apart. Subplots (like the conflict/past history between Andrew and Clarissa's mother) get dropped. Clarissa, who was previously characterized as an intelligent girl who acts out to get attention and has a shallow streaks starts to act in out-of-character and downright foolish ways. But the biggest problem is the way that the author chooses to force the "coming clean" moment between Andrew and Maeve. Up til now, the story has been character-driven. The issues the characters face are personal and psychological, not physical. The man responsible for Maeve's plight has existed as a memory, as fear, as scars on her heart. It's this inner demon, not the physical man, that needs to be excised from her life. But in the last few pages, the author decides to bring him out as an actual character, and have him intimately involved with the climax of the novel--which unlike the rest of the book is action-y and plot-driven. It comes across as very rushed and contrived, and doesn't fit with the tone of the rest of the book.My other beef is with the way the reveal of Maeve's big secret is handled. Now, I absolutely love a heroine with a big secret. BUT only if there is a good reason why she cannot reveal this secret to the hero. Maeve's secret definitely qualified...but the author doesn't treat it that way in the reveal. In Regency England, a governess who revealed to her employer what Maeve reveals to Andrew was practically guaranteed to be dismissed at once, probably without references. Yet Andrew basically goes, "why didn't you just tell me earlier?" as if it's no big deal. Come on, he can't be THAT oblivious to societal mores and expectations.Don't get me wrong. This was still a good book and I did enjoy it greatly. (I give it three and half stars)If this was a mediocre book, I wouldn't care so much about the ending. But with a book that had such strong, interesting characters and such a solid beginning and middle, it's frustrating to see it stumble so badly in the finish.Read moreRead less
