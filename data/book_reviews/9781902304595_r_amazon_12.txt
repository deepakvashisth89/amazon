A couple of years ago I had the privilege of meeting Ivor "Blondie" Foster, at a family birthday party in Plymouth. He is the uncle of my partner and was introduced to me, a serving police officer, as a retired police sergeant. Cops being cops, we obviously found things in common to discuss. It was during this conversation that he commented about travelling to Canada to visit his 'pilot' and the realisation dawned upon me that for the first time I was was aware I was talking to a man that had flown in a Lancaster! As a child of the early sixties this was a iconic aircraft to me and the discovery of that Philip Gray had wrote this book about his experiences, including a man I had actually met elevate "Ghosts of Targets Past" to my 'must read list'.I have not been disappointed.Philip Gray covers his almost too easy approach to becoming a bomber pilot in the initial stages though the training and realisation of the dangers of his elected role to the real life drama of bombing raids over Germany. This so not a "tally Ho!" Boys own account of the war but it comes across as a genuine reflection of how young men dealt with the unreal situation in which they found themselves. The fact that the book exists means you now that the crew make it through it all but that doesn't detract from the descriptions of the raids and way that the war changed people's thinking, especially around relationships, how they develop and how they end.There was enough technical details to keep me happy but this remains a fairy easy book to read with a style that puts life into the account rather than a meticulously accurate account of the Lancaster raids, night fighting and radar guided bombing, there's enough of that to make it understandable but not to swamp the tale.It's a sobering thought that these young men went through so much to secure our freedom today, have we had it too easy?
