I wish I could thank Merle Haggard personally for writing and actually narrating this story. I have no doubt that this audio version, actually narrated by Merle himself will be a collectors prize and an invaluable historical piece in American history with a place in the Smithsonian.  The story is powerful and moving and the honesty with which Merle's mistakes and triumphs are conveyed is admirable. I am left feeling that I have been allowed access to a very private and personal story that only a trusted friend would be aware of. Many of the lessons that Merle learned the hard way, and the personal feelings, urges and passions that he dealt with during his life, are ones that I think many men in America could identify with.I am very, very sad though because this audio presentation which I feel is a invaluable gift to us all, is not available in a digital format, (audio cd or MP3). I want to try to contact the publisher or Merle to convince them to make this available in cd or to give me permission to pay the fee, which I imagine will be high, to have it converted.Thank you, Mr Haggard, and God bless you for this creation. I recently listened to your pals new autobiography in audio book, (Willie Nelson), although he didn't narrate his, and Niel Young's latest story too, which he did narrate.  Great stuff!
