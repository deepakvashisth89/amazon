This is an anthology history of Christianity of the highest academic standards, yet perfectly accessible for the lay audience it aims at.  Its historical insights are priceless. Each chapter is written by an expert in their area, but the book is sufficiently well-structured and edited to allow for a coherent and streamlined narrative. It offers for instance a helpful analysis of the struggles to keep Christianity united amidst political fragmentation and power struggles in Europe. Particularly illuminating is thus the interaction of Christianity with broader political and economic developments, in Europe and beyond. The chapter on Latin America crucially illuminates the link between religion and economic institutions and policy. As quoted in the book: “ Latin America, it has been said, can be understood only by those aware of the intellectual baggage bequeathed by Dante, St Thomas Aquinas, and Machiavelli.” A similar judgement can be made more generally. A good understanding of Christianity’s history (as offered by this book) is indispensable from understanding history in Europe and the Americas, more generally.
