This is the introductory book in the Robert Fairfax series.  I've read these a little out of order, but I have a sense of Fairfax now and his strengths and flaws.  Hannah March (a pen name for Tim Wilson) does a pretty good job with character development, although it seems to ebb and flow from book to book.  The atmospheric setting in each book is very good and I think that (plus the knowledge that Fairfax is working to overcome his flaws) keeps me coming back.  This particular book is about a young boy whom Fairfax is tutoring and how he gets himself in hot water (and prison) as a murder suspect within only a few days of arriving in London.  I thought the deceased love interest (Lucy Dove) of the youngster was exceptionally flat and could have been much better.  Also, this book does what many mysteries do and that is, namely, introduces a bunch of potential suspects to the vast confusion of the reader.
