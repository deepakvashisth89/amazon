I am reviewing this mainly for its literary merit, as I am a reader of fighting fantasy books and gamebooks rather than an AD&D player, I am also interested in a lot of AD&D books as much of their content has entered into pop culture and systems of magic, character sheets and other elements like that are referenced in other books, film, media.I really liked this book, I have no idea why there are so many negative reviews, I think perhaps that while clerics have been the most consistent character throughout AD&D history, they are perhaps also the most disliked in preference to other types, considered not to possess as much magic as a wizard, not enough weapons prowess as fighters, not enough archery, spear, dagger or throwing/projectile skills as other types like fighters, ninjas, elves etc.This book is very good in describing how to create religions, including a great number of sample religions which can involve deities, philosophies or reverence for a force (I liked the divinity of man, philosophy, and love, deity, the most), the difference between deities, philosophies or reverencing a force is described first before the specifics of religions are considered. I think that perhaps given that this was the case, and its a little of a departure from other books which describe types/ranks of the character type, then "kits" pertaining to the character, then spell casting and other weapons and non-weapon proficiency is the reason why it could be so disliked.It was quite interesting to me who was judging the book on its literary merits and I think it would be useful for anyone who was intending on spending a lot of time on a campaign with a lot of detail in deities, indeed there's discussion of creating a pantheon of deities, religions, philosophies, forces within a campaign. This seems like a great idea to me, if you are interested in in depth role playing then this would seem to be the way to go, the book does give some advice on how to simplify the process stating that it is possible to simply state if a cleric is a devote to "good" or "evil", determining the other alignments too, but that if you want to develop depth the provided samples and sheets give a good idea how to do so.Besides AD&D players, or fans of fighting fantasy or gamebook fiction, like myself, or pop culture (one time geek or nerd culture but its grown more mainstream with time) buffs, like myself, I think I would recommend this book to anyone who is a fantasy writer themselves, it can give you some ideas about world building.Read moreRead less
