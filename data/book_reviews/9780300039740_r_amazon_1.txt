In these brilliant essays, J. M. Coetzee dissects the (sometimes hidden or omitted) social, political, racial, cultural and geographical themes in the prose and poetry of white South-African writings before World War II.These in depth analyses of regionalist writing are also superb reflections on literature, colonialism, language and mankind in general.The ideology of colonialismOne of the dogmas of the West used to justify its expansive imperialism was the argument that `those who make best use of the earth deserve to inherit it'.For the Western establishment, the spectacle of the wholesale idleness of the black man was a scandal. Man had `to eat his bread in the sweat of his brow.'But, also, the sloth of the Boer and his heartlessness towards the natives betrayed fundamentally the whole imperial enterprise.Western civilization and mankindThe wandering shepherd with his meager possessions and his easily satisfied wants was seen as a threat to Western civilization and its `superficial' needs. Missionaries had to teach the black man to sell his labor. They only succeeded by holding before him the temptation of desirable articles for purchase. `Money instantly rose in his estimation.'Locations, racismThe virtues of the garden and the stability of the farm (simplicity, peace, immemorial usage) stood in sharp contrast with the vices of the city (luxury, competitiveness, easy women).Sexual unions crossing the racial lines were seen as unnatural and would yield degenerate offspring.(South-African) literatureA writer has to interpret the real in order to unveil the significations behind or within reality and give it a structure of meaning. For some writers, the order of signification was linked to a (good or bad) `moral' order.The main themes of Pauline Smith are the virtues and the vices of peasant life.Olive Schreiner depicts the farm as an entity.Sarah Gertrude Millin is obsessed by the `tragedy' of mixed blood.For C.M. Van den Heever, the unit of life is the lineage, not the individual. But, inheritance divisions are creating too small and unviable farms. His main themes are inheritance strives, unscrupulous land speculators, landless farmers drifting into the mining towns or class boundaries between the landed and the landless.These masterful reflections are a must for all those interested in South-African writing and for all lovers of world literature.Read moreRead less
