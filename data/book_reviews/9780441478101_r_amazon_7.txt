Deeply engaging, deeply compelling, and a beautiful return to Le Guin after many years. Her Earthsea books were ones I enjoyed, but ones that I maintain I read at the wrong time. It has always been my intention to return to them, but not before delving into some of her science-fiction—for which she is often lauded—and after my experience with The Left Hand of Darkness I can certainly see why.The Left Hand of Darkness is a sci-fi novel that values ideas, emotions, and conversations, over things like action, intrigue, or excitement; though it has all of those too. Le Guin is a master in voice, tone-setting, atmosphere, and she displays that here, on the alien world of Gethen (Winter, colloquially) through the Ekumen's envoy, Genly Ai. We explore a new world through his eyes. An alien world through the eyes of an alien, and when that sort of thing is done right (building a world through context, and experience) it really lands for me. The culture clash. The learning about something or someone utterly unlike yourself. And I think that's it's not just done 'right' here, but perfectly. The novel is explorative in many ways, though. Le Guin was far ahead of her time here, I think, exploring gender roles through the sexual physiology on Gethen.
