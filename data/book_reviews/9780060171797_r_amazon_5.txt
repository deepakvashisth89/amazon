First off: Kudos to Scottie Fitzgerald for not being destroyed by her parents' neglect. (Zelda, because she was mentally ill, and Scott, because he was a drunkard and preferred to live in hotels and apartments sans his daughter.) Scottie spent her youth at boarding schools and Vassar college with Fitzgerald's agent Harold Ober's family, only visiting her father occasionally. This book, by Scottie's daughter Eleanor, doesn't examine Scottie's past (except for the occasional mentions of dealing with F. Scott Fitzgerald's literary legacy) but rather focuses on her life after marrying lawyer Jack Lanahan and having 4 kids. And what a generic society life: DC parties, DC variety shows, writing the occasional magazine article, contributions to campaigns for every Democratic candidate from Stevenson to Carter. All the while dealing with a son who killed himself and a son arrested for transporting drugs, and two daughters with unhappy marriages. (All kids trying to "find themselves" via multiple degrees and pottery/art classes.) UGH. As the pages went on, I found myself despising Scottie's kids and their entire generation. Ultimately, Scottie moves to Montgomery, Alabama (her mother Zelda's home town), where she dies in 1986.But here's a quote from a Scottie letter in the book (one of many that Eleanor Lanahan provides, so it's not all a waste):"I don't quite understand how the women's libbers are ever going to get around the fact that somebody has got to take care of the very young and the very old, and in neither case can it be entirely delegated to professionals, or even entirely shared with husbands. They seem to think that if women's attitudes were only different, why the conditions would change, whereas it seems to me that the conditions remain the same and that the liberated woman only feels more frustrated by them than when she thought it was her destiny and natural life's work."It's a mildly interesting book, primarily for sociological reasons: How a woman with a famous pedigree and her privileged kids turned out. Scottie had her DC society life, divorced twice, died at 64; her kids...just as crummy and/or bland as the rest of us.What this book does is put an end to the F.
