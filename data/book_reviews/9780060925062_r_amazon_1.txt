I have often said that this book, In My Mother's House, is the best book I've ever read. I've loaned it only when I had received sufficient assurance that it would be returned to me.  This year I grabbed it as we left for vacation. I had just seen Dick Cavett's interview in the NYT's Review of Books in which he said "Don't read new books. Reread those that you read long ago and you'll see them as if for the first time."When we arrived at our resort, I picked up my 30-year old paperback book and realized it was falling apart. The cheap paper had turned brown and brittle. I went on Amazon and found I could download a digital copy for $2.99,so I did. What I found was not only the original book, but also its author's update written in 2002.  This was a wonderful surprise, one I would not have discovered unless I read the book all the way to its conclusion.I'm not Jewish, nor am I a radical. I lived through the era of Rose Chernin without ever having heard of her. But I love the story because it is so alive and the personalities are so vividly portrayed.
