1) Plot (3 stars) - With dreary communism descending like an unwanted fog over Czechoslovakia, a man takes a mistress, then another mistress, and then that mistress takes another lover, and so on.  This is more a novel of themes and characters than of any particular goal or destination the actions are building toward.2) Characters (5 stars) - Tomas it the intellectual womanizer.  Tereza is the innocent villager he sweeps up--fragile, romantic, imaginative.  Sabina is the mistress, confident and sexual, testing the boundaries of power and roles.  Franz is Sabrina's other lover, the academic who loves the idea of revolution, and so loves Sabina because her Czech-ness represents that, even though she could care less.  All these characters were unusual, all were deep, all were drawn and dissected fascinatingly.3) Theme (5 stars) - This is a story about relationships.  About love and sensuality and sex and faithfulness and jealousy and betrayal and misunderstanding.  It's about how we all see the world differently, yet must interact with each other.  But this is also a story about invaders and the oppressed.  About how you respond, and how they respond, and what that means.4) Voice (5 stars) - Kundera clinically dissects and investigates the psyches of his characters, but he is not cold and sterile in his dissections.  He is warm, caring, compassionate.  In this way he reminds me of a father to his grown children of characters.  He knows them well, is curious about their decisions, gives them their distance, but in the end always roots for them to come out okay.  This is an unusual approach towards characters and story, and I found it brilliant and engaging.5) Setting (4 stars) - Czechoslovakia in 1968 during "Prague Spring" is a fascinating time and place to stage a novel--how a city responds to a bloodless invasion, and the farce of the police state the follows.6) Overall (5 stars) - The Unbearable Lightness of Being is a beautiful piece of art, and Kundera is a philosopher poet.
