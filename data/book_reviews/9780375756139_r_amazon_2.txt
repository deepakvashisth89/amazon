One of the earliest compilations of movie history, this book serves motion picture buffs on several levels. First, it gives a view of films as art in the silent era. Secondly one sees film techniques thought innovative fifty years later. Finally, the people involved were the author's contemporaries, allowing a view both compelling and provocative.By 1915 motion pictures were maturing. To glean an understanding of their development is essential if one is a student of their history. This book adds to the realization that already many in the industry were making sophisticated productions captivating eager audiences.At the same time, one would be remiss not to inform the reader that the archaic style of this book may be troubling.
