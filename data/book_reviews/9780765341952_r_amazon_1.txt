This book is what you get when someone reads Asimov and says "Those are some interesting ideas but crappy execution, I could do it better" and then goes ahead and does exactly that.Asimov's big ideas are all here.  Fairly severe flaws in those big ideas, that Asimov apparently didn't notice, are also here; that's what drives the plot - having the Grand Galactic Plan suddenly be derailed for reasons nobody foresaw but in retrospect are totally obvious and apparently insoluble.  The characters are fully fleshed out, believable, and for the most part relatable.  The action is solid.  The sheer scale of a galactic civilization is portrayed very well, with sketches of the multitude of details of countless star-system civilizations, in a way that Asimov never bothered with.  The technology and its impact on human minds is completely believable, and especially given the impact of smartphones and social media today it allows this book to serve one of science fiction's primary purposes: to extrapolate a trend and warn of possible consequences.  The high level informational mathematics is plausible enough that in discussing this with some physics and mathematics PhDs I could not find one who would say "that can't work".This is one of the best Big Ideas novels ever written.  I highly recommend it.  I recommend it MORE than the Foundation novels themselves - you might enjoy it more if you've read those, but you don't need to; everything you need is here.Read moreRead less
