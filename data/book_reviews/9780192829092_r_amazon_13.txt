ObsessionPlato was obsessed with oligarchic power. 'Republic' is essentially an outspoken prescription for oligarchic rulers on how to keep the strings of power in their hands at all costs. The top rulers had to be chosen from the (very few) philosophers (like him). Philosophers 'are those who are capable of apprehending that which is permanent and unvarying'.Selective breedingIn order to create the perfect community, one has 'to maximize the potential of the flock. Sexual partners should be paired by the rulers. Only the offspring of the 'good' ones should be brought up. The pairing should be hidden by a subtle lottery! In order 'to avoid increasing or decreasing the community's size, the children  of 'worse' parents and handicapped children have to be put away in some secret and secluded spot ...No trust in humanityThe way the population is treated in his 'Republic' is appallingly inhuman. In order to guarantee the unity of the ruling class and its guardians, 'all the women are to be shared among all the men. The children are also to be shared, with no parent knowing which child is his, and no child knowing his parent. They'll own nothing except their bodies. Consequently, they'll be free of all conflict that arises when people have money, children, and relatives.For the children, the rulers shouldn't provide education but indoctrination; for the guardians, the auxiliaries and the workers, they should provide social conditioning. Overall censorship (no poetry which 'represents things as they appear to be, not as they are') should be installed.Xenophon's comment in 'Hellenica' 'For I know that in Persia everybody except one man is educated to be a slave rather than stand up for himself', must have been an absolute abomination for Plato: nobody should stand up for himself in his Brave New World.Socrates and the democratic beastFor Plato, 'democracy starts when the poor members of the community are victorious. They kill some of the rich, they expel others, and they give everyone who's left equal social and political rights.'Xenophon in 'Conversations of Socrates': 'Where offices were filled by men who satisfied the legal requirements, he considered the constitution to be an aristocracy; where they were filled in accordance with a property qualification, a plutocracy; where they were filled by anybody, a democracy.'Socrates was attacked by the democrats of Piraeus (Anytus), condemned and killed for preaching against democracy and for promoting oligarchic rule, not individualism.The real face of the oligarchic beastAfter the defeat of Athens in 403 BC, we can read in Xenophon's 'A History of My Times': The Thirty oligarchs (with two uncles of Plato) 'went on a killing spree, murdering all democratic opponents, in casu, more Athenians than all the Peloponnesians did in ten years of war. They confiscated illegally the property of resident aliens and when people could vote, it was in full view.'A major political text defending tooth and nail a bloody tyranny.N.B.
