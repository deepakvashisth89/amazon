Psychological study of the Stockholm syndrome of the white heroine and psychopathic personality of the Native Indian hero set in the realities of colonization of America in the 18th century.I have a problem with the rating of this book.While reading the first few chapters, I excitedly thought to myself, WOW! finally something more realistic, something other than a sweet fairy tale about a powerful, handsome Indian and a beautiful, strong and stubborn white woman falling in love with each other, and the power of their love overcomes all obstacles associated with the differences of their cultures cultures and then they ride toward the setting sun holding their hands.And then, after when heroine was captured  my struggle begins.The hero's psychopathic obsession with the heroine. His violence and cruelty excused by his obligations to his people. He says to himself (and only himself!) that he loves her, but not hard enough as to quit the very cruel, bloody public punishment. He almost beats her to death only because he has to show his strength and power in front of his people. I couldn't stand it…Also, it was very annoying to read the repeated patterns every few pages. Hate, sex, love, disobedience, punishment, cruelty, hate, sex, love and so on, over and over againA sequence of behaviors and feelings that are often mutually exclusive.These are two sentences, exactly one after the other:“she will come to understand she can be happy if she comes to me of her own free will. There is no choice but to accept me and all my ways”Free will = no choice??? Really??? Come on!Hero excuses that heroine herself is to blame for the cruelty she ignites in him with her inappropriate behavior ... How well do I know this scheme, from reality and from scientific studies on psychopathic personalities and their victims.But despite all this things that annoyed me about this book, I couldn't stop reading. There is some kind of magnetism in the way of writing that will not let me put it down. Several times I had to stop reading for a while and think about what I had just read. However, each time I reached for the book again.Maybe because I have been interested in the history of the American Indians for a long time and I know that what I read in this book is certainly closer to the truth than all the fairy tales of love and devotion in other historical Native American romances.I have sworn several times that this is definitely the first and last book in this series which I’m going to read.And guess what? I read it to the end and just started reading the second in this series.3,5 stars rounded to 4.Read moreRead less
