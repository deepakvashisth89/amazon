I haven't read a Martha Grimes book in quite a while, and I forgot what a solidly good writer she is, and how enjoyable are her Inspector Jury mysteries.  This one has all the requisite characters -- Wiggins the stalwart assistant, Jury's ding-a-ling supervisor, and Melrose Plant, the aristocratic amateur sleuth; plus all the village characters that constitute a bevy of suspects.  We have the brutal murder on the night of a costume party; we have the isolated village on the North Sea in winter that's a backdrop to Jury's  contemplative personality, and the courageous young boy and his dog.  Many have criticized Grimes for dwelling on the plight of children, as well as their precociousness, in her novels, but I don't happen to find this objectionable at all.  Whether it's realistic or not is not as important as whether her use of children is entertaining and adds to the plot.  In this book, young Bertie Makepeace and his loyal dog Arnold definitely are an asset.  We care about them and worry about them.  We love Jury for caring also.
