The story is set in the 1930s, in the days of prohibition, the New Deal, WPA, and stay-home wives. In it we meet good people, whose acquaintance enriches our lives.I love Sooner. Born out-of-wedlock (hence her name, “Sooner”), raised without affection by a vile, bible-quoting, whisky-making old woman, she learns love through her relationships with the wild creatures. She has two precious pets, a crippled redwing that perches on her shoulder, and a chipmunk she carries in her pocket. She has deep affection for William, the old woman’s feeble old mule, who only she can manage.Sooner has a tender heart for all wild creatures and receives vicious whippings for releasing the critters the old woman catches on her trap line. Sooner has never known human love until she attracts the attention of Mac, the town veterinarian, and Phil, the sheriff.Mac, his wife Elizabeth, and Phil and his long-time girlfriend Selma have flaws. There’s some cussing—and Elizabeth doesn’t respond to Sooner’s love and neediness the way you want her to. But the story has a satisfying conclusion for all the people you care about.The sex scenes struck me as offensive, until I considered that—unlike modern novels—they occur within a loving marriage.What a pity that such a book has long  been out of print!Read moreRead less
