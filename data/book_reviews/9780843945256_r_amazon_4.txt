Some nightmares don’t feel like dreams. The images are too foul, too alien, too specific. It feels like an invasion or infection from outside your consciousness. A tendril of awareness from a serial killer or a Lovecraftian cosmic horror, who the hell knows? Then you wake up. Mercifully, all that’s left is a fading palimpsest of images. These images are horrifying enough. Mercifully, you forget the main narrative. John Shirley’s “Wet Bones” lacks that tender mercy. He remembers (and relates) the dream in cruel detail. Specific as it is, the horror lacks the twisted titillation of most splatter fiction. (His story’s about entities, human and/or inhuman, that get off on the shock of a masturbatory horrorshow.) Shirley’s horror is bloody horrible — period. He draws you into the nightmare with compassion, empathy and identification at the casualties of unspeakable evil.
