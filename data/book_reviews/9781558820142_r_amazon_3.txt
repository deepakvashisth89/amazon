My professor of English History as an undergraduate recommended this book as not only an excellent detective story by a master of the art, but probably solving the case of the murder of a Protestant judge during the reign of Charles II and the wild days of Titus Oates and the "Popish Plot." With the end of Cromwell's Commonwealth, the Restored Monarch had a Protestant Parliament fearful of Catholic plots, even as the parliamentary leaders secretly undercut Charles in his attempts to defend England from the Catholic Monarch in France. Who really killed Sir Edmund Godfrey is less important than how his death was used as an excuse for the judicial murder of many apparently loyal Catholic peers. In the run-up to the overthrow of James II and the "Glorious Revolution" that was the source of some of our own constitutional history (e.g., Locke's Two Treatises), this book shows us that political infighting and cruelty based on differences of religion and fear of "the other" are nothing new, and abuses of power then should give us pause as we look at abuses of power and demonizing of those who "are different" in our own era.Read moreRead less
