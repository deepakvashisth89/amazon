I enjoyed reading this book. It adds a new angle and dimension to our knowledge of the overland expeditions of Sir John Franklin, as well as telling the life story of Back.I've knocked one star off because for me the author didn't quite bring Back to life as much as I'd have liked. I'd always wondered how it was that Back seemed to rub people up the wrong way, including Franklin himself, and I'd hoped to get more of an insight into this. Sadly, the author simply repeats what previous writers have said on the matter without adding any further detail. Perhaps there simply isn't any further detail, but I was disappointed.Another star was knocked off because the author seemed to be too easily swayed by earlier critics, people with an axe to grind against British explorers in North America. This led to some contradictory moments where after praising Back and Franklin he tossed in criticisms at odds with his own findings.For example on he P277 refers to Back's 'Terror debacle', whereas on p272 in a more reasoned summing up he called the voyage 'a triumph of coping with overwhelming odds'.And although he is generally positive about Franklin, he can't let go of the old canards trotted out by his detractors. Not long after describing the almost flawlessly planned and executed second overland expedition, which Franklin's detractors usually completely and conveniently ignore, he writes 'Whatever one may think of Franklin's method - or lack of it...' (p196).But overall, a good read and a welcome addition to the body of literature concerning Arctic exploration.Read moreRead less
