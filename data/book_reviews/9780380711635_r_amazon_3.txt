For some reason I'd overlooked this book by T. R. Pearson after buying and reading his first three books, A Short History of a Small Place, Off For the Sweet Hereafter, and the The Last of How it Was, all of which were highly entertaining.  Pearson was born in Winston Salem, NC and these four books take place in NC.  The same characters more or less inhabit all four books although not all are featured players in each book.  Pearson is a master at humor and it sneaks up on you.  He'll be laying out a situation and suddenly you realize he's set up his reader for some serious laughing.  This occurred several times in Call and Response, I'd begin laughing until tears blinded me, I'd finally manage to read another sentence which made the situation even funnier, more tears, another sentence, even funnier.  What a delight!  Nearly each character's name will be written in full, i.e. Mr. Phillip J. King, Mrs. Phillip J. King, the widow Mrs. Askew, Miss Bernice Fay Frazier and her sister Mrs. Estelle Singletary and Mrs. Estelle Singletary's husband, Mr. Estelle Singletary whose name was actually Eugene, Miss Mary Alice Celestine Lefler from Berkeley Springs, West Virginia, who had a thing about her with the hair, the teeth and the fingerends that touched forearms, and mesmerized widowers Mr. Nestor Tudor, Mr. Dick Atwater along with Mr. Tiny Aaron who had never been married.  There is a masterfully written chapter about Mr. Tiny Aaron's snake troubles.  It enters his house from the crawl space up beside a vent pipe and after Mr. Tiny Aaron sees it in his living room and later spies it going under the closet door anyone could identify with the emotions it unleashed as he tried to deal with it, first with a kitchen sponge mop and later a more formidable weapon.
