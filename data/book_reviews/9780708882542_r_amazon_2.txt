If you enjoyed 'Lord of the Rings' then give this novel a try. The dialogue is typically hammy but often in a good way. Incidental characters have 'canon fodder' all but tattoed on their foreheads but the journey itself is worth the ride. Sometimes Rohan's descriptive language can reach lyrical heights. I admit being fond of this trilogy because as an 80s teenager I had to wait for books two and three to be released, to finish the story (this is book two). However, compared to some of the tosh that Amazon Prime offers as free fantasy books of the month, this distinguishes itself by virtue of not assuming we're all shambling zombies who need artificial stimuli just to pull our pants on each morning. The Forge in the Forest offers a sense of strange minds and nature itself struggling to accept that humans deserve a place in this world. I suspect many of us may sympathize with such a notion. It'll definitely take your mind off the morning commute, with it's grumpy faces and banal oafishness. And if you do enjoy The Forge in the Forest, then you'll definitely want to catch the trilogy's finale: The Hammer of the Sun. To offer a (tenuous) cinematic analogy, the atmosphere generated in the pages of this novel feels like watching sci-fi flick 'Pandorum' (2009).
