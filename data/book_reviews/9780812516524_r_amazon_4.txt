My main complaint about some of these stories is that they are sometimes too vague. The point of the stories seems to be more about the overall vibe, one of uncertainty, crafted to create a feeling of dread in the reader. Campbell’s prose no doubt succeeds in creating a disorienting atmosphere.While these stories often achieve that, some of them were so ambiguous I simply said “huh?”But with that out of the way, when these stories hit, they really hit. The Chimney, The Man in the Underpass, and Down There are fantastic tales of supernatural.Also noteworthy is how many of the short tales—particularly Conversion and Heading Home—end in such an impactful way. These tales masterfully hint at their conclusions before the end, causing to me to go back to the beginning and see the clues that were hidden right before my eyes. A little bit of literary magic.The ideas here, one example being In the Bag, are brutally creative.Overall, despite some being a bit too unresolved for me, there’s no doubt the awards these stories were given are well-earned. This is horror that’s been elevated from simply being scary to actually creating a haunting narrative.
