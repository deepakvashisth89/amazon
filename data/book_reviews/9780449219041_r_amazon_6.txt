I've just re-ordered this after my last copy (library clearance, 10p) came apart.  It's one of those books that I keep going back to if I need some undemanding comfort reading, but still want something above the level of escapist pap.It's a tale of everyday moneyed or artistic, or would be moneyed or artistic, folk living a privileged 'lifestyle' around one of Cape Cod's many 'ponds'.  Smashing.  I should be so lucky.  Living by the waterside, doing your own thing, growing veggies in between earning your living in the way you've chosen?  Lemme at it - I could put up with the odd winter storm and ecological limitations.  Bliss.  So why then are so many of these guys so unsatisfied, selfish, snobbish, shallow and screwed up?  Because otherwise there wouldn't be a story, would there?Some characters you'll identify with, others you'll want to kick in the teeth and scream at. (I won't spoil.)  But it's a fat, rich read, with decent writing despite one or two lapses, such as when as a waning relationship is summed up as the 'sizzle had gone out of the zing' or should that be the 'zing had gone out of the sizzle'?  Ouch either way.  But I quibble; on the whole it's satisfying, though with a rather saccharine 'feelgood' ending but what the hell.  After all that's gone on you feel the guys deserve it.I don't care for every one of Ms Piercy's novels; my favourite will always be 'Woman on the Edge of Time' despite the badly edited and produced Women's Press editions, but I reckon 'Summer People', though very different, is a close second.Read moreRead less
