Don't expect a great story here, but rather a firework of elaborate thinking processes  expressing the random contents of Saul Bellow's mind, a dazzling talker indeed. Sharply drawn eccentric characters make for a satisfying reading, but the story itself - the demise and unglorious death of a great author who could not parlay his genius into fame and money - hardly manages to produce a real pageturner.I can understand why this glorious soliloquy  about the decay of American values led to a Nobel prize. The beauty of the language explains it all.But fifty years later, the modern reader might want a more carefully crafted intrigue, a real novel creating that kind of craving anticipation - "suspension of disbelief" - that we now all expect from a literary work.Read moreRead less
