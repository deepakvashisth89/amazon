In his fourth Phillip Marlow book, Chandler rebounds from his disjointed, low energy third novel, The High Window. The Lady In The Lake is a more intricate mystery, sometimes bordering on contrived, and has the pacing of Chandler’s first two novels. Marlowe is back to his witty, cynical form, with some added introspection, which covers for some excessiveness with analogues that flirt with tedium at times. While the plot is very interesting, the characters are either a bit bland or not fully developed. The one strong woman character who is not killed ends up having nothing to do with the mystery, which feels like a cheat. The denouement is too long, and the resolution is anticlimactic.
