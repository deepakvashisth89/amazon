In "Dreams of My Father," Barack Obama writes with such cinematic clarity, with such feeling and conciseness that it borders on the poetic!  This is more than simply a coming-of-age story; it is more than a mere search for an absent father or a reconciliation of identity.  "Dreams" is, in essence, the story of what it means to be an American and it speaks to just anyone of any race!Obama's story is of course very well-known:  The product of an African father (who returned to his native Kenya shortly before the Obama's birth) and a White American mother, Obama was supremely blessed not only with an attentive, inquisitive, ambitious and passionate parent,  but also doting maternal grandparents who greatly helped inform and expand on his view of the world.  To be sure, he conveys a deeply-felt appreciation of his mother and grandparents, although he acknowledges their limitations.  Having grown up in Hawaii--where issues of class and race were less pronounced than on the mainland, Obama was eventually prompted to wade into the thick of politics--first upon arrival for college in the Pacific West (University divestment in South Africa was all the rage then, in the early to mid-eighties), then by memorable stints in New York City, the South Side of Chicago, Harvard Law School (where he became the first African American editor of the Harvard Law Review) and, finally, back in Chicago (where he eventually became only the second Black senator since Reconstruction)."Dreams" ends with a very detailed and lengthy description of Obama's journey back to his father's native Kenya, where he meets a colorful--and seemingly endless--multitude of paternal relatives. It is finally this  Kenyan clan that fills him in on his ancestry, thus allowing him some answers to the many questions surrounding his missing father.  All told, Obama's "outsider" status appears to have helped rather than hindered his odyssey--despite the angst during different stages of his life.  By the end of "Dreams," we feel as if we know Obama more, but he never completely tips his hand:  he remains somewhat aloof, mysterious and intriguing.  Oddly enough, this may well in part explain his near-universal appeal. Of course one of the most delicious pleasures of reading this book right now is that we know how his story will turn out! "Dreams of My Father" surely ranks among the very best autobiographies (no less by a sitting President). On a scale of one-to-five, I'd give a ten!Read moreRead less
