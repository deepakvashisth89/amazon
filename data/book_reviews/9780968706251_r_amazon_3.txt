The true lover of history can understand the maddening frustration of knowing something compelling existed once so very close nearby, but with the passage of time is now so far, far away, never to be touched or seen  again. So it is with this saga of the mysterious Camp X. The author abley  goes beyond the mere reference to "...a man called Intrepid", and  adds flesh and bones, and breaths life into the ghosts of a secret  establishment that is credited with shortening the war in Europe by some  six months. To anyone even remotely interested in the secret machinations  of warfare, this is must reading. Purveyors of agent 007 need look no  further than this compelling book to see the real stuff of "...quiet  warfare"---how it was developed, how it was trained, and how it was  executed, in all its unheralded successes, and equally muted failures...Read moreRead less
