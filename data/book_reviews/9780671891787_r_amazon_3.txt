The author discusses many types of cats in this excellent book, but the most fascinating parts are her accounts of doing research in an area where lions and humans had lived side by side for generations. Both the lions and the humans developed rules for dealing with each other. If lions came to the edge of the human settlements, the humans asked them, firmly and clearly, to go--and the lions left! In return, the humans didn't shoot lions, or attack them in any way. Two of the world's most dangerous predators, living together in peace. It was amazing to read. The author discusses how and why the rules worked, going as far as she can into cats' thought processes and having a healthy respect for things she's observed but can't explain.She has a thought-provoking section on interspecies communication, and points out that humans may take the upper hand in communicating with other species not because we're better at understanding other species, but because we're worse. It's easier to ignore a request when you don't know what the other creature is requesting. Think about that next time you're negotiating with your cat--would it be as easy to take the "I'm the human, you're the cat, my rules win" standpoint if you understood your cat as well as (some cats, at least) understand you?The author's respect, compassion, and admiration for cats of all kinds shines through this book and makes it a wonderful read for anyone who loves animals.Read moreRead less
