This book shows that science is much more than a collection of facts that point to a simple answer.  The author uses a diversity of subject matter to illustrate that the quest for scientific knowledge can be hindered by cultural, political, and personal ideals, but that the self-correcting mechanism of science will eventually prevail.  From the formulation of Darwin's ideas to the controversial claim that race plays a factor in athletic ability, the author shows how scientific ideas are constructed and either built upon or rejected.The information contained in this book makes for a very interesting read, but used as the tool it was written to be, it can show rational thinkers how to be more aware of their own biases that may cloud their thinking.Read moreRead less
