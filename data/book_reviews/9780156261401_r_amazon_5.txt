I looked forward to reading Mark Helprin's work which came highly recommended by friends who cooed about his lush romanticism, but I found this early effort uneven. "Dove of the East" is made up of stories he wrote in the 1970s.Helprin's language has a poeticism and rhythm that is sometimes almost incantatory. It has moments of beauty but reading story after story it became predictable and monotonous, at times almost ripe for parody. Most of the stories are also told from a distant, omniscient point of view that for me made the characters seem like symbols or abstractions rather than flesh and blood people.The one selection that touched me on an emotional level was the title story, "A Dove of the East." A Holocaust-related story, it effectively conveyed the trauma of losing all of one's loved ones, of being utterly alone and shorn of identity. It's quite a devastating piece but is heavy-handed with its symbols (the said dove) and contains wildly uneven prose. How is it that a person can write an elegant sentence like this:"The weather was dry and cool, and wavelike winds moved the trees and grass like breathing as they lay together for the first time in the diminishing October sun, its rays weakening into a cool sigh."And in the same story, write clunkers like this (about livestock)?"As many steers as they had could not be moved spontaneously and freely - it was hardly a place for Isadora Duncan - for various reasons."Editor!
