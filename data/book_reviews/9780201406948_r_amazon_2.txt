This was a huge disappointment. I fell for the "Brazelton" name and thought it would be a great way to prepare my child for a check-up. It is a great idea in theory (photographs of a check up and letter to the parents on preparing your child) but poorly executed book which I will not be reading to my child. It is extremely wordy for a 3-4 year old  but more importantly, the choice of words are really poor. Instead of making a child feel good I imagine it would only increase anxiety. I feel badly being so critical as I have found several of the Brazelton parent books to be quite helpful, but on the other hand, I don't want others to make the same mistake. The other reviews were stellar which is why I feel compelled to put my two cents in so at the very least people can make an informed decision. Some examples of sentences I did not find helpful:1."Many children worry about the shape of their bodies and whether they are too fat or too thin." Really? Is that necessary?2."It can be fixed by an awfully easy operation". Why use the word awful here? Yes, I know it is doesn't mean terrible in this context but kids' association with this word is not a good one and in my opinion it defeats the purpose of making it sound simple and easy. Just an example of the poor editing choices made in this book.My biggest issue, however, is  with the child's illustrations accompanying the explanations.This surprised me because in theory I would think this would be a lovely addition, but in fact, several of the child's interpretation's are quite scary looking and would only serve to frighten a child of age 4-6 in my estimation. For instance, the drawing of the doctor checking reflexes with a tap on the knee, looks like someone is swinging a large hatchet over the patient who is tied to the bed. Unlike the choice of words which you can skip or alter as you read to a young child, the drawings are hard to ignore. Suffice to say, I would not recommend this book for children. Especially not children ages 3-6 which is the intended audience according to the description. It may be helpful for a much older child depending on the kid but I would check it out at your local library before purchasing it.  There are several other drawings in the book that are also disturbing which I don't have time to get into.If you are looking to prepare a pre-school/kindergarten child for a check up with a book that normalizes the experience and is apropriately emotionally responsive, I recently found "It's Time For Your Checkup" by Shani Thornton.  It's not perfect, but it's a much better read than this one and achieves the goal that I think Brazelton intended.Read moreRead less
