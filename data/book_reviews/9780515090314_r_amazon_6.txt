E. R “Rick” Braithwaite has penned an eloquent autobiography about his first year teaching young teens in London’s tough East End, where kids had long been given up on due to their lack of class, privilege and manners. Rick understands this social classification. He grew up in the British colony of Guyana and excelled in the British school system to become a respected industrial engineer who served in the RAF. After the war, however, he came face to face with the ugly side of British society — a black man who faced extreme prejudice. Unable to find a job in the industry he had prepared for, he had to settle for the only job he was offered, a schoolteacher for England’s poor white youth.
