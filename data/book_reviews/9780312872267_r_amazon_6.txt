Here we have one the later books in the Recluse series, which chronologically should be considered the first.  Many things and events mentioned in earlier volumes are discussed or explained.  It is a good, typical Recluse type book.  There are many plots and subplots throughout.  And as usual, Mr. Modesitt imparts much philosophy and wisdom.It is a time of transition for the Magi'i and the Kingdom of Cyador.  Barbarians are continually attacking in the north and in such numbers that Fire lances and dedicated soldiers can barely hold them back. Now the lances are running out of charges due to failing chaos towers.  In the east, the magical Accursed Forrest challenges to breakout of its walls and again rule the land.Into this bleak world, Lorn is noted as too good of a chaos mage and as insufficiently dedicated to the Magi'i world.  He is forced into the Lancers, where it is hoped he will be killed fighting the Barbarians.  However, he does so well there, he is transferred to the east to contain the forrest, where again, it is hoped he will fail and be killed.  Why?  It could be made a little easier to grasp, but the reasons eventually come out.  This, it appears, is the main purpose of this volume, along with describing the failure of the chaos towers.The Magi'i of Cyador is a 2 volume story.  I do not consider this volume to be on a par with the other Recluse books.  Still, it is a worthwhile and entertaining story.  A solid 3.5 stars for me.Read moreRead less
