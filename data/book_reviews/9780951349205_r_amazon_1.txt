I have bought this book several times, for myself and as gifts, particularly for young people. It is not just a book it is a movement, a campaigning organisation of children, teachers, students and officials confronting racism.I love this book, it’s not just a magical adventure but a fabulous and safe way of introducing the difficult and ugly issues of racism and prejudice with children from 0 to 100!The issues raised by this book are as relevant, or even more relevant, today in a world of Trump and Brexit, as it was in the 80s when it was written. The book does not preach, it leaves you to make up your own mind and tells the history of the British in India from a different angle which you rarely see. The project arising from the book gives space for reflection on the part of the perpetrators, those who have racist views, and have acted on them, they are given space to consider why, and what a different approach may mean. Children have gone home after reading this book at school and confronted their parents about their racist views.The Heartstone Odyssey has become the centre of a national project challenging prejudice and bigotry with a dedicated group of people who want to see the ‘mouse world’ brought to life. A group of ordinary people who loved the book, wanted to see it in print and helped to make Allied Mouse, the publishers, take shape to produce it. Without that, it would not have happened. It led to the formation of Heartstone, the non-profit organisation, whose existence is to make sure the book continues to be read as far and as widely as possible. We are all still waiting to hear the end of the story.I’ve read the comments about the author’s pseudonym. The name was the suggestion of Indians as a fitting reflection of the author’s role. Mr Horse Messenger, riding across the world to spread this story.This is not a case of cultural misappropriation, but a case of combined ownership and authorship; this story is not about the author, it is about a magical network of mice fighting racism and discrimination.Chandra is strong and independent and does not give up her goals despite being a victim of racism. She is an empowering role model for all girls and young women and particularly those of Asian origin where you do not see Asian heroines in books. Groups of young women have been reading this in Indonesia, and this is one of the things they love most about it.Let there be no doubt, I am part of this campaign against racism, prejudice and bigotry and will be until my dying breath.Please read it, share it and discuss this book.Read moreRead less
