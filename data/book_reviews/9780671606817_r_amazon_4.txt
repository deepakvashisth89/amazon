Bair's bio complements de Beauvoir's autobiographies, but fails to rise to the level of its subject. I read "Tete-a-Tete" by Hazel Rowley before picking up this book.  Rowley's book is very well written and well researched, and her understanding of Beauvoir and her relationship with Sartre is much more objective and culturally aware.  Nevertheless, by trying to be complete, Bair adds interesting details, such identifying people to whom Beauvoir gives code names.  Like a journalist, she sometimes latches on to simplified interpretations of this complex woman, and goes too far in trying to support them. Her editing is sometimes careless.
