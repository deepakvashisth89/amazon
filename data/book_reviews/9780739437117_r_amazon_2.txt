I'm struggling to figure out a way to make it so that this review doesn't sound completely hokey.  Well, first, it's horror.  Second, it's written quite well, though I'd like to have a talk with Little's editor about silly mistakes.  Third, it's about insurance.  Yes, the type you buy so that you can stay safe.  I know!  It sounds utterly ridiculous, but this book actually turned out to be pretty flippin' decent.Our protagonist is named Hunt Jackson.  The first pages of the story report that he is recently divorced and enjoying a new found freedom he hasn't had in many years due to the restraints of marriage and the bickering between himself and his wife.  Free of these ties, he decides to move back to his home town of Tucson, AZ.  There he meets up with old friends, Joel in particular and his wife Stacy, makes new friends, Jorge and Edward, and meets a new love interest, Beth.  However, the crux of the story is not centered around his eventual restarting of his life, but rather about keeping his life.He is offered insurance for his car, his rental, and eventually everything.  A very unnerving insurance agent frequently visits him and the people he knows in town.  When these individuals do not take the insurance, whatever the agent was prospecting to insure goes wrong.  So, for example, dental insurance.  If you don't take it you never know what might happen to your teeth!  And then something does happen to your teeth and you're sent to some hokey doctor who does a shoddy job and you're stuck being funky looking.  You can see how it gets hairy when life insurance is offered or injury insurance or whatever!It's definitely a spooky tale.  Complete with a touch of traditional, haunt-horror where, out of the corner of your eye, you spot something in the shadows, or something isn't quite right about your home.  It's not your traditional story, of course, as it centers around insurance, but the ideas behind it are, therefore, refreshing.Perhaps my only gripe is that the ending is a bit lackluster.  Of course, it's not an action-based story, so don't expect an action-based ending.  Hell, Bentley Little even calls attention to the fact that the show-down between humans and insurance agents was never meant to be intense.
