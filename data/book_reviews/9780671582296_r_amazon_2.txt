BLOODSTREAM [1998] By Tess GerritsenMy Review Four Stars****This was Gerritsen’s third medical thriller which followed the bestseller LIFE SUPPORT [1997]. I read several of Gerritsen’s novels in the early 2000’s, but I was reliant upon rented Unabridged Audio Books in those days, and I missed out on the opportunity to read this one back then. It’s interesting because BLOODSTREAM is a departure from its two predecessors and impresses me more as a sci-fi/horror hybrid interspersed with her trademark frantic ER scenes which graphically depict the escalating panic of emergency medical situations in an impending crisis.The narrative didn’t so much pull me in as grab me by the collar and toss me right into the story line with a racing pulse. My first thought was THE BODY SNATCHERS when I reached Page 29. Five-year-old Isabel who adores her older sister Mary is suddenly confronted by a cold and angry stranger. The little girl is horrified and races downstairs:“As Isabel came tearing into the kitchen, her mother turned and said, “It’s seven o’clock. Isn’t your sister getting up?” “Mommy,” Isabel wailed in despair. “That’s not Mary Rose!”The passage where 5-year-old Isabel informs her mother that the girl upstairs in bed was not her sister Mary Rose is chilling.The protagonist is Dr. Claire Elliott, a GP with a teenaged son named Noah. She relocates to the small resort town of Tranquility, Maine, leaving the city of Baltimore and all of its bad influences on her adolescent son behind. The boy is still reeling from the loss of his father and he is angry about the move and leaving his school and peers. The harsh unrelenting Maine winter is only just beginning when one of her patients, a teenaged boy, commits a brutal and shocking act of horrific violence directed at his family. The narrative is alternating among several simmering situations in the small town of Tranquility among its teenaged population so the tension and momentum of the story escalates silently like a King Cobra rising from its basket in the presence of a Snake Charmer. A chain link fence of psychotic violence, murder and mayhem is about to encircle the small town.Claire is of course a scientist and she begins a rather exhaustive journey toward learning the underlying etiology behind the psychotic rage that fuels the teenagers to kill. There were several investigations that led to dead ends, like the colorful little blue mushrooms and the mysterious green phosphorescence on the surface of the water. I particularly enjoyed the author’s invention of an ingenious new brain-altering parasite that had spread through the small resort town. That said, injecting the Indian “curse” on “Slaughter River” and the scourges of violence occurring at 50-year intervals due to climate conditions while intriguing, was another stop in the Twilight Zone to arrive at the real truth of the matter. There was the obligatory “push back” from the town council and all of its small business owners when it is suggested that Locust Lake may hold the key and needed to be quarantined. The town’s economy is dependent upon the tourist trade. JAWS revisited. The other all too familiar conspiracy plot was injected as well. Will the big pharmaceutical companies ever give us a break? Are they always looking at everything through the lens of its potential application as a biological weapon?There are numerous scenes of gory violence that are cringe-worthy in the pages of this imaginative and riveting medical thriller/sci-fi/horror story. Gerritsen is among my favorite authors and this novel is but another example of her creative talent and flexibility in “taking on” a tale of horror in a little town in “Maine”. It’s not Castle Rock, but it’s d—n good!In fact, I would have awarded Five Stars in a heartbeat but for two reasons: 1-The author’s repeated portrayal of Claire (a General Practitioner) as exceedingly more skilled and knowledgeable than is believable or plausible. By the way, I am not counting the expertise she demonstrated in the area of Infectious Disease Medicine. She makes a diagnosis in an emergency case quicker than Dr. Gregory House (HOUSE), just before she was going to take over and perform a difficult intubation in an emergency medical life and death crisis! Later in the book Claire astutely interprets brain tissue under a microscope more accurately than the Neurosurgeon who removed the mass from the brain, while a Pathologist stands by to congratulate her and gleefully explain the surprising findings. 2-Claire repeatedly performed acts that were admittedly heroic, but completely unbelievable. For example, she pursues a big teenage boy who is homicidal and just chewed his thumb off to escape from the hospital, alone, with no back up into the woods. She locates him after he has plunged into a fast-moving frigid stream of water. Wading into a frigid fast-moving stream to find a violent psychotic patient is both incredulous and implausible:“She waded in deeper, up to her thighs, but the water was moving too fast and she felt the torrent pulling her downstream.”Claire is later knocked unconscious and handcuffed to the steering wheel of her car and rolled into a frozen lake, where the weight of the vehicle pulled her under the ice. Well, HOUSE and HOUDINI both start with an “H”. Naturally she went into a situation with no backup in the first place.So, all righty, then. Claire is portrayed as a warmhearted, selfless genius who has an Angel sitting on her shoulder. Suspend disbelief a few times, sit back and just ride this adrenaline laced roller coaster ride for the sheer delight of it!Read moreRead less
