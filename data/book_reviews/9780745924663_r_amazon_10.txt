I first read this book and it's "siblings" as a teenager and was captivated. It sparked something in me then that it still does now I'm forty. Although it alarms me that it has been quarter of a century since my first reading, The Paradise War has not lost but in fact has gained yet more effect for re-reading from a more 'experienced' point of view.I find myself reading sections over and over because of a beautifully written description or an incredibly well crafted scene. In twenty-five years, I have read few authors that can use simile and metaphor like Lawhead does. I know, especially after revisiting the Song of Albion trilogy, that his style of writing and story telling has very much coloured my own, it has that powerful an effect.Lawhead has a knack of putting you absolutely in the scene. You don't just read about the people and places, you feel them as though that wind were blowing through your own hair, or that honey mead were burning your own throat. It is powerful writing and absolutely picturesque.Your eyes get teary at moments for the power of the events, you shiver with the cold and bask in the sun with the main protagonist. It is an entire world crafted skilfully and vividly that makes the real world pale by comparison.I have enjoyed this book, reading on my Kindle at forty, every bit as much as I did in paperback in my teenage bedroom. I am glad I wasn't mis-remembering its transportive effect. It still works its magic and I can well believe it will become a much loved classic alongside Tolkien and other masters of the genre.
