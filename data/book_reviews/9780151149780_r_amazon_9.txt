In The Burning mountain, Coppel imagines a slightly contrived what if scenario, essentially what if America did not have the nuclear bomb by the end of WW2 as we know it, meaning that it was forced to invade Mainland Japan, Beginning with the southern half of Kyushu and the a massive amphibious landing on the Kanto plain. The military plans used in the book are based upon the real life plans for operations Olympic and Coronet, and Coppel has evidently done a massive amount of research into the time, in particular into the technology, but also into the mindset of both sides, that of the Japanese being particularly well explained. He also shows us exactly why the nuclear drops were deemed necessary, as nothing short of outright genocide would have ever made the Japanese surrender to the only foreign force ever to successfully invade japan, and ever last man woman and child would have blown themselves up or attacked in mass Banzai attacks at the prospect of killing a single American soldier. (please note this is not pointing out that all Japanese people at the time were fanatics, but, as stated Japan has even to this day never been invaded, and Coppel makes it clear that many of the people feel that they have no choice in their actions.)The book also has an excellent level of characterisation, to the point where it almost feels like a book of interconnected short stories about some very interesting, albeit short lived characters. He takes a very wide, sweeping view of almost every imaginable character that he could, from frightened marines to Kamikaze pilots, and this builds a very comprehensive picture of the conflict that he creates. The weakness of this is that it takes him forever to actually say anything, and the book, whilst not particularly long can move slowly- particularly as the majority of it is set over the first two days of Coronet, and it feels like it can take two full days to read. Also, my other  major criticism is that the Americans feel slightly overpowered, but I am no expert on WW2 pacific naval warfare so I cannot truly criticise for this.In short, a excellent work of alternate history that seems to sadly have been forgotten, but is excellently written and significantly better than the other work I have read on the invasion of Japan, Robert Conroy's 1945, which admittedly was the best book by said author, (maybe as he keeps his jingoism and obsession with  contrived and gratuitous sex scenes under control for once in his career), but this one feels far more intense, and goes  in terms of storyline where Conroy's only suggested, as that one only covers the invasion of Kyushu, whilst this one goes far deeper down the alternate timeline.Read moreRead less
