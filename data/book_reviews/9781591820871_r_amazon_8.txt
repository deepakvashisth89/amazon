MARS started out as a romance exploring the relationship of charismatic, motorcycle-riding Rei Tachino and the quiet, gifted artist Kira Aso. The series really excelled in early volumes as the two characters learned more about each other and themselves through their hobbies. (Volume 3 especially was really beautiful). Now at volume 8, MARS is suffering from typical anime tropes such as the spoiled princess-type childhood love interest who won't let go from an earlier volume and the bishounen psychopath murderer in this volume.Also, the focus has constantly been on how attractive Rei is (even coming from an otherwise normal teacher which felt absurd), what Rei likes, what Rei has been through, what Rei needs, and what Kira needs to do for Rei. Every relationship is focused far too much on Rei. It feels unbalanced and unrealistic. And when we do finally get some backstory on Kira in this volume, it's as traumatizing as you could imagine, and it feels suddenly decided from the end of volume 7 into 8. Trauma of this magnitude should have affected her from the beginning of the series, and the way Kira said she had coped with it (and the ways she and bishounen psychopath murderer say they are alike) all this time was never shown. The reveal of that trauma may also be really triggering for some readers, so I think it's better to know that before you read. This series is old, which might be part of why the trauma was revealed in such a cavalier way.These characters are really going through a lot of traumatic experiences and to be honest every single one of them needs to see a therapist. The story seems unrealistic at this point with how deadly the stakes are and theway certain characters change personalities for the convenience of the plot. The art is still extremely beautiful.
