Like another reviewer, I was quite prepared to dislike this book. I was not even prepared to read it, primarily because Robin had interfered with Rafe's story rather prominently, and the Robin-Margot affair had been very distasteful for me, to the extent that I wanted another, suitable heroine for Rafe. But I'm glad I bought this one and read it, because this book is absolutely delightful. Robin as a man in the bed of another hero's woman was appalling, but Robin as a hero with his own Maxie is irresistible. His pain, his memories, his shortcomings, and then even more, his whimsical humor and chivalry are a delight. The conversation between Maxie and him is scintillating and you just fall for the man Robin is, hard. Maxie herself is also no less attractive...courageous without being foolhardy, intelligent without being absurdly clever, and having all the qualities of an interesting regency heroine without making her either a doormat or a shrew. It's a tribute to MJP's craft that she has done what very few authors of regency era manage...making a heroine interesting and likable without making her partly modern. A well brought up noblewoman taking charge of her life and embarking on a trip like Maxie is ludicrous, but Maxie is not. In fact, the 'Sheltered Innocent' thing becomes a cause of much hilarity. Maxie truly cannot comprehend why anyone would be worried by her undertaking, and it's perfectly understandable, too. I loved Giles and Desdemona, too, and they are so delightful you look forward to seeing them again and again. A second lead pair is not a bad idea, so long as they don't become involved with the main lead. The scene between Giles and Robin was poignant and very real. But again, I was uncomfortable with Margot and Robin's 'friendship', and I took a star off for that. Remaining friends with your ex is all very nice, but expecting your current partner to like it is not. Rafe welcoming Robin with an open heart and Maxie becoming such fast friends with Margot as to wear her presented gown to her own wedding is a bit too much. As a woman, all I would ever feel for a friendly ex of my man is civil tolerance, nor would I go into paroxysms of joy to see any ex of mine blissfully happy with another woman. It's human nature, and I'm sure there would be exceptions, but seeing them in Robin and Margo and Maxie and Rafe just doesn't do it for me. This alone soured it a bit for me. Otherwise this book is a pure delight, quite in the league of Thunder and Roses. If you are even a bit reserved about Robin as a hero, don't be and read this one. You won't be sorry.Read moreRead less
