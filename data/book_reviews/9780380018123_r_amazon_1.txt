I'm a big Heinlein and PKD fan (for reference).  This book is quite simply truly magnificent.  If you're a fan of Futurama, one story will have you rapt as our hero dresses as a robot, on an all-robot world, with specific mention of meeting a robot/computer named "Calculon."  Certain stories, and aspects of the main character are very Futurama-esque, or perhaps it's that futurama was Tichy-esque.  However, this book also heads well into intellectualism amidst magnificently farcical elements and simply divine wordplay.  I can't imagine the difficulty of translating something, and keeping it funny in the 2nd language.  The translator has done an amazing job.  The vocabulary in this guy's translations is worthy of a Harvard Coffee Shop in 1988.  The intellectual aspects of these stories are truly wondrous, with historical references that, upon research, end up being well grounded and truly surprising.  Did you know that Gulliver's Travels mentions that Mars' moons rotate the wrong direction as well as their approximate distance from Mars, well BEFORE it was known to be true?  Wikipedia agrees!  This book does have multiple sections (2-3) that are, at the same time philosophically brilliant AND like wading through wet concrete...but, they do have their payoff, and really add to the intellectual depth of this book.  The only caveat I have is one of the greatest things about this book...  It is written at a level that probably requires an intellectual reader with a great vocabulary....though a lower level reader would still get quite a bit, but would be learning patience.  Contrast that with most sci-fi that can be read pretty well at a 5th grade level.  But, otherwise... there have been very few books that I have read where I was actually laughing out loud, let alone smiling throughout, and this is one of those books.  Call it graduate level Hitchhiker's Guide... call it high brow Futurama.... Call it sciencey Vonnegut.  If this review spoke to you and your tastes, you owe it to yourself to check it out.  From my own experience, Lem's books are all over the place...psycho mind-melters (Futurological Congress?), dark slogs (Fiasco), utterly ridiculous farce (Star Diaries)...and the more I read, the more I realize I should probably re-read the stuff I've already read, as I might have had the wrong mindset.  But, all do seem to contain very interesting philosophy and consideration of the human spirit on a quite high level.  I'll be continuing my way through his catalog...  and it seems there are other books of this hero's adventures in his catalog too.  Enjoy!P.S.
