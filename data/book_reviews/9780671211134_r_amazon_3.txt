The Slax company, owned by Harry Zimmerman and his sister Norma Zimmerman Lippert, has a factory in Puerto Rico. Suddenly, the factory is being sabotaged. Repeatedly. The Slax company had borrowed 3 million from the Sloan bank's Commercial Credit division to set up their Puerto Rico factory.  The International Investments division's position is that this mess at the Slax factory could have been avoided if Commercial Credit had sent the request to them and their Puerto Rican banking experts. This internal argument ends up in Senior VP John Putnam Thatcher's office. He makes a trip down to San Juan, Puerto Rico to investigate the trouble and keep the  Sloan from losing 3 million dollars. When he arrives at the factory for a meeting, it is discovered that a foreman has been murdered.  Eventually, reluctantly, John Thatcher figures out who is the murderer and assists the Puerto Rican Police Captain investigating the case in capturing the murderer.  Lots of red herrings. I did not figure out who the murderer was. Parts of this mystery were funny. The authors (the author's name is a pseudonym for two authors writing jointly) were adept, as always,  at finding irony in the world of banking. Even minor characters are deftly drawn.
