I find it interesting (and a little sad) to read all of the high school honors English students who were assigned to read the book and got little or nothing out of it, hacking out their resentment in pungent adolescentese.  One of the few things I remember from my high school honors English class was our teacher's response to a similar rant on how "stupid" and "boring" Hamlet was.  Our teacher's took all this in and said:  "Shakespeare is not on trial here.  Your ability to understand Shakespeare is."  Craven's work has been a minor classic for a half-century.  It isn't an anthropological study, correct in every detail of culture and history, nor does it always stand up to our exquisitely enlightened (that's sarcasm) sense of politically-correct moral self-righteousness.  I have never lived among the Native Americans of any tribe or era, but I have lived in third-world countries with cultures far different than mine and learned to truly love people I had little in common with, at first, and came to receive their love also, despite my rough and often bumbling attempts to find my place in their world.
