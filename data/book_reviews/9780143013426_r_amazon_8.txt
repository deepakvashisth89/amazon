As the story opens, we learn of a Thomas Vanderlinden, the next-door neighbour of the man who will finally hear a story that has been kept secret for many years.  Thomas is the son of Rachel and Rowland Vanderlinden.  Though the next part is difficult to fathom, Rowland, an anthropologist, leaves Rachel whom he loves,  to study the people of a faraway tiny island and never returns.  But then a man calling himself her husband, Rowland Vanderlinden, shows up at her door years later and she lets him in.  She makes him promise to never tell her his true identity and they reside together happily just like in the fairy tales.  Improbable as that sounds, that is the story.But then we are told the tale by Thomas, her son, as he lies dying in the hospital.  When Rachael's second, Rowland is killed in action during the war, she turns her life into helping others through a doctor she met.  Later in life, she asks her son Thomas to find the first Rowland and bring him home so that she can get some answers from him regarding her earlier life.  Now the book turns into an Indiana Jones-type movie with Thomas experiencing some of the most difficult times of his life to find this man tucked away on a tiny island in the middle of the Pacific ocean.This book was amazing and like nothing I have ever read before.  The research on the people of these islands, their traditions, and centuries-old beliefs, as well as the many ways a person can die just from living in that environment, is astonishing.
