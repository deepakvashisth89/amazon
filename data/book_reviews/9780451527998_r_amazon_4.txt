What to say about Ivanhoe? To be fair, the author, Sir Walter Scott, already had a strike against him. I had read another of his books in college for class, Waverly (at least, I think it was in college), and it was terrible. Or at least that’s all I remember of it. Seriously, I don’t even remember what that book was about. But Ivanhoe, I’ve seen that book mentioned in other fiction and non-fiction books for years. I couldn’t tell you what books they were, but I remember Ivanhoe. I got it through Bookbub some time ago, either because it was free or discounted. And it sat waiting to be read. Now I have read it.And the verdict is: I need to watch a movie version of it first.It was interesting because it was written in the 1800s but was set in the 1200s, and the language was mirroring that of the 1200s. From the language side, that’s kind of cool, but it also did require extra brain power at times to translate it mentally to modern-day English. That’s not a bad thing; it just means extra effort in reading.It also felt like a fan fiction, a historical fan fiction, perhaps? It was set during the time of King Richard the Lionheart and Robin Hood, making this either historical fiction (if Robin Hood was a real person) or historical fan fiction. I liked the story but got so lost in the plot that I think watching an adaptation would be helpful, and then maybe I would enjoy the book more if I were to read it again in the future.All in all, it has potential; I just probably should watch the movie first before tackling the book again.Read moreRead less
