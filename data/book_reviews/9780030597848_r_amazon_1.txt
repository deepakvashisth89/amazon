Falling Star is the fifth novel by Patricia Moyes featuring Chief Inspector Henry Tibbett of Scotland Yard.  Moyes takes a different approach in this story from her usual third person narrative.  This story is told from the first-person point of view of Anthony “Pudge” Coombe-Peters.  Pudge is described as a stout balding young man of 28 who comes from a wealthy family. Pudge has become the primary investor in a film company, and when the novel opens is trying desperately to keep the production on budget while dealing with the personally insulting behavior of his partners who also seem to have to no concerns about following a budget.  This is an interesting novel.  About a third of the way through, I decided to see what reviewers said about the book, which is not at all what I usually do.  Most if not all reviewers talked about how unpleasant Pudge is (a frequent word used to describe him is “priggish”) and how he was a poor choice of narrator.  I don’t know what it says about me, but I actually identified more with Pudge than any of the other characters, who all seemed pretty awful to me.  Pudge makes some poor decisions throughout the novel, but the reader can understand why he makes most of them.The plot concerns that attempt to keep the movie production moving forward despite problems with weather and stereotypically temperamental stars.  When the leading man dies in what appears to be an accident, it appears the production is doomed.  The novel takes off from there – was the leading man murdered?  Blackmail, more unexplained deaths,  and the entrance of Henry Tibbett make the second half of the novel, especially the last third, far more interesting to me.  Halfway through, I thought this might be the first Moyes book that I gave 2 stars.  I was having trouble wanting to read more.  I decided to plow through it and found to my surprise that it got much better. I ended up enjoying it thoroughly and was satisfied by the conclusion.  So how does one rate a novel like this, when the first part of the novel was a bit of an unpleasant experience?  I expect I will enjoy the entire book more in a second reading.  Again, I wish that we would rate on a 10-point scale.  I think I appreciate the novelty of having a first-person account with a character that has faults more than most people.
