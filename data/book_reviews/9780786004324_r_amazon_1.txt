This is not bad writing but I was not impressed by the author's attempt to rip off Mary Godwin Shelley.Also, I could not understand how two genteel siblings could have been raised by such a common acting and speaking father.  I was expecting to find out that they were the kidnapped children of some peer.  And the idea of of Jocelyn being friends with Mary Godwin strains credulity.And there were the obligatory  historical errors when it was obvious that the author was trying to get most things right.  There would have not been any 'police' to summon as the Metropolitan Police did not exist until 1829 and the author set the story at about 1816.  Blackstock likely would not have hanged for any crime since he would have been tried by the House of Lords and could have pleaded privilege to escape a death sentence.Finally naming a heroine from the Regency Era 'Jocelyn' would be the equivalent of the author naming her own daughter 'Kevin'.Read moreRead less
