Joe Klein's biography of Woody Guthrie was published in 1980, before Klein became known as a liberal political writer and columnist. It's still, in my view, the definitive study of the Dust Bowl singer-songwriter who composed "This Land is Your Land" and condensed John Steinbeck's "The Grapes of Wrath" into a six-minute ballad.Klein had access to Guthrie's private papers, and his book is well-detailed and documented and generally shows a thoughtful writing similar to Klein's columns. Klein is balanced in discussing Guthrie's political involvements, and ultimately tends to see him as a kind of Whitmanesque American bard and activist. Unlike later biographers, Klein doesn't get side-tracked in sniping at other folklore scholars, and his book has the definite advantage of being well-written.
