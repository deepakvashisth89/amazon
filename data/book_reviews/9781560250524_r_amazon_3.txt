I read the mixed reviews on this book with interest, and finally decided to give it a whirl. I grew up in Miami and I can remember the first influx of refugees coming out of Cuba after the overthrow of Batista. I grew up there, and though I am an Anglo I lived in 'Little Havana' for many years. Growing to adulthood in that environment I couldn't help but be somewhat aware of the goings on in the Cuban community.That and being a 'student' of the terrible events of November 22nd gave enough knowledge of the main players to follow this book with no problem. I was just 15 years old when JFK was assassinated, and needless to say, it made an impression on me that continues to this day. I've read many of the plethora of books/articles that have come out since the event, and I am a firm believer in a conspiracy.What makes me so enthusiastic about Gaeton Fonzi's The Last Investigation is that the content is more about the workings of the Church Committee, and the HSCA than about the assassination itself. There were some new revelations for me personally. For instance, I'd stuck with the assumption that JFK, quoted as saying he was going to break the CIA into a thousand pieces, and spread it to the four winds, as the final policy change that assured his demise.Fonzi details that though Kennedy fired Dulles, Bissel, and Cabell, he actually utilized the agency more afterwards, and gave it greater power in his attempt to overthrow the Castro regime. That, and detailing how neither the Church committee, nor the House Subcommittee on Assassinations, were serious about a thorough investigation. The obfuscation of the various government bodies immediately after the event, and continuing throughout the years, to the present day is remarkable.To better understand what the media is doing in this current presidential election cycle, along with the powers that be, this is a great book to read. When I was ten years old my 78 year old grandfather told me, "Believe half of what you see, and nothing that you read." At the time I didn't understand that at all, but the older I get, the more sense it makes.Read moreRead less
