My whole Frank Baum journey began after watching "The Great and Powerful Oz" in the theatre, while I liked it fine enough it started me wondering about the oz books themselves. This led me to download the 14 book wizard of Oz collection, and while reading these books and gathering side information, I was forced to stop to read two side books as they related to characters who were about to be introduced into the main series.In many ways Sky Island and The Sea Fairies are superior to Oz books, but at the same time the first of these books has what I feel is a fatal flaw within it. In the Oz books, to protagonists have a hand in the adventures, actively participating in whatever heroics are required.Spoilers for The Sea Fairies, as this is not the case here. They are, for all intensive purposes just along for the ride. It is a good ride, granted, and the descriptions of the underwater world is fun and interesting (Since many of these creatures they meet are real creatures, it tends to not fly too far into the usual weird).For me it was a let down not to have Captain and Trot be at all useful, and their helplessness takes something from the book that even the slightly better descriptive writing cannot make up for.For the most part, I liked this book, even if some of the lessons might be questionable, the main lesson is still worthwhile. I also enjoyed the conversations and relationship Trot and Captain Bill had, and while under the sea is slightly less magical, and crazy as Oz, it is a refreshing change of pace after having read over half a Dozen books in the anything goes Fairy land of Oz.Read moreRead less
