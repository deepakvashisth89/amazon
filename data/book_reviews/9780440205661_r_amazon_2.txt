From beginning to end, it is well done.  I find this series with Brady Coyne very comfortable.  This novel was read back to back with A Void in Hearts, which is also well done, but with a clunky conclusion.  There are three murders herein that are rather unsolvable until Tapply runs out of room at the end of the novel and leaves the reader only one alternative for a solution, excepting that the motive is well covered by a red herring.  Brady happens to continue slugging down Jack Daniels (and with a measure of "three fingers" in a tumbler at that!) and smoking Winston's.  He doesn't expose himself to Rebel Yell until Client Privilege.  Brady likes to fish, which gives the reader a homey feeling, and he travels around New England to places I'm very familiar with.  Whenever I encounter a particularly tough read, I return to Brady Coyne to straighten out my mind.  The books are not overly long and Tapply manages to encapsulate the appropriate images and character development without unnecessary wordiness as in the Goldfinch.  I think you folks will enjoy this series, although Coyne later gives up smoking and exposes himself to different alcoholic beverages for relaxation.Read moreRead less
