Emshwiller's Watley Caiper series is an unsung classic. The first begins in a dystopian future in which the poor are not allowed to breed (sounds like our future, if certain people get elected...). The poor can fulfill their natalistic dreams only by letting their bodies be used as "hosts" for the rich, who want to play/have sex/murder in the bodies of the poor; this seems like an old sci fi trope now, but it was fairly unexplored when Emshwiller was writing about it. By the end of the first book, the protagonist Caiper is stuck in the body of the main antagonist, Sentiva. In this sequel, Watley/Sentiva finds himself in a position of power--and pregnant. The book cleverly spoofs gender roles with an erotic charge while at the same time the plot follows the buildup to a social revolution. This should so be a movie!
