This is a cross between a mystery and a spy novel which might detract from the whole. It also includes some violence--and danger to our hero, Campion. While his wife Amanda does have a part, unlike the other books with her in them (Mr. Campion's Lady: The Second Allingham Omnibuswhich includes:--Preface with description of the development of Amanda Fitton as a love interest--Campion #5=Sweet Danger or Fear Sign or Kingdom of Death (1933)--Campion #10=The Fashion in Shrouds (1938)--Campion #11 =Traitor's Purse or The Sabotage Murder Mystery (1941)--Word in Season: Story for Christmas [a fine funny short story]:p. 509: "As has been known for centuries, all domestic animals have been granted the power of human speech during the hour before midnight on Christmas Eve. The reason why they so seldom avail themselves of this privilege is not quite so well understood."However, I detect a strange virtual parallel between the p. 509 quote and Campion #18's E.S.P. (i.e. telepathy) via the boys' "Iggies" and animals talking--especially as both were published in 1965.On the plus side, Allingham introduces some interesting and complex characters such as Lord Ludor and reintroduces Thomas Knapp. Many of the characters are ambiguous--more colorful than Black or White. Indeed, she handles the ESP breakthrough and its ramifications in a similar manner--raising questions for the reader to consider rather than some pat solution of her own. Iggies remind me of the invention of the Internet! It may not seem as big a change for us in the West--but it's long-term effects on the 3rd world, especially in totalitarian countries, is and will be quite profound. Not all of its effects are yet known and not all are positive either. It is interesting that Allingham does take a stand in that she supports "breakthroughs" being shared with the world vs. inventions which don't require such sharing.Overall, it's a good book, certainly worthwhile if you enjoy Allingham and Campion. There are also some pithy quotes for my collection (and your consideration):p. 3: "He did not stand in the dead center of his own universe.p. 14: It's some damned innocent. Some little head so full of wool it doesn't know it's bleating!"Self knowledge vs. arrogance and ignorance (i.e. foolishness). Something we, apparently, need continuing reminders regarding ourselves and others. Food & drink for thought and practice IMHO.p. s. This is the 18th Campion novel of the 19.
