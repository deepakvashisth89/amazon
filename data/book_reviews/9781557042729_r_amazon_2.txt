This has all the elements to for me to love it.  I lived in Paris at about the time of the book's beginning and actually went to the American School there (where the wife's author was a teacher).  When we left France I was the age of Matt, the son at the beginning of the book,  I am a woodworker, a home fixer, have a (summer) home on the water, and appreciate the ability needed to use the English language effectively.  I read the book in only a few sittings because I enjoyed it so much.  Some may find that the procedural narrative tedious, but I reveled in all of it.So why didn't I give it 5 Stars?  The problem was the last 20-30 pages of the book.  By that time Wharton had finished telling his story and clearly just wanted to wrap things up.  I couldn't wait for the book to end so that I could put it down. To me, that's just horrible.  A 5 Star book will make me sad that it's over, not happy.I was also left curious about his lack of fluency in French.  After 20+ years in Paris, did he not get fluent? We don't really know.  Was his world so confined in the ex-pat community that he never felt the need to really get to know where he lived beyond where it intruded on his life?  I personally attest to the fact that if you aren't speaking and thinking in French, you cannot ever see the world through the eyes of France no understand either (some of) its people or its real culture.  He confesses to not speaking French well but not doing anything about it.  That is a commentary on him but it also is a commentary on his book.  He shows some delightful interactions with French people around him, but as a memoir of an ex-pat I would hope for a little more self examination of his failure to get fluent.Read moreRead less
