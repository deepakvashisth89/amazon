Actual true account of what happened to Fantome the tall ship that was lost not that long ago due to misjudgment in hurricane Mitch's predicted path.  Sad but true story and something that can happen as sea to anyone who ventures off shore. As a sailor myself, I have been in several squalls that have had winds between 45 and 50 knots and one time due to my complacency as Captain on my rented bareboat in St. Vincent, seeing a squall line and not furling in my Jib sail and reefing my Main sail when first considering to, had a brief knockdown sailing a 44' Beneteau C.C. with wind that hit broadside in front of torrential rain that blew sideways and stung like hell on bare skin. Crew was myself, Wife and another couple. Crew for first minute or so was terrified when mast spreaders on port side knocked down over to sea level for a few seconds and while I was on the helm, I was able to release the main sheet and boat rounded up into the wind as it was designed to. Thank God everyone was able to stay on board. Crew after gathering composure was able to double reef the main and furl in the jib. After wind hit, within a few minutes seas went from 3-4 feet to 10-12 feet. We sailed on with 2 reefs in the main sail and squall only lasted for about 30 minutes then was gone as fast as it came. Lesson learned as taught and practiced except for this one incidence, "time to reef, when you first think about it" must always be the rule and never second guessed. Conditions changed so fast, it was very scary. If someone would have gone over the side, and no one had any floatation devices on, since we were in the Caribbean and enjoying a nice sail on starboard tack in 10-15 knot breeze, seas 3-4 feet, it would have been impossible to find someone in the breaking seas that kicked up minutes after the wind hit us.  To comprehend what Capt. Marsh of Fantome and crew went through in a Category 5 hurricane sailing a 300' 4 mast tall ship, is incomprehensible and something no one would eve be able to understand. They all paid the ultimate cost for a mistake that may have been prevented acting with a plan much sooner than they did.
