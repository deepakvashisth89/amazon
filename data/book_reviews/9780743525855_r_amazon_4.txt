“In those days there were ghosts everywhere.” Stephen King’s “The Man in the Black Suit” is a story that shows King’s unique writing style and ability to bring the story to life as well as make the reader feel the same emotions as the main character.  Stephen King is showing the readers all the fears that may be encountered in life. He introduces some fears at the beginning the readers can relate to. One example is when  Gary when he goes into the woods alone to go fishing and is encountered by a strange man, who was the devil. The thought of something like that actually happening was a lot to handle. Another example of a fear that the readers have to deal with is death, especially with the death of a loved one.  A lot of people think about death and it’s circumstances. The last example is just how the black suited devil is described.  Stephen King does a great job to make you visualize all the fears the readers may have and reminding us that these fears are very real.  Apart from making the readers think about all of life’s fears, it is also just scary.  One of the biggest reasons people read Stephen King stories is the horror, and this story delivers plenty of that. In terms of narration, I found it unreliable. I could never fully understand if Gary was dreaming or not. Gary’s father says that he might have been, but there are still other clue in the story, such as burnt grass,  to suggest otherwise.  As scary as this story is, it does have some reliving moments. One of the most revealing moments is when you refer back to the beginning of the story only to remember this is a memory. This story is one of Stephen King’s best; it is written in such a way that it will fill the reader with anxiety, and make us hope we don’t encounter a black suited man in the woods.  In brief, “ The Man in The Black Suit” is a shining representation of what Stephen King is capable of. He make the readers anxious to flip the page and see where the story will go.
