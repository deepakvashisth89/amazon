I've read most, but not all of the Brady Coyne Novels, but with this book, I completed the alpha and omega, the book ends of the series.  Tapply has a way of making the reader comfortable with his character.  With this first stand alone Brady Coyne novel,  I was a little surprised to find Coyne already divorced, but how could it be otherwise?  Tapply is master of the mundane and one can easily envision doing the everyday things done by Coyne as reality.  His descriptions of places like Cape Ann, Rockport, and Marblehead are fantastic, and sitting at the long tables of Durgin Park for a prime rib are spot on.  Coyne, in this novel, picks up on the troubles of the time, the Abbie Hoffmans of Clark, the Weather Underground, and Black Panthers.  He also proffers an in depth analysis of an autopsy that is very interesting.  However, Coyne does commit a tactical blunder at the end of the story that costs him dearly, I. e., on the physical end, and even though his financial reward was substantial (which he probably needed for hospital bills).
