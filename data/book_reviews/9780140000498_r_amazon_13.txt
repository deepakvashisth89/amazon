Given that this book was written over a 100 years ago and is widely regarded as a "literary classic"  I guess that it should need no real introduction as to why you should pick it up. However,for those of us like me who had previously  never heard mention of this work then it perhaps you need some incentive ??I came across this work when I heard mention of on a t.v show in some what revered terms and was curious to learn more.Having read it I fully understand why.Given its age it is written in a style of its time but this in no way detracts from its ability to truly move the reader and much of the subject matter has real historic value and also political relevance to today's economic and social climate.In a nut shell the story follows the trials and tribulations of an immigrant family to the u.s.a and the hardship they encountered. Whilst the book is a work of fiction it is fact based and actually led to the introduction of a change in the law to protect people from the appaling workplace practices commonplace in the meat packing industry at the time. Some of the stuff you will read will both horrify and shock you and make you really empathise with the suffering of the family.If you have also read more contemporary fact based works such as "Fast Food Nation" or "Bad Science" then the subject matter really chimes and you can see that some of the problems in the food and drugs industry are historically endemic.Whilst the author clearly had a socialist agenda - the book stands up as a novel as well as a being a political statement about the wrongs of the capitalism of the time.I found this a truly moving book and for both these reasons.I am struggling with the fact that it appears to be something of a "hidden classic" , as this book is one that can emote you in many different ways and is a must read. It is certainly worthy enough to grace my top 10 reads for this year.Read moreRead less
