I have been reading Terry Pratchett's Discworld series since the late 80s. Most of them I have read and re-read. Probably "Reaper Man" is the one I have read most often, mostly because it was required "reading-aloud"  for 15 years of teaching my advanced, in-residence shamanic practitioner trainings. For 2 weeks we would host up to 4 students, and Pratchett was "part of the training". We normally would choose one of his books to share with the students, and each evening, read it aloud. They also received a reading list of relevant books- the above mentioned, plus "Witches Abroad", "Mort"... if you know his work, and anything about shamanism, you get the picture."Small Gods" is an exception-- I found my first reading so painful that it took me many years to return to it-- because the subject matter is actually pretty difficult, if one has issues with fundamentalism. This is not to say that it is a bad book-- Pratchett has never written a bad book. It is, in fact, brilliant, humane, profound. In my 60s, I was able to return to it from a different viewpoint. I have to say I am enjoying replacing the old paperbacks with these hardback editions, which are sturdy and comfortable in the hands. I look forward to completing my "new" library of Discworld novels.Pratchett is surely one of the most phenomenal writers of our time, of any genre, and towers above all other writers in his chosen genre, in my opinion. This book, like all of the Discworld novels, will have you laughing and crying in practically the same breath, and marveling at how he can teach a good moral lesson, and teach you how to be a good human, without preaching and while being exceptionally entertaining. His passing is a great loss, but he has left us an incredible legacy. Read "Small Gods", by all means, but don't stop there.Read moreRead less
