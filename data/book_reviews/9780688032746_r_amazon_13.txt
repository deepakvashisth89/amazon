I don't often write reviews, but I do read them. If I can encourage even one other person to read this brilliant book I will be very glad. Jerry Mander's arguments are well conceived and clearly set out. Having grown up in an environment in which daily TV immersion was the norm, unquestioningly so, it is reassuring to me at least to appreciate the research and ideas presented here. They absolutely resonate with my own experience. There is so much I could say about this hugely important book on many levels, but it's more beneficial that you simply go and read it, with an open mind, today, as soon as you can and reflect on your own experience.  It would be interesting to start a forum discussion as a place of communal support - the kind you might not have from friends and family who wish to remain immersed and struggle to understand your desire to live outside that environment. It can feel lonely at times, if everyone else you know has a TV habit, but the benefits to mental, emotional and physical health are huge. You get your life back, make your own decisions, are no longer beset by nudges to assume norms or want things. I would like to see a grass roots movement enabling people to discern the impact of their TV  habit on overall well being and life purpose. It's incredible to think this book was written nearly 40 years ago and to see how pervasive the media culture is now in shaping and dictating our consciousness. Pop culture, high culture, documentaries, dramas, sport, 'news'........ The 'technique' (as Jaques Ellul called it) is the same.
