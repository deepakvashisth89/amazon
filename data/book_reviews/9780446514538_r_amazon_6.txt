As alternative histories go, George Bernau's "Promises to Keep" is merely okay. His premise was simple: "What if JFK had survived the assassination attempt? And to a lesser degree: "What if there WAS a second gunman, on the hill, not just a pathetic little man in a window of the Texas Book Depository?"From there, you would expect a full-blown conspiracy novel, full of details of why the Cuban exile community, aided by American Mafia, wanted the president dead. But details of that made-up conspiracy, while numerous, were treated like afterthoughts, apparently presented to make the book more appealing to the majority of US citizens who cling to various conspiracy theories about JFK's assassination.Bernau's real story--his alternative history--dealt with what would happen with JFK living. Unfortunately, that history, while plausible, is disappointing: not much change at all. In fairness to the author, that may well have been the case. We'll never know, will we?No other "spoilers" will be presented here to ruin future readers' enjoyment; on the other hand, I wouldn't recommend that anyone bother. The story is too cautious in its plotting, too timid in its portrayal of the characters involved, and too reserved in its view of possible historical outcomes--as if Bernau was terribly afraid of being sued by the real-life characters or berated by future historian. Indeed, Bernau went so far as to change the names of everyone involved: JFK, RFK, J. Edgar Hoover, Lyndon Johnson, Barry Goldwater, Hubert Humphrey--everyone except Fidel Castro.I prefer my alternative history books to at least use real names, not fictional place-holders modeled on real people--where's the fun in that?Read moreRead less
