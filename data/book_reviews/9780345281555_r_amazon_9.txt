I first bought this book back in the 1980s from a closing down branch of Ryman in Acton High Street, the store accepted duff stock from the other Rymans and we just sold whatever we had. In one of the crates was a consignment of books and one of those was The Death Freak. I've always been a fan of the trend of Men's Action fiction from the 70s - 80s so this one was a no brainer. Since then I've read it to death, literally as I've now had to buy a replacement copy.Truth is, on rereading it for the 6th time I have no idea why I love it as much as I do. The story sees two specialists, technicians -  one CIA one KGB who spend their days creating death dealing devices to assassinate opposition spies. One day both men start to think of retiring, a decision that two super computer employed by each side has already predicted and orders are given to kill the men.However, unbeknown to either the KGB or the CIA, the two men share a mistress, the wife of one of the heads of the CIA, who learning of their impending elimination introduces the two men to each other leading to a reworking of the classic 'Two Strangers on a Train' plot as the men take on each other's spy agencies.What follows is a series of ingenious kills as our anti-heroes travel the world picking off the other's bosses.Not great literature, but a great read and I love a good plot and this book keeps me coming back every so often. Plus I've just discovered there's a sequel called The Sleeping Spy.Anyways, if you enjoy ingenious deaths, spy action and bursts of glorious violence, this one's for you.Read moreRead less
