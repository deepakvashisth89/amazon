I was gifted my first copy of this edition around 2000 or so, and in the process of moving it was lost. More than once I mourned not having it.I was no stranger to the Strong's (my first hardback copy was given to me by a minister in the late '80's) and I won't try here to convince you of the book's merits. If you're looking at this edition then you probably are sold already. What I want to address in this review is the portability of the cloth-bound softcover.The hardback is fine for studying at home, but if you are like me then you are no stranger to studying in the library, a coffee shop or even sitting up in bed. I wanted a Strong's that could travel with me and not take up a lot of space or be as unwieldy as the hardcover. The obvious solution was the mass-market paperback edition, and that would have been fine except for one fatal flaw: all the paperback editions I have seen do not include the Hebrew/Greek Lexicon. In my opinion, this accounts for about 75% of the Strong's appeal. Any exhaustive concordance can help me locate passages but what I want is the ability to do word studies. This is impossible without the lexicon.This edition does include the Hebrew/Greek Dictionaries and it is small enough to fit inside a bookbag or briefcase, or even a purse. The pliable cover guarantees it will rest flat when you open it up.
