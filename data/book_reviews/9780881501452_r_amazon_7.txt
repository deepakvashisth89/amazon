This was a story full of possible murderers, set at a house party in Cape Cod. Our heroine, Prudence Whitsby, dashes off to Cape Cod with her friend Rowena Fible (if nothing else, that's a name that sticks in your head) - and the two women are invited to a neighboring house party to serve as chaperones for a newly-arrived young heiress named Desire (there's another one).Where the entire houseful is locked in by a devastating winter storm, the host is murdered, the heiress acts suspicious, all power stops working, the water runs out, and the guests are completely isolated with the unknown murderer, the local doctor and...to their great fortune, the local handyman / police detective, Asey Mayo. Asey is the hero of some 20 Taylor books and stories.I do wish Taylor had followed the lead of so many of her contemporary mystery writers and listed her players in front of the novel - this story, in particular, had such a lot of players that it was difficult to recall who did what when and to whom. I think there were six men and three women as guests in the house, plus at one point six staff members - that's a lot of people to track as potential murderers - with only Rowena and Desire having those delightfully memorable names.  Having said that, I've never read any Phoebe Atwood Taylor before and I found the story to be superior to the modern mystery, being more complex, with more activity, and the primary murder method exceptional and creative.[I should mention mine was a Pocket Books 1943 Edition.]Read moreRead less
