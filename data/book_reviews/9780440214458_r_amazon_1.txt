This book is just the sort I like.  It is a multi-generational saga - four generations of women and the men who love them.  There is a mystery, romance, friendship, a dazzling location (sort of makes you want to move to Colorado, even though the title mountain is fictional), environmental issues, disasters, luck, and just the faintest touch of the supernatural.Ari McAlister, just graduated from college, is wondering how she will support herself after losing the part-time job she has had during school.  She receives a call asking to purchase the mountain that has been her family's property for four generations now, but she doesn't want to sell, even though doing so would solve all her financial problems.  She packs a lunch and heads out for a picnic, and while eating she pulls out a paper and thinks to check the numbers for the Sweepstakes - a kind of lottery that she has recently bought a ticket for.  It turns out she has won!We are treated to the stories of her great-grandmother, Morna, her grandmother, Bridey, and her mother, Trish.  We also find out how they and their menfolk and friends have interacted with various members of Denver's high society over the years.  Much of the influence of the society folks has been detrimental to Ari's family.Ari winds up having to choose between the scions of two of the families who were important in her great-grandfather's time.  Along the way she meets up with a genealogist who helps her unravel the secrets of Morna's life that nobody in the family was previously a party to and wages a difficult campaign to save her mountain and the surrounding area from development.Read moreRead less
