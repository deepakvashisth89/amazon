Raymond Chandler is one of the inventors of Noir Mystery.  His main character, Phillip Marlowe, is one of the most intelligent and, when necessary, brutal detectives until the coming of Jack Reacher. But, when reading a Chandler mystery you get an extra gift. You get laughs.  You get a level of prose seen by me only in one other writer- Dashiell Hammett, one of the other inventors of noir mystery. I think that Chandler is even better.  The style is luscious while attempting to portray Marlowe as a hard character.  Behind the hard there is a sense of justice, intelligence, and a wonderful way of seeing people in all of their imperfections.  This writer is the only one I can remember in which I enjoy reading descriptions of the characters.  The book is written in the first person, so Marlowe doing the describing.  His take on people is clear, instantly picturable, and very often very funny. The prose used in these is often delicious and yet never seems too long.In this story, Marlowe is hired by a not very likeable rich woman from which a very valuable coin has been stolen.  She blames the wife of her son of whom she does not approve.  The wife has disappeared.  Marlowe is to hunt her down, get the coin back and persuade her to divorce the momma's boy son. Along the way, someone is making Marlowe's contacts dead.  He now has to find out who and make sure that the law doesn't think it was him. A copy of the coin shows up. From whom?  Why?  Is it the real thing or just a good fake? Twenty dollars worth of gold in 1942 is three thousand dollars now. So, the fake is not without value.  But the real coin with a history would be worth thousands even then. The stakes are high enough.  Add to that, the complicated relationships between the mamma's boy and his creditors, the wife, who is tired of the dull life offered by the boy and the penny-pinching smothering, nasty mother and an assortment of architypes of the noir mystery catalog and you have a grand mix.Chandler is a master at juggling the involvement of those living and dying in his stories and the tap dance Marlowe does with the representatives of the legal authorities. It's a grand journey.  You'll love it if you like mysteries, the movies of Bogart, or any no-nonsense leading characters. The silky prose of Chandler is the bonus.Read moreRead less
