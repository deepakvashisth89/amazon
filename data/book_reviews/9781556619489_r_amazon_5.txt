After reading the first book in the series 'The Widow Of Larkspur Inn',I couldn't wait to revisit the characters and the village Of Gresham.Once again this author has amazed me with her ability to fill one novel with so much going on,while at the same time making it all flow so smoothly,as another reviewer stated. I did not get the feeling of 'jumping around too much',as some other books have made me feel.I'm not sure how she makes so much going on feel like such a fluid transition,but she succeeds,amazingly! The depth of coverage she gives her characters,make you feel as if you personally know them,that and the fact that readers can relate to so much that the characters experience.Who hasn't experienced torment at the hands of bullies,or those in Cliques?Who hasn't experienced the sadness of a dear friend moving away,or shared the joy of a friend's joy,or feelings of insecurity,and feelings of hope? All those are covered here and so much more,as well.We get to know the young woman Mercy Sanders,the only(so far)decent member of the village's most disreputable family who fears she will never be able to marry,since her widowed father and brothers will not allow anyone to court her for fear of losing their cook and housekeeper. Mrs. Kingston,a lodger at the Larkspur Inn comes to her rescue with a hilarious plan.We also get to know some new characters. The heartwarming story of Seth Langford who moves to Gresham with Thomas, the 7 year old boy he adopted,and Lydia Clark,the 34 year old spinster school teacher who returns to her family in Gresham.Plus a man from the past. We will read more of Lydia Clark in book 3.The non-romantic elements of the characters lives and happenings in Gresham are just as engrossing as they were in the first book.This is such a wonderful series,the worst part is knowing that there are only two more books,and I am going to be so sad to have the series end!Yet I will always be glad that I was able to experience it.Read moreRead less
