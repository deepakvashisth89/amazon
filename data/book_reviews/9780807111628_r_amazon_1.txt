"Gray Ghosts of the Confederacy", Guerrilla Warfare in the West, 1861 - 1865, by Richard S. Brownlee.The author has done a surprisingly good job of presenting an objective look  at a segment of our History that has long been overwrought and misreported with political venom, regional and national lore and blatant fabrication.... especially for a book originally copyrighted in 1958.Very much to his credit, and credibility, Brownlee is quite forthright about the attitudes and behavior of the Union Army that occupied Missouri under "martial law" for virtually the entire Civil War. That's something no public school teacher is likely to have ever mentioned.Also to Brownlee's credit, the reading is interesting as well as accurate and well-organized, and thus moves at a nice paceThis treatise also raises two obvious questions about the Confederate conduct of the war in Tennessee and the Eastern Theatre. If Lee and Braxton Bragg had copied Quantrill's obviously successful tactics of "total war" (as Sherman did later) would the Confederacy have made the war so terrifying that the North would have abandoned Mr. Lincoln and his war and left the Confederacy live in peace?Lastly, is there a lesson here for the Federal government of today?Read moreRead less
