Summary:The point of Dunn’s book is, essentially, to flesh out the following statement: “The spiritual caregiver focuses on pacing and then leading adolescents into fully experiencing their thoughts, feelings, and choices in relationship with the living God” (64). The three key aspects of that statement that flow through the rest of the book are (1) pacing, (2) leading, and (3) a framework of holistic growth/development in the realms of the head, heart, and mind. “Pacing” specifically involves seeking to truly understand the motivation for an adolescent’s behavior and caring for the adolescent genuinely; “leading” involves challenging the student to respond to God’s call for righteous acts from a changed heart (63). The book’s thesis calls for these twin actions to work in tandem.In the first section of the book, Dunn focuses on defining what he means by pacing and leading both practically and theologically, as well as how this looks in our postmodern world. In the second section of the book, in a series of five chapters, Dunn fleshes out even further how exactly we are to pace with and lead our youth (by listening, speaking the truth, confronting and resolving conflict, nurturing the adolescent self, and creating space for spiritual journeys). In the last section, Dunn walks us through the early, middle, and late stages of adolescence (roughly equivalent to junior high, high school, and college). Dunn concludes his book with an instructive chapter to assist youth ministers in keeping from burning out.Review:In my opinion, Dunn’s book is, without a doubt, the best book I've read on youth ministry in a long time. It was both scholarly and practical; fairly comprehensive without feeling bogged down. Most books seem to focus on the problems of our youth; Dunn’s book is obsessed with actually helping youth leaders to better interact with their youth. What I appreciated most is that Dunn’s book is down-to-earth and realistic about conversations with adolescents. Many “gospel” conversations in books are a little too neat and tidy; Dunn’s book portrays conversations as they actually sound.Also, though this was not the book’s main purpose, the book was also immensely helpful regarding spiritual disciplines as well. While some books will preach their list of spiritual disciplines as, in some sense, an end-all solution, Dunn recognizes that youth do not need spiritual disciplines to be presented as yet another “to-do” item that will solve their high school problems.
