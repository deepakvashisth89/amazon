This book was written in 1938, twenty years after WWI when anti-was fever in this nation was still high, and is still one of the great anti-war books of all time. Dalton Trumbo then had it suppressed himself after Pearl Harbor and it had been forgotten for thirty years, when the Vietnam protesters rediscovered it. Mr. Trumbo was still alive at the time and must have been very gratified to see his novel resurrected for a war that was worth protesting against.Trumbo here used a unique and imaginative plot device; instead of just writing about a man's experiences "during" the war he describes them "afterwards", when regular soldier Joe Bonham (just an "Average Joe," yo) wakes to find his arms and legs amputated and his face blown right off, so he can't move, see, hear, or even smell. But what's important is that his brain is intact, so he can still "think."And what he thinks is the jist of the book, as it all takes place in his head. In a stream-of-conscience he remembers his past life, his girlfriend, and starts to notice things going on around him through his sense of touch, his only remaining sense.  He can experience great triumphs when he can tell whether it's night or day, which nurse is attending him and even when he manages to thinks it's New Year's! But he is extremely bitter about what has happened to him.  He wallows in anger rather than self-pity, which finally leads to two hysterical screeds against war, which is what the book is about. At one point Joe even asks if it's worth it to fight for plain old Liberty, and then "what" kind of liberty. (Try telling that to people who were run over by the Germans, the Japanese, Russia, North Vietnam, and others.  It's easy to protest a war that's thousands of miles away: it's quite another when it's right outside your front door.)This book was supposed to opoen with an introduction by E. L. Doctorow, who presumably would have talked about its great literary merits as well as its social effects. Instead the Kindle version opened with a couple of hysterical screeds from war protesters Ron Kovic (of BORN ON THE FOURTH OF JULY fame) and Cindy (George-W-Bush-killed-my-boy) Sheehan, both describing how the book influenced their own over-wrought efforts for peace.  Well and good for them, but we'll have to what and see what they'll do once ISIS starts blowing up a few places right here in "this" country.
