I'm currently re-reading the series on the Kindle, and I am getting rid of the bulky paperback books as I purchase the Kindle versions.  The Kindle editions are the "latest and greatest" versions which update some minor items in the paperback books, including misspellings and text that required correction as a result of inconsistencies found after publication.As to the book itself, it is one of the early books in the WOT (Wheel of Time) series, which means that is fairly fast paced and does not suffer from the plodding pace of the later books, which have too many storylines to keep moving along. There's a couple warning signs here of the troubles to come - the almost childish view of male/female interaction, some unnecessarily detailed and repetitive descriptions.  Despite all this, I recommend this book itself without hesitation to anyone who loves this type of fantasy - however, make sure you read the first two books, which are great also, before reading this one or you'll be totally lost.If you're becoming a WOT fan and reading these books for the first time, be warned that the next book in the series begins to slow down noticeably, but is still enjoyable, while books 5-8 especially are very tough reads.  Way too many minor characters and plotlines are introduced, pages of description are written for unimportant things, and the male/female interactions are grating.  The net effect of all this is a ton of material which only serves to fill out books that are way too long to begin with, and kills the pacing of the books.  The main characters scatter, and major storylines only advance by a tiny bit in an entire book, while hundreds of pages are dedicated to seemingly trivial matters.  However, the pace of the series picks back up from book 9 or so on, and the threads from these books are absolutely essential to enjoying and understanding what is going on.  For this reason, the WOT series is very conducive to a "re-read" which is what I'm doing now in preparation for the final book coming out in January 2013.I recommend WOT as a whole, but only to those who are willing to be patient and slog through some very difficult books to get to what all signs seem to be saying will be a very satisfying conclusion.  For those who are not aware of it, Rober Jordan passed away a few years ago, and Brandon Sanderson has written books 10-11 of the series, and will be writing the finale.  He's done a very nice job of keeping the best of Jordan, while streamlining the material and tying up a lot of open threads. I suspect this would have happened no matter who wrote the books, as the series heads towards it's conclusion, but it has made for some extremely enjoyable books that really reward readers who have stuck with the series.Read moreRead less
