Some of the reviews say there's not enough of Michael Ventris' personal life in a book entitled "The Man Who Deciphered Linear B."  But there is enough to show what type of person he was - seemly cool, aloof, but obviously capable of great passion when it came to, well, Linear B.  As he died in the mid-1950's I think, and didn't keep a journal, most of the outline of the man comes from scholarly-type correspondence and comments made by friends and family long after M.V.'s death.  But there is, as I said, there is sufficient info on his personal life and the information surrounding the deciphering of Linear B is well written and my eyes did not glaze over while reading the technical parts.
