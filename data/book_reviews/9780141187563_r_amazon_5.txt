Moonraker is a James Bond novel that is sometimes exhilarating and captivating and yet at others, mind-numbingly dull and fanciful.  In the main, it (just about) works as one of Fleming's better novels, but is seriously flawed on several levels.The most infuriating thing about this book is the middle third section which gets bogged down in pointless detail about rocket ships, missiles and physics.  We also get more pointless details about food and drink in the casino scene and this obsession gets so dull that at one point, I almost put the book down for keeps.  Should you decide to read Moonraker be warned that it gets EXCEEDINGLY boring at points and will require some perseverence and resolve to get through.  Another negative for me was the formulaic quality of some of the scenes.  They appear to be lifted straight from Casino Royale without the freshness and tension of that first novel - there's the casino stand-off, the night-time car chase, the kidnapping of the girl, the kidnapping of Bond and so on.  It does appear that Fleming was struggling with ideas at this point and it shows.However, there are definitely some very positive aspects of Moonraker.  The early section, for example, is devoted to Bond's every day work life and here the overload of detail really works.  Being in the employ of Her Majesty's Secret Service appears to be an utterly tedious job punctuated by those odd electric shock of moments when he gets called up for assignments, moments that he spends every waking hour dreading because he knows that it's only a matter of time before his profession will result in his death.  This dread gives him a far more credible air than his self-assured cinematic counterpart and is also reflected in his relationships with women which seem to be driven more by a desperation to forget his inevitable fate than any Casanova-esque womanizer antics.  Speaking of which, Moonraker boasts of a killer downbeat ending, the calibre of which you simply never got from the vast majority of the films.For those still unaware, the only common thread between Moonraker the book and Moonraker the film is the name.  This is an utterly different animal from that most turgid of Bond movies and those that loathed the movie (all of us?) should not allow that to prejudice their decision to read the novel.I can recommend this one.
