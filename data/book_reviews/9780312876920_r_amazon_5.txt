Hominids is the 2003 Hugo Award winning novel.  Personally, this isn't the greatest Hugo award winning novel I've ever read, mostly because I've read a fair share of science and of science fiction and a lot of what's in the novel I've read in some way elsewhere.  I would consider this an excellent novel for those in their teens or 20's.Don't let the 444 pages daunt you, the margin spacings are generous making for a faster reading than the number of pages suggest.  Parts of the novel are simplistic such as the interactions between people, comments on religion and human culture, and even quantum physics.  But there are a fair number of redeeming features.  Sawyer brings up a number of issues as food for thought.  I did like being reminded of the appearance of anatomically modern humans 100,000 years ago and that human society remained somewhat the same for 60,000 years until 40,000 years ago when an explosion of culture occurred.  Sawyer uses quantum physics at this time to explain how Neanderthals became the dominate species on the Earth of their parallel universe.  I didn't know that Neanderthals actually had a larger brain, however I thought there were other reasons why Cro-Magnon won over Neanderthals for dominance of the planet. I don't know what I've read on Neanderthals that's still relevant, been disproved or discovered, or what Sawyer needs for his novel's suspension of disbelief.  The Neanderthals live in a peaceful society.  Sawyer doesn't comment that Neanderthals started out more peaceful, but rather that the introduction of a monitoring sentient-computer wrist implant helped, among other things, monitor any criminal activities and that the recordings of these activities can be played back in court for unambiguous evidence of guilt.  And once the guilty party is implicated, him or her, and anyone that shares half of his or her genes are sterilized.  This is an interesting concept for a sci-fi novel, but this just ain't gonna happen.  The distribution of any monitoring devices will probably be spotty, and the potential for abuse is a possibility, even if banal, such as monitoring one's shopping habits, and there will be legions of lawyers contesting any monitoring evidence results.  The issue of sterilization alone will not happen.  It's suggestion will bring up issues ranging from racism, economic disadvantages, to inhumane treatment.Reading the novel reminded me a little of the Planet of the Apes movie series.  Although somewhat different, both are used as a conduit to comment on what is wrong with our societies.  This is what makes award winning science fiction.  This is not to say that there are not wonderful aspects of our societies, but rather of our species efforts to improve it.  Four full stars.Read moreRead less
