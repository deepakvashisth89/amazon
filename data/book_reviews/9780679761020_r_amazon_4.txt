The first three-quarters of the book are devoted to stories written by Maxwell over a period of decades. These pieces (some of which read like memoir) evoke, in superb and elegant detail, the lives of people in New York City, small-town Illinois, and other locales. My favorites include such gems as “The Pilgrimage, “The French Scarecrow,” “The Gardens of Mont-Saint-Michel,” “The Thistles in Sweden,” and “What He Was Like.” What I appreciate most about Maxwell’s style is his deep patience in bringing houses and apartments, urban and rural scenery, and a wide range of memorable characters to life. There is a cumulative effect that results from reading the complete set of stories, which sometimes have overlapping themes and characters. The last hundred or so pages of the book feature a series of brief “improvisations,” which are less my cup of tea.
