I now have all three of Lloyd jones books on US military aircraft.They are all outstanding and this is the very best. You hear about books which are hard to put down but I read this from cover to cover in one afternoon. It covers not just bombers which made it into service, but prototypes and even sketches which were not even allocated a number. and all in the time scale from the 20s to modern times.Thus you get for each government specification the efforts of various manufacturers to win the contract. douglas, for all their success in airliners could not translate this into a successful bomber though for some time they had just one example of the worlds largest plane flying. Convair had the opposite ; a series of bruising and ever larger bombers (until the hustler and F111 anyway) and no success in the civilian market. and then there is Boeing....The first theme of the book is that America was the first country to believe in and develop the intercontinental bomber. From way before the second world war The second theme is that the advent of the jet age produced some bizarre designs while they got the hang of wartime German research and the human results of Operation Paperclip. Who could not be excited by the Douglas XB 43, Martin XB 48  or XB 51and Convair XB46. All swept away by not only the swept wing Boeings but also the straight and short winged English Electric Canberra.The failures from minor to classic are also explained ; the Marauder could be a deathtrap to train in due to its  small wing design. Then there was the heavily armed escort destroyer version of the Fortress...which was so slow that the bombers had to hang around to escort it home. Lucky the UK backed Mustang was on its way.An outstanding work of history.Read moreRead less
