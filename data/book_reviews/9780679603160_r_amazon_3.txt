I studied Russian literature for years and would ultimately rank the prose biggies as follows:  Tolstoy, Chekhov, Gogol, Dostoyevsky, Turgenev.  I frankly prefer Chekhov, however, to the megalomaniac Tolstoy. Reading  Chekhov is truly uncanny.  He utterly refutes our common cliche'd notions  about "Russianness."  His is really the most modern voice of  nineteenth century literature, without the "modernism" of our  century that has so easily dated. I fell in love with Chekhov partly  because his Russian is the simplest and most prosaic of any Russian  writer  and I was consequently able to read him without mediation.
