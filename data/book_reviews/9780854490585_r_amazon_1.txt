While the reference to Cleland's "Fanny Hill (Memoirs of a Woman of Pleasure)", the erotic novel from the XVIII century, is self evident this is not a period tale but it is set in England in our times.There are obviously many sex scenes but the author seems actually more interested in the emotional coming of age of the lead. While the passions Danny feels at times seem to be genuine and intense, the sex is quick, described with a plain, neat prose.All the unsavoury aspects of a prostitute's life are dealt with explicitly but as if in passing.This work is strange. It is too matter of fact and realistic to be entirely erotic; on the other hand sex scenes are too many -and clearly put there for the enjoyment of the reader- for this to be an ordinary story with sex included.
