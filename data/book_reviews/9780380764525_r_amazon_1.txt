I picked this book up at a second hand book store as an impulse buy.  It  was one of those stories that compelled you to continue reading it.  I  couldn't put it down and finished it overnight.  I really liked this story,  although there were areas that I felt could have been improved.Aimee  LaMotte's father was dead and he had left her without funds.  So what's a  girl to do, why adopt the name Aimee Fortune and become a Lady Gambler.  She was going to win enough money to move away from New Orleans with her  old nanny and live off her winnings.  So she begins her short career as a  Lady Gambler by booking passage on a river boat.The book begins with  Nick and Amiee meeting over the gambling table where he proceeds to win all  the money she's won so far.  She's desperate she can't let him take all her  savings so with no money left her body is wagered. She's positive with the  card she's got that she can't loose, but she does.  Of course she could  refuse to honour her wager but she does and surrenders her virginity to  Nick, although he doesn't know it at the time.  Nine months later Amiee  gives birth to a son and she hates Nick - he had won all her money, taken  her virginity and didn't even know it, left her room the next morning  before she awoke so when she did she felt like a whore and he had left her  pregnant and destitute.Five years later Amiee has married and lost her  husband to the American Civil war and that said war is drawing to a close.  It is pure chance that our two characters meet up again only this time Nick  is a Yankee Captain that has orders to occupy her home.I liked this  book, I thought the characters were well developed and the story written  well.  However some aspects of the plot were predictable and/or corny.  Despite the faults (which were few and not enough to really detract from  the story) this was a very good story.
