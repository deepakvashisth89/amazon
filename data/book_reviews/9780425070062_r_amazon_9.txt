I had to wait until I stopped crying before I could write my review, so be prepared for a maelstrom of happy/sad emotion at the end of the book.Before you get to that, though, you'll meet one of the most easy-to-love protagonists that I've come across in a very long time.  Jake Pickett is an elderly man living a life of quiet contentment & simplicity.  The only thing that mars his life is his beloved grandniece Amanda's paralysis, sustained after a terrible bus crash when she was just a little girl.  What no one else knows is that the two of them have the ability to contact each other's mind.  Jake lives near to an illegal chemical dumpsite, which a huge corporation is trying to cover up.  In the process of this, an unprincipled executive, Huddy, turns up at his home to try & get him to move away as Jake has been flagged as a potential problem as he has noticed the high incidence of people getting cancer in the area.While Huddy is there, he witnesses Jake entertaining the local kids, who love him, with a little magic trick.  Huddy asks him to do it again, which Jake is happy to do.  Through this one innocent action Jake's life is forever changed.Alan Dean Foster has created two of the most repellent bad "guys" in the shape of the aforementioned Huddy & the nightmare in designer clothes, Ruth Somerset.  Words cannot adequately describe the sucking black hole that is Somerset , a woman without a single redeeming quality!  At the other end of the scale lie Jake & Amanda, the problem is how do these two innocent people take on the might & greed of a corporation that stops at nothing to get what it wants & believes it is entitled to.To say I loved this book is a wild understatement!  It had heart & integrity; beautifully realised & balanced.  Jake is forced into an unimaginable position & as he works through the problems we are with him every inch of the way.  I'm comparatively new to Alan Dean Foster's writing & I'm so impressed.  He has a delicate touch, delivering tension & horror but never losing sight of the humanity of Jake's struggles with what is happening, both to him & because of him.  It's certainly more restrained than many other writers in this genre but that's where it's power lies.  It's in the love that Jake has for Amanda & vice versa.  He's been swept up in a tsunami of other people's black intent with no escape route.
