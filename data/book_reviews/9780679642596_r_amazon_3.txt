This is John Irving.  He is a Master Storyteller.  He writes beautifully in the English language..The first novel I read of his was The World According to Garp.  Here you are introduced to the bastard son of Jenny Field.  Here are your first experiences with coined expressions that will stick with you forever.  People do die in John Irving books but only to strengthen the story and my what yarns he weaves.The next J.I. book I read was Hotel New Hampshire, which will always be my favorite.  Here you meet his first person who doesn't grow, you run into phrases like "keeping passing the open windows," and "everyone needs a bear."  We get introduced to the first farting Labrador Retriever, and the first dog that dies (sort of) (that I'm aware of), in one of his novels.  The characters in this book are stupendous and you'll never forget them.  You get plot twists and laughs that bring you to tears.Then I read Cider House Rulers which is a relatively linear story but what a humdinger.  Again Irving's vast imagination gives us characters that imprint themselves in our brains.  This is not a funny story but what a story.  It grabs your heart and won't let go.And now A Prayer for Owen Meany.  I had a bit of trouble getting into this one because he opens with a long discussion about churches, and about their different beliefs and practices.  He discusses the various voluntary positions in the various Christian denominations and the titles and duties of each.  By the time he's done, fifty or more pages into the story my head was spinning with church information.  But this is a set up, a dry comedy.  This novel is written in the first person singular, but the person: Johnny Wheelwright is connected to Owen Meany by the hip, figuratively speaking.  Owen Meany is the second of Irving's characters that do not grow past a certain point, and another Black Lab dies, this time when he runs into a moving milk truck.I don't know what it is with these New England writers and their killing of dogs.  In IT Stephen kills a dog so pitifully and in such minute detail that I was crying.  It's the only King book I didn't finish.To digress:  Owen Meany is very light and his classmates enjoy passing him around, but he is also a genius and he knows how to make friends.  Johnny (our narrator) and Owen become inseparable (sorry) pals.  Owen wants to know everything about everyone he meets and he collects these facts for later us.  His genius is that he sees the future and knows what will happen to everyone.  There are huge sea changes in this story.  There is a production of Dickens' A Christmas Carol in which Owen plays Tiny Tim (of course) and a cooperative (combined churches) Christmas Pageant where Owen gets to be Baby Jesus after some character adjustments, and here is where we are introduced to the REAL Owen Meany.In all of John Irving's books that I've read, he goes to some length to set up a big scene which, as it explodes upon us becomes so funny that if people are sleeping in your house while you are reading, or if you find yourself on a public conveyance, your guffaws will attract attention and you will be asked to leave the house or the bus as the case may be.A must read.  The only reason I gave it a 4 is because of the interminable discussion on the differences between churches.  If I could have given it a 4.5 I would have.A Master Craftsman.Enjoy.Read moreRead less
