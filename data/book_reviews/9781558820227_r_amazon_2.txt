The second of Craig Rice’s John J. Malone mysteries, this is a fast-paced and sometimes LOL book with entertaining characters. The mystery itself isn’t especially clever or hard to figure out, but the writing is so amusing that it doesn’t really matter. There are a few things reflective of the time in which it was written that a modern reader may find offensive, but not enough to dismiss the book as a whole. If you enjoy a light, quick, easygoing mystery, this is a great choice. (And while it isn’t necessary to have read the first book in the series, Eight Faces At Three, to enjoy this one, it does help set up the dynamic between the main characters that Rice continues in this book.)Read moreRead less
