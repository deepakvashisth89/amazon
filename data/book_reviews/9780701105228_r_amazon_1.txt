I first encountered this work in the year of its publication - too young at the time to wrap my head around it, I gave up...but never forgot its catchy title.  I bought it recently on a whim as a Kindle download and read it through in a couple of days.Story outline (no spoilers):  Set in London circa 1958, 27-year-old and single Jane Graham is kicked out her father's house for falling pregnant.  She takes up a room in a dodgy-as flophouse.  Initially filled with self-pity and self-loathing that she, a middle-class woman, should fall so low in the world.  Before long however her attitude changes as she's befriended by the odd-bods who occupy the premises.  She discovers qualities in herself she never imagined and....(Sorry, that would be telling :P!)Reactions:1. I can see why it was so popular back the day.  It's a well-paced story filled with human moments.2. Although badly dated in 2014 terms for its treatment of social mores and values, the novel provides a fascinating time-capsule of how matters stood when your parents/grandparents were young.  Ironic but wonderfully evocative.3. Jane's external character is less well drawn than her psychological counterpart.  No bad thing, but I suspect a modern reader would expect more balance in this area.  Her troubled psyche is, of course, the entire point of the book.4. Well written at the technical level.  There's a pared-back spareness when necessary and full-flowered extravagance when required.  Dotted with many true-to-life evocative lines.I don't regret buying this amazingly good novel.  It's one that I shall doubtless read again in future when the mood is right.Read moreRead less
