I had no idea what to expect when I started reading this book -- it was a freebie and those are hit or miss and more often than not, misses. But Peter Emshwiller's "Levels: The Host" is a carefully imagined, richly described world. Emshwiller wisely presents the details of his futuristic Manhattan by showing his characters interacting with gadgets and guns and social situations. We hear them use language in slightly altered ways than we're used to. Nothing is explained to us -- why would it be? This is the way things are in their world and they'd no sooner feel the need to explain things to us than we would feel the need to explain a TV to someone from North Korea. The North Korean might have a few questions about what they see on the TV but they get generally what function a TV has in our house. It's an immersive experience and it works well and even though I'm not clear on how all the things in Emshwiller's world might look it doesn't matter, I understood enough to keep going.And boy did I keep going. This is a page turner that is so wonderfully imaginative and rarely drags. I say "rarely" because I do have one quibble. While I admire the writing I did find it a little longwinded in the last half. I was able to skim along through paragraphs and not miss much in the action. For example, -- and these are not quotes from the book -- instead of saying, "With each step he took, the feeling of anxiety rose", the narration takes the long road by saying, "He took a step and felt the anxiety grow. He took another. More fear filled his heart. He took another..." Skim a couple of graphs and you'll get to the last step. Fights between characters went on for pages without much being said. Needless monologue and rumination from the main character tended to put the brakes on the otherwise careening action. A little trimming to keep the narrative light on its feet would be in order, I think.Still, even with that caveat, this book is a keeper.One last comment to those readers who are sensitive to content that is sexual or violent or language that is course: Please, don't read this book and then write a review. Spare us. You are forewarned, there are cuss words, there is sex and there is violence. Steer clear.
