Everyone who loves Bellow will need to read this book.  It is breathtaking in its thoroughness.  It is a very detailed, masterful description of Bellow's life and work, though perhaps a bit more "life" than "work". There is a question of whether quite as much life, especially love life, is really needed, but then the reader of this biography will get insights not only into Bellow's life but also into the life of our time.  Atlas obviously has tremedous admiration for Bellow, and the reader of  this biography -- THIS reader did-- will go away with a far greater appreciation of Bellow than he had before.  And yet there is a problem in Atlkas's disapproval of aspects of Bellow's life.  There are no doubt moments in Bellow's exhuberant public pronouncements where prudence would have required more tact and more taste, but Atlas surely goes too far when he accuses Bellow -- repeatedly ! -- of such  non-PC lapses as "racism" and "misogyny".
