Many of the positive reviews here state they are a friend of the author, served with him, or were there, followed by a five star review. I can respect that, however, that does not mean the book itself is a good read per say. It's obvious biases and loyalties come into play, and perhaps some find value and interest in reliving the glory days through an acquaintance with the author, but that  says little as to the books itself.Much of the book was VERY dry in its tone, language, and voice. At times it reads like a service manual, while taking for granted that the reader will know what the hell the author is referring to when he uses certain terms. Many times I referenced the glossary looking for  a definition of a term used only to find no reference.I am a former police officer. This would be like me writing a book full of 10 code slang and terminology with no reference for the reader to gain any knowledge or understanding  to what I am talking about. It would be like me saying "so the other night my 101 starts going 10-8 on me".....most people would be like huh?....."101 is 10 code for female, and 10-8 "means in service", commonly used as slang in the LE world for someone getting pissed etc...instead of saying "my old lady started in on me yelling and screaming", the above slang terms would be used.Many times this book uses such language with no guide for the reader to understand what the author is referring to..i.e. one time he says "Alpha strikes were plentiful"....ok....WTF are Alpha strikes?  there was no explanation given and no glossary reference.I was disappointed with this book. I was really expecting an action packed book full of missions and by the seat of my pants reading.  I wanted to feel pressure and the intense scenarios of flying over the hostile skies of Vietnam.Instead, this book drags. It took almost half way through the book before it gets into any missions over Vietnam. When it finally dives into the action, it only covers two missions, briefly at that. There was little detail and then it’s back stateside for more mundane details about training. The author states that many missions has come and gone, but omits any of the details and instead talks about life on base and everything else other than the missions!If you’re like me, you’re picking up this book to feel the Gs…you want to get a taste of aerial combat and dive into a world few have experienced. Hell, anyone can talk about sitting around base. There is nothing unique deserving of multiple chapters talking about life on base and down time. The book also seems to kind of ramble at times. It doesn’t follow a very clear path from one scene to the next. It almost seems like the author suffered from ADD and lacked the ability to stay focused on one area, then gradually lead the reader into the next.One page will be talking about an incident taking place, but then go off into an engineering training class, where the discussion completely shifts into specifics of how an injection seat works, or some other lengthy lecture.I really wanted to like this book. I was so excited when it came in the mail, but it was devoid of much real action at all. Here you have a book about a pilot in Vietnam, and rather then get into the many missions flown, he simply states he flew many missions before heading back to the states, and instead fills the pages with everything but the missions!Instead of “Phantom over Vietnam”, it should has been titled “Hanging out in Vietnam”. If you’re looking for a book that dives into the meat and nitty gritty of air combat, I’m afraid this book is far from hitting the mark.Wave off Delta One, you’re off target.Read moreRead less
