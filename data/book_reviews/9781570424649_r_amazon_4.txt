I like Levar Burton.  He's someone I admire.  His novel, Aftermath, however, only gets a midling rating from me.  It's the work of a journeyman writer.  The first thing that disappointed me was the minor bait and switch of the marketing campaign/pitch for the book.  The back cover describes a gripping sequence of events which are called prescient, with regard to our current wierd reality, that lead into a story about 4 people who come together to achieve the goal of the story.Upon reading the book what you discover is that the gripping sequence of events which made you (or at least me) buy the book, is only a series of bullet points at the start of the story told in only the sketchiest outline form.  It's the table setting for the real story, which is about these 4 people who have to chase the "Maguffen" of the book.  It feels like the Maguffen story was written first and then the publishers told Burton it lacked pizzazz (because it does) and recommended he put a "sizzle" wrapper around the more pedestrian story.  Yes, the gripping events bullet points are referred to in the course of the Maguffen story but only in passing and only adding a tiny bit more detail.  It feels like a patch job.I wish Burton had cracked his knuckles and written the bullet points preamble into a full story.  It would have been a gripping read.  The main story is only meh.  It 'tells' rather than 'shows' the story.  Too much third person narrator exposition and single person inner monologues.
