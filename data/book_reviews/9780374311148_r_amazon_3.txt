I should preface this by saying that I adore the Carl books.  They are beautiful and whimsical, and since they lack all but the most minimal text, encourage story telling. You should totally buy these for your kids.That said, this is the story that I saw in Carl's Christmas.Baby (his parents couldn't bother to name him, apparently-- even his stocking just says "baby") is left in the care of the family dog while his parents go to church and then grandma's.  If you are familiar with these books, then you know that this is not the first time they have left a very small baby in the care of a very large dog.  Luckily for the baby, Carl is THE BEST DOG EVER. He entertains the baby, makes sure he doesn't get hurt, bestows a lesson about the importance of looking out for the needy during the holiday season, and eventually meets Santa.Which all seems nice, until you stop and think.First, why doesn't the grandmother want to see her grandchild, especially on Christmas Eve?  Is this a secret baby?  Is she in ICU?  Does she just hate babies?  And even if you don't like babies, wouldn't you prefer that your own children not flirt with a felony child abuse/negligence charge?  And church?  These people routinely leave their infant son in the care of the family pet.  I cannot believe that these people are god-fearing enough to go to a midnight mass.I concluded that the parents likely went to a pub instead, especially since they are GONE ALL NIGHT ON CHRISTMAS EVE.Santa appears on the scene, and is very jolly.  You hope that he might have brought the baby a pair of responsible parents for Christmas and a nice juicy bone for Carl.  Instead, he just replaces the baby's hat (lost in the course of the evening's adventures), and gives Carl a new collar.  It is a festive reminder of Carl's continued bondage to these terrible, terrible parents.  Then Ol' Saint Nick goes up the chimney, despite having parked on the front lawn and therefore having no reason to be on the roof.Carl's Christmas is a little strange, but small kids probably won't pick up on these issues and it really is a beautiful book.
