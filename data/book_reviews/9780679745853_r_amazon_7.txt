Sometimes a book contains just one idea that can change your view of the world, with the bulk of the remaining text fading into irrelevancy.  For me, this book contained the simple observation that the most likely purchaser of a self-help book is a previous purchaser of a self-help book.  This suggested to me that these books weren't really doing people much good if they felt they needed another one.  Perhaps the books generated a never-ending cycle whereby the reader hoped that the NEXT book they read would enable them to solve their problems, since the one they just read didn't do that.  I decided that the reasons that these books failed to solve peoples' problems is that people failed to apply what they learned by changing the way they behaved; they hoped that insight alone would change the reality they experienced.  This is similar to the way that a dieter might try one diet after another, when any one of them would actually work if they stuck to the program.So here's the take home:  read a book or two on the particular self-help area that interests you and then work on implementing what you've learned; continuing to devour these books will only lead to analysis paralysis, as you look for an easy way to do what will, in actually, require hard work.Read moreRead less
