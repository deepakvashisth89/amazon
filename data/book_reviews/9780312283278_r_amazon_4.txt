I'm far younger than Danny, and I didn't go to Yale; nor did my parents own a lunch truck.  But I really could relate to the awkwardness of a student at an elite college from a middle class background and a small town that didn't often send its kids to elite colleges,  the immaturity that led him often to handle things rather poorly - the selfish way he treated Cindy, the thought that he could avoid those concerns at school, the bubble-like way that an elite campus can seem, the bravado that led him to talk back to the mob, the way he focused so much on a girl, the cleverness that shows in his narration - all of it.  The ending does feel abrupt, but in a way, it fits; Danny hasn't grown THAT much, but then real life doesn't often have those types of epiphanies and while things have "worked out" for the most part, it isn't a clean resolution either.
