Beautifully written, elegiac account of Shirley Hazzard's encounters with Graham Greene on Capri which is a warts and all,  perceptive, witty, wry attempt to get a hold on his character and, occasionally unpleasant behavior, in order to cast light on what made him such a great writer. Overall, Greene comes across as human, with all the positives and negatives which go with being as much, but also a man, increasing, out of his time. Running in tandem with the theme of Greene is that of Capri Itself, it's history, and it's accelerated modernization: the harsh manner in which the island's old ways are increasingly being swept away by the tide of modernity and mass tourism. As with all of Hazzard's books, there is the sense of reading a writer who really knows her craft, whose style draws its strength and beauty and construction from observation of grammar and awareness of the need for clarity.
