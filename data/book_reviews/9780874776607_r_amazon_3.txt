This book works for me because it has inspired my teaching about unstructured and structured doodling. This not a new book but was copyright in 1991 before the new trendy about doodling. This type of Doodling has a history mainly in the 1950s where artists made artwork of simply combining general shapes and forms.The author talks about the relationship doodling has to Abstract Art.  The author quotes "If you decide to take doodling seriously and develop it into finished pieces, you will be working in a form of abstract drawing that can be very sophisticated."The authors distinguishes random scribbling from doodling that doodling has design patterns which she states "you build one line off another until the drawing groups into a designed patterns.I agree with the author that doodling can be unstructured and structured.  The unstructured has the stereotype of the mindless scribbles you made in the margin of your notebook or talking on a phone. The author explains that the doodling can be elaborately structured as the freehand or by using mechanical templates.On page 9 the author instructs the person on how to do a unstructured and structured doodling artwork.  First the author instructs the person to "Set aside 1 hour of uninterrupted time for your first session, creating a quiet, relaxed atmosphere, perhaps with some soft music."To Doodling Artists who doodle with Geometric forms, Abstract Doodles, Kaleidoscope Doodles, Infinity Doodles, and my favor is Alphabet Doodles.  I highly recommend this book to Doodling Artists (or check it out at your local Library) to read "Chapter 1 Exploring Different Styles" where the author dispel that doodling is just scribbles and has also eliminate the common belief that it is only define in the stereotype of meaningless drawing that people do when they are bored.Happy pen and ink doodling!!!PenandInk777Read moreRead less
