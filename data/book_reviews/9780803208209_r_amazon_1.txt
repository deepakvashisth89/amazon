Cather seems to need length to do her best work. The two long stories in this book UNCLE VALENTINE and COMING, EDEN BOWER are the best, but neither would win any prizes. EDEN BOWER carries on Cather's theme of the human price an artist must pay for being a great artist. Trouble is, Cather doesn't persuade us that Eden Bower is a great singer. So she seems no more than a Carrie Meeber, using her beauty to toy with men, discarding them when her career calls. UNCLE VALENTINE has a theme of an artist being crushed by the philistinism of nineteeth century America. Cather was to reverse that attitude when she wrote THE OLD BEAUTY, also a long story. Again, the notion that Valentine Ramsay is an important artist is just stated,not shown. He seems like a pretentious trifler.The most interesting story in the collection is "Consequences." It is the most unexpected story; it's what we would expect Edgar Poe to write. The trouble is, having set up a situation where a man must confront his evil doppelganger, Cather kills the protagonist, collects her check from McCLURE'S MAGAZINE and goes off to write a novel. This is an effort by a magazine editor who had "learned the tricks" of writing magazine stories, and cashed in. What could have been a great story of innocence fighting itself became a read-it-and-forget-it tale that earned Cather some money. There's a reason Cather didn't collect any of these stories (except EDEN BOWER, which Cather collected as COMING APHRODITE.)
