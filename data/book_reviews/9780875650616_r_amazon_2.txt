This book was rife with animal cruelty and incestuous thoughts from a young boy towards his sister. I felt deeply disturbed throughout the whole book. I know many authors write from what they've experienced, which makes this book even more disturbing. While the world is full of unpleasant happenings, so I understand it's inevitable that writers will feel the need to write about such stuff, I personally read to escape.  While I do enjoy having my thoughts, emotions, and preconceptions challenged - yes, even by books featuring topics that disturb me -this book does not do that well. It's an unrelenting bludgeoning over the head of gross, thoughts and actions performed by a cast of thoroughly unlikable, unrelatable, cruel characters. There wasn't a ray of hope or pleasant imagery to offset the tone of abject misery permeating this tale. I've read many stories, and heard many story tellers tell of equally hard times they've experienced, and each of those other story tellers knew their trade well enough to pepper their stories of woe with glimmers of hope, and to give us, as readers and listeners, at least one character worth rooting for. The one thing that may have saved this book, at least somewhat,  would have been skill with language, but instead we get overly simple, clipped sentences, which give the sensation of reading this book as sentence after sentence of guttural stops that jerk you out of the story. I admire an author who is able to write as his characters speak, but you should not have to sacrifice good writing in order to do so. Maybe it's unfair to Mr. Fleming to compare his writing to that of John Steinbeck or Mark Twain, but both Steinbeck and Twain are perfect examples of how it is possible to capture the language of the common person, while also keeping intact a natural flow to the writing.Back to my original criticism, and to further illustrate just how disturbing this book is, a visiting friend happened to pick up this book. He got about halfway through before abandoning it altogether, stating the incestuous subplot and thoughtless or purposeful cruelty was too much. He told me he had to go online and read something even more disturbing - in this case, the wildfires currently rampaging through his beloved Texas panhandle - in order to cleanse the gross impressions this story imprinted in his mind.I highly recommend skipping this book.Read moreRead less
