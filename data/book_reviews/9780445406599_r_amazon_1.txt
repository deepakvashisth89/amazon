This is the second book in the wonderful “Yellowthread Street” series, first published in 1976.  The series is set in British Hong Kong before the Chinese takeover at the end of the ninety-nine year lease in 1997.  For older readers it is a reminder of how things were, and for new readers it is a glimpse of a lost world.I don’t like spoilers, so I will not outline the plot, but I am giving nothing away by saying that the main arc of the book follows the detectives of the Yellowthread Street station of Hong Bay (a fictional district of Hong Kong) as they try to solve a series of murders committed in cinemas by a serial killer.  The police, led by Chief Inspector Harry Feiffer, and including European and Chinese personnel, have to deal with all sorts of matters besides the main one in this fast-paced book.One thing which struck me, and reminded me of how things used to be, was the smoking.  Almost everyone smokes cigarettes, even in movie theaters, something which was not permitted in the United States of my youth.  Even the medical examiner chain smokes French cigarettes.  I don’t know if fewer people smoke in Hong Kong these days, although I imagine they do, but the diminishment of cigarette smoking is not something I think much about and it was interesting to be reminded of how pervasive it used to be.I am so glad that I read this book, and I wholeheartedly recommend it.I was given an ARC from the publisher through NetGalley for my honest opinion.Read moreRead less
