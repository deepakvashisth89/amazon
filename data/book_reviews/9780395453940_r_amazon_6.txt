I was fortunate enough to discuss the ideas of this book when it was re-released in 1992 with the author on the David Brudnoy Show. I owned the book and had read it. I was persuaded. Almost. But the idea that human excellence can be delivered by a way other than the traditional one of competition didn't persuade me then. Nor does it now.Several recent bestsellers by people like Matt Ridley's Origins of Virtue or Todd Buchholz's Rush: Why You Need and Love the Rat Race do a better job of summarizing recent evidence from science, which largely go against Kohn's communitarian premises, regarding human nature. Simply put, Kohn is a romanticist about human nature. Realists like me don't read Howard Gardner, except when required to at a degree-issuing institution.At the time of the discussion with Mr. Kohn, I brought up the great libertarian thinker Ludwig von Mises and his take on the irreplaceable role of competition in sorting out winners and losers.Mr. Kohn is to be commended, though, on being a fine thinker and writer. His book is worth reading. His premises, however, are unconvincing. And it's too bad his ideas are so rampant at schools of education.With the hindsight of twenty years, I feel comfortable saying that what we need is not less competition--i.e. relaxed standards of home ownership--but a revitalization of traditional ideas, competition being good being one of them.Read moreRead less
