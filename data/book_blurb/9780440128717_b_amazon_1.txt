Published under her Mary Westmacott pseudonym, Giant’s Bread reveals a side of Agatha Christie few readers expect: serious, inward, and deeply literary.This is the story of Vernon Deyre, a composer of rare and unsettling genius, and of the emotional forces that shape — and fracture — his life. Moving from childhood to artistic maturity, the novel traces how love, ambition, and loss collide in the making of art. As Vernon’s music grows bolder and more modern, his relationships strain under the pressure of genius that demands everything and gives little back.Written with restraint, psychological insight, and surprising emotional depth, Giant’s Bread is not a mystery but a meditation: on creativity, on marriage, on the cost of brilliance, and on the quiet damage done when devotion and art compete for the same soul.Rediscovered here as a serious modern novel, Giant’s Bread stands among the most ambitious and personal works Christie ever wrote — a book that belongs beside the great literary novels of the interwar period, not merely as a curiosity, but as a lasting achievement.Read more
