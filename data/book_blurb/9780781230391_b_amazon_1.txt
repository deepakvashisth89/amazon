The House Of The Seven Gables: And The Snow Image And Other Twice-Told Tales by Nathaniel Hawthorne weaves a Gothic tale of a New England house and the family bound to it. Central is the House of the Seven Gables, where Holgrave the daguerreotypist and Phoebe Pyncheon navigate a legacy of inherited power, land disputes, and a haunted past. An embedded legend—Maule’s demand for the house and a missing parchment—turns the house itself into a character as Alice Pyncheon endures the wizard's coercive influence, culminating in tragedy. The novel explores past vs present, wealth and legitimacy, superstition against reason, and female agency amid patriarchal schemes. Interwoven with this narrative are Hawthorne’s Canterbury Pilgrims sketches and other brief pieces in the Twice-Told Tales manner, using a story-within-a-story frame to examine memory, property, and moral responsibility in early America.Read more
