McKinley) in 1903, he expects heroic struggles against snow, ice, and altitude. What he delivers instead is something far more unusual: a brutally candid, darkly comic portrait of a small expedition coming apart at the seams. In a genre crowded with chest‑thumping tales of glory,The Shameless Diary of an Explorerstands out for its unvarnished honesty, modern feel, and razor‑sharp wit.Dunn records everything: the breathtaking Alaskan landscape, the constant danger, the crushing labor of hauling loads toward a seemingly impossible summit—and the bickering, sulks, private bravado, and petty jealousies of men who have been too long together in cold and hunger. He spares no one, least of all himself. The result reads less like a stiff Edwardian travelogue and more like a contemporary Krakauer‑style narrative: propulsive, self‑aware, often laugh‑out‑loud funny even as the stakes remain deadly serious.This failed 1903 attempt was only the beginning for Dunn. He had already survived a harrowing 1898 expedition, and he would go on to explore the remote Kamchatka River, report from the front lines of World War I for theNew York Post, serve as a U.S. naval intelligence officer, and ride with General John “Black Jack” Pershing into Mexico in pursuit of Pancho Villa. But it is here, in this “shameless” diary, that his gifts as a stylist and observer of human nature are at their sharpest.Praise for Robert Steed Dunn andThe Shameless Diary of an ExplorerAmazon.com Review—More adventure books should be like this.
