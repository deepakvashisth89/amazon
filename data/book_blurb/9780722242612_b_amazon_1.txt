Life Of Franklin Pierce by Nathaniel Hawthorne. This biography presents Franklin Pierce as a man whose public life and private character illuminate the early American republic. Hawthorne sketches Pierce’s birth in Hillsborough, New Hampshire, the influence of his Revolutionary father General Benjamin Pierce, and the education that shaped his disciplined, humane temperament. It follows Pierce from Bowdoin College to the bar at Hillsborough, his rise in the New Hampshire legislature where he served as speaker, and his ascent to national politics as a U.S. Congressman and Senator. The narrative then chronicles his Mexican War service as a volunteer and brigadier general, and his reputation for courage, integrity, and compassionate leadership. Hawthorne’s account stresses that Pierce’s decisions—on internal improvements, the slavery question, and national unity—flow from a steady, principled patriotism rather than rhetorical display. The memoir culminates in Pierce’s nomination for the presidency in 1852 and places his career within the debates over the Union, the Constitution, and the evolving politics of the era.
