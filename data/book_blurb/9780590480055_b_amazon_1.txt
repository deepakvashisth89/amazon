Elizabeth Ann, who is nine years old, has often overheard her Aunt Frances speak in whispers about her "horrid Putney cousins." Elizabeth Ann, however, is forced to leave her comfortable existence and move in with those far-off relatives in the wilderness of Vermont when her aunt is unable to care for her any longer.Elizabeth Ann is first stunned by rural life—pets are allowed to sleep inside and kids are expected to help out with housework! Elizabeth Ann eventually finds herself making friends and appreciating her new family since living in the country brings freedom and responsibility. When the school year is through and Aunt Frances returns to pick up her niece, she discovers a happier, healthier child who has a new name—Betsy—and a fresh perspective on life.
