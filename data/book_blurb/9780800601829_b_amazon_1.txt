Christian Liberty by Martin Luther presents a concise, forceful reformation-era argument that true Christian Liberty arises from faith in the gospel rather than from outward works. Luther declares that a Christian is both free lord of all and servant of all, united to Christ by faith so that the believer's life is ordered by love rather than by legal obedience. He distinguishes the inward, spiritual man from the outward, corporeal life, arguing that justification cannot be achieved by rites, diets, or rituals, but by faith, grounded in the Word of God. Faith, he says, justifies, sanctifies, and unites the believer to Christ, making the believer a participant in Christ's righteousness and victory over sin, death, and hell. Yet true freedom also manifests in service to neighbor: voluntary works done out of love, not to earn salvation. The work critiques clerical overreach and ceremonialism, defending the priesthood of all believers and urging a balanced path between zeal for works and antinomian license.
